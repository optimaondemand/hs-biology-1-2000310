The Kidney & Nephron
Reading 28.01 and Lesson 28.01
Before you begin: finish Reading 28.01 and work through Lesson 28.01, including the excretory system, the four steps of the nephron, and how ADH adjusts water reabsorption.
Lesson focus: The kidney as a homeostasis organ
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: put the nephron's four steps in order, match each step to what it does, and explain in a sentence or two how ADH helps the kidney maintain homeostasis. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does one organ remove waste and, at the same time, keep the body's water and salt in balance?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Order the steps (4 pts, 1 each). Number these four nephron steps 1–4 in the order a drop of fluid passes through them: Reabsorption · Excretion · Filtration · Secretion.
2
Part B — Match step to function (4 pts, 1 each). Match each step to its description from the list below.
Nephron step Description (write the letter)
Filtration 
Reabsorption 
Secretion 
Excretion 
A. Blood pressure in the glomerulus forces water and small molecules out of the blood into the tubule  ·  B. Useful materials — water, glucose, ions — return to the blood  ·  C. Extra wastes and ions are moved from the blood into the tubule for disposal  ·  D. The leftover fluid (urine) drains to the bladder to leave the body
3
Part C — Homeostasis (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain what happens to ADH and to water reabsorption when a person becomes dehydrated, and why that keeps the body in balance.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A order: 1. Filtration, 2. Reabsorption, 3. Secretion, 4. Excretion.
☐
Part B: Filtration → A, Reabsorption → B, Secretion → C, Excretion → D.
☐
Part C: when dehydrated, ADH rises, so the nephron reabsorbs more water back into the blood (little, concentrated urine), conserving water — a negative-feedback correction toward the set point.
