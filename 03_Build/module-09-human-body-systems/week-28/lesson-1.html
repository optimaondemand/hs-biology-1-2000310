Lesson · Filtering the Blood and Balancing the Body
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Your cells make waste every second, and too much water or too little would kill you. What organ cleans the blood and keeps that balance — all without your noticing?
This is the first lesson of Week 28, and it opens the body's regulation systems with the organ that quietly guards your inner balance: the kidney. You will learn what the excretory system removes, trace a drop of fluid through the nephron — filtration, reabsorption, secretion, excretion — and see the kidney as a homeostasis organ, tuned by a hormone, echoing the feedback loops of Lesson 26.02.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to describe the job of the excretory system and identify the nephron as the functional unit of the kidney.
2
I will be able to put the nephron's steps in order — filtration, reabsorption, secretion, excretion — and say what each one does.
3
I will be able to explain how the kidney maintains homeostasis, including how ADH adjusts water reabsorption.

💧 PART 1 · WHY THE BODY MUST FILTER ITSELF
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before today's lesson: when you drink a big glass of water, what do you think decides whether your body keeps it or gets rid of it? (No wrong answer — just check in.)

Waste, water, and salt

When your cells break down protein for energy, they leave behind nitrogen, which the body turns into the waste molecule urea. The excretory system removes this nitrogenous waste from the blood in the form of urine. But the same organ that removes waste also decides how much water and salt to keep — which is what makes the kidney a homeostasis organ, not just a garbage chute.

🔄 PART 3 · THE KIDNEY AS A HOMEOSTASIS DIAL

ADH turns the water dial

How much water the nephron reabsorbs is not fixed — it is adjusted by the hormone ADH. When you are dehydrated, ADH rises and the kidney reabsorbs more water (little, concentrated urine); when you are well-hydrated, ADH falls and the kidney reabsorbs less (more, dilute urine).
