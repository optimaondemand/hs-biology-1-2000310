Week 28 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Observe a Drug Acting at a Synapse (Mechanism → Effect)
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will step inside a working synapse and first watch normal signaling: a neurotransmitter released from the presynaptic terminal, crossing the cleft, binding receptors, then being cleared. Then you will introduce a drug at the synapse and observe how each mechanism changes the signal — an agonist (mimics the neurotransmitter), an antagonist (blocks receptors), and a reuptake blocker (stops clearance so the signal lingers) — and trace the downstream effect each produces. You capture labeled snapshots along the way. This is clinical observation of a mechanism, not a demonstration of use; it pairs with Lesson 28.03 and gives you evidence for your Module 9 CER.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 28.03 and Lesson 28.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Synapse Interior / Neuroscience Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: stand at the observation platform beside the synaptic cleft, marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — watch one normal signal cross the gap before you add any drug.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Watch normal signaling. Observe one full cycle: release of the neurotransmitter, it crossing the cleft and binding a receptor, then being cleared (reuptake or enzyme breakdown). Capture a snapshot labeled normal synapse.
2
Add an agonist. Introduce an agonist and watch it fit and activate the receptor, adding signal the body did not send. Capture a snapshot labeled agonist — mimics neurotransmitter → extra signal.
3
Add an antagonist. Introduce an antagonist and watch it plug the receptor without activating it, so the real neurotransmitter cannot bind. Capture a snapshot labeled antagonist — blocks receptor → signal reduced.
4
Add a reuptake blocker. Introduce a reuptake inhibitor and watch the neurotransmitter fail to be cleared, so it lingers in the cleft and keeps signaling. Capture a snapshot labeled reuptake blocker — signal prolonged / amplified.
5
Trace mechanism to effect. For each of the three drugs, note in your notebook the effect on the postsynaptic signal (more, less, or prolonged). If the scene shows dopamine, note how a prolonged or amplified signal relates to the reward pathway — the through-line to your CER.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your labeled snapshots (normal synapse plus the three drug mechanisms) to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note for each drug stating its mechanism (agonist / antagonist / reuptake blocker) and its effect on the signal. A brief spatial recording narrating the three mechanisms may be submitted instead of the snapshots.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Keep the framing clinical and mechanistic — this observes how molecules change a signal, never how any substance is used. Two common errors to watch for and correct: confusing agonist and antagonist (agonist activates the receptor; antagonist blocks it without activating), and thinking a reuptake blocker adds a new signal (it does not — it prolongs the existing one by preventing clearance). A strong submission labels all four states (normal + three mechanisms) and correctly pairs each mechanism with its effect on the postsynaptic signal.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
The normal synapse is observed and captured (release → bind → clear), before any drug is added.
☐
All three drug mechanisms (agonist, antagonist, reuptake blocker) are observed and captured, each labeled with its effect on the signal.
☐
Labeled snapshots (or a narrated recording) are submitted with a mechanism-to-effect note for each drug — usable evidence for the Module 9 CER.
