Synapses, Neurotransmitters & How Drugs Act

Before you begin: finish Reading 28.03 and work through Lesson 28.03, including how a synapse works, the three drug mechanisms (agonist, antagonist, reuptake inhibitor), and the dopamine reward pathway.
Lesson focus: Chemical signaling and how drugs alter it
✏️ THIS PRIMES YOUR MODULE 9 CER — IT IS NOT THE ESSAY
This short mechanism trace is practice for the argument you'll make in the graded Module 9 CER essay. Keep it clinical and mechanistic — describe the biology, never how a substance is used.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: identify one drug's mechanism, write a three-step mechanism trace (drug → receptor action → effect), and connect it to the reward pathway. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does a single molecule at a synapse produce an effect on the whole body — and why can that lead to addiction?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Name the mechanism (3 pts). Choose one example from the lesson — caffeine (adenosine-receptor antagonist), nicotine (acetylcholine-receptor agonist), or cocaine/amphetamines (dopamine reuptake inhibitors). State which of the three mechanisms it is: agonist, antagonist, or reuptake inhibitor, and define that mechanism in one sentence.
2
Part B — Trace the mechanism (4 pts). For your chosen example, write a three-step trace: (1) what the drug does at the receptor, (2) how that changes the signal in the synapse, and (3) the resulting effect. Keep it to biology — do not describe use.
3
Part C — Connect to addiction (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain how many addictive drugs converge on the dopamine reward pathway, and what tolerance and dependence mean at the level of the brain's receptors.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A names the correct mechanism (e.g., caffeine = antagonist; nicotine = agonist; cocaine/amphetamines = reuptake inhibitor) and defines it accurately.
☐
Part B trace is correct — e.g., cocaine: (1) blocks dopamine reuptake at the transporter, (2) dopamine lingers in the cleft and keeps binding receptors, (3) its signal is amplified far beyond normal. (Caffeine: blocks adenosine receptor → drowsiness signal can't act → more alert.)
☐
Part C: addictive drugs flood the dopamine reward pathway; the brain adapts by reducing receptors (downregulation), so the same dose does less (tolerance) and the brain relies on the drug to feel normal (dependence).
