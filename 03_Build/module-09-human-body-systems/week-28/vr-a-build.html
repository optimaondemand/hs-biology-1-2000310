Week 28 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Neuron & Fire an Action Potential to a Synapse
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a giant, walk-through neuron and label it in the direction the signal travels: dendrites (receive), cell body, axon (transmit), myelin (insulation for speed), and axon terminals (hand-off). Then you will animate a signal: fire a spark from a dendrite, send it as an action potential down the axon, and end at a synapse where the axon terminals release a neurotransmitter across the gap. Finally you record a short walkthrough naming each part and the resting → action potential → synapse sequence. Building the neuron as a space you can walk through makes an invisible electrical message concrete. This is Lessons 28.02 and 28.03, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 28.02 and Lesson 28.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Neuron Interior / Neuroscience Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty build frame marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — build from the receiving end (dendrites) toward the sending end (terminals).
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Receiving end. Build the branching dendrites and label them: receive incoming signals. Attach them to the cell body and label it: holds the nucleus, keeps the cell alive.
2
The cable. Extend the long axon from the cell body and label it: transmits the signal away. Wrap it in segments of myelin and label it: insulation — makes the signal travel faster.
3
The hand-off end. At the far end of the axon, build the axon terminals and, just beyond them, mark the synapse — the gap to the next cell. Label the terminals: release neurotransmitters into the synapse.
4
Fire the signal. Now animate or trace the message: start at a dendrite, send the action potential down the axon (mention it is all-or-none), and finish at the terminals, where a neurotransmitter is released across the synapse to the next cell. Mark the neuron's resting state before it fires.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 60–90 seconds, walk your neuron from dendrite to terminal, naming each part and its function, and narrate the resting → action potential → synapse sequence as the signal travels.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one full snapshot of your labeled neuron (all parts visible) plus typed labels naming each part from dendrite to terminal and its function, and a sentence describing the resting → action potential → synapse sequence.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the neuron by building it in the signal's direction and firing a signal through it. Two common errors to watch for and correct: reversing the signal direction (it flows dendrites → cell body → axon → terminals, never backward), and treating the action potential as variable in size (it is all-or-none — a full-strength spike or nothing). A strong submission shows all five parts labeled in order, the synapse marked as a gap the signal crosses chemically, and a walkthrough that correctly narrates resting → action potential → neurotransmitter release.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All five neuron parts are built and labeled in signal order (dendrites, cell body, axon, myelin, axon terminals), with a synapse marked beyond the terminals.
☐
A signal is animated or traced from a dendrite down the axon to the synapse, where a neurotransmitter is released across the gap.
☐
A spatial recording (or full snapshot + typed labels) is submitted, naming each part and its function and narrating the resting → action potential → synapse sequence.
