**1. (Reading 26.01 — tissue type → function; epithelial vs. connective)**
Which tissue forms tightly packed sheets that cover the body's surfaces and line its cavities, glands, and organs?

- A) Muscle tissue
- B) Nervous tissue
- C) Epithelial tissue **✓**
- D) Connective tissue

*Epithelial tissue is built from tightly packed cells forming continuous sheets, which suits its jobs of covering, lining, protecting, absorbing, and secreting. Connective tissue does the opposite — its cells are spread out in a matrix to support and connect.*

**2. (Reading 26.01 — connective tissue includes blood; common surprise)**
Bone, cartilage, tendon, fat, and blood are all examples of which tissue type?

- A) Connective tissue **✓**
- B) Epithelial tissue
- C) Muscle tissue
- D) Nervous tissue

*All of these are connective tissue — cells surrounded by a matrix, built to support and connect. The surprising member is blood, whose matrix is the liquid plasma. Students often forget that blood counts as connective tissue.*

**3. (Reading 26.01 — levels of organization, correct order)**
Which sequence lists the levels of organization correctly, from smallest to largest?

- A) Cell → organ → tissue → organ system → organism
- B) Chemical → cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism **✓**
- C) Cell → tissue → organism → organ → organ system
- D) Tissue → cell → organ → organism → organ system

*The body is built in layers, each made of the one below it: chemical (atoms and molecules) → cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism. A common error is placing "organ" before "tissue," but tissues combine to build organs, not the reverse.*

**4. (Reading 26.02 — negative feedback opposes the change, not "keeps it exactly constant")**
In a negative feedback loop, what does the body's response do?

- A) It holds the value perfectly and unchangingly constant
- B) It amplifies the change, pushing the value further from the set point
- C) It opposes the change, returning the value toward the set point **✓**
- D) It has no effect on the internal condition

*"Negative" means the response opposes the change. A rise triggers a response that lowers the value; a drop triggers one that raises it, so the value is always being pulled back toward the set point. It does not stay perfectly constant — it drifts and is corrected, like a thermostat cycling on and off.*

**5. (Reading 26.02 — positive vs. negative feedback)**
During childbirth, each contraction triggers more oxytocin, which causes stronger contractions, until delivery. This is an example of:

- A) Negative feedback, because it stabilizes the body
- B) Positive feedback, because each step amplifies the change and drives it to completion **✓**
- C) Negative feedback, because it opposes the change
- D) Neither — it is not a feedback loop

*Childbirth is positive feedback: the response amplifies the change, building the contractions stronger and stronger until the process finishes. Positive feedback amplifies and drives to completion; negative feedback opposes change and stabilizes. Blood clotting is another positive-feedback example.*

**6. (Reading 26.02 — blood-glucose regulation; insulin lowers, glucagon raises)**
After a meal, blood glucose rises. Which hormone does the pancreas release to bring it back down, and how?

- A) Glucagon, by telling the liver to release stored glucose
- B) Insulin, by signaling cells to take up and store glucose **✓**
- C) Insulin, by telling the liver to release stored glucose
- D) Oxytocin, by breaking down glucose in the blood

*When blood glucose is high, the pancreas releases insulin, which signals cells (especially liver and muscle) to take glucose out of the blood and store it, lowering blood sugar. Glucagon does the opposite — it raises low blood sugar. Remember: insulin lowers, glucagon raises.*

**7. (Reading 26.03 — skin is an organ / the largest organ)**
Why is the skin correctly called an organ, and the body's largest one?

- A) Because it is a single sheet of one kind of cell
- B) Because it is made only of dead cells
- C) Because it combines several tissue types working together on shared tasks **✓**
- D) Because it is not attached to any other body part

*An organ is two or more tissues working together on a task. Skin combines epithelial, connective, nervous, and muscle tissue plus blood vessels — so it is a genuine organ, and spread flat it is the largest one. It is far more than a passive wrapper.*

**8. (Reading 26.03 — thermoregulation; skin responses oppose the change)**
When your body overheats, how does the skin help cool you down?

- A) Blood vessels in the skin constrict and sweating stops
- B) Sweat glands release sweat and skin blood vessels dilate to shed heat **✓**
- C) The skin thickens to trap more heat inside
- D) The skin produces extra vitamin D to lower temperature

*When overheated, the skin sweats (evaporation carries heat away) and its blood vessels dilate to bring warm blood to the surface and release heat. Both responses cool you, opposing the rise — negative feedback in action. Constriction and stopping sweat are the cold-day responses.*

**9. (Reading 26.04 — ligament vs. tendon)**
What is the difference between a ligament and a tendon?

- A) A ligament connects muscle to bone; a tendon connects bone to bone
- B) A ligament connects bone to bone; a tendon connects muscle to bone **✓**
- C) Both connect muscle to muscle
- D) A ligament is a muscle; a tendon is a bone

*A ligament connects bone to bone, holding a joint together; a tendon connects muscle to bone so a muscle's pull can move the bone. A memory hook: a liGament Glues bones together, and a Tendon Ties a muscle to a bone.*

**10. (Reading 26.04 — muscles pull, not push; antagonistic pairs)**
Your biceps contracts to bend your elbow. Since a muscle can only pull and never push, how does your arm straighten again?

- A) The biceps pushes the forearm back down
- B) The bone straightens itself elastically
- C) The triceps, the biceps' antagonistic partner, contracts and pulls the arm straight **✓**
- D) Gravity alone always straightens the arm

*A muscle can only pull (contract and shorten), never push. To reverse a movement, a second muscle must pull the other way, so muscles work in antagonistic pairs: the biceps bends the elbow and the triceps straightens it. This is why nearly every joint is served by opposing muscles.*
