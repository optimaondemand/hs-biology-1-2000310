Week 26 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build the Body's Organization Hierarchy
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a giant, walk-through ladder of organization with six levels stacked from smallest to largest: cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism (with the chemical level named at the base). Then you will build one worked chain all the way up — a muscle cell → muscle tissue → the heart → the circulatory system → the whole organism — and record a walkthrough naming each level and how it is built from the one below. Standing inside the ladder makes the idea concrete: each step is made of the step beneath it, and each can do something the smaller level could not. This is Lesson 26.01, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or a desktop VR app · your completed Reading 26.01 and Lesson 26.01 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Human Body Lab / Levels of Organization (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty six-shelf ladder frame marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — build from the bottom (smallest) upward.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Lay the six levels. On the ladder, place and label the six levels bottom to top: chemical, cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism. Under each, add a one-line note: each level is built from the one below it.
2
Start the worked chain — cell. On a second track beside the ladder, build a single muscle cell and label it: the smallest living unit; it can contract.
3
Build up — tissue and organ. Group many muscle cells into muscle tissue (many similar cells doing one job), then build those into the heart (an organ — several tissues that together pump blood).
4
Finish the chain — system and organism. Connect the heart to blood vessels to form the circulatory system (organs cooperating on one function), then place it inside a whole-body figure: the organism. Draw arrows up the chain: cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 60–90 seconds, walk your worked chain from the muscle cell up to the organism, naming each level and saying how it is built from the level below. End by stating the big idea — each level can do something the smaller one cannot.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one full snapshot of your labeled ladder and worked chain (all six levels visible) plus typed labels naming each level in order and one sentence on how each is built from the level below.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the levels of organization by stacking them and building one worked chain from cell to organism. Two common errors to watch for and correct: placing "organ" before "tissue" (tissues combine to make organs, not the reverse), and confusing "organ" with "organ system" (the heart is one organ; the circulatory system is the heart plus vessels working together). A strong submission shows all six levels in correct bottom-to-top order and a complete worked chain (muscle cell → muscle tissue → heart → circulatory system → organism) with a walkthrough that states each level is built from the one below.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All six levels are built and labeled in correct order (chemical, cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism).
☐
The worked chain is complete: muscle cell → muscle tissue → heart → circulatory system → organism, with arrows connecting the levels.
☐
A spatial recording (or full snapshot + typed labels) is submitted, naming each level and stating how each is built from the one below.
