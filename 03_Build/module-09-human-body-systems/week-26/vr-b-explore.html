Week 26 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk a Negative-Feedback Loop
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk through a life-size feedback-loop scene and trace one negative feedback loop from start to finish, stopping at five stations: stimulus → receptor → control center → effector → response. Choose one loop to follow — either blood glucose (rising after a meal, brought down by insulin) or body temperature (rising on a hot day, brought down by sweating and vessel dilation). At each station you capture a snapshot and note what that part of the loop is doing. Walking the loop as a path makes the abstract cycle from Lesson 26.02 something you can physically follow — and see how the final response loops back to oppose the original change.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 26.02 and Lesson 26.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Homeostasis Walk / Feedback Loop Trail (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the trailhead marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there tells you to pick one loop — blood glucose or body temperature — and follow the numbered stations in order.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Station 1 — Stimulus. Stand at the start and identify the change that sets the loop going (blood glucose rises after a meal, or body temperature rises on a hot day). Snapshot it and note: this is the stimulus.
2
Station 2 — Receptor. Walk to the sensor that detects the change (a glucose sensor in the pancreas, or temperature receptors in the skin and brain). Snapshot it and note: this receptor detects the change.
3
Station 3 — Control center. Move to the decision-maker (the pancreas, or the hypothalamus in the brain) that compares the reading to the set point. Snapshot it and note: this control center decides the response.
4
Station 4 — Effector. Go to what carries out the action (body cells taking up glucose after insulin, or sweat glands and skin blood vessels). Snapshot it and note: this effector produces the response.
5
Station 5 — Response, and record. Reach the final station where the value returns toward the set point (blood glucose falls, or the body cools). Snapshot it, then start a spatial recording and walk the whole loop in about 60–90 seconds, naming each station and ending by explaining how the response opposes the original change — the defining move of negative feedback.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit your five station snapshots (one per part of the loop) plus typed labels naming each part — stimulus, receptor, control center, effector, response — and one sentence explaining how the response opposes the original change.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students trace one negative feedback loop as a physical path, capturing each of the five parts. Two common errors to watch for and correct: mixing up receptor and control center (the receptor only detects; the control center decides), and describing the response as adding to the change rather than opposing it (a correct loop always pulls the value back toward the set point). A strong submission follows one chosen loop in order, correctly names all five parts, and ends by explaining that the response opposes the original change — not amplifies it.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
One loop (blood glucose or body temperature) is followed through all five stations in order.
☐
Each part is correctly named: stimulus, receptor, control center, effector, and response.
☐
A spatial recording (or five station snapshots + typed labels) is submitted, ending with an explanation that the response opposes the original change.
