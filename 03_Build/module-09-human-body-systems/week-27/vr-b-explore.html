Week 27 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Explore Alveolar Gas Exchange & the Digestive Tract
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will enter two scenes and observe, rather than build. In the first, you shrink down to an alveolus and watch oxygen diffuse into the blood and carbon dioxide diffuse out across a one-cell-thick wall. In the second, you travel the digestive tract like a piece of food, from mouth to large intestine, seeing where each organ does its work. Along the way you capture labeled snapshots. Standing inside these processes turns two diagrams into places you have actually visited — and shows the same idea, diffusion across a huge thin surface, at work in the lungs. This explore pairs with Lessons 27.01 and 27.04.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Readings 27.01 and 27.04 and their lessons · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Alveolus & Digestive Tract / Body Systems Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: start at the Whiteboard IFX between the two scene portals. A sticky note there tells you to visit the alveolus scene first, then the digestive tract.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Enter the alveolus. Step into the air sac and find the capillary wrapped around it. Notice that both walls are one cell thick. Capture a snapshot and label the alveolus, the capillary, and the thin wall between them.
2
Watch the gases move. Follow the animated gas markers. Capture a snapshot showing O₂ diffusing from the alveolus into the blood and CO₂ diffusing from the blood into the alveolus. In your notebook, write one sentence saying why each gas moves the way it does (down its concentration gradient, by diffusion).
3
Travel the digestive tract. Enter the second scene and move through it in order: mouth → esophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine. At the small intestine, look closely at the villi lining the wall.
4
Capture the tract. Take at least two labeled snapshots along the digestive tract — one must show the villi of the small intestine with a note on how they increase surface area for absorption. Then, if you can, start a short spatial recording naming each region and its job as you pass through.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your labeled snapshots (at least one from the alveolus scene showing gas diffusion, and at least two from the digestive tract including the villi) to this Canvas assignment. A short spatial recording is welcome but optional. Include your two notebook sentences: one on why each gas diffuses the way it does, and one on how the villi increase surface area.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: This explore lets students observe two exchange surfaces side by side. Two common errors to watch for and correct: thinking the gases are actively pumped (they are not — O₂ and CO₂ move by passive diffusion, each down its own concentration gradient), and missing why both the alveolus and the intestine are so folded/numerous (both maximize surface area for exchange — the surface-area-to-volume principle from Module 3). A strong submission shows correctly labeled snapshots and two accurate notebook sentences connecting diffusion and surface area.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A labeled alveolus snapshot shows O₂ diffusing into the blood and CO₂ diffusing out across the one-cell-thick wall.
☐
At least two labeled snapshots of the digestive tract are captured, including one of the small-intestine villi with a note on surface area.
☐
Two notebook sentences are submitted: one explaining why each gas diffuses the way it does, and one explaining how the villi increase surface area for absorption.
