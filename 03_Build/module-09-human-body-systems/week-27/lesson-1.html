📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A sandwich is far too big to enter a single cell. How does your body take it apart into pieces small enough to use?
This opens Module 9, the study of the human body's systems. We begin where your food does — the digestive system. Today you follow food from mouth to large intestine, meet the organs that help from the sidelines, and learn why the true workhorse of digestion is the enzyme: a protein whose folded shape decides exactly what it can cut.

🍽️ PART 1 · THE DISASSEMBLY LINE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
You chew a piece of bread for a while and it starts to taste slightly sweet. Before you read on, what do you think is happening?

One long tube, each region a job

Food travels a single tube. In the mouth, chewing (mechanical) mixes with salivary amylase, which starts on starch. The esophagus pushes food down by peristalsis — muscle waves. The stomach churns food and adds strong acid and pepsin, which begins on protein. The small intestine is where most digestion and nearly all absorption happen. The large intestine mainly reabsorbs water, compacting what is left. Three accessory organs help: the liver makes bile (breaks fat into droplets), the gallbladder stores that bile, and the pancreas supplies a full set of digestive enzymes.

🔑 PART 3 · WHY ENZYMES ARE PICKY

Shape decides the cut

An enzyme is a protein — a folded chain of amino acids from Module 2 — and its fold creates an active site shaped to fit just one kind of molecule, its substrate. Like a key in a lock, amylase fits starch and pepsin fits protein; neither works on the other. Reason through the consequence below.

🧠 REASON IT THROUGH · ENZYME SPECIFICITY
Pepsin breaks down proteins. If you put pepsin in a test tube with only starch, what would you expect?

This is form-follows-function at the molecular scale: the structure of a protein (its folded shape) determines its function (what it can cut). Backward link: the protein folding of Module 2 is exactly what makes an enzyme selective. Forward link: what these enzymes free from your food is absorbed and carried off by the circulatory system — the subject of tomorrow's lesson.

💪 THE IDEA THIS BUILDS

Digestion is a disassembly line: mechanical breakup plus shape-specific enzymes take the large molecules of a meal apart into absorbable units, which cross into the blood through a hugely folded surface. Hold onto one idea above all — an enzyme's shape is its function. Next lesson, we follow what the blood does with everything digestion delivers.
