Cardiovascular Health
Reading 27.03 and Lesson 27.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 27.03 and work through Lesson 27.03, including atherosclerosis, how a blockage causes a heart attack or stroke, the risk factors, the LDL/HDL difference, and prevention.
Lesson focus: When the pipes clog — biology meets public health
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: order the plaque-to-blockage sequence, sort risk factors and cholesterol types, and write a short evidence-based recommendation for keeping arteries healthy. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How can the same biology explain both why arteries clog and how we can keep them clear?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Order the sequence (3 pts). Put these steps of a heart attack in order, from first to last: tissue downstream is starved of oxygen and begins to die; plaque builds up in the artery wall; a clot blocks the narrowed artery; the artery becomes narrower and stiffer.
2
Part B — Sort and identify (4 pts). (i) From this list, name the three risk factors a person can change through their own choices: age, smoking, diet, family history, exercise level. (ii) Which cholesterol type is the “bad” one that deposits cholesterol in artery walls — LDL or HDL — and which one carries it away?
3
Part C — Make a recommendation (3 pts). In two or three sentences, give one evidence-based recommendation for keeping arteries healthy, and explain why it works using something you learned this lesson (for example, how exercise or not smoking affects the vessels or cholesterol balance).
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A order: plaque builds up → artery becomes narrower and stiffer → a clot blocks the narrowed artery → tissue downstream is starved and begins to die.
☐
Part B: changeable risk factors are smoking, diet, exercise level (age and family history cannot be changed). LDL is the “bad” one that deposits cholesterol; HDL carries it away.
☐
Part C gives a real recommendation (e.g., regular exercise, not smoking, a lower-fat diet, managing blood pressure) AND a correct reason tied to vessels or cholesterol.
