Digestion & Enzymes
Reading 27.01 and Lesson 27.01
Before you begin: finish Reading 27.01 and work through Lesson 27.01, including the path of food along the digestive tract, the accessory organs, villi and surface area, and why an enzyme's shape makes it substrate-specific.
Lesson focus: Taking big molecules apart, one shape-specific cut at a time
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: put the digestive tract in order, match each organ or structure to its job, and explain in a sentence or two why an enzyme only works on its own substrate. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the body take a whole meal apart into pieces small enough for a cell to use?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Order the tract (3 pts). Put these five organs in the order food passes through them, from first to last: large intestine, mouth, small intestine, esophagus, stomach.
2
Part B — Match structure to job (5 pts, 1 each). Match each item to its function from the list below.
Structure / organ Function (write the letter)
Stomach 
Small intestine (villi) 
Large intestine 
Pancreas 
Liver 
A. Churns food and adds acid and pepsin to begin breaking down proteins  ·  B. Most digestion and nearly all absorption; folds increase surface area  ·  C. Mainly reabsorbs water from what remains  ·  D. Supplies digestive enzymes to the small intestine  ·  E. Makes bile, which breaks fat into small droplets
3
Part C — Enzyme specificity (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why the enzyme amylase can break down starch but cannot break down protein. Use the word shape in your answer.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A order: mouth → esophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine.
☐
Part B: stomach → A, small intestine (villi) → B, large intestine → C, pancreas → D, liver → E.
☐
Part C: amylase's active site is shaped to fit starch, not protein; because an enzyme's shape determines its substrate, amylase cannot act on a protein it does not fit.
