Lesson · A Four-Chambered Pump, Two Loops, and the River of Blood
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Digestion loads nutrients into the blood — but blood must reach every cell. How does one pump keep the whole body supplied?
Today you trace blood through the four-chambered heart and its two loops, tell an artery from a vein from a capillary, and name what plasma and the three kinds of blood cell do. You will also meet William Harvey, who proved that blood circulates — by measuring.

❤️ PART 1 · THE PUMP AND ITS TWO LOOPS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
The left ventricle has a much thicker, more muscular wall than the right ventricle. Before you read on, why do you think that is?

One drop of blood, all the way around

The heart has four chambers: two atria that receive blood and two ventricles that pump it out, with one-way valves that stop backflow. Follow one drop: it returns from the body to the right atrium, drops to the right ventricle, is pumped to the lungs (the pulmonary loop) to drop off CO₂ and pick up O₂, returns to the left atrium, drops to the left ventricle, and is pumped out to the whole body (the systemic loop). Then it begins again.

📚 A MOMENT IN THE HISTORY OF SCIENCE
William Harvey does the arithmetic

For almost 1,400 years, European doctors trusted the ancient physician Galen, who taught that the liver continuously made new blood which the body used up. In 1628 the English physician William Harvey published De Motu Cordis and tried something few had bothered to do: he counted. He estimated the blood pushed out per heartbeat and multiplied by the beats in an hour. The total was far more blood than any liver could manufacture from food.

The only explanation that fit the numbers was that the same blood is reused — it must circulate in a closed loop, out through arteries and back through veins. Harvey was ridiculed at first, but his measurements could not be argued away, and he was soon vindicated. It is the same lesson you met in Module 1: in science, careful evidence outranks even the oldest authority.

🧠 PART 3 · THE ARTERY THAT BREAKS THE RULE

Why the pulmonary artery is the exception

Most arteries do happen to carry oxygen-rich blood — but that is a coincidence, not the definition. The pulmonary artery proves it. Reason through the case below.

🧠 REASON IT THROUGH · ARTERY VS. OXYGEN
The pulmonary artery carries blood from the heart to the lungs. Is that blood oxygen-rich or oxygen-poor, and why does it still count as an artery?

Hold the definitions by direction: arteries away, veins toward — whatever they carry.
