Lesson · Why You Breathe — and What Your Cells Do With the Air
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You breathe without thinking — but why? What is your body actually doing with the oxygen you inhale?
Today closes a loop that opened in Module 4. You trace air to the alveoli, watch oxygen and carbon dioxide trade places by diffusion, and answer the deep question: you breathe because your cells run cellular respiration, which needs the oxygen you inhale and makes the carbon dioxide you exhale.

🫁 PART 1 · AIR TO THE ALVEOLI, THEN DIFFUSION

Where the air goes, and what crosses

Air travels nose/mouth → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli, tiny sacs wrapped in capillaries, each wall one cell thick. There, gases cross by diffusion. Answer each question.

🧠 PART 2 · THE DEEP REASON YOU BREATHE

Rate each statement

Here is the payoff from Module 4: the oxygen you inhale is the final electron acceptor of the electron transport chain, and the carbon dioxide you exhale is the waste released during the breakdown of glucose (pyruvate oxidation and the Krebs cycle). For each statement, pick True or False.

📏 TRUE OR FALSE · BREATHING vs. RESPIRATION
“Breathing and cellular respiration are just two names for the same process.”
“You need the oxygen you inhale because it is the final electron acceptor in your cells' electron transport chain.”
“The carbon dioxide you exhale is a waste product made by your cells during cellular respiration.”

Put it together: you breathe because your cells respire. The two gases of breathing are exactly the two gases of cellular respiration — oxygen consumed, carbon dioxide produced. Breathing is the whole-body service that supplies a reaction happening in trillions of cells. This is the microscopic-to-macroscopic spine of the whole course, closing between Module 4 and your own chest.
