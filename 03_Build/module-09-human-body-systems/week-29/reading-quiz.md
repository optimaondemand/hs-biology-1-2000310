**1. (Reading 29.01 — innate vs. adaptive traits)**
Which set of words best describes the **innate** immune system?

- A) Slow, specific, and with memory
- B) Fast, general, and with no memory **✓**
- C) Fast, specific, and with lifelong memory
- D) Slow, general, and with some memory

*The innate system acts within minutes to hours (fast), treats most invaders alike without identifying them (general), and responds the same way every time (no memory). The slow, specific, remembering traits belong to the adaptive system.*

**2. (Reading 29.01 — first line of defense)**
Skin, mucus, cilia, and stomach acid are all examples of:

- A) The second line of defense, which attacks microbes inside the body
- B) Antibodies made by B cells in response to an earlier infection
- C) The first line of defense, barriers that keep invaders out of the body **✓**
- D) Memory cells that remain after an earlier infection has cleared

*Skin (the integumentary barrier), mucus and cilia in the airways, and stomach acid are all first-line barriers whose job is to keep invaders out of the body entirely. The second line (inflammation, phagocytes) acts only after a barrier is breached.*

**3. (Reading 29.01 — second line, phagocytes)**
During inflammation at a cut, a white blood cell engulfs and digests bacteria without identifying exactly which species they are. This cell is a:

- A) Phagocyte, an innate cell that acts on invaders in general **✓**
- B) B cell, an adaptive cell that makes specific antibodies
- C) Memory T cell, an adaptive cell that recalls past infections
- D) Red blood cell, a cell that carries oxygen to the tissues

*A phagocyte ("eating cell") engulfs microbes whole and digests them — a general, innate defense that needs no specific identification of the invader. B cells and memory T cells are adaptive; red blood cells carry oxygen and play no defensive role here.*

**4. (Reading 29.02 — B cells and antibodies)**
What is the role of a **B cell** in adaptive immunity?

- A) It raises body temperature to produce a fever
- B) It engulfs and digests any microbe that it happens to meet
- C) It forms a physical barrier on the skin's surface
- D) It produces antibodies that bind a specific antigen **✓**

*B cells produce antibodies — Y-shaped proteins that bind a specific antigen. Fever is an innate response, general engulfing is done by phagocytes, and skin is a first-line barrier.*

**5. (Reading 29.02 — antibody–antigen specificity, Module 2 protein shape)**
An antibody binds to only one specific antigen, "like a lock fits one key." This specificity comes from the antibody's:

- A) Electric charge, which repels all other molecules
- B) Speed of movement, which lets it reach the antigen first
- C) Folded three-dimensional shape, which matches the antigen **✓**
- D) Ability to copy the antigen's DNA, which marks it for removal

*An antibody is a protein, and a protein's function follows from its folded three-dimensional shape — the idea introduced in Module 2. The antibody's tips are shaped to fit one antigen and no other, which is why binding is so specific.*

**6. (Reading 29.02 — helper vs. cytotoxic T cells)**
Which correctly pairs each T cell with its job?

- A) Helper T cells destroy infected cells; cytotoxic T cells coordinate the response
- B) Helper T cells coordinate the response; cytotoxic T cells destroy infected cells **✓**
- C) Helper T cells produce antibodies; cytotoxic T cells engulf microbes whole
- D) Helper T cells form skin barriers; cytotoxic T cells form mucous barriers

*Helper T cells coordinate the adaptive response by activating B cells and other defenders; cytotoxic (killer) T cells destroy the body's own cells that a virus has infected. Antibody production is the B cell's job, and barriers are first-line innate defenses.*

**7. (Reading 29.02 — how immunity is remembered; key EOC misconception)**
Years after recovering from an illness, a person is still immune to it. What is chiefly responsible for this lasting protection?

- A) Antibodies that stay in the blood at high levels for the rest of life
- B) The innate system, which learns to recognize that microbe
- C) A permanent change to the person's DNA that records the microbe
- D) Long-lived memory B cells and memory T cells that persist for years **✓**

*Immunity lasts because of memory B and T cells, which persist for years and mount a fast, strong response on re-exposure. Antibodies themselves fade after an infection — they are not what provides long-term memory. This is the most common misconception on this topic.*

**8. (Reading 29.02 — clonal selection)**
In clonal selection, which lymphocyte multiplies into an army of identical cells during an infection?

- A) The one lymphocyte whose receptor matches the invader's antigen **✓**
- B) Every lymphocyte in the body, all multiplying at the same time
- C) Only the red blood cells that are located near the site of infection
- D) Whichever lymphocyte happens to be closest to the wound or cut

*Clonal selection means the lymphocyte that happens to match the antigen is selected to divide repeatedly, producing many identical clones tuned to that one enemy. Finding and growing that single matching cell is why the first response takes days.*

**9. (Reading 29.03 — why a vaccine can't cause the disease)**
Why can a vaccine **not** give you the disease it protects against?

- A) It contains a full-strength pathogen that replicates freely, but the immune system destroys it before any symptoms can appear
- B) It permanently rewrites the DNA in the person's cells so that the immune system is no longer needed, which blocks the disease from ever developing
- C) It has either no pathogen able to replicate (inactivated, subunit, mRNA) or one weakened to replicate only slightly and cause no disease in healthy people **✓**
- D) It replaces the person's natural immune system with a stronger artificial one that destroys any pathogen before illness can start

*A disease requires a pathogen that replicates enough to damage the body. Inactivated, subunit, and mRNA vaccines contain nothing able to replicate, and attenuated vaccines (like MMR) contain a deliberately weakened pathogen that replicates only slightly and does not cause disease in healthy people — so each presents the antigen and builds memory cells without causing the illness. Any brief soreness or low fever is the innate response, not the disease.*

**10. (Reading 29.04 — gametes from meiosis; systems integration)**
Gametes (sperm and eggs) are produced by meiosis so that each carries half a chromosome set. This matters because:

- A) It allows a single parent to produce offspring that are identical clones of itself
- B) Fertilization then restores the full chromosome number and combines two parents' genes **✓**
- C) It removes the need for hormones from the endocrine system to coordinate reproduction
- D) Half a chromosome set is all that a complete human being needs to develop

*Meiosis (Module 5) halves the chromosome number in each gamete; fertilization unites a sperm and an egg to restore the full set and combine genes from two parents, producing a genetically unique offspring. The reproductive system is still coordinated by endocrine hormones, and a full set is required for a complete organism.*
