Lesson focus: A vaccine builds immunity without a live, replicating pathogen
🎯 DRIVING QUESTION
Using evidence from immunology, explain why a vaccine does not cause the disease it prevents.
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
Write a short paragraph (4–6 sentences) answering the driving question, using the three CER steps below.
1
Claim (3 pts). State clearly in one sentence that a vaccine cannot cause the disease it prevents.
2
Evidence (4 pts). Give at least two specific facts from immunology — for example, that disease requires a live, replicating pathogen; that a vaccine contains only a weakened, inactivated, subunit, or mRNA form that cannot replicate; and that its purpose is to present an antigen so the body builds memory cells.
3
Reasoning (3 pts). Connect the evidence to the claim: because there is nothing that can replicate and spread, the body gets the immune lesson (memory cells) without the infection. You may note that a sore arm or mild fever is the innate response engaging the antigen, not the disease.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Claim clearly states a vaccine cannot cause the disease it prevents.
☐
Evidence names the key fact: a vaccine contains no live, replicating pathogen (only a weakened, killed, subunit, or mRNA form) and works by presenting an antigen to build memory cells.
☐
Reasoning ties it together: no replicating pathogen means no infection, so the body gains immunity (memory) without the disease.
