Adaptive Immunity

Before you begin: finish Reading 29.02 and work through Lesson 29.02, including B cells and antibodies, helper and cytotoxic T cells, clonal selection, and how memory truly works.
Lesson focus: A specific defense that learns one enemy and remembers it
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each adaptive cell to its job, correct the antibody-versus-memory misconception, and explain clonal selection in a sentence or two. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the body learn one specific enemy, defeat it, and remember it for years to come?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match the cell to its job (5 pts, 1 each). Match each adaptive-immunity term to its role from the list below.
Term Role (write the letter)

A. Coordinates the whole adaptive response by activating B cells and other defenders  ·  B. Produces antibodies  ·  C. Long-lived cell that remembers an antigen and gives a faster, stronger second response
2
Part B — True or false (2 pts). Write True or False and correct it in a few words if false: “Immunity lasts because antibodies stay in your blood forever, standing guard.”
3
Part C — Explain clonal selection (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain what clonal selection is and why it makes the first response to a new infection take several days.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: B cell → B, helper T cell → A, cytotoxic T cell → D, antibody → C, memory cell → E.
☐
Part B: False — antibodies fade after an infection; immunity lasts because of long-lived memory B and T cells, which make fresh antibodies on re-exposure.
☐
Part C: clonal selection = the lymphocyte that matches the antigen is selected to multiply into many clones; the first response is slow because the body must first find and grow that one matching cell.
