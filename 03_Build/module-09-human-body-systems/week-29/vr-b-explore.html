Week 29 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk an Immune-Response Scene: Innate → Adaptive
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will enter a walk-through scene of an infection unfolding inside the body and observe the defense in the order it happens: a pathogen breaches the barrier (the first line is down) → the innate response arrives (phagocytes engulfing microbes, inflammation, fever) → the adaptive response takes over (B cells and antibodies, helper and killer T cells, and memory cells left behind). You will capture a labeled snapshot at each stage. Walking the battle from breach to memory lets you see why the innate system is fast and general while the adaptive system is specific and remembers. This VR pairs with Lessons 29.01 and 29.02.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Readings 29.01–29.02 and Lessons 29.01–29.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Immune Response Scene / Infected Tissue (Optima VR library)
On arrival: start at the skin surface marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — follow the pathogen from the breach inward.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
The breach. Begin at the skin. Watch a pathogen cross the broken barrier. Capture a snapshot and label it: first line down — the barrier (skin) is breached.
2
The innate response. Move into the tissue and watch phagocytes engulf microbes amid inflammation (widened vessels, incoming fluid) and rising fever. Capture a snapshot and label it: innate — fast, general, no memory.
3
The adaptive response. Follow the scene into the lymph node, where helper T cells coordinate, B cells release antibodies that bind the antigen, and killer T cells destroy infected cells. Capture a snapshot and label it: adaptive — specific and slower, but precise.
4
The memory. After the infection clears, find the memory B and T cells that remain. Capture a snapshot and label it: memory cells — what makes you immune, ready for a faster second response.
5
Reflect. In your notebook, write one sentence contrasting the innate and adaptive responses you saw — which was faster, which was specific, and which one left memory behind.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four labeled snapshots (breach, innate, adaptive, memory) to this Canvas assignment, plus your one-sentence contrast of innate versus adaptive. A short spatial recording of the walkthrough may be submitted instead of the snapshots if you prefer.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students observe the defense unfold in sequence and label each stage. Two common errors to watch for and correct: calling the fast phagocyte/inflammation stage "adaptive" (it is innate — general and memoryless), and attributing lasting memory to antibodies (memory lives in memory B and T cells). A strong submission has four correctly labeled snapshots in order and a contrast sentence that identifies the innate response as fast/general and the adaptive response as specific and memory-forming.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Four snapshots are captured and labeled in order: breach of the barrier, innate response, adaptive response, and memory cells.
☐
The innate stage shows phagocytes/inflammation; the adaptive stage shows B cells/antibodies and T cells; memory cells are identified as the source of immunity.
☐
A one-sentence contrast of innate (fast, general, no memory) versus adaptive (specific, slower, remembers) is submitted.
