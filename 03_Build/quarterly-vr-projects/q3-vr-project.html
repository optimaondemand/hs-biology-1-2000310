🌲 WHAT THIS PROJECT IS
Having established that life shares common ancestry (Q2), this quarter you organized the household that ancestry produced. You learned how biologists classify life into three domains using molecular sequence versus morphology (Module 7) and studied one great branch in depth — the plants, their tissues and their transport (Module 8). Your project builds a walkable “tree of life” tour: a three-domain path with representative organisms, anchored by a detailed plant anatomy & transport station.
The big question your tour answers: “How is the diversity of life ordered into the three domains — and what does looking closely at one branch, the plants, reveal about how structure serves function across the whole household?”
🧠 WHY THIS IS A VR PROJECT
A tree of life is a structure of relationships — branches, distances, and nested groups. Flattened onto a page it becomes a diagram to memorize; walked as a spatial path, it becomes a place whose branch lengths and groupings you can feel. VR also lets you shift scale mid-tour: step from a room-sized branching tree down into a leaf cross-section big enough to walk through and trace water climbing the xylem. That branch-to-tissue scale-shift — whole household down to a single working cell — is exactly the microscopic↔macroscopic move VR does better than any other medium.
🔨 WHAT YOU WILL BUILD — A THREE-DOMAIN WALK + PLANT STATION
Working inside the provided Tree of Life Gallery Location, compose a walkable tour with these four elements, annotated with sticky notes and a Whiteboard IFX.
1
The three-domain trunk. Build the three branches — Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya — with a representative organism placed on each. Sticky-note the key cell-level differences and why molecular sequence data (not just appearance) put Archaea on their own branch.
2
The Eukarya kingdoms. Off the Eukarya branch, place one representative each for Protists, Fungi, Plants, and Animals. Sticky-note one defining feature of each. Include a “molecular vs. morphology” note: a case where DNA reorganized a group appearances alone had misplaced.
3
The plant anatomy & transport station (the deep dive). Build a walk-through leaf cross-section or root-to-leaf plant showing the tissue systems, then model xylem and phloem transport. Sticky-note the cohesion-tension mechanism that pulls water up and how transpiration drives it — the mechanism behind your Module 8 potometer CER.
4
The dichotomous-key checkpoint. Place a small identification station — two or three unknown specimens and a short dichotomous key on a Whiteboard IFX — so a gallery visitor can practice placing an organism in the household themselves.

🎓 PORTFOLIO ARTIFACT & THE VIRTUE THIS PRACTICES · COURTESY
Your tree-of-life tour and leaf close-up enter your portfolio as a spatial classification artifact. The module’s virtue is courtesy — the ordered, respectful naming of the household of life. From Linnaeus to Woese, classification is an act of careful attention: giving each living thing its proper place and name. Building that household as a walkable tour is a way of practicing that ordered attention yourself.
🎓 FACILITATOR NOTE
Two conceptual traps to watch. First, students often treat the three domains as “small / medium / large” or plant/animal/other — hold them to the actual cell-level and molecular distinctions that put Archaea apart. Second, the plant station is where most points are won or lost: many students can label tissues but cannot explain cohesion-tension, so a Week-23 lab demonstrating the transpiration pull (tie it to their potometer CER) is worth the time. The plant deep-dive is deliberately the largest piece — it is the “look closely at one branch” move that keeps the project from being a shallow survey. Week-27 gallery walk is the universal public moment.
