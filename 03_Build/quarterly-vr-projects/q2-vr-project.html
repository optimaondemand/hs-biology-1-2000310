Quarter 2 VR Unit Project
From Genes to Common Descent: An Evidence Gallery
Assigned Week 10 · due end of Week 18 · counts toward the Laboratory & Notebooks category (20%)
🧬 WHAT THIS PROJECT IS
This quarter you learned how information in DNA becomes a protein (Module 5) and how the variation that creates — acted on over deep time — produces the evidence for evolution (Module 6). Your project builds a walkable evidence gallery in VR that makes those two modules into one continuous argument: it starts at a single mutation and ends at the case for common descent.
The big question your gallery argues: “How does variation begin at the level of a single DNA base, and how do independent lines of evidence build that variation into the case that today’s species share common ancestors?” This is the course spine climbing from molecule to population to the history of life.
🧠 WHY THIS IS A VR PROJECT (NOT A SLIDESHOW)
An argument from converging evidence is spatial by nature: several independent witnesses, standing apart, all pointing at the same conclusion. A gallery you walk through makes that structure physical — a visitor moves from your mutation→protein station out to separate fossil, anatomy, and molecular stations, and feels the paths converge. You are not summarizing evidence; you are designing the route by which someone else is persuaded. That authored, walkable argument is something no essay or slide deck delivers as well.
🔨 WHAT YOU WILL BUILD — A GENE STATION + THREE EVIDENCE STATIONS
Working inside the provided Evolution Evidence Hall Location, you will compose four annotated stations connected by a walkable route. Each station uses IFX, sticky notes, and a Whiteboard IFX.
1
The origin-of-variation station. Model the sickle-cell chain: a single base substitution in DNA → changed mRNA codon → one different amino acid → a bent hemoglobin protein → a phenotype natural selection can act on. Sticky-note each step. This is where variation comes from.
2
Evidence Station A — the fossil record. Arrange a rock-layer / transitional-fossil display (e.g., Tiktaalik, fish→tetrapod, ~375 mya, or whale ancestors with hind limbs). Sticky-note why deeper-is-older and intermediate forms point to descent with modification.
3
Evidence Station B — comparative anatomy. Display homologous structures (the same forelimb bones in human arm, whale flipper, bat wing). Sticky-note the homologous-vs-analogous distinction and why the same bones, different jobs pattern argues for a shared ancestor.
4
Evidence Station C — molecular evidence. Show a DNA/protein sequence-similarity comparison (more shared sequence = more recent common ancestor) and the near-universal genetic code. This station links straight back to Station 1 — the same molecule that starts variation is the one that records ancestry.
The one rule that makes this an argument: your three evidence stations must be independent — gathered by different methods, on different data. Your route and a closing Whiteboard IFX must make the convergence explicit: independent witnesses agreeing is what makes the case strong.

🎓 PORTFOLIO ARTIFACT & THE VIRTUE THIS PRACTICES · COURAGE
Your thesis-tour recording and gallery caption join your portfolio as evidence you can build and defend a full argument from converging data. The module’s virtue is courage: Darwin gathered independent lines of evidence for some twenty years and answered objections before he published. Designing a route that faces the hardest question — homologous versus analogous — rather than stepping around it is the same evidence-first courage on a smaller scale.
🎓 FACILITATOR NOTE
The intellectual heart of this project is independence and convergence — the most common weak submission has three stations that are really the same line restated (three anatomy examples, say). Push students to vary the method, not just the example. A Week-16 workshop on route design (using a Whiteboard IFX to storyboard before placing IFX) pays off. Also watch for students who model the sickle-cell chain but never connect it to variation selection can act on — Station 1 must reach a phenotype, or the gallery has no starting point. The Week-18 gallery walk is the universal public moment; solo students walk the same assembled hall. Note the mid-quarter Midterm (Wk 15) — remind students not to try to build during exam week.
