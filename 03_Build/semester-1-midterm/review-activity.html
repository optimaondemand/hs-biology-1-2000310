🔗 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Semester 1 was not five separate units — it was one continuous argument, built from the smallest things outward. Chemistry explains how cells can exist; cells explain how energy is captured and released; and molecular genetics explains how cells reproduce and pass life on. This activity asks you to put that whole chain back together before the midterm, so you study the connections, not just the facts.
Pairs with: the Week-15 Review Lesson (The First Half of One Long Argument).
📋 ACTIVITY OVERVIEW
Three parts. Part A — put the five modules in their microscopic-to-macroscopic order and state each one's big idea. Part B — name the bridge that connects each module to the next (the connections are the whole point). Part C — write the argument in your own words. Plan about 25–30 minutes.
📝 PART A · ORDER THE SPINE (8 PTS)
The five module big-ideas below are scrambled. In the table, write them in the correct order — smallest scale to largest, the order we studied them — and write the module number (1–5) for each. One point per correct big-idea placement, one point per correct module number.
SCRAMBLED BIG-IDEAS (use each once)
• Cells capture, store, and release energy (photosynthesis & respiration).
• Science produces reliable knowledge through evidence, testing, and revision.
• Information is stored, copied, and expressed — and inherited.
• Life is built from atoms and molecules; structure determines function.
• The cell is the basic unit of life, and its membrane controls what enters and leaves.

🔗 PART B · NAME THE BRIDGE (8 PTS, 2 EACH)
Each row crosses from one module into the next. From the bank, write the letter of the bridge that connects them — the idea from the earlier module that the later one depends on.
Crossing Bridge (write A–D)
M2 → M3: chemistry of life → the cell & its membrane 
M3 → M4: the cell → cell energy 
M2 → M5: chemistry of life → gene to protein 
M1 → all: nature of science → every later unit 
BRIDGE BANK (use each once)
A. Phospholipids and proteins — learned as macromolecules — become the fluid-mosaic membrane and the enzymes that run the cell.
B. The claim–evidence–reasoning (CER) method, and the difference between a hypothesis, theory, and law, are the thinking tools used in every unit and every assessment.
C. A protein's shape determines its function: change one amino acid and you change the protein — the rule that makes a single DNA base substitution able to cause sickle-cell disease.
D. Mitochondria and chloroplasts — cell organelles — are the sites where respiration and photosynthesis actually capture and release energy.
✏️ PART C · WRITE THE ARGUMENT (4 PTS)
In three to five sentences, explain in your own words how the first half of biology forms one connected argument — starting with atoms and water and ending with a trait that can be inherited. Use at least three of these terms and connect them: polarity / hydrogen bonding, membrane, enzyme, mitochondria or chloroplast, DNA, protein, mutation, phenotype. You are not listing facts — you are showing how each idea makes the next one possible.
