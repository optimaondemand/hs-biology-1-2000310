Week 10 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build the Carbon Atom’s Round Trip
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🌿 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build the loop a single carbon atom travels — with your hands, in three dimensions. Starting as CO₂ in the air, your atom is fixed into glucose by the Calvin cycle inside a chloroplast, then released again as CO₂ by respiration inside a mitochondrion, and back to the air. A flat arrow diagram makes this look like a list of steps; building it as a closed loop makes the big idea obvious: the carbon comes back around, but the energy does not. This is Lesson 10.04, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 10.04 and Lesson 10.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Carbon Cycle Workshop (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the loop platform marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Start in the air. Grab a CO₂ model from the air-bin and place it at the first station of the loop platform. This is your carbon atom’s starting point.
2
Fix it into glucose. Move the carbon into the chloroplast station and swap the CO₂ for a glucose model. Label this arrow photosynthesis — Calvin cycle.
3
Release it again. Move the glucose into the mitochondrion station and swap it back for a CO₂ model, then return that CO₂ to the air station — closing the loop. Label this arrow cellular respiration.
4
Add the energy arrow. Now place a separate straight arrow across your loop: sunlight in at the chloroplast, heat out at the mitochondrion. Label it to show energy enters and leaves — it does not close into a loop the way the carbon does.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk the loop and explain aloud, in about 60–90 seconds: where the carbon goes at each station, and why matter cycles while energy flows one way. Use the phrase matter cycles, energy flows.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your labeled loop (with the carbon loop and the straight energy arrow both visible) plus a few typed sentences explaining why matter cycles while energy flows one way.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the carbon cycle by building a closed carbon loop (air → chloroplast/glucose → mitochondrion/CO₂ → air) and a separate, non-closing energy arrow (sunlight in, heat out). Common sticking points: drawing the energy arrow as a loop (it must be one-way), and confusing which organelle fixes vs. releases carbon. Watch for students who omit the energy arrow entirely — that arrow is what makes the big idea visible. A strong submission shows a closed carbon loop, a one-way energy arrow, correct organelle labels, and a recording that uses “matter cycles, energy flows.”
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A closed carbon loop is built: air → glucose (chloroplast) → CO₂ (mitochondrion) → air, with each arrow labeled.
☐
A separate, one-way energy arrow (sunlight in, heat out) is placed and labeled — not drawn as a loop.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted that explains why matter cycles while energy flows.
