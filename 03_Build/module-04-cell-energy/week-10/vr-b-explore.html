Week 10 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Fermentation vs. Aerobic Respiration
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will enter two scenes set side by side. In one, yeast ferment sugar — bubbling off CO₂, producing ethanol, and harvesting only 2 ATP per glucose. In the other, a cell with plenty of oxygen runs full aerobic respiration and harvests about 36–38 ATP. Seeing the two outputs and yields next to each other makes the cost of going without oxygen concrete — the very comparison you tallied in Lesson 10.02.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 10.01, 10.02 and Lessons 10.01, 10.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Energy Lab — Two Scenes (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the two labeled scenes. Begin there.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
The fermentation scene (no oxygen). Enter the scene marked yeast fermenting. Watch the CO₂ bubbles rise, find the ethanol output, and read the ATP counter (it stops at 2). Place a sticky note: where does that 2 ATP come from? (Glycolysis.)
2
The aerobic scene (oxygen present). Step into the scene marked aerobic respiration. Find the oxygen entering the electron transport chain and read the ATP counter (about 36–38). Place a sticky note: which stage made most of that ATP, and what role did oxygen play?
3
Compare the two. Stand between the scenes and compare the outputs (ethanol + CO₂ vs. CO₂ + water) and the yields (2 vs. ~36–38 ATP). Place a sticky note naming the size of the difference.
4
Capture your evidence. Take two named snapshots — one of each scene’s output and ATP counter. Name each snapshot with its pathway (“fermentation — 2 ATP” and “aerobic — ~36–38 ATP”).
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your two named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: for each scene, the products and the ATP yield — and one sentence on why oxygen makes the difference (it is the final electron acceptor that lets the electron transport chain run).
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students observe fermentation and aerobic respiration side by side and compare their outputs and ATP yields. Common sticking points: thinking fermentation makes “some extra” ATP (it makes none of its own — the 2 ATP are from glycolysis), and assuming oxygen is consumed to make ATP directly rather than serving as the final electron acceptor. Push students to explain why the aerobic yield is so much larger. A complete submission shows two named snapshots and a note that correctly contrasts the products and yields and names oxygen’s role.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Both scenes are entered and the outputs (ethanol + CO₂ vs. CO₂ + water) and yields (2 vs. ~36–38 ATP) are observed.
☐
Sticky notes record where the ATP comes from in each scene and oxygen’s role in the aerobic one.
☐
Two named snapshots and the typed note (including the oxygen sentence) are submitted to Canvas.
