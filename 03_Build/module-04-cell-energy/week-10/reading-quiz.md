**1. (Reading 10.01 — purpose of fermentation)**
When a cell has no oxygen, what is the main purpose of fermentation?

- A) To make a large amount of ATP directly
- B) To regenerate NAD⁺ so that glycolysis can keep running **✓**
- C) To produce oxygen for the electron transport chain
- D) To break glucose all the way down to carbon dioxide

*Fermentation makes no ATP of its own. Its job is to dump electrons from NADH back onto pyruvate, regenerating NAD⁺ so glycolysis (and its small ATP yield) does not stall when oxygen is gone.*

**2. (Reading 10.01 — lactic-acid vs. alcoholic fermentation)**
Which products correctly match the type of fermentation?

- A) Lactic-acid fermentation produces ethanol and CO₂
- B) Alcoholic fermentation produces lactate (lactic acid)
- C) Lactic-acid fermentation produces lactate; alcoholic fermentation produces ethanol and CO₂ **✓**
- D) Both types produce only oxygen

*Lactic-acid fermentation turns pyruvate into lactate (the burn in muscle; yogurt and cheese). Alcoholic fermentation, carried out by yeast, turns pyruvate into ethanol and CO₂ (rising bread; brewing).*

**3. (Reading 10.01 — ATP yield of fermentation)**
How much ATP does a cell harvest per glucose when it relies on fermentation (anaerobic conditions)?

- A) About 36–38 ATP
- B) About 18 ATP
- C) Only the 2 ATP from glycolysis **✓**
- D) Zero ATP

*A fermenting cell never runs the Krebs cycle or the electron transport chain, so its entire harvest is the 2 ATP made in glycolysis. Fermentation itself adds no ATP — it only regenerates NAD⁺.*

**4. (Reading 10.02 — where most ATP is made)**
In aerobic respiration, which stage produces the large majority of the ATP?

- A) Glycolysis
- B) The Krebs cycle
- C) The electron transport chain **✓**
- D) Fermentation

*Glycolysis and the Krebs cycle each yield only about 2 ATP directly. The electron transport chain, fed by the NADH and FADH₂ from the earlier stages, makes roughly 32–34 ATP — the large majority.*

**5. (Reading 10.02 — aerobic vs. anaerobic totals)**
About how much more ATP does aerobic respiration yield per glucose than anaerobic fermentation?

- A) They yield about the same amount
- B) Aerobic yields about 36–38 ATP; anaerobic yields about 2 ATP **✓**
- C) Aerobic yields 2 ATP; anaerobic yields about 36 ATP
- D) Anaerobic yields slightly more because it is faster

*Aerobic respiration harvests about 36–38 ATP per glucose, while anaerobic fermentation harvests only about 2 — a roughly eighteenfold difference. Oxygen is what makes the larger harvest possible.*

**6. (Reading 10.02 — role of oxygen)**
Why does the presence of oxygen allow a cell to make so much more ATP?

- A) Oxygen is broken down to release energy directly
- B) Oxygen is the final electron acceptor, which lets the electron transport chain keep running **✓**
- C) Oxygen is used as a raw material in glycolysis
- D) Oxygen replaces glucose as the cell's fuel

*Oxygen accepts the spent electrons at the end of the electron transport chain. Without it, the chain backs up and stops; with it, the chain runs and produces most of the cell's ATP. Oxygen does not make ATP itself — it clears the line so the chain can.*

**7. (Reading 10.03 — complementary, not reverse)**
A student claims cellular respiration is simply photosynthesis run in reverse. What is the best correction?

- A) The two are identical, so the student is correct
- B) Although their overall equations look opposite, the two use different organelles, enzymes, and steps, so they are complementary, not exact reverses **✓**
- C) The two have nothing in common and cannot be compared
- D) Respiration happens only in animals, so it cannot relate to photosynthesis

*The summary equations look like mirror images, but photosynthesis (chloroplast) and respiration (mitochondrion) run through different enzymes and intermediate steps in a different order. They reach mirror-image endpoints by separate pathways — complementary, not literal reverses.*

**8. (Reading 10.03 — do plants respire?)**
Which statement about plants is correct?

- A) Plants only carry out photosynthesis; they do not respire
- B) Plants carry out cellular respiration as well as photosynthesis, day and night **✓**
- C) Plants respire only at night and never photosynthesize during the day
- D) Plants get all their usable energy directly from sunlight without respiration

*Photosynthesis only stores energy in glucose. To actually use that energy, plant cells must break glucose down in cellular respiration — so plants do both. They respire continuously, including in the dark when photosynthesis stops.*

**9. (Reading 10.04 — matter cycles, energy flows)**
A carbon atom moves from the air into a leaf, into an animal that eats the leaf, and back into the air. How do matter and energy behave in this journey?

- A) Both the carbon atom and the energy are recycled endlessly
- B) The carbon atom is used up and lost, while energy is recycled
- C) The carbon atom cycles and can be reused, while energy flows one way and is gradually lost as heat **✓**
- D) Neither the carbon atom nor the energy moves

*Matter cycles: the same carbon atom can travel air → leaf → animal → air again and again. Energy does not cycle — it enters as sunlight, is passed along as ATP, and is gradually lost as heat that living things cannot recapture. Hence: matter cycles, energy flows.*
