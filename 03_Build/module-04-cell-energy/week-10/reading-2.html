Reading 10.02
Counting the ATP: Aerobic vs. Anaerobic
Anchor Reading · ATP accounting
📖 WHAT YOU ARE READING TODAY
Yesterday you saw that fermentation keeps a cell alive on a thin trickle of energy. Today we do the accounting and ask the natural follow-up: exactly how much more does oxygen buy? This is the anchor reading for Lesson 10.02. By the end you should be able to tally roughly how much ATP each stage of respiration yields, compare aerobic and anaerobic totals, and explain why oxygen makes such a dramatic difference.

A note before we count

The numbers below are estimates, not exact constants. The amount of ATP a cell harvests from one glucose molecule depends on the cell, its conditions, and small “leaks” along the way, so biologists give a range rather than a single figure. That is honest science: the magnitudes are what matter here, not a memorized decimal. Aim to understand the pattern — where most of the ATP is made, and why.

Tallying aerobic respiration, stage by stage

Recall the three stages from earlier this module. Glycolysis splits glucose and yields about 2 ATP directly, plus 2 NADH. The Krebs cycle (the citric acid cycle) yields about 2 ATP directly, plus several NADH and FADH₂. So far that is only a handful of ATP. The big payoff comes last: the electron transport chain takes all those loaded NADH and FADH₂ carriers and uses them — with oxygen as the final electron acceptor — to make the large majority of the ATP, roughly 32–34 ATP.

Stage Direct ATP (about) Electron carriers made
Glycolysis ~2 ATP 2 NADH
Krebs cycle ~2 ATP NADH and FADH₂
Electron transport chain ~32–34 ATP (the large majority) Uses the carriers from the first two stages; needs O₂
TOTAL (aerobic) about 36–38 ATP per glucose

Aerobic vs. anaerobic: the size of the gap

A fermenting (anaerobic) cell never reaches the Krebs cycle or the electron transport chain — it stops after glycolysis. So its entire harvest is the 2 ATP from glycolysis. Set the two totals side by side:

Pathway Oxygen? ATP per glucose (about)
Aerobic respiration Yes ~36–38
Anaerobic (fermentation) No 2

The difference is enormous — roughly eighteen-fold. A cell that can use oxygen gets nearly twenty times as much usable energy from the same molecule of sugar.

Why oxygen is worth so much

Here is the heart of it: oxygen is the final electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain. Without something to accept the spent electrons, the chain backs up and stops — exactly the problem fermentation works around. With oxygen waiting at the end, the chain runs freely, the loaded NADH and FADH₂ carriers unload, and the cell harvests that large majority of its ATP. Oxygen does not appear in the ATP-making step itself; it makes that step possible by clearing the line. That single role — quietly accepting electrons at the very end — is the difference between 2 ATP and nearly 40.

💪 WHY THIS MATTERS

When you can account for the energy, you understand the cell. The tally tells you why animals breathe, why oxygen is precious, and why the leaf that first captured the sunlight is worth so much to everything downstream of it. Hold the pattern, not the decimal: most of the ATP comes from the electron transport chain, and oxygen is what unlocks it.
