🧮 PART 1 · TALLYING THE THREE STAGES

A note before we count

These numbers are estimates, not exact constants — the harvest from one glucose varies with the cell and its conditions, so biologists give a range. Aim for the pattern, not a memorized decimal. Glycolysis: ~2 ATP (plus 2 NADH). Krebs cycle: ~2 ATP (plus NADH and FADH₂). Electron transport chain: the large majority — roughly 32–34 ATP — built from the carriers the first two stages loaded up, with oxygen waiting at the end. Add them and the aerobic total is about 36–38 ATP per glucose.

💨 PART 3 · WHAT OXYGEN BUYS

One quiet role, a huge payoff

Here is the heart of it: oxygen is the final electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain. Without something to take the spent electrons, the chain backs up and stops — the exact problem fermentation works around. With oxygen waiting, the chain runs freely, the loaded carriers unload, and the cell harvests its huge majority of ATP. Oxygen does not appear in the ATP-making step itself; it makes that step possible by clearing the line. That single role is the difference between 2 ATP and nearly 40 — the reason that breathing, where it is possible, is worth so much.
