Reading 10.03
Mirror Processes: Photosynthesis ↔ Respiration
Anchor Reading · Complementary, not reverse
📖 WHAT YOU ARE READING TODAY
Look at the two summary equations of this module side by side and they seem to be perfect opposites — one builds glucose, the other tears it down. Are photosynthesis and cellular respiration simply the same reaction run backward? It is a tempting idea, and a useful first impression. But it breaks down the moment you look closely. This is the anchor reading for Lesson 10.03, where you will practice arguing this very question.

Why they look like opposites

Written as summary equations, the two processes do appear to be mirror images:

Photosynthesis: CO₂ + H₂O + light → glucose + O₂
Cellular respiration: glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + ATP

What one consumes, the other produces. Photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide and water and gives off oxygen; respiration takes in oxygen and gives off carbon dioxide and water. At the level of the overall ingredients, they really do balance each other — and that balance is no accident, because it is what links every breathing creature to every green leaf.

Why “reverse” breaks down

An equation hides the machinery. If you could film the two processes step by step, you would not see one running the other in rewind. They use different organelles (the chloroplast for photosynthesis, the mitochondrion for respiration), different enzymes, and different intermediate steps in a different order. The Calvin cycle is not the Krebs cycle played backward; the electron transport chain of a mitochondrion is not the light reactions of a chloroplast in reverse. They reach mirror-image endpoints by entirely separate roads.

So “reverse” is the right first instinct about the bookkeeping and the wrong conclusion about the mechanism. The two are best called complementary: they fit together, each supplying what the other needs, without being the same reaction.

A misconception to correct: do plants respire?

Many people believe plants only photosynthesize and animals only respire. That is wrong, and the error is worth fixing now. Photosynthesis only stores energy in glucose; it does not run the plant. To actually use that stored energy — to grow roots, open flowers, pump materials — a plant cell must break the glucose back down in cellular respiration, exactly as your cells do.

So plant cells carry out both processes. In the light they photosynthesize and respire; in the dark, when there is no light for photosynthesis, they keep right on respiring. A plant is not the opposite of an animal — it is a producer that also lives off its own product, every hour of every day.

💪 WHY THIS MATTERS

Seeing the two processes as complementary — not identical, not opposite, but fitted to each other — is the key that unlocks the rest of biology. The oxygen you breathe was released by a leaf storing energy; the carbon dioxide you exhale will feed the next leaf. That quiet exchange is the deepest expression of this module’s thread of service: the producer that feeds itself also, in the same breath, feeds everything else.
