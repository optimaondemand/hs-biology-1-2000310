**1. (Reading 8.01 — ATP as energy currency)**
A cell needs to power hundreds of different reactions. Why is ATP described as the cell's "energy currency"?

- A) ATP is the only molecule that contains any energy
- B) ATP is a small, portable packet of energy the cell can spend on nearly any job **✓**
- C) ATP is stored in huge amounts so the cell never needs more
- D) ATP can only be used to build proteins

*ATP works like spendable cash: the cell converts the energy of food into ATP, then carries it to any job. It is not the only molecule with energy, and cells keep very little ATP on hand — they recycle it constantly.*

**2. (Reading 8.01 — releasing energy from ATP)**
How does ATP release energy for the cell to use?

- A) The bond holding the third phosphate group is broken, forming ADP plus a phosphate **✓**
- B) The entire ATP molecule is destroyed and cannot be remade
- C) ATP absorbs sunlight directly
- D) A fourth phosphate is added to the molecule

*Energy is released when the bond to ATP's third phosphate is broken, leaving ADP (two phosphates) and a free phosphate. The cell later uses energy from food to reattach a third phosphate and recharge the ATP — it is recycled, not destroyed.*

**3. (Reading 8.01 — enzymes and activation energy)**
An enzyme speeds up a chemical reaction in a cell. How does it do this?

- A) By adding heat to the reaction
- B) By raising the activation energy needed
- C) By lowering the activation energy needed **✓**
- D) By becoming permanently used up in the reaction

*An enzyme is a protein catalyst that lowers the activation energy — the starting "push" a reaction needs — so the reaction runs quickly at body temperature. Enzymes are not used up and do not work by adding heat.*

**4. (Reading 8.02 — the photosynthesis equation)**
Which equation correctly summarizes photosynthesis?

- A) C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy
- B) 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ **✓**
- C) 6CO₂ + 6O₂ → C₆H₁₂O₆ + light energy
- D) C₆H₁₂O₆ + light energy → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O

*Photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide, water, and light energy to build glucose and release oxygen. Choice A is actually the equation for cellular respiration — the reverse process, which you will study in Module 9.*

**5. (Reading 8.02 — chloroplast structure and function)**
Inside a chloroplast, where do the light-dependent reactions take place?

- A) In the fluid stroma surrounding the thylakoids
- B) In the thylakoid membranes, which are stacked into grana **✓**
- C) In the double outer membrane
- D) In the cell wall

*The light reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes (stacked into grana), where light-absorbing pigments are embedded. The Calvin cycle, in contrast, takes place in the stroma — the fluid around the thylakoids.*

**6. (Reading 8.03 — the source of oxygen)**
During photosynthesis, a plant releases oxygen gas. Where do these oxygen atoms come from?

- A) From splitting water (H₂O) **✓**
- B) From breaking apart carbon dioxide (CO₂)
- C) From the glucose the plant builds
- D) From oxygen the plant absorbs through its roots

*The oxygen released is a by-product of splitting water in the light reactions. Chlorophyll loses electrons to light and replaces them by pulling electrons from water, which breaks apart and frees O₂. The carbon and oxygen of CO₂ instead go into building sugar.*

**7. (Reading 8.03 — products handed to the Calvin cycle)**
The light-dependent reactions produce two energy-carrying molecules that the Calvin cycle then uses. What are they?

- A) Glucose and oxygen
- B) Carbon dioxide and water
- C) ATP and NADPH **✓**
- D) DNA and RNA

*The light reactions capture light energy as ATP and NADPH, which are passed to the Calvin cycle to power the building of glucose. Glucose and oxygen are end products, not the carriers handed off; CO₂ and water are inputs.*

**8. (Reading 8.04 — the "light-independent" misconception)**
The Calvin cycle is sometimes called the "light-independent reactions." A student concludes it must happen only in the dark. Why is this conclusion incorrect?

- A) The Calvin cycle actually does use light directly to power itself
- B) The Calvin cycle depends on the ATP and NADPH made by the light reactions, which run out quickly in the dark **✓**
- C) The Calvin cycle never happens in real plants
- D) The Calvin cycle happens in the thylakoid, where light always reaches

*"Light-independent" means no light strikes the cycle directly — not that it needs darkness. The cycle runs on the ATP and NADPH made by the light reactions, and those are used up within minutes once light is gone. So in real life it runs during the day.*

**9. (Reading 8.04 — interpreting the leaf-disk assay)**
In a leaf-disk experiment, sunken spinach disks rise and float as they photosynthesize. Why does a floating disk indicate that photosynthesis is occurring?

- A) The disk absorbs water and becomes lighter
- B) Oxygen produced by photosynthesis collects in the disk and makes it buoyant **✓**
- C) The disk loses chlorophyll and floats
- D) Carbon dioxide is pulled out of the disk, leaving it hollow

*As a disk photosynthesizes, the light reactions release oxygen, which collects in the leaf tissue and makes the disk buoyant so it floats. The faster the disks float, the faster photosynthesis is running — which is why a dark control, making no oxygen, stays sunk.*
