Module 4 CER Writing Assignment
Reading the Light: A Leaf-Disk Investigation
Claim · Evidence · Reasoning · Integration · 12 pts · counts toward the CER writing category
📖 WHAT THIS IS
This is the writing that pulls Module 4 together. It is a real piece of scientific work: you are handed a data set from a leaf-disk assay — the same investigation you met in Lesson 8.04 — and asked to figure out what it tells you about how light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis, and why, in terms of the mechanism inside the chloroplast. It is assigned at the start of Week 8 and due at the end of Week 10, so the whole module’s energy story can inform your reasoning. Your teacher will read and score this one personally — it is not auto-graded.
🧬 THIS IS OPEN-INQUIRY WRITING
Unlike a guided prompt, this assignment gives you data and no answer. You decide what the numbers show, you state the claim, and you defend it. This is exactly what a scientist does with a fresh data table — and exactly the kind of reasoning the Biology EOC rewards on its data-analysis questions.
🎯 YOUR TASK
Using the leaf-disk assay data below, determine how light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis — and justify your claim with the mechanism that actually produces the result you see.
🧪 THE INVESTIGATION
A student punches small disks from a spinach leaf and pulls the air out of them so they sink in a beaker of dilute baking-soda solution (which supplies dissolved carbon dioxide). She places identical beakers, each with 10 sunken disks, under four conditions and counts how many disks have floated back to the surface every two minutes. As a disk photosynthesizes, oxygen gas collects in its tissue until the disk becomes buoyant and rises — so the number of floating disks is a measure of the rate of photosynthesis.

📝 BUILD YOUR ARGUMENT IN FOUR MOVES
Write in connected paragraphs, not a list. You decide the wording — these are the moves a strong CER makes, not a fill-in-the-blank.
1
Claim. In one or two sentences, state the relationship the data reveal between light intensity and the rate of photosynthesis. Make it specific and arguable — not “light matters,” but a claim someone could test.
2
Evidence. Pull specific numbers from the table — do not just say “more disks floated.” Compare the conditions at the same time point (for example, how many disks floated at 8 minutes in low vs. medium vs. high light), and use the dark control as evidence too. Explain what each number shows, since rising disks mean oxygen production, and oxygen production means photosynthesis.
3
Reasoning — the mechanism. This is the heart of the essay. Explain why more light produces more oxygen. Connect it to the light-dependent reactions in the thylakoid membrane: light energy excites electrons and drives the splitting of water, which releases the O₂ the disks collect. More photons striking the chlorophyll means more excited electrons, more water split, and more oxygen — so the disks rise faster. Then address a limitation or counterargument: could something other than light explain the result (temperature, carbon dioxide supply)? Why did the controlled design rule those out? If you think the rate would eventually stop climbing no matter how bright the light, say why (the reactions can only run so fast — light saturation).
4
Integration. Close by tying the investigation to the bigger picture. The oxygen you measured is a by-product; the energy captured in the light reactions is handed to the Calvin cycle to build glucose — the sugar that feeds the plant and, through the food web, nearly every living thing. That is this module’s thread of service: a leaf in the light is quietly provisioning the rest of life.
🧡 THE VIRTUE THIS PRACTICES · SERVICE
This module’s virtue is service. A green leaf asks nothing and gives everything: it captures sunlight and packs it into sugar and oxygen that the entire living world depends on. Your CER honors that quiet work by doing the patient thing a scientist does — not guessing what the data “should” say, but following the numbers to a claim and then explaining the mechanism that makes them true. Let the evidence lead, and let the reasoning carry the claim.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
A specific, arguable claim states that the rate of photosynthesis increases as light intensity increases.
☐
Evidence cites specific numbers from the table (compared at the same time point) and uses the dark control; it explains that floating disks indicate oxygen production.
☐
Reasoning ties the result to the light-dependent reactions (light → excited electrons → water split → O₂) and addresses a counterargument or limitation.
☐
The essay reads as one connected argument and links the captured energy forward to the Calvin cycle and the food web (~400–700 words).
📊 CER SCORING RUBRIC (3 PTS × 4 CRITERIA = 12 PTS)
Criterion Strong (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1)
Claim Specific and arguable; names the relationship between light intensity and photosynthesis rate. Somewhat vague or only partly captures the relationship. Missing, or just restates the prompt.
Evidence Cites specific data points, compares conditions, uses the control, and states what floating disks indicate. Some data referenced, but general (“more floated”) or the control is ignored. No real data used, or data described inaccurately.
Reasoning Explains the light-reaction mechanism (light → electrons → water split → O₂) and addresses a counterargument or limitation. Connects light to oxygen but with gaps in the mechanism, or no counterargument. States the result without explaining the cause.
Integration Reads as one coherent argument and links the captured energy forward to the Calvin cycle / food web; within the word range. Components present but loosely connected. Listed independently without connection.
Total possible: 12 pts
