Week 9 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk the Electron Transport Chain
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will shrink down and walk along the inner mitochondrial membrane, following the final stage of respiration as it happens around you. You will watch high-energy electrons pass down the electron transport chain, see H⁺ pumped into the intermembrane space, watch those H⁺ rush back through ATP synthase to make ATP — chemiosmosis — and observe oxygen accept the spent electrons to form water. Seeing the gradient build and the turbine spin makes the most productive stage of respiration concrete.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 9.04 and Lesson 9.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Inner Membrane Walk — The Electron Transport Chain (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX at the start of the membrane shows your explorer checklist. Begin there and walk the chain in order.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Hand off the electrons. Start where NADH and FADH₂ arrive and drop their high-energy electrons into the chain. Watch the electrons pass from protein to protein down the membrane, releasing energy at each step.
2
Watch the gradient build. See that released energy pump H⁺ from the matrix into the intermembrane space. Note how crowded that space becomes — this is the proton gradient, water building up behind a dam.
3
Spin the turbine. Find ATP synthase and watch the H⁺ rush back through it into the matrix, spinning it like a turbine to make ATP. This coupling is chemiosmosis — the source of most of the cell’s ATP.
4
Meet the final acceptor, and capture evidence. Go to the end of the chain and watch oxygen accept the spent electrons and H⁺ to form water. Take three named snapshots: one of the H⁺ pump building the gradient, one of ATP synthase making ATP, and one of oxygen forming water.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: in your own words, explain how the proton gradient leads to ATP (chemiosmosis), and what happens at the very end of the chain when oxygen accepts the electrons.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students observe the electron transport chain and chemiosmosis on the inner membrane and capture the three key moments. Common sticking points: confusing the direction of H⁺ movement (pumped OUT into the intermembrane space by the chain, flowing BACK through ATP synthase), and missing that oxygen is the FINAL electron acceptor forming water. Push students to articulate why this stage makes the most ATP and why the chain stops without oxygen. A complete submission shows three named snapshots and a note correctly describing chemiosmosis and oxygen’s role.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
The full chain is walked: electrons handed off, H⁺ pumped, ATP synthase spun, oxygen forming water observed.
☐
Three named snapshots (H⁺ pump / ATP synthase / oxygen forming water) are captured.
☐
The typed note explains chemiosmosis and oxygen’s role as the final electron acceptor.
