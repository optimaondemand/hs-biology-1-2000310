Lesson · The Second Stage of Respiration
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
The carbon dioxide you just exhaled was, moments ago, part of a sugar. Where in the cell was it set free?
Today the pyruvate from glycolysis enters the mitochondrion. In the Krebs cycle (the citric-acid cycle), in the matrix, the cell releases the CO₂ you breathe out and — most importantly — loads the electron carriers NADH and FADH₂ that feed the next stage.

🔄 PART 1 · INTO THE MATRIX
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
You breathe out carbon dioxide all day long. Those carbon atoms had to come from somewhere inside you. Where do you think they originate? (No wrong answer — just check in.)

A doorway step, then the wheel

Each pyruvate (three carbons) entering the mitochondrion is first reworked into acetyl-CoA (two carbons), releasing one CO₂ and loading one NADH. This happens twice per glucose. Acetyl-CoA then enters the Krebs cycle — also the citric-acid cycle, named for its first product, the sour molecule of citrus — which runs in the matrix. It is a cycle because it ends with the molecule it began with, ready to turn again like a waterwheel. Because each glucose makes two acetyl-CoA, the wheel turns twice per glucose.

🏛️ A HUMAN STORY · THE PAPER THAT WAS TURNED DOWN
The German-born biochemist Hans Krebs worked out the steps of this cycle in 1937. When he submitted his findings, the journal Nature declined to publish the paper, and he had to send it elsewhere. The work that was at first turned away proved to be one of the central discoveries of twentieth-century biology — the very hub of how living things release energy — and in 1953 it earned Krebs the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine. It is a quiet reminder that even foundational ideas can be doubted or delayed when they are new, and that careful evidence, patiently defended, tends to win out in the end.
💨 PART 2 · FOLLOW THE CARBON, FOLLOW THE ELECTRONS

Six carbons in, six CO₂ out

Glucose had six carbons. Two leave as CO₂ in the doorway step (one per pyruvate); the other four leave over the two turns of the cycle. Six in, six out — the carbon balances, and all of it leaves as the CO₂ you exhale. But the cycle’s real product is not CO₂ and not the small amount of ATP it makes directly. Its real job is to load the electron carriers NADH and FADH₂ — loaded delivery trucks carrying high-energy electrons to the inner membrane for the final stage. Think of the cycle as a loading dock.

🔗 PART 3 · THE HAND-OFF TO THE NEXT STAGE

Why the carriers matter more than the ATP

Judge the Krebs cycle by its ATP alone and you will miss the point. Its value is the NADH and FADH₂ it loads. These carriers hold the energy stripped from glucose’s carbon skeleton, and they carry it a short distance to the inner mitochondrial membrane, where tomorrow’s lesson — the electron transport chain — will cash them in for the great majority of the cell’s ATP. Hold the thread: CO₂ out, carriers loaded, carriers delivered.
