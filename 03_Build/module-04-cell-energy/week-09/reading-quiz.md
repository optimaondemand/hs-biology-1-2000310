**1. (Reading 9.01 — respiration vs. breathing)**
A student says, "Cellular respiration is just a fancy word for breathing." Why is this incorrect?

- A) They are the same thing; the student is correct
- B) Breathing is gas exchange in the lungs, while cellular respiration is the energy-releasing chemistry inside every cell **✓**
- C) Cellular respiration happens only in the lungs, while breathing happens everywhere
- D) Breathing requires oxygen but cellular respiration does not

*Breathing moves air in and out of the lungs (gas exchange). Cellular respiration is the chemistry inside every cell that releases energy from glucose. Breathing supplies the oxygen and removes the CO₂ that respiration produces, but a single cell with no lungs still respires.*

**2. (Reading 9.01 — the respiration equation)**
Which equation correctly represents cellular respiration?

- A) 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
- B) C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (ATP) **✓**
- C) C₆H₁₂O₆ → 6O₂ + 6CO₂ + energy
- D) 6O₂ + energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6H₂O

*In cellular respiration, glucose and oxygen are the reactants; carbon dioxide, water, and usable energy (ATP) are the products. Choice A is photosynthesis (respiration run in reverse). Respiration is aerobic — it uses oxygen.*

**3. (Reading 9.01 — locating the stages)**
In which part of the cell does **glycolysis** take place?

- A) The mitochondrial matrix
- B) The inner mitochondrial membrane
- C) The cytoplasm, outside the mitochondrion **✓**
- D) The nucleus

*Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and does not require a mitochondrion at all. The Krebs cycle happens in the matrix, and the electron transport chain is on the inner mitochondrial membrane.*

**4. (Reading 9.02 — glycolysis facts)**
Which statement about glycolysis is correct?

- A) It requires oxygen and produces about 30 ATP
- B) It splits one glucose into two pyruvate and nets 2 ATP, with no oxygen needed **✓**
- C) It occurs in the matrix and releases carbon dioxide
- D) It combines two pyruvate into one glucose

*Glycolysis splits one 6-carbon glucose into two 3-carbon pyruvate molecules in the cytoplasm, with no oxygen required. The cell spends 2 ATP and makes 4, for a net of 2 ATP (plus 2 NADH).*

**5. (Reading 9.02 — where the energy is)**
After glycolysis, only 2 ATP have been produced from a whole glucose molecule. Where is most of glucose's original energy at this point?

- A) It has all been released as those 2 ATP
- B) It was lost as heat and cannot be recovered
- C) It is still stored in the two pyruvate molecules and the NADH **✓**
- D) It was used up making oxygen

*Glycolysis only cracks the sugar open. The great majority of glucose's energy is still held in the bonds of the two pyruvate and in the electrons carried by NADH. The cell collects it later, inside the mitochondrion.*

**6. (Reading 9.03 — source of exhaled CO₂)**
The carbon dioxide you breathe out is produced mainly when…

- A) oxygen is split apart in the lungs
- B) pyruvate is converted to acetyl-CoA and as the Krebs cycle turns in the matrix **✓**
- C) water is broken down on the inner membrane
- D) glucose is first split during glycolysis

*Carbon leaves glucose as CO₂ during the conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA and during the Krebs cycle, both in the mitochondrial matrix. That CO₂ travels to the lungs and is exhaled. Glycolysis itself does not release CO₂.*

**7. (Reading 9.03 — the real job of the Krebs cycle)**
The Krebs cycle makes only a small amount of ATP directly. What is its most important contribution to respiration?

- A) It loads the electron carriers NADH and FADH₂ for the next stage **✓**
- B) It produces the most ATP of any stage
- C) It splits glucose into pyruvate
- D) It uses oxygen to make water

*The Krebs cycle's chief role is to harvest high-energy electrons onto NADH and FADH₂, which then deliver them to the electron transport chain — where most of the ATP is actually made. The cycle is best thought of as a loading dock.*

---

**8. (Reading 9.04 — oxygen's role)**
What is the role of oxygen in cellular respiration?

- A) It is split to release the energy in glucose
- B) It is the final electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain, forming water **✓**
- C) It is produced as a waste product and exhaled
- D) It is the molecule that ATP synthase pumps across the membrane

*Oxygen accepts the spent electrons (and H⁺) at the end of the electron transport chain, becoming water. Without oxygen to accept them, the chain backs up and stops — which is why aerobic respiration requires oxygen. (Oxygen is a reactant, not a product; CO₂ is the waste exhaled.)*

**9. (Reading 9.04 — which stage makes the most ATP)**
Which stage of cellular respiration produces the most ATP, and by what mechanism?

- A) Glycolysis, by splitting glucose
- B) The Krebs cycle, by releasing carbon dioxide
- C) The electron transport chain, by chemiosmosis (a proton gradient driving ATP synthase) **✓**
- D) All three stages produce equal amounts of ATP

*The electron transport chain produces the large majority of the cell's ATP. As electrons pass down the chain, H⁺ are pumped to build a gradient; the H⁺ then flow back through ATP synthase, driving ATP production — a process called chemiosmosis. This is a key EOC point: the ETC yields the most ATP.*
