🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Living things give off carbon dioxide as they respire. How can a jar of yeast or a handful of sprouting seeds make that invisible chemistry visible?
📝 PART A · RUN ONE INVESTIGATION (4 pts)
Pick one option. Set up the experiment, then record what you observe over about 20–30 minutes.
Option 1 — Yeast and sugar.
In a bottle, mix warm water, a packet of yeast, and a spoonful of sugar; stretch a small balloon over the opening. Set up a second bottle exactly the same way but with no sugar as your control. Watch over 20–30 minutes. The gas inflating the balloon is the CO₂ the yeast release as they break down sugar.
Option 2 — Germinating seeds.
Soak a handful of dried beans or peas overnight so they begin to sprout. Seal the sprouting seeds in a small bag or jar with a strip of moist pH (or red-cabbage) indicator paper held above them; set up an identical container with boiled (dead) seeds as your control. The living, sprouting seeds release CO₂ as they respire, which shifts the indicator; the dead seeds should not.

1
Which stage makes the most ATP, and why? (2 pts) Name the stage of respiration that yields the most ATP, and explain in one to two sentences how chemiosmosis (a proton gradient driving ATP synthase) produces it.
