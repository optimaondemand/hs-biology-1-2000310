📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three parts. In Part A you label the mitochondrion and locate the three stages. In Part B you write the respiration equation. In Part C you correct a common misconception in two or three sentences. The whole assignment should take about 20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If life is a kind of slow, controlled fire, what is the cell burning, where does it burn it, and what does it leave behind?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Label the mitochondrion and locate the stages (4 pts). Match each structure to its description, then name which stage of respiration happens in each place.
Structure Describe it Stage that happens here (if any)

2
Part B — Write the equation (3 pts). Write the overall equation for cellular respiration, then label which substances are the reactants and which are the products. (Hint: glucose and oxygen go in; carbon dioxide, water, and usable energy come out.)
3
Part C — Correct the misconception (3 pts). A classmate says, “Cellular respiration is just another word for breathing.” In two to three sentences, explain why this is wrong — what breathing actually is, what cellular respiration actually is, and how the two are related.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Each structure is described correctly, and glycolysis (cytoplasm), Krebs cycle (matrix), and electron transport chain (inner membrane) are placed in the right rows.
☐
The equation is correct (glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy) with reactants and products labeled.
☐
Part C correctly separates breathing (gas exchange in the lungs) from cellular respiration (energy-releasing chemistry in every cell) and links them.
