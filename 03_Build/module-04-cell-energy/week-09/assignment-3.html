📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
The Krebs Cycle
Reading 9.03 and Lesson 9.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 9.03 and work through Lesson 9.03, including where the cycle runs, what gas it releases, and what it loads onto the electron carriers.
Lesson focus: explain that the cycle releases CO₂ and loads NADH/FADH₂ for the next stage
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Answer three short questions that trace the carbon and the electrons through the Krebs cycle. The whole assignment should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
The Krebs cycle makes very little ATP itself — so why is it one of the most important steps in releasing the energy of your food?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Where the exhaled CO₂ comes from (3 pts). In two to three sentences, explain where the carbon dioxide you breathe out is produced inside the cell. Trace it back to glucose, and name the steps where CO₂ is released (the conversion of pyruvate, and the cycle itself).
2
What the cycle hands to the ETC, and why it matters (4 pts). In two to three sentences, name what the Krebs cycle loads (the electron carriers NADH and FADH₂), say where those carriers go next, and explain why that hand-off matters for making ATP.
3
Quick location check (3 pts). Name the exact part of the mitochondrion where the Krebs cycle runs, and explain in one sentence why that location is inside the mitochondrion rather than out in the cytoplasm where glycolysis happens.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Exhaled CO₂ is traced to glucose and to the steps that release it (pyruvate conversion and the cycle).
☐
NADH and FADH₂ are named, sent to the electron transport chain, and linked to making most of the ATP.
☐
The cycle is correctly placed in the matrix.
