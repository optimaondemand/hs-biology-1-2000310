The Electron Transport Chain & Chemiosmosis
Lesson · The Third Stage — Where Most ATP Is Made
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Glycolysis netted two ATP; the Krebs cycle made only a little. So where does the great majority of your energy actually come from?
Today the loaded carriers reach the inner mitochondrial membrane and the cell collects its largest share of ATP. You will follow electrons down the electron transport chain, see how a proton gradient spins ATP synthase — chemiosmosis — and learn why oxygen, the final electron acceptor, is the reason you must breathe.

⚡ PART 1 · ELECTRONS DOWN A STAIRCASE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
A dam holds water high behind it; when the water is allowed to rush through a turbine, it spins the turbine and generates power. How might a cell use a similar trick to make energy? (No wrong answer — just check in.)

The chain on the inner membrane

The electron transport chain (ETC) is a series of proteins set in the inner mitochondrial membrane — the folded cristae from Monday, their surface area now put to use. The carriers NADH and FADH₂ arrive and drop off their high-energy electrons. The electrons pass from protein to protein, tumbling down a staircase and releasing a little energy at each step. The cell spends that energy to pump H⁺ from the matrix into the intermembrane space, building a steep proton gradient — water behind a dam.

⚒️ PART 2 · CHEMIOSMOSIS AND THE ROLE OF OXYGEN

The turbine, and the bottom of the stair

The crowded H⁺ flow back into the matrix through one gate — the enzyme ATP synthase — spinning it like a turbine and driving the production of ATP. This coupling of a proton flow to ATP-making is chemiosmosis. And the staircase needs a bottom step: oxygen is the final electron acceptor, catching the spent electrons and H⁺ to form water. Without oxygen to catch them, the whole chain backs up and stops — which is the deep reason you must breathe.

You saw this trick last week. In the light reactions of photosynthesis, a chloroplast pumped H⁺ to build a gradient and spun the very same enzyme — ATP synthase — to make ATP. Two organelles, two energy stories, one shared mechanism. Chemiosmosis is one of biology’s great repeated ideas.

🧮 PART 3 · ADD IT ALL UP

Where the energy really came from

Glycolysis netted 2 ATP; the Krebs cycle made a little directly. But the electron transport chain, through chemiosmosis, makes the large majority, bringing the total to roughly 30 or more ATP per glucose. The earlier stages were mostly preparation — loading the carriers that are cashed in here.

Exam point: of the three stages, the electron transport chain produces the most ATP. If a question asks which stage gives the greatest energy payoff, this is the answer.
