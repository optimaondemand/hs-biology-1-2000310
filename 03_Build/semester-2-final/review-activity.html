Walk the Whole Spine: One Long Argument, Atoms to Ecosystems
Synthesis Activity · 20 pts
🔗 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
The whole year was not ten separate units — it was one continuous argument, built from the smallest things outward. Chemistry explains how cells can exist; cells capture and pass on energy; molecular genetics explains how life reproduces and varies; that variation drives evolution; evolution produces the diversity we classify; and every organism — plant or person — lives inside an ecosystem of flowing energy and cycling matter. This activity asks you to put that whole chain back together before the final, so you study the connections, not just the facts.
Pairs with: the Week-36 Review Lesson (The Whole Argument, Atoms to Ecosystems).
📋 ACTIVITY OVERVIEW
Three parts. Part A — put all ten modules in their microscopic-to-macroscopic order and state each one's big idea. Part B — name the bridge that carries you across five key crossings (the connections are the whole point). Part C — write the year's argument in your own words. Plan about 35–40 minutes.
📝 PART A · ORDER THE SPINE (10 PTS)
The ten module big-ideas below are scrambled. In the table, write them in the correct order — smallest scale to largest, the order we studied them — and write the module number (1–10) for each. One point per correctly placed module.
SCRAMBLED BIG-IDEAS (use each once)
• Organ systems cooperate to maintain homeostasis (the human body).
• Cells capture, store, and release energy (photosynthesis & respiration).
• Species share common ancestors, and natural selection produces adaptation.
• Science produces reliable knowledge through evidence, testing, and revision.
• Energy flows and matter cycles through ecosystems — and humans are part of them.
• Information is stored, copied, and expressed — and inherited.
• Life is built from atoms and molecules; structure determines function.
• Plants are the photosynthetic foundation of terrestrial ecosystems.
• We classify life to reflect its evolutionary history.
• The cell is the basic unit of life, and its membrane controls what enters and leaves.

🔗 PART B · NAME THE BRIDGE (6 PTS, ~1.2 EACH)
Each row crosses from one module into the next. From the bank, write the letter of the bridge that connects them — the idea from the earlier module that the later one depends on.
Crossing Bridge (write A–E)
M4 → M10: cell energy → the carbon cycle 
M5 → M6: genetics → evolution 
M6 → M7: evolution → classification 
M3 → M9: membrane transport → the neuron 
M8 → M10: plants → ecosystems 
BRIDGE BANK (use each once)
A. Photosynthesis and respiration move the same carbon atoms between glucose and CO₂ — the cell-scale chemistry that becomes the global carbon cycle.
B. Mutation and the shuffling of meiosis create the heritable variation that is the raw material natural selection acts on.
C. Common descent produces a nested pattern of relatedness — exactly the pattern a classification system (and cladograms) is built to capture.
D. The Na⁺/K⁺ pump — active transport across the membrane — sets up the neuron’s resting potential so it can fire an action potential.
E. Plant photosynthesis is the energy that enters nearly every food web, making plants the producers at the base of the energy pyramid.
✏️ PART C · WRITE THE YEAR’S ARGUMENT (4 PTS)
In five to seven sentences, explain in your own words how all of biology forms one connected argument — starting with atoms and water and ending with the living planet. Use at least five of these terms and connect them: polarity / hydrogen bonding, membrane, enzyme, mitochondria or chloroplast, DNA, protein, mutation, variation, natural selection, common descent, food web, carbon cycle. You are not listing facts — you are showing how each idea makes the next one possible.
