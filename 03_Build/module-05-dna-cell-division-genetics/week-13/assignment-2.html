Before you begin: finish Reading 13.02 and work through Lesson 13.02, including independent assortment, the dihybrid 9:3:3:1 ratio, and the multiplication rule.
Lesson focus: independent genes assort separately, and probabilities multiply
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Three parts: report the four gamete types and the 9:3:3:1 ratio for a dihybrid cross, then use the multiplication rule on two probability problems. The whole assignment should take about 20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why can you find the chance of a specific two-trait offspring by multiplying two simple fractions instead of drawing sixteen boxes?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
All parts use the cross RrYy × RrYy, where R (round) is dominant to r (wrinkled) and Y (yellow) is dominant to y (green).
1
Part A — Gametes and ratio (4 pts). List the four kinds of gamete an RrYy parent can make (2 pts), then write the full phenotype ratio expected from the RrYy × RrYy cross (2 pts).
2
Part B — Probability problems (4 pts, 2 each). Use the multiplication rule. Show your multiplication for each.
Question Your fraction (show the multiplication)
b1. Fraction expected to be round AND yellow 
b2. Fraction expected to be wrinkled AND green 
3
Part C — Explain the rule (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why you are allowed to multiply the two separate probabilities. Use the phrase independent assortment.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A lists RY, Ry, rY, ry and gives the 9:3:3:1 phenotype ratio.
☐
Part B: round-yellow = 3/4 × 3/4 = 9/16; wrinkled-green = 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16, with the multiplication shown.
☐
Part C says the two genes assort independently, so the events are independent and their probabilities multiply.
