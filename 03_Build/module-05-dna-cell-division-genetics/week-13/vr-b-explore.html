Week 13 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk a 3-Generation Pedigree — Diagnose the Pattern
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk through a three-generation family tree rendered in space — grandparents at the top, their children in the middle, the youngest generation below. Squares are males, circles are females, and filled symbols are affected by a trait. By reading which family members are affected and which are not, you will reason out two things: is the trait dominant or recessive, and is it autosomal or sex-linked? Standing inside the pedigree, instead of squinting at a flat chart, makes the generations and matings easy to follow. This is Lesson 13.04, walked.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 13.04 and Lesson 13.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Family History Hall — Pedigree Walk (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows the symbol key and your diagnosis checklist. Begin there.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Generation I (grandparents). Stand at the top of the tree. Note who is affected (filled) and who is not (open), and whether they are male (square) or female (circle). Place a sticky note recording what you see.
2
Generation II (children). Follow the vertical lines down to the next generation. Look especially for any affected child of two unaffected parents — that pattern is a strong clue. Place a sticky note.
3
Generation III (grandchildren). Walk to the bottom row. Tally how many affected individuals are male versus female — a strong lean toward males hints at X-linked inheritance. Place a sticky note.
4
Diagnose & capture your evidence. Decide: dominant or recessive, and autosomal or sex-linked. Then take three named snapshots, one at each generation, naming each so you can use them later.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note stating your diagnosis — whether the trait is dominant or recessive and autosomal or sex-linked — with one reason drawn from the pedigree.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students walk a three-generation pedigree and reason backward to a pattern. Common sticking points: confusing square/circle with affected/unaffected, and naming a pattern without citing evidence. The intended scene has two unaffected parents producing an affected child (recessive) with affected individuals skewed toward males (sex-linked) — but accept any diagnosis that is correctly justified from the symbols the student actually sees. A complete submission shows three named snapshots and a note that states both calls (dominant/recessive and autosomal/sex-linked) with at least one reason. This is the claim-evidence-reasoning move at the heart of every CER.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All three generations are walked and read (affected vs. unaffected, male vs. female).
☐
A sticky note at each generation records what was observed.
☐
Three named snapshots and a typed diagnosis (dominant/recessive and autosomal/sex-linked, with one reason) are submitted to Canvas.
