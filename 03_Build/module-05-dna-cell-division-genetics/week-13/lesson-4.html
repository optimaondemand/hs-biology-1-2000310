📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
We can’t run crosses on people. So how do we trace a trait through a human family?
We read the family’s history as a diagram — a pedigree — and we read an individual’s chromosomes as a karyotype. Today you will decode pedigree symbols to infer dominant vs. recessive inheritance, see how nondisjunction in meiosis changes chromosome number, and connect that error to Down (trisomy 21), Turner (XO), and Klinefelter (XXY).

👪 PART 1 · READING A PEDIGREE

The fixed symbols

Pedigree symbols are standardized so any geneticist can read them: a square is a male, a circle is a female; a filled symbol is affected, an open one unaffected; a horizontal line is a mating and a vertical line drops to the offspring. Reading one is detective work — you reason backward from the evidence the family gives you, exactly the claim-evidence-reasoning habit of every CER.

🧬 PART 2 · WHEN COPYING FAILS: NONDISJUNCTION

Meiosis (Week 11) usually delivers exactly one copy of each chromosome to each gamete. But sometimes a pair fails to separate — nondisjunction. One gamete then gets an extra chromosome, another gets one too few. Fertilize such a gamete and every cell of the new individual carries the wrong number. A karyotype — an organized photo of all the chromosomes in pairs — makes the error visible: a triple where there should be a pair, or a single where there should be two.

📚 PART 3 · WHO NAMED THE CHROMOSOMES OF SEX

The very idea that sex and many traits ride on specific chromosomes was hard-won — and the credit for it was not always fairly given.

🏛️ A HUMAN STORY · NETTIE STEVENS AND THOMAS HUNT MORGAN
In 1905, the American biologist Nettie Stevens examined the cells of mealworm beetles and saw it plainly: females carried two large matching chromosomes, males one large and one small. She concluded that sex is determined by chromosomes — the X and the Y. It was a foundational result, reached independently around the same time by E. B. Wilson, yet much of the credit in her lifetime flowed to others, and her name slipped out of the textbooks for decades. Soon after, Thomas Hunt Morgan and his students, breeding fruit flies, showed that particular genes are physically carried on particular chromosomes — finally tying Mendel’s abstract “factors” to real structures you can stain and see. Stevens’s story rhymes with Mendel’s ignored decades and Rosalind Franklin’s contested credit earlier in this module: doing science well includes giving honest credit — the module’s thread of responsibility again.

💪 THE IDEA THIS BUILDS

A pedigree lets you read inheritance backward from a real family; a karyotype lets you read chromosome number directly. Both show the module’s theme from two sides — faithful copying that passes traits on whole, and the consequences when a single meiotic step fails.
