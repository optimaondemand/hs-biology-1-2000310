📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
If red and white snapdragons make pink offspring, was Mendel wrong?
No — nature is just richer than his tidy peas. Today you will distinguish four patterns that go beyond simple dominance: incomplete dominance, codominance, multiple alleles (the ABO blood-type system), and sex-linkage — and you will see why some traits show up far more often in males.

🌸 PART 1 · BLEND OR BOTH?

Incomplete dominance vs. codominance

In incomplete dominance, the heterozygote is an intermediate — a true blend. Red × white snapdragons give pink. In codominance, the heterozygote shows both alleles fully and separately — not a blend. A roan cow has red hairs and white hairs side by side; a person of blood type AB makes both the A and B markers. The test question is always the same: does the heterozygote look like a mix (incomplete) or like both at once (codominant)?

🩸 PART 2 · MULTIPLE ALLELES: ABO BLOOD TYPES

A gene can have more than two alleles in a population — though any one person carries only two. ABO blood type uses three: IA and IB (codominant with each other) and i (recessive to both).

Genotype(s) Blood type
IAIA or IAiA
IBIB or IBiB
IAIBAB
iiO
⚡ SELF-CHECK · TAP TO REVEAL
A mother is type O and a father is type AB. Can they have a type-O child?
(Work it out, then tap to reveal.)
No. The mother (ii) can only pass an i allele. The father (IAIB) can only pass IA or IB — never i. So every child is either IAi (type A) or IBi (type B). A type-O child would need two i alleles, and the father has none to give.
♂️ PART 3 · SEX-LINKAGE

Females are XX, males are XY. The X chromosome carries genes for things like color vision and blood clotting. A female has a second X that can mask a recessive allele; a male has only one X, so whatever is on it shows — he is hemizygous. That is why X-linked recessive conditions like red-green color blindness and hemophilia appear more often in males.

Carry this worked case into the quiz. A carrier mother (XCXc) and a normal-vision father (XCY): every son gets his single X from his mother, and half of her X chromosomes carry c — so half of the sons are color blind. The daughters all get a normal XC from their father, so none of them are color blind (though half are carriers).

💪 THE IDEA THIS BUILDS

These patterns extend Mendel rather than break him — the alleles are still copied faithfully and passed on whole; only their expression together varies. Knowing them lets you read real human inheritance: a blood type that rules a parent in or out, a daughter who is a carrier without ever showing a trait, a condition that skips from grandfather to grandson.
