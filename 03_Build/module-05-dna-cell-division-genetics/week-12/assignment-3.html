📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
When the Code Changes
Reading 12.03 and Lesson 12.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 12.03 and work through Lesson 12.03, including substitutions (silent, missense, nonsense) and frameshifts.
Lesson focus: a substitution changes at most one codon; a frameshift garbles every codon downstream
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Two short parts: classify three substitutions as silent, missense, or nonsense in a table, then explain in one or two sentences why a frameshift is usually more damaging than a substitution. The whole assignment should take about 15 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
When one letter of the genetic message changes, why does it sometimes matter enormously and sometimes not at all?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Classify each substitution (6 pts, 2 each). For each codon change, mark whether it is silent, missense, or nonsense. (Phe = phenylalanine; Leu = leucine; a dash = stop.)
Codon change Silent, missense, or nonsense?
UUU (Phe) → UUC (Phe) 
UUA (Leu) → UUU (Phe) 
UAU (Tyr) → UAA (stop) 
2
Part B — Why is a frameshift worse? (4 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why deleting a single base (a frameshift) is usually more damaging than a single substitution. Use the idea of the reading frame.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A is correct: row 1 = silent (same amino acid), row 2 = missense (Leu→Phe), row 3 = nonsense (codon becomes a stop).
☐
Part B explains that a frameshift shifts the reading frame so every codon after the change is garbled, while a substitution changes at most one codon.
