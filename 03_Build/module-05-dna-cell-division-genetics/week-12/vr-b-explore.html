Week 12 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Trace a Sickle-Cell Mutation from Base to Bent Protein
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk the famous chain of sickle-cell disease, one stage at a time: a single DNA base change → the mRNA codon GAG→GUG → the amino acid Glu→Val → altered hemoglobin molecules clumping → a sickled red blood cell. Seeing each link as a place you can stand makes the abstract trace concrete — and gives you the exact backbone of your Module 5 CER. This is Lesson 12.04, walked end to end.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 12.04 and Lesson 12.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: From Gene to Disease — The Sickle-Cell Walk (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the five stages. Begin there.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Stage 1 — The DNA. Stand at the beta-globin gene and find the single substituted base. Take a named snapshot: “1 – DNA base change.”
2
Stage 2 — The mRNA codon. Move to the mRNA and read the affected codon. Confirm it has changed GAG → GUG. Snapshot: “2 – codon GAG to GUG.”
3
Stage 3 — The amino acid. At the ribosome, see the amino acid placed change from glutamic acid (Glu) to valine (Val). Snapshot: “3 – Glu to Val.”
4
Stage 4 — The protein. Watch the altered hemoglobin molecules clump into stiff rods when oxygen is low. Snapshot: “4 – hemoglobin clumping.”
5
Stage 5 — The phenotype. Stand beside the finished cell and see it bent into a rigid sickle. Snapshot: “5 – sickled red blood cell.”
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your five named snapshots (one per stage) to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note that states the codon change (GAG→GUG) and the resulting amino-acid change (Glu→Val), and names this as a missense mutation.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students walk the five-link trace as five physical stations (DNA → codon → amino acid → protein → phenotype). Common sticking points: reversing the codon change (writing GUG→GAG), or naming the wrong amino acid — reinforce GAG = glutamic acid, GUG = valine, a missense change. Push students to connect shape to function: the hydrophobic valine is what makes hemoglobin clump. A complete submission shows five named snapshots and a note correctly stating GAG→GUG and Glu→Val. This walk gives students the exact spine of their Module 5 CER essay.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All five stages are visited in order (DNA, codon, amino acid, protein, phenotype).
☐
A named snapshot is captured at each of the five stages.
☐
Five named snapshots and the typed note (stating GAG→GUG, Glu→Val, missense) are submitted to Canvas.
