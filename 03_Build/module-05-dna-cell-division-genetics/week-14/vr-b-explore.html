Week 14 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Explore a CRISPR-Cas9 Scene Editing a Target Gene
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will step inside a cell and watch CRISPR-Cas9 edit a gene in slow motion. You’ll follow a guide RNA as it travels along the DNA until it finds its matching sequence, watch the Cas9 protein cut the DNA there, and see the cell’s machinery edit the gene. Along the way the scene reminds you that CRISPR was not invented from scratch — it began as a bacterial immune system for cutting up invading viruses. Seeing the guide→cut→edit sequence unfold in three dimensions makes Lesson 14.04 concrete.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 14.04 and Lesson 14.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Inside the Genome — CRISPR Editing Scene (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the four scene stops. Begin there.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Meet the guide RNA. Find the guide RNA riding on the Cas9 protein. Read its short sequence. Place a sticky note: what does the guide RNA do, and how does it recognize its target?
2
Find the match. Follow the Cas9-and-guide complex along the DNA until the guide RNA base-pairs with its matching target sequence. Pause there. Place a sticky note marking the target site.
3
Watch the cut and the edit. Trigger the scene to play: Cas9 cuts the DNA at the target, then the cell’s repair machinery edits the gene. Observe the order carefully — guide finds, Cas9 cuts, cell edits.
4
See the origin & capture evidence. Visit the side panel showing CRISPR as a bacterial defense against viruses. Then take three named snapshots: (1) guide RNA locating the target, (2) Cas9 cutting, (3) the edited gene.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note describing the sequence in order — guide RNA → Cas9 cut → edit — and one sentence noting that CRISPR began as a bacterial immune system.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students follow the guide-RNA→Cas9-cut→edit sequence and learn CRISPR’s bacterial origin. Common sticking points: thinking Cas9 (not the guide RNA) chooses the target — the guide RNA finds the match by base-pairing, and Cas9 does the cutting; and missing that the cell’s own repair machinery completes the edit. A complete submission shows three named snapshots in the right order and a note correctly sequencing guide→cut→edit, plus the bacterial-defense origin. This scene gives students the mechanistic picture behind the Lesson 14.04 ethics discussion.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All four scene stops are visited (guide RNA, target match, cut & edit, bacterial origin) and observed.
☐
A sticky note marks how the guide RNA recognizes its target and where the cut occurs.
☐
Three named snapshots and the typed note (guide → cut → edit, plus bacterial origin) are submitted to Canvas.
