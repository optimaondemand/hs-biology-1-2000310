When Control Breaks Down
Reading 14.01 and Lesson 14.01
Before you begin: finish Reading 14.01 and work through Lesson 14.01, including the three checkpoints, tumor-suppressor and proto-oncogene genes, and why cancer is a genetic disease.
Lesson focus: Cancer is, at the level of the cell, a genetic disease — a failure of cell-cycle control caused by mutations
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each checkpoint to what it verifies, classify mutation sources as inherited or acquired, and explain in one or two sentences why cancer is called a genetic disease. The whole assignment should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A cell copies itself trillions of times. What inspects each copy — and what happens when the genes that run those inspections are mutated?
