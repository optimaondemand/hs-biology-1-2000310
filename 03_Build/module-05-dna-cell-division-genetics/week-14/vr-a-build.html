Week 14 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Model a Gel-Electrophoresis Run — Load & Separate Fragments
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will run a gel electrophoresis with your hands. You’ll load DNA samples of different fragment sizes into the wells at one end of the gel, switch on the current, and watch the fragments migrate toward the positive (+) electrode. As they move, you’ll see the key idea become visible: smaller fragments travel farther through the gel mesh while larger fragments stay near the wells. Then you’ll read the band pattern — turning an abstract rule from Lesson 14.02 into something you can see and point at.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 14.02 and Lesson 14.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Molecular Biology Lab — Gel Bench (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the gel-electrophoresis bench marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Set up the gel. Place the gel slab in the tray and identify the two ends: the wells (where samples load) sit at the negative (−) end; the positive (+) electrode is at the far end. Place a label on each end.
2
Load the samples. From the rack, pipette three samples into three wells: a large fragment, a medium fragment, and a small fragment. Note which well holds which.
3
Run the current. Switch on the power. Watch the fragments move toward the + electrode (because DNA is negatively charged). Let the run finish.
4
Read the bands. Find where each band ended up. Confirm with your own eyes that the smallest fragment traveled farthest from the wells and the largest stayed closest. Place a sticky note on the band farthest from the wells reading “smallest fragment.”
5
Capture your evidence. Take a labeled snapshot of your finished gel (or start a short spatial recording), showing the well end, the + end, and the three bands in their positions.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your labeled snapshot (or short spatial recording) of the finished gel to this Canvas assignment, plus one sentence explaining why small fragments travel farther than large ones.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students load fragments of three sizes, run the current, and observe size-based separation toward the + electrode. Common sticking points: thinking the gel sorts by charge (it sorts by size — all fragments carry the same negative charge), and expecting larger fragments to travel farther (smaller travel farther). Nudge any student whose “smallest” note lands near the wells. A strong submission shows three bands correctly positioned, the + end labeled, and a one-sentence explanation that smaller fragments slip through the gel mesh more easily.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Three samples are loaded and the current is run, with the wells (−) and + electrode ends labeled.
☐
The band pattern correctly shows the smallest fragment farthest from the wells and the largest closest.
☐
A labeled snapshot (or recording) and one sentence on why small fragments travel farther are submitted to Canvas.
