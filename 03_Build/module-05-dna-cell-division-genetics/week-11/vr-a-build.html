Week 11 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a DNA Double Helix — Base Pairing & the Replication Fork
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a DNA double helix with your hands — snapping together nucleotides (sugar, phosphate, base) into two antiparallel sugar-phosphate backbones, then pairing the bases correctly: A with T, C with G. Once your helix is right, you will open a replication fork and act out the copying: helicase unzips the two strands, and DNA polymerase builds a new complementary strand against each old one. Flat diagrams hide how the two strands run opposite ways and how each becomes a template; building it in three dimensions makes it obvious. This is Lessons 11.01 and 11.02, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 11.01 and 11.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Molecular Modeling room (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the workbench marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Assemble a nucleotide. From the parts bin, snap together one sugar, one phosphate, and one base to make a single nucleotide. Make a few of each base (A, T, C, G).
2
Build one backbone. Link your nucleotides’ sugars and phosphates into a single sugar-phosphate backbone — one rail of the ladder — in any base order you like.
3
Pair the bases. Build the second strand by adding the complementary base across from each of yours: A–T and C–G. Set the second backbone running the opposite direction (antiparallel), then give the whole ladder its twist into a helix.
4
Open a replication fork. Grab the helix at one end and pull the two strands apart to make a Y-shaped fork. Place a sticky note labeled “helicase — unzips here.” Then, on each separated old strand, add new nucleotides by the pairing rule and label one “DNA polymerase building new strand.”
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk along your helix and explain aloud, in about 60–90 seconds: the parts of a nucleotide, the base-pairing rule, why the strands are antiparallel, and how each old strand templates a new one. Use the words complementary, helicase, and semiconservative.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your labeled helix at the replication fork plus a few typed sentences naming the base pairs (A–T, C–G) and explaining how helicase and polymerase copy each strand.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize DNA structure (nucleotide parts, antiparallel backbones, A–T/C–G pairing) and then act out semiconservative replication at a fork. Common sticking points: pairing A with G or C with T (wrong), forgetting the strands run opposite directions, and treating both new strands as wholly new rather than each pairing with an old template. Nudge students who pair bases incorrectly back to the rule. A strong submission shows a correctly paired antiparallel helix, an opened fork with helicase and polymerase labeled, new strands built by the rule, and a recording that uses “complementary,” “helicase,” and “semiconservative” correctly.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A double helix is built with two antiparallel sugar-phosphate backbones and bases paired A–T and C–G.
☐
A replication fork is opened with helicase and polymerase labeled, and new strands built by the pairing rule.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted that explains the build.
