Before you begin: finish Reading 11.02 and work through Lesson 11.02, including semiconservative replication and the jobs of helicase, DNA polymerase, and ligase.
Lesson focus: DNA is copied semiconservatively by a crew of enzymes
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: explain semiconservative replication, match the three enzymes to their jobs, and use a template strand to build its new complement. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does a cell make two perfect copies of its DNA from one — and why does keeping an original strand make the copy more faithful?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Semiconservative, in your own words (3 pts). In two to three sentences, explain what it means that DNA replication is semiconservative. Be sure to say what each new double helix is made of (one old strand and one new strand).
2
Part B — Match the enzyme (6 pts, 2 each). For each job, name the enzyme responsible: helicase, DNA polymerase, or ligase.
Job at the replication fork Enzyme
Unzips the helix by breaking hydrogen bonds between bases 
Adds complementary nucleotides along each template strand 
Seals the joints between segments into one continuous strand 
3
Part C — Build the new strand (1 pt). A template strand reads A – A – C – G – T. Write the new complementary strand polymerase would build.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A states each daughter molecule keeps one old strand and one new strand.
☐
Part B is correct: helicase (unzip), DNA polymerase (build), ligase (seal).
☐
Part C reads TTGCA.
