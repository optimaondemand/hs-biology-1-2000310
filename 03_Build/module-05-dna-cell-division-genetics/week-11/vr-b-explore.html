Week 11 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk Through a Cell in Mitosis (PMAT Stations)
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk through a giant dividing cell laid out as four stations — Prophase → Metaphase → Anaphase → Telophase — in order. At each station you will observe what the chromosomes are doing: condensing and becoming visible, lining up at the equator, separating to opposite poles, and being re-wrapped in new nuclear envelopes. Walking the phases in sequence makes mitosis a process you move through, not a row of still pictures — and it makes the order of PMAT stick. This is Lesson 11.03, walked in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 11.03 and Lesson 11.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Inside a Dividing Cell — PMAT Walk (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the four station markers. Begin at the Prophase station.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Station 1 — Prophase. Watch the loose DNA condense into visible chromosomes (joined sister chromatids) as the nuclear envelope breaks down. Place a sticky note: what just happened to the chromosomes and the nucleus?
2
Station 2 — Metaphase. Watch the chromosomes line up single-file across the cell’s equator (the metaphase plate). Place a sticky note: where are the chromosomes, and why might lining them up matter before they separate?
3
Station 3 — Anaphase. Watch the sister chromatids separate and get pulled to opposite poles. Place a sticky note: what is moving apart, and why does each pole need a full set?
4
Station 4 — Telophase & capture your evidence. Watch new nuclear envelopes re-form around each set, ready for cytokinesis to split the cell in two. Then take four named snapshots, one at each station (Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase), naming each so you can use them later.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: list the four phases in order and write one sentence saying what the chromosomes do in each.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students walk the four phases of mitosis in order and observe chromosome behavior at each. Common sticking points: confusing Metaphase (lining up) with Anaphase (pulling apart), and reversing the order of the two. Push students to tie each phase to what the chromosomes are doing — condense (P), line up (M), separate (A), re-wrap (T). A complete submission shows four named snapshots in correct PMAT order and a note that correctly describes the chromosomes’ behavior at each station. This walk gives students the moving picture behind the cell-cycle ordering in their Module 5 work.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All four stations are visited in order (Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase) and observed.
☐
A sticky note at each station records what the chromosomes are doing in that phase.
☐
Four named snapshots and the typed note (phases in order, with chromosome behavior) are submitted to Canvas.
