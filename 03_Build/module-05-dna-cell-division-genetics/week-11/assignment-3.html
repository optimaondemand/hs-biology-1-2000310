📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
One Cell Becomes Two
Reading 11.03 and Lesson 11.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 11.03 and work through Lesson 11.03, including the cell cycle (G1, S, G2, M) and the four phases of mitosis (PMAT).
Lesson focus: the cell cycle and mitosis make two genetically identical cells
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: put the cell-cycle stages in order, match each PMAT phase to what the chromosomes are doing, and state the product of mitosis and its purpose. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
In what exact order must a cell grow, copy its DNA, and divide — and how does mitosis give each new cell a complete, identical set?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Order the cell cycle (2 pts). Put these stages in the correct order and write, next to each, what happens in it: S, G2, M (mitosis & cytokinesis), G1.
2
Part B — Match the phase (6 pts, 1.5 each). For each event, name the phase of mitosis (Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, or Telophase).
Event Phase
Sister chromatids separate to opposite poles 
Chromosomes condense; nuclear envelope breaks down 
Nuclear envelopes re-form around two sets of chromosomes 
Chromosomes line up single-file at the cell’s equator 
3
Part C — The product (2 pts). In one sentence, state how many daughter cells mitosis makes, whether they are genetically identical or unique, and one job mitosis does in the body.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A order is G1 → S → G2 → M, each with a correct one-line description.
☐
Part B is correct: Anaphase, Prophase, Telophase, Metaphase.
☐
Part C says two genetically identical cells, used for growth/repair/replacement.
