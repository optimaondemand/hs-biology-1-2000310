**1. (Reading 2.01 — what a scientific theory is)**
In science, which statement best describes a **theory**?

- A) A guess made before an experiment begins
- B) A well-substantiated explanation supported by a large body of evidence **✓**
- C) A fact that can never be questioned again
- D) A mathematical rule that has been proven beyond all doubt

*Targets the "a theory is just a guess" misconception. A scientific theory is a broad, well-tested explanation — among the strongest things science can say.*

**2. (Reading 2.01 — theory vs. law)**
A scientific **law** differs from a scientific **theory** because a law:

- A) Has more evidence behind it than any theory
- B) Is a theory that has finally been proven true
- C) Describes *what* consistently happens, while a theory explains *why* **✓**
- D) Can never be changed, while a theory always changes

*Targets the "a theory grows up into a law" misconception. Laws describe patterns; theories explain causes — different kinds of statements, neither becoming the other.*

**3. (Reading 2.01 — hypothesis)**
Which of the following is best classified as a **hypothesis**?

- A) Energy cannot be created or destroyed in a closed system
- B) All organisms are made of one or more cells
- C) Increasing the light intensity will increase the rate at which this water plant releases bubbles **✓**
- D) Many infectious diseases are caused by microorganisms

*A hypothesis is a specific, testable, falsifiable prediction set up for an experiment. A is a law, B and D are theories.*

**4. (Reading 2.02 — reproducibility)**
A research team reports a surprising result. Before the wider scientific community accepts it, what matters most?

- A) That the result was announced in a press release
- B) That independent researchers can reproduce the result using the same methods **✓**
- C) That the lead scientist has many followers online
- D) That the result is surprising and exciting

*Reproducibility by independent labs is what turns a single result into trustworthy knowledge; a striking result alone is only a question.*

**5. (Reading 2.02 — self-correction / replication)**
When scientists publicly report that some past published studies could not be reproduced and then reform how studies are run, this is best understood as:

- A) Proof that science cannot be trusted at all
- B) A scandal that should be hidden from the public
- C) The self-correcting process of science working as intended **✓**
- D) Evidence that all scientific theories are merely opinions

*Targets the misconception that error in science means science is unreliable. Detecting and correcting weak results is exactly what makes the process trustworthy.*

---

**6. (Reading 2.03 — falsifiability / pseudoscience)**
Which feature most clearly marks a claim as **pseudoscience** rather than science?

- A) It uses scientific-sounding vocabulary
- B) It is new and not yet widely accepted
- C) It is written so that no possible evidence could ever prove it wrong **✓**
- D) It was proposed by a single researcher

*A genuinely scientific claim is falsifiable — it specifies what would disprove it. Being new (B) or from one researcher (D) does not make a claim pseudoscientific; Wegener and Marshall were both.*

---

**7. (Reading 2.03 — nature and limits of models)**
A biologist uses a simplified diagram of a cell to teach its parts. Which statement about this **model** is most accurate?

- A) The model is useless because it leaves out details
- B) The model is a perfect, complete copy of a real cell
- C) The model is useful for its purpose even though it leaves some details out **✓**
- D) A good model must include every feature of the real object

*All models simplify on purpose; a model is judged by whether it is useful for the question being asked, not by whether it is complete.*

**8. (Reading 2.04 — controlled experiment: Redi/Pasteur)**
In Pasteur's experiment, broth in a swan-neck flask stayed clear, but broth grew microbes once the flask was tipped so the liquid touched the neck's curve. This result supports the conclusion that the microbes came from:

- A) The broth itself, generated spontaneously
- B) Dust and microorganisms trapped in the curved neck **✓**
- C) The heat used to boil the broth
- D) A vital force present in the air

*Because only the broth's contact with the trapped dust changed, the growth must have come from microbes already in the air — disproving spontaneous generation ("all life comes from life").*
