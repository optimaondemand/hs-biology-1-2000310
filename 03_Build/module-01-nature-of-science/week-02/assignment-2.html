📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
How Science Checks Itself
Reading 2.02 and Lesson 2.02
Before you begin: finish Reading 2.02 and Lesson 2.02. Keep the three safeguards in mind: peer review, reproducibility, and consensus.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
For each of three short research scenarios, you’ll decide how much it should be trusted and name the safeguard your decision rests on. About 15–20 minutes.
🎯 THE THREE SCENARIOS
A. A wellness influencer posts that a tea “resets your metabolism,” citing a single study they funded themselves and that no other lab has repeated.

B. A finding that a certain gene raises heart-disease risk has been published in a peer-reviewed journal and independently reproduced by four research teams on three continents.

C. A new lab reports a startling result — bacteria that seem to survive without water. It’s exciting and not yet peer-reviewed; the lab has shared its full methods and invited others to try to reproduce it.
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Rate the trust (3 pts). For each scenario (A, B, C), give a trust level: Low, Cautious / wait and see, or High.
2
Name the safeguard (6 pts). For each scenario, write one or two sentences explaining your rating by naming the safeguard that decided it — peer review, reproducibility, or consensus — and how it applies.
3
Apply the idea (3 pts). Scenario C is exciting but unconfirmed. In two or three sentences, explain what would have to happen for it to earn high trust — and why a single exciting result isn’t enough on its own.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All three scenarios are given a defensible trust level.
☐
Each rating names the correct safeguard and connects it to the scenario.
☐
Part 3 explains why replication is needed before a striking result is trusted.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Proficient Developing Beginning Pts
