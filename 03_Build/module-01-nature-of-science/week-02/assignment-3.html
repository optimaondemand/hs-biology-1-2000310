📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Models, and How to Spot Pseudoscience
Reading 2.03 and Lesson 2.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 2.03 and Lesson 2.03, and open the MedlinePlus “Evaluating Health Information” guide linked in the lesson.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Find one real-world health or “wellness” claim and put it on trial using the four-question filter. You’ll deliver a short verdict supported by reasoning — a warm-up for this week’s full CER essay. About 20–25 minutes.
🎯 THE FOUR-QUESTION FILTER
1. Could it be proven wrong? (Is it falsifiable, or vague enough to explain anything?)
2. What’s the evidence? (Controlled studies others reproduced — or testimonials and cherry-picked cases?)
3. Does it change with evidence? (Would the claim be revised, or are doubters always blamed?)
4. Why should I believe it? (Open methods anyone can check — or “trust me / secret knowledge”?)
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Find and quote a claim (2 pts). Locate a real health/wellness claim (a product ad, a social-media post, a “miracle cure” headline). Quote it in a sentence or two and give the source (site or platform). Keep it school-appropriate.
2
Run the four questions (6 pts). Answer each of the four filter questions for your claim — one or two sentences each, with specifics from the claim itself.
3
Deliver a verdict (3 pts). State whether the claim behaves more like science or pseudoscience, and give the single strongest reason from your analysis. (This is a claim + reasoning — CER in miniature.)
4
Fairness check (1 pt). In one sentence, note what evidence would change your verdict — because a claim being new or unpopular is not the same as being false.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
A real, sourced health claim is quoted.
☐
All four questions are answered with specifics from the claim.
☐
A clear verdict with reasoning, plus a fair statement of what would change it.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Proficient Developing Beginning Pts
Claim & source
2 pts		 Real claim quoted with source. Claim given but vague or unsourced. No usable claim. 2 pts
Four questions
6 pts		 All four answered with specifics. Two or three answered, or generic. One or none. 6 pts
Verdict + fairness
4 pts		 Clear verdict with reasoning and a fair “what would change it.” Verdict given but thin reasoning or no fairness check. No real verdict. 4 pts
Total: 12 pts
