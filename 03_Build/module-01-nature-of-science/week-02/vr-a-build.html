Week 2 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build the “Hypothesis, Theory, Law” Sorting Wall
Solo VR activity · ~25 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a three-column sorting wall in VR and place real scientific statements where they belong: Hypothesis, Theory, or Law. Sorting cards with your hands — and having to defend each placement aloud — makes the difference between describing a pattern (law) and explaining a cause (theory) stick far better than rereading a definition. This builds directly on Lesson 2.01.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lesson 2.01 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Classical Lecture Hall (Optima VR library)
On arrival: face the large blank wall marked by three empty Whiteboard IFX panels labeled Hypothesis, Theory, and Law. A stack of statement cards sits on the table in front of it.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Set up the three columns. Confirm the three labeled panels on the wall: Hypothesis, Theory, and Law. If a label is missing, write it on a sticky note and post it.
2
Sort at least six cards. Pick up each statement card and place it under the correct column. Include at least two cards in each of the three columns. (Example cards: “All cells come from pre-existing cells,” “Adding fertilizer will make these seedlings grow taller,” “In a gas at constant temperature, pressure rises as volume falls.”) If you run low on cards, write your own on sticky notes.
3
Add a “myth-buster” note. On a sticky note placed between the Theory and Law columns, write one sentence answering: Why does a theory never “become” a law? Use the words describe and explain.
4
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk the wall and explain, in about 60–90 seconds, why each of three cards (one per column) belongs where you put it — especially what distinguishes your theory card from your law card.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your full sorted wall plus a few typed sentences explaining your theory-vs-law distinction and your myth-buster note.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 25 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the hypothesis/theory/law distinction as a spatial sort. The most common errors mirror the EOC misconceptions: filing a broad explanation (theory) under “law,” or treating “law” as a promoted theory. Listen in the recording for whether the student frames a law as describing a pattern and a theory as explaining a cause. A strong submission sorts all cards correctly and gives a myth-buster note that uses “describe” vs. “explain” accurately.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
At least six statement cards are sorted, with two or more in each of the three columns.
☐
A myth-buster note explains why a theory does not become a law, using “describe” and “explain.”
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) defends three placements, including theory vs. law.
