Week 2 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk the Spontaneous-Generation Timeline
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk a three-station timeline that traces how one stubborn question — can life arise from nonliving matter? — was finally answered. You’ll stand at Aristotle’s belief, Redi’s covered jars, and Pasteur’s swan-neck flasks, observe each setup, and capture the evidence. Walking a discovery in order, instead of reading it, makes the logic of how the question was settled unforgettable — and it’s the perfect lead-in to your Module 1 CER.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · Reading 2.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: History of Science Gallery — Spontaneous Generation Walk (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX at the entrance shows your three-station observation checklist. Begin at Station 1.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Station 1 — The old belief. Observe the scene of meat left out with maggots appearing. On a sticky note, write the conclusion someone before controlled experiments would draw — and why it seemed obvious.
2
Station 2 — Redi’s jars (1668). Examine the open, sealed, and gauze-covered jars. On a sticky note, identify which jar is the control and explain what the gauze jar proves that the sealed jar alone could not.
3
Station 3 — Pasteur’s flasks (1859). Compare the clear swan-neck flask with the broken-neck flask clouded with growth. On a sticky note, explain why leaving the flask open was essential, and what the broth’s clearness proves.
4
Capture your evidence. Take three named snapshots — one at each station — each showing your sticky note. At the end of the walk, find the “Omne vivum ex vivo” banner and read it aloud.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed answer to: How did the question move from a belief to a settled conclusion? Name what each experiment added.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students walk a three-station historical timeline and annotate the logic of each experiment. Common sticking points: placing and typing on sticky notes, and capturing a named snapshot (not a system screenshot). Push students to explain why the gauze jar and the open swan neck mattered — both exist to defeat the “you blocked the air/vital force” objection. A complete submission shows three annotated stations and a clear account of how evidence, not argument, settled the question.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All three stations are visited and annotated with a sticky note.
☐
The notes correctly identify Redi’s control/gauze logic and Pasteur’s open-flask logic.
☐
Three named snapshots and the typed reflection are submitted to Canvas.
