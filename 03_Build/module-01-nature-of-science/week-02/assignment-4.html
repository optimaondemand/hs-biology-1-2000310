Analyze Pasteur’s Experiment
Assignment · 12 pts
📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
The Question That Took 2,000 Years
Reading 2.04 and Lesson 2.04
Before you begin: finish Reading 2.04 and Lesson 2.04. This assignment pulls together everything from Module 1 — experimental design from Week 1 and the swan-neck experiment from this week.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
You’ll dissect Pasteur’s swan-neck experiment like a scientist — naming its variables and control — then predict and justify the result of a new variation. About 20 minutes.
🎯 THE EXPERIMENT (RECAP)
Pasteur boiled identical nutrient broth in two flasks. Flask 1 had a long S-shaped “swan neck” that was left open to the air but trapped dust and microbes in its curve. Flask 2 had its neck snapped off straight, leaving the boiled broth directly exposed to the air. Both sat side by side in the same room for weeks. Flask 1 stayed clear; Flask 2 clouded with microbial growth.
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Name the design (6 pts). Identify, for this experiment: the independent variable, the dependent variable, two controlled variables (held constant), and which flask serves as the control. Label each answer.
2
Explain the genius (3 pts). In two or three sentences, explain why leaving the swan-neck flask open (rather than sealing it) was the key to the experiment — what counterargument did it defeat?
3
Predict & justify (3 pts). Suppose Pasteur took an untouched, still-sterile swan-neck flask and tipped it so the broth ran up into the dusty curve and back. Predict what would happen, and justify your prediction using the conclusion “all life comes from life.”
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
IV (neck shape / microbe access), DV (microbial growth), two constants, and the control flask are correctly identified.
☐
The explanation shows that an open flask defeated the “sealing ruins the air / vital force” objection.
☐
The prediction (broth grows microbes) is justified with correct reasoning.
