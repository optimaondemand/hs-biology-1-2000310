Hypothesis, Theory, and Law
Reading 2.01 and Lesson 2.01
Before you begin: finish Reading 2.01 and Lesson 2.01, including the sorting widget.
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
You will sort six real scientific statements into hypothesis, theory, or law, then explain — in your own words — the single most-misunderstood idea about these terms. About 15–20 minutes.
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Sort the six statements (6 pts). For each statement below, label it Hypothesis, Theory, or Law:
a. “Objects near Earth’s surface accelerate downward at about 9.8 m/s².”
b. “All living things are composed of one or more cells.”
c. “Adding salt to water will raise its boiling temperature in this beaker.”
d. “Species change over time through natural selection acting on inherited variation.”
e. “In a gas at constant temperature, pressure rises as volume falls.”
f. “This fertilizer will make my radish seedlings grow taller than plain soil.”
2
Explain one choice (3 pts). Pick any one statement you labeled a law and one you labeled a theory, and in two or three sentences explain how you could tell them apart — what is each one doing (describing a pattern vs. explaining a cause)?
3
Correct the myth (3 pts). A classmate says, “If a theory gets enough evidence, it becomes a law.” In two or three sentences, explain why this is wrong, using the words describe and explain.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All six statements are labeled, with hypotheses, theories, and laws correctly distinguished.
☐
The explanation distinguishes describing a pattern (law) from explaining a cause (theory).
☐
The myth correction explains why a theory does not become a law.
