📚 PART 1 · AN IDEA THAT SEEMED OBVIOUS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Meat left out grows maggots within days. To someone before microscopes, what’s the “obvious” conclusion?

For two thousand years, spontaneous generation — life arising from nonliving matter — seemed obvious because it matched what people saw. That’s what made it dangerous: a comfortable idea that fit the evidence loosely. Overturning it required someone to stop arguing and start testing.

🩸 PART 2 · TWO EXPERIMENTS THAT SETTLED IT
💡 A MOMENT IN HISTORY · REDI (1668) & PASTEUR (1859)
Francesco Redi put meat in jars: some open, some sealed, some covered with gauze that let air in but kept flies out. Maggots appeared only where flies could reach — the gauze jars rotted but stayed maggot-free, killing the objection that sealing simply “cut off the air.” Two centuries later, microbes revived the debate. Louis Pasteur boiled broth in swan-neck flasks — left open to the air, but with a long curve that trapped airborne microbes before they could reach the broth. It stayed sterile for months. Then he tipped the flask so the broth touched the trapped dust — and it teemed with life within a day. Omne vivum ex vivo: all life comes from life.

Tap to reveal how each design isolated one variable

⚡ TAP TO REVEAL · THE LOGIC OF EACH DESIGN
Redi’s controls — tap to reveal
Independent variable: fly access. Everything else — same meat, same room, same time — held constant. The open jars were the control; the gauze jars ruled out the “sealing blocks air” objection. Result: maggots only where flies could land.
Pasteur’s swan neck — tap to reveal
The flask stayed open, so “air was let in” — defeating the vital-force objection. The only thing the curved neck changed was whether dust and microbes could reach the broth. Sterile while curved; swarming once tipped. One variable, cleanly isolated.
The shared move — tap to reveal
Both men anticipated the strongest counterargument and designed it out. That’s the same CER lesson from Week 1: the most convincing case answers the best objection before the critic can raise it.
