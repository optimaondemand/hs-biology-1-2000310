What Counts as Science?

Before you begin: finish Reading 1.01 and work through Lesson 1.01, including its “Observation or Inference?” practice.
Lesson focus: telling observation from inference, and qualitative data from quantitative
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two short parts. In Part A you classify ten statements — deciding whether each is an observation or an inference, and whether the data are qualitative or quantitative. In Part B you become the scientist: you record five observations of your own from the world around you and label each one honestly. The whole assignment should take about 20–25 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
When you write down what you notice about the world, how do you keep what you saw separate from what you concluded?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Classify (5 pts). For each of the ten statements below, answer two questions in the boxes provided: (a) Is it an observation or an inference? (b) If it reports data, are those data qualitative or quantitative? (If a statement is purely an inference with no data, write “n/a” for part b.)
# Statement
1The beaker of water reached 82 °C after four minutes on the hot plate.
2The mouse is hungry.
3The leaf is bright red with three brown spots near the stem.
4The bacteria died because the antibiotic worked.
5There are 14 ants on the left side of the container and 3 on the right.
6The solution gives off a sharp, vinegar-like smell.
7This fish prefers warmer water.
8The seedling grew 2.6 cm over the week.
9The dog’s coat is thick and shiny.
10The plant turned toward the window because it wanted more sun.
2
Part B — Your own observations (5 pts). Go somewhere — a window, a kitchen, outside — and record five observations of living things or the natural world. For each one, write the observation, label it qualitative or quantitative, and then write one inference you could reasonably draw from it. Keep the observation and the inference in separate columns.
3
Submit your answers as a typed response or a photo of your scientific-notebook page. Either is fine — just make sure both parts are clearly labeled and readable.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Each of the ten statements in Part A is correctly labeled observation or inference, with the data type identified where data are present.
☐
Part B contains five genuine observations, each labeled qualitative or quantitative.
☐
For each Part B observation, the inference is kept clearly separate from the observation it is built on.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Proficient Developing Beginning Pts
