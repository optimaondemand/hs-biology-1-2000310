**1. (Reading 4.01 — dehydration synthesis)**
Two glucose molecules join together to form a disaccharide, and a single water molecule is released. What is this process called?

- A) Hydrolysis, because a bond is formed
- B) Dehydration synthesis, because water is removed to join the monomers **✓**
- C) Denaturation, because the molecule changes shape
- D) Hydration, because water is added

*Dehydration synthesis (condensation) bonds monomers by removing a water molecule. Hydrolysis is the reverse — adding water to break a bond.*

**2. (Reading 4.01 — polysaccharides)**
Which polysaccharide is used by animals to store energy in the liver and muscles?

- A) Cellulose
- B) Starch
- C) Glycogen **✓**
- D) Glucose

*Glycogen is the animal energy-storage polysaccharide. Starch stores energy in plants, cellulose is plant structure, and glucose is a single monosaccharide, not a polysaccharide.*

**3. (Reading 4.02 — why phospholipids form a bilayer)**
Why do phospholipids spontaneously arrange into a bilayer when placed in water?

- A) Their hydrophilic heads face the water while their hydrophobic tails turn inward, away from water **✓**
- B) Their hydrophobic heads face the water while their tails turn inward
- C) Phospholipids are nonpolar, so they dissolve completely in water
- D) The phosphate tails are attracted to one another by hydrogen bonds

*A phospholipid is amphipathic: the hydrophilic phosphate head faces the water and the hydrophobic fatty-acid tails tuck away from it, producing the bilayer that forms every cell membrane.*

**4. (Reading 4.02 — saturated vs. unsaturated fat)**
A fat is liquid at room temperature (an oil). Which best describes its fatty-acid tails?

- A) Saturated, with no carbon–carbon double bonds, so the straight tails pack tightly
- B) Unsaturated, with one or more carbon–carbon double bonds that kink the tails so they pack loosely **✓**
- C) Saturated, with many carbon–carbon double bonds
- D) A polymer of glycerol monomers

*Unsaturated fats have C=C double bonds that kink the tails, preventing tight packing — so they stay liquid. Saturated fats have straight tails that pack tightly and are solid at room temperature. (Note: lipids are not true polymers.)*

**5. (Reading 4.03 — structure determines function)**
An enzyme is heated far above body temperature and loses the ability to do its job, even after it cools. The function was lost most directly because heat changed the protein's —

- A) amino-acid sequence
- B) three-dimensional shape **✓**
- C) number of carbon atoms
- D) electronegativity

*Heat breaks the bonds holding the fold, so the protein denatures and its shape unravels. Because the active site no longer fits its substrate, function is lost — structure determines function. The amino-acid sequence (primary structure) itself is not changed.*

**6. (Reading 4.03 — levels of protein structure)**
Which level of protein structure refers to the specific sequence of amino acids in the chain?

- A) Primary structure **✓**
- B) Secondary structure
- C) Tertiary structure
- D) Quaternary structure

*Primary structure is the linear order of amino acids. Secondary is local folds (α-helix, β-sheet), tertiary is the overall 3-D fold, and quaternary is two or more chains assembled together.*

**7. (Reading 4.04 — monomer of a nucleic acid)**
What is the monomer that makes up a nucleic acid such as DNA?

- A) An amino acid
- B) A monosaccharide
- C) A nucleotide, made of a phosphate group, a sugar, and a nitrogenous base **✓**
- D) A fatty acid

*The nucleotide is the monomer of nucleic acids. Amino acids build proteins, monosaccharides build carbohydrates, and fatty acids are part of lipids.*

**8. (Reading 4.04 — base pairing)**
In a molecule of DNA, the base adenine (A) always pairs with which base?

- A) Cytosine
- B) Guanine
- C) Uracil
- D) Thymine **✓**

*In DNA, A pairs with T and C pairs with G, held by hydrogen bonds. Uracil is found in RNA, where it replaces thymine — so uracil is not a DNA base at all.*
