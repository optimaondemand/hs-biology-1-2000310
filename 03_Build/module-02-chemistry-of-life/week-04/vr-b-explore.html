Week 4 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk Through a Phospholipid Bilayer
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will step inside a phospholipid bilayer — the structure that forms every cell membrane — and walk through it. Phospholipids self-assemble in water because each one has a hydrophilic (“water-loving”) head and two hydrophobic (“water-fearing”) tails. Seeing the heads turn outward to the water and the tails tuck inward, from the inside, makes that arrangement obvious in a way a flat diagram never can — and it sets up the cell membrane you will meet in Module 3.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · the lipid section of Reading 4.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Phospholipid Bilayer — Membrane Scene (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist. Begin there.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Find the heads and the tails. Walk up to the bilayer and locate the hydrophilic phosphate heads — they face outward toward the water on both sides — and the hydrophobic fatty-acid tails tucked inward, away from the water. Place a sticky note on the heads and another on the tails, naming each and noting whether it faces or avoids water.
2
Walk through and watch what crosses. Pass through the bilayer. Observe how a small nonpolar molecule slips between the tails and crosses, while a charged ion is blocked at the hydrophobic interior. Place a sticky note explaining why the nonpolar molecule passes but the ion does not.
3
Capture your evidence. Take three named snapshots — one of the hydrophilic heads facing the water, one of the hydrophobic tails in the interior, and one showing the nonpolar molecule crossing while the ion is blocked.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: which part of the phospholipid faces the water and which hides from it, and why a nonpolar molecule crosses the bilayer but an ion does not.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students explore a bilayer Location, annotate the hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails with sticky notes, observe selective crossing, and capture snapshots. Common sticking points: placing and typing on sticky notes, and taking a named snapshot rather than a system screenshot. Encourage students to explain why the arrangement forms — heads seek the water, tails flee it — rather than just naming the parts. A complete submission identifies heads and tails by their relationship to water and explains why a nonpolar molecule crosses while an ion is blocked. This scene sets up the cell membrane in Module 3.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
The hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails are found and annotated with sticky notes by their relationship to water.
☐
A sticky note explains why a nonpolar molecule crosses the bilayer but an ion does not.
☐
Three named snapshots and the typed note are submitted to Canvas.
