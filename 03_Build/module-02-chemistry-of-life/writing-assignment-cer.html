Module 2 CER Writing Assignment
Why Water Is the Molecule That Makes Life Possible
Claim · Evidence · Reasoning · 12 pts · counts toward the CER writing category
📖 WHAT THIS IS
This is your first CER essay of the year’s chemistry — the writing that pulls Module 2 together. It draws on everything from this module: the bent shape and polarity of water, hydrogen bonding, and the remarkable properties — cohesion, high specific heat, floating ice, the universal solvent — that those bonds make possible. It is assigned at the start of Week 3 and due at the end of Week 4. Your teacher will read and score it personally — this one is not auto-graded.
🎯 YOUR TASK
Why is water essential to life? Explain how water’s bent molecular geometry produces at least three properties that make life possible — and connect each property to a specific consequence for living things.
📝 HOW TO STRUCTURE YOUR ESSAY (FOLLOW THESE FOUR PARTS)
Write in paragraphs, not a list. The sentence starters are scaffolding to get you going — make the words your own.
1
State your claim. In one clear sentence, assert that water’s life-giving powers trace back to its polar, bent molecular shape. Starter: “Water is essential to life because…”
2
Lay out your evidence. First describe water’s bent geometry (about 104.5°) and why it makes the molecule polar — oxygen pulls the shared electrons harder, gaining a partial negative charge while the hydrogens stay partially positive. Then present at least three properties of water: choose from cohesion / adhesion / surface tension, high specific heat, ice floating, and the universal solvent. Starter: “Because the molecule is bent, … This shape gives water three important properties: …”
3
Reason through the mechanism. For each property, walk the chain: bent shape → polarity → hydrogen bonding → the property → a real consequence for living things. For example: high specific heat keeps cell and ocean temperatures stable; floating ice lets fish survive a frozen winter; the universal solvent means biochemistry happens in solution; cohesion pulls water columns up a tall tree. Starter: “This property matters for life because…, and it happens because the hydrogen bonds…”
4
Integrate — tie it back together. Close by connecting your three properties to a single cause: one small, bent, three-atom molecule, and the many life-enabling consequences that follow from its shape. Starter: “All of these properties trace back to one thing…”
🧭 THE VIRTUE THIS PRACTICES · WONDER
This module’s virtue is wonder — that the properties keeping you alive come from nothing more exotic than the shape of a three-atom molecule. The same oxygen and hydrogen that formed in stars, bent into a single quiet angle, are why oceans hold their temperature, why ice shelters the life beneath it, and why the chemistry inside your cells can happen at all. To see how much follows from so little is the beginning of scientific wonder.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
A clear claim ties water’s importance to its polar, bent molecular shape.
☐
Water’s bent geometry and polarity are described, and at least three properties are presented.
☐
Each property is explained mechanistically (shape → polarity → hydrogen bonding) and linked to a real consequence for life.
☐
A closing paragraph ties the properties back to one cause; the essay reads as one connected argument (~400–700 words).
📊 CER SCORING RUBRIC (3 PTS × 4 CRITERIA = 12 PTS)
Criterion Strong (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1)
Claim Specific and accurate; clearly ties water’s importance to life to its polar, bent molecular shape. Somewhat vague or partly inaccurate. Missing, or just restates the prompt.
Evidence Describes bent geometry and polarity and presents at least three properties accurately and specifically. Presents one or two properties, or describes them only partially. Properties missing, vague, or inaccurate.
Reasoning For each property, explains the mechanism (shape → polarity → hydrogen bonding) and links it to a real consequence for life. Explains some mechanisms or consequences but with gaps. States properties without explaining cause or consequence.
Integration Reads as one coherent argument; properties trace to a single cause; within the word range. Parts present but loosely connected. Disconnected; listed without flow.
Total possible: 12 pts
