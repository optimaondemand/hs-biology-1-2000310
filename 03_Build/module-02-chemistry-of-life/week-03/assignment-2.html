Lesson focus: how hydrogen bonds between water molecules produce cohesion, adhesion, and surface tension
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is a short home investigation in two parts. In Part A you pile water drops onto a coin and count how many it holds before the dome collapses — a test of cohesion and surface tension. In Part B you watch water climb up a paper-towel strip — a test of adhesion and capillary action. You record your observations, then explain both results using hydrogen bonding. Plan on about 25–30 minutes, most of it watching and recording.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How can the same weak attraction — the hydrogen bond — both hold a dome of water on a coin and pull water up a paper towel against gravity?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Gather your materials. You will need a coin (a penny or nickel works well), an eyedropper or a drinking straw, a cup of water, a strip of paper towel about 2 cm wide, and a shallow dish. A small amount of food coloring is optional but makes Part B easier to see.
2
Part A — Drops on a coin (cohesion / surface tension). Lay the coin flat. Add water one drop at a time, counting as you go, until the water spills over the edge. Watch the dome of water bulge above the rim before it breaks. Record both the number of drops (quantitative) and a description of what the dome looked like (qualitative). Repeat once and record the second count.
3
Part B — Water climbing a towel (adhesion / capillary action). Stand the paper-towel strip with its bottom edge just touching the water in the dish. Wait two minutes. Record how far the water climbed (measure in cm if you can — quantitative) and what you saw (qualitative). Note whether it kept rising or stopped.
4
Record your data in a table like the one below, then write your explanations.

5
Explain (one short paragraph each). For Part A, explain how hydrogen bonds between water molecules (cohesion) let the dome hold together and create surface tension. For Part B, explain how hydrogen bonds between water and the paper-towel fibers (adhesion) pull the water upward. Submit a typed document or a photo of your notebook page with the table and both explanations.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐

☐

☐
