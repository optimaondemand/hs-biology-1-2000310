Week 3 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk the Properties of Water
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk through a scene of three water-property stations — a pond where a water strider rides the surface, a tank where ice floats on liquid water, and a station where salt dissolves into the water around it — and become an observer collecting evidence. Each station shows one striking behavior of water, and behind every one of them is the same cause you built in VR-A: a bent, polar molecule and the hydrogen bonds between molecules. Walking through these properties, rather than reading a list, makes the link from one molecule’s shape to the way water acts in the world something you can see.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · Readings 3.02 and 3.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Science Laboratory — Properties of Water Walk (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entrance shows your observer checklist. Begin there.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
The cohesion pond. Watch the water strider ride on top of the pond without sinking. This is cohesion and surface tension — water molecules hydrogen-bond to one another, forming a “skin” at the surface. Place a sticky note naming the property and its cause.
2
The ice tank. Observe the ice floating on liquid water. As water freezes, hydrogen bonds lock the molecules into an open lattice that is less dense than liquid water — so ice floats and insulates the water below. Place a sticky note naming the property and its cause.
3
The dissolving-salt station. Watch salt dissolve as polar water molecules surround each ion in a hydration shell — this is why water is called the universal solvent. Place a sticky note naming the property and its cause (water’s polarity).
4
Capture your evidence. Take three named snapshots — one at each station — showing the behavior and your sticky note.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed list: for each station, name the property you observed and the hydrogen-bond or polarity cause behind it.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students walk a staged Location with three water-property stations, annotate each with a sticky note, and capture snapshots. Common sticking points: placing and typing on sticky notes, and taking a named snapshot rather than a system screenshot. The reasoning is the point — push students to name the cause (hydrogen bonding for cohesion and ice density; polarity and hydration shells for the solvent) and not just the property. A complete submission flags all three stations, each with its property and its molecular cause traced back to the bent, polar water molecule from VR-A.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All three stations are visited and each is annotated with a sticky note naming the property (cohesion/surface tension, ice floating, universal solvent).
☐
Each sticky note names the hydrogen-bond or polarity cause behind the property, not just the property’s name.
☐
Three named snapshots and the typed list are submitted to Canvas.
