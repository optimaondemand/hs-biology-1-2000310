Week 3 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Water Molecule & Hydrogen-Bond Network
Solo VR activity · ~25 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a water molecule with your own hands — one oxygen and two hydrogens, bent at about 104.5° — then label where the partial charges sit and link several molecules into a hydrogen-bond network. Reading that water is “polar and bent” is one thing; assembling the molecule in space, seeing why the bent shape leaves one end slightly negative and the other slightly positive, and then watching those charges pull neighboring molecules together makes the single most important fact of this module physical and memorable. This is the structure behind every property you meet this week.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lesson 3.01 · your scientific notebook · the terms polar covalent, electronegativity, and hydrogen bond close at hand.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Science Laboratory — Molecular Modeling room (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the modeling bench marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Build one water molecule. From the ENGAGE library, place one oxygen atom IFX and two hydrogen atom IFX. Bond the two hydrogens to the oxygen and arrange them in a bent shape — the H–O–H angle near 104.5°, not a straight line. This is real water’s shape.
2
Label the partial charges. Place a sticky note on the oxygen reading δ− (partial negative — oxygen pulls the shared electrons harder) and a sticky note on each hydrogen reading δ+ (partial positive). Add one more note that says the bond is polar covalent: the electrons are shared, but not evenly.
3
Build the network. Clone your molecule so you have three or four identical water molecules. Arrange them so the δ+ hydrogen of one molecule points toward the δ− oxygen of a neighbor. Mark each of these attractions with a sticky note labeled hydrogen bond — the weak attraction that links water to water.
4
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk around your network and explain aloud, in about 60–90 seconds: why the bent shape makes the molecule polar, where the δ+ and δ− charges sit, and how those charges create the hydrogen bonds that hold neighboring molecules together.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your full labeled hydrogen-bond network plus a few typed sentences explaining why the bent shape makes water polar and how the partial charges form hydrogen bonds.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 25 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: This activity asks students to externalize water’s polarity and hydrogen bonding as a spatial model. Common sticking points: arranging the molecule in a bent shape rather than a straight line (the bend is the whole point — a linear molecule’s charges would cancel), cloning an IFX to make identical molecules, and aligning a δ+ hydrogen toward a neighbor’s δ− oxygen. Watch for students who place the δ− label on a hydrogen — nudge them back to electronegativity: oxygen pulls the shared electrons harder, so the oxygen end is the negative one. A strong submission shows a bent molecule with correct δ+/δ− labels and a network where the hydrogen bonds clearly connect δ+ H to δ− O.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A bent water molecule is built (1 oxygen, 2 hydrogens, angle near 104.5°) and labeled with δ− on oxygen and δ+ on each hydrogen.
☐
Three or four molecules are connected into a hydrogen-bond network, with each hydrogen bond labeled where a δ+ hydrogen meets a δ− oxygen.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted that explains why the bent shape makes water polar and how the charges form hydrogen bonds.
