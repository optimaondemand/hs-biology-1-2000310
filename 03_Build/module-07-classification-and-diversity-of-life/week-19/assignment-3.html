📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Using a Dichotomous Key
Reading 19.03 and Lesson 19.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 19.03 and work through Lesson 19.03, including how to follow a key’s paired choices to an identification.
Lesson focus: A repeatable, observable path to a name
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
You will use the dichotomous key below to identify three unknown small animals, showing the steps you followed — then answer one short question about why keys work. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🔑 THE KEY · SMALL ANIMALS OF THE GARDEN
1a. Has wings → go to 2
1b. Has no wings → go to 3
2a. One pair of wings → Housefly
2b. Two pairs of large scaly wings → Butterfly
3a. Eight legs; body in two parts → Garden spider
3b. Many legs; long segmented body → Centipede
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Identify three unknowns (6 pts, 2 each). For each unknown below, use the key to name it, and list the step numbers you followed (for example, “1b → 3a”).
  • Unknown 1: no wings; eight legs; body in two parts.
  • Unknown 2: has wings; two pairs of large scaly wings.
  • Unknown 3: no wings; many legs; long segmented body.
2
Part B — Why observable, either/or traits? (4 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why this key uses only traits you can see (wings, number of legs) and phrases each step as a two-way choice, rather than asking something like “is it a pretty insect?”
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Unknown 1 = Garden spider (1b → 3a); Unknown 2 = Butterfly (1a → 2b); Unknown 3 = Centipede (1b → 3b).
☐
Part B explains that observable, binary traits let anyone reach the same answer — a subjective question would get different answers and break the key.
