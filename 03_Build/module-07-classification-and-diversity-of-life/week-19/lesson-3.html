Lesson · A Repeatable Path to a Name
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You find an organism you don’t recognize. How do you discover its name without guessing?
You can now name and rank organisms — but how do you name one you have never seen? Today you learn to use a dichotomous key: a tool that identifies an organism through a chain of paired, either/or choices. Each choice rules out half of what remains, until one name is left. It is identification made repeatable — a courteous, shared procedure that leads every careful user to the same answer.

🔑 PART 1 · IDENTIFICATION WITHOUT GUESSING
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
A dichotomous key never asks “what species is this?” directly — only a chain of yes-or-no questions about what you can see. Why might that be more reliable than an expert’s snap judgment? (No wrong answer — just check in.)

What a dichotomous key is

A dichotomous key identifies an organism through a series of paired, either/or choices (dichotomous = “divided in two”). At each step you are given two contrasting statements and choose the one that fits; your choice sends you to the next step or gives an identification. Each decision eliminates half of what is left. Because the key depends only on observable traits — not on already knowing the organism — it is repeatable: two careful users reach the same name.

🔍 PART 3 · WHY THE TRAITS MUST BE OBSERVABLE

Binary, observable, and fair to all

Every good step was binary (exactly two options) and rested on a trait you could see — blade shape, edge, leaflet arrangement. A key never asks “is this leaf pretty?” or “is this tree common?” because those are opinions or outside facts, not observations, and two people could answer differently. Restricting a key to observable, either/or traits is what makes it fair and repeatable: it hands everyone the same honest path to the answer. That reliability is a small courtesy built into the tool itself.
