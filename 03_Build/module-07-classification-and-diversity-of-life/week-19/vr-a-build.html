Week 19 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Linnaean Classification Ladder
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a spatial classification ladder for one organism — the gray wolf, Canis lupus — with one labeled rung for each of the eight ranks, from Domain at the bottom (broadest) up to Species at the top (most specific). Then you will record a short walkthrough naming each rung. Building the hierarchy as a ladder you can climb makes the ordering of life concrete: each rung up is a smaller, more closely related group. This is Lessons 19.01 and 19.02, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 19.01 and 19.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Hall of Classification (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty ladder frame marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Frame the ladder. Set up eight empty rungs, bottom to top. Label the bottom rung Domain (broadest) and the top rung Species (most specific). Add a small sign: “Up = smaller group, more shared traits.”
2
Fill the eight rungs for the gray wolf, bottom to top, with the correct taxon on each: Domain Eukarya → Kingdom Animalia → Phylum Chordata → Class Mammalia → Order Carnivora → Family Canidae → Genus Canis → Species Canis lupus.
3
Write the scientific name correctly. On the top rung, display the full species name as Canis lupus — genus capitalized, specific epithet lowercase, whole name in italics (or underlined). This is your check on Lesson 19.02.
4
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Climb the ladder from Domain to Species, naming each rung aloud in about 60–90 seconds, and say one sentence about what happens to the group as you climb (it gets smaller and members share more traits). Use the words taxon, genus, and species.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your full labeled ladder (all eight rungs, with Canis lupus correctly written on top) plus a few typed sentences naming the ranks in order and explaining what changes as you climb.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the Linnaean hierarchy as a climbable ladder and practice writing a scientific name correctly at the top rung. Common sticking points: reversing broad and specific (Domain is broadest, Species most specific), and mis-formatting the species name (watch for a capitalized epithet — it should read Canis lupus, not “Canis Lupus”). A strong submission shows all eight rungs correctly labeled bottom-to-top, the species name correctly formatted, and a recording that uses taxon, genus, and species and notes that climbing means smaller, more closely related groups.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All eight rungs are built and correctly labeled in order (Domain → Species) with the right taxa for the gray wolf.
☐
The species name Canis lupus is written correctly (genus capitalized, epithet lowercase, italic or underlined).
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted naming the ranks and what changes as you climb.
