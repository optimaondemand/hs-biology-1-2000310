Lesson 19.04
Constructing a Dichotomous Key
Lesson · Building an Orderly Path for Others
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You can follow a key someone else made. Now: how do you build one a stranger can trust?
This is the week’s capstone skill. To construct a dichotomous key, you choose observable, binary, contrasting characteristics, start with a trait that splits the whole group roughly in half, and keep splitting until every organism stands alone. Building a key is ordering the world for others — the very heart of this module’s virtue, courtesy.

🧩 PART 1 · CHOOSING THE RIGHT TRAITS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Suppose your first key step asked, “Is this animal beautiful?” Would that make a good, reliable key? (No wrong answer — just check in.)

What makes a good characteristic

A key is only as good as the traits it splits on. Good characteristics are observable (you can see or measure them on the specimen), binary (exactly two clear answers, no in-between), and contrasting (true opposites, so every organism fits one side only). Lean on structural traits — parts and their arrangement — not opinions (“is it pretty?”) or outside facts a user can’t check in front of the specimen (“where does it live?”). If two people could disagree on the answer, the trait is a poor choice.

🔨 PART 2 · SPLIT, THEN SPLIT AGAIN

Building a key for four organisms

Start with a trait that divides the whole group roughly in half, then split each half again, until every organism sits alone. Take four organisms — a housefly, a spider, an earthworm, and a snail. “Has jointed legs?” splits them neatly: fly and spider (yes) versus worm and snail (no). Then split each pair with a new contrasting trait:

1a. Has jointed legs → go to 2
1b. No jointed legs → go to 3
2a. Has wings (six legs) → Housefly
2b. No wings (eight legs) → Spider
3a. Has a hard external shell → Snail
3b. No shell; long soft segmented body → Earthworm

🔄 PART 3 · LOCK IN THE RULE

The test of a good characteristic

Before you go on, fix the rule firmly in mind with the card below — you will use it to build your own key in the assignment.

🔄 FLIP CARD · TAP TO REVEAL
What three qualities make a characteristic a good one to split on when building a dichotomous key?
(Tap to flip.)
✅ THE ANSWER
A good key characteristic is observable, binary, and contrasting.
Observable — you can see or measure it on the specimen. Binary — exactly two clear answers. Contrasting — true opposites, so every organism fits one side and only one side. Avoid opinions (“pretty?”) and unknowable facts (“where does it live?”).
