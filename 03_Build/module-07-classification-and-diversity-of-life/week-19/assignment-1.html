Before you begin: finish Reading 19.01 and work through Lesson 19.01, including the eight ranks of the Linnaean hierarchy and the rule that narrower ranks share more traits.
Lesson focus: Ordering the diversity of life into a shared, ranked system
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: put the ranks in order, name the broadest and most specific levels, and explain in a sentence what moving down the hierarchy does to shared traits. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the ranked order of the Linnaean hierarchy organize life — and what does moving down the ranks tell us about how alike the members are?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Put the ranks in order (4 pts). The eight ranks below are scrambled. Rewrite them in order from broadest to most specific:
Species · Order · Domain · Family · Class · Kingdom · Genus · Phylum
2
Part B — Broadest and most specific (3 pts). Name the broadest rank (the one that holds the greatest variety of organisms) and the most specific rank (whose members share the most characteristics).
3
Part C — What happens as you move down? (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain what happens to the number of shared characteristics among members as you move down the hierarchy from Domain toward Species, and why.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A lists, in order: Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species.
☐
Part B names Domain as broadest and Species as most specific.
☐
Part C states that members share more characteristics as you move down (groups get smaller and more similar).
