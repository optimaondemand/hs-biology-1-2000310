Module 7 CER Writing Assignment
Reading Life by Its Molecules: The Case for Three Domains
Claim · Evidence · Reasoning · Integration · 12 pts · counts toward the CER writing category
📖 WHAT THIS IS
This is the writing that pulls Module 7 together. Over four weeks you learned how living things are named and ranked (the Linnaean hierarchy and binomial nomenclature), how phylogenetic trees map relationships from shared characters and molecular sequences, and how the diversity of life is organized into three domains — Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. Now you will argue for the classification system itself. For most of history, biologists sorted life by how it looked. In the late 1970s molecular sequence data began to reveal that appearance had hidden a deep division no eye could see. Your task is to build a reasoned case for why a classification grounded in molecular sequence data reflects life’s true relationships better than one built on morphology alone. It is assigned at the start of Week 19 and due at the end of Week 22, so the whole module can inform your argument. Your teacher will read and score this one personally — it is not auto-graded.
🧬 THIS IS AN OPEN-INQUIRY CER
Unlike the more guided essays earlier in the year, this one leaves the construction to you. You decide which evidence carries the most weight, you decide the order, and you build the reasoning that ties morphology and molecules into one judgment. The evidence bank below is a memory aid, not a script — a strong essay selects from it and explains, in its own words, why molecular data is the more trustworthy guide when the two kinds of evidence disagree.
🎯 YOUR TASK
Justify the three-domain classification system using molecular sequence data versus traditional morphology — explaining not only what each kind of evidence shows, but why molecular data reveals relationships that appearance alone conceals.
🧬 YOUR EVIDENCE BANK
These are the facts you studied this module. Choose the ones that best build your argument — you do not need every row, but a strong case weighs morphology against molecules rather than ignoring either.
Evidence What it shows · detail to cite
What morphology saysBy appearance, all cells without a nucleus (prokaryotes) look alike, so older systems lumped Bacteria and Archaea into a single group — they are hard to tell apart under a microscope.
What the molecules sayComparing ribosomal RNA (rRNA) sequences (Carl Woese and George Fox, 1977; the three-domain system was formally proposed in 1990) revealed that Archaea differ so deeply from Bacteria that they are in fact more closely related to Eukarya than to Bacteria — a split invisible to the eye.
Hidden differencesArchaea and Bacteria differ in cell-wall chemistry (only Bacteria use peptidoglycan), membrane makeup, and gene-reading machinery — molecular traits, not visible ones.
Appearance can deceiveFrom Module 6: analogous structures (a bird’s and an insect’s wing) look alike from convergent evolution, not shared ancestry — so shared appearance is not a reliable sign of relationship.
Why sequences record historyAll life shares DNA and a nearly universal genetic code (Modules 2 & 5); mutations accumulate in sequences over time, so the more two species’ sequences differ, the longer since they shared an ancestor.
A note on the words: morphology means an organism’s form and structure — what it looks like. A domain is the broadest rank of classification, above kingdom. Your argument is about which kind of evidence should decide how we group life.
📝 BUILD YOUR ARGUMENT IN FOUR MOVES
Write in connected paragraphs, not a list. The wording is yours — these are the moves a strong CER makes, not a fill-in-the-blank.
1
Claim. State, in one or two sentences, that the three-domain system — because it is grounded in molecular sequence data — reflects life’s true relationships better than a system built on morphology alone. Make it a real claim you intend to defend.
2
Evidence. Contrast the two kinds of evidence with specific detail. What did morphology conclude (all prokaryotes grouped together because they look alike)? What did the rRNA sequence data reveal instead (Archaea more closely related to Eukarya than to Bacteria)? Cite at least one concrete molecular difference — e.g., peptidoglycan in bacterial walls but not archaeal ones. Specificity is what separates an argument from an opinion.
3
Reasoning — why molecules win when the evidence disagrees. This is the heart of the essay. Explain why sequence data is a more reliable guide to relationship than appearance: sequences record inherited history (mutations accumulate along a lineage), while appearance can mislead — look-alikes need not be relatives (analogy, from Module 6), and Archaea and Bacteria look alike yet are not close kin. Then be fair to the other side: morphology is quick, useful, and was all early biologists had — but where morphology and molecules conflict, the larger, independent molecular record should decide. Explain why.
4
Integration. Close by connecting classification to the bigger story. Grouping by shared ancestry means classification is applied evolutionary theory — the tree of life from common descent (Module 6), now read in the molecules whose changes you studied in Modules 2 and 5. You may also reflect on the module’s virtue, courtesy: naming and ordering life is a shared, honest courtesy among scientists — and Woese’s willingness to redraw the whole tree when the evidence demanded it, though he was ignored for years, is that courtesy joined to courage and honesty.
🧡 THE VIRTUE THIS PRACTICES · COURTESY
This module’s virtue is courtesy — the ordered, generous naming of the household of life. A classification is a shared language: it works only because everyone agrees to name and group things the same careful way. But courtesy is not mere politeness; it is a willingness to keep the shared order honest, correcting it when better evidence arrives. Carl Woese did exactly that — his molecular data overturned a tidy, long-accepted picture, and biology reordered the tree of life to match. Your CER practices the same habit: taking each kind of evidence seriously, giving the older view its due, and then following the evidence to a fair, well-reasoned judgment.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
A clear claim argues that the molecularly grounded three-domain system reflects relationships better than morphology alone.
☐
Evidence contrasts what morphology concluded with what rRNA sequence data revealed (Archaea closer to Eukarya), with at least one specific molecular difference.
☐
Reasoning explains why sequences record ancestry more reliably than appearance, and fairly addresses the value and limits of morphology.
☐
The essay reads as one connected argument and ties to common descent (Module 6) and/or the molecules of Modules 2 & 5 — (~400–700 words).
