Lesson focus: Classifying animals and reading their body plans
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: classify five animals as invertebrate or vertebrate, explain why invertebrates are the largest group, and describe one body-plan trend. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What is the first great division of the animal kingdom, and which side of it holds most animal species?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Invertebrate or vertebrate? (5 pts, 1 each). Label each animal I (invertebrate) or V (vertebrate).
Animal I or V?
Honeybee (insect) 
American alligator 
Octopus 
Roseate spoonbill (bird) 
Blue crab 
2
Part B — The bigger pile (2 pts). In one sentence, state whether most animal species are invertebrates or vertebrates, and name the group of invertebrates that makes this true.
3
Part C — Read a body plan (3 pts). In one or two sentences, describe one body-plan trend — either radial vs. bilateral symmetry or cephalization — and explain how it suits an animal that moves to find food.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: honeybee = I, alligator = V, octopus = I, spoonbill = V, blue crab = I.
☐
Part B states that most animals are invertebrates and names the arthropods (insects/crustaceans/arachnids) as the reason.
☐
Part C correctly describes a trend and links it to movement (e.g., bilateral symmetry gives a front and back for moving forward; cephalization puts senses and a brain at the leading end, where the animal meets its world first).
