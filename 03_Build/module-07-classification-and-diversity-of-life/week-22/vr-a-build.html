Week 22 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Florida-Biodiversity Collection Across Kingdoms
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a small spatial biodiversity collection set in a Florida landscape. Place and label at least one representative organism from each of five branches of life: bacteria/archaea, protists, fungi, plants, and animals. Then record a walkthrough naming each organism and its kingdom or domain. Building the household of life as a space you can walk makes the whole module concrete — nearly the entire tree of life, gathered in one state. This is Lessons 22.01–22.04, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 22.01–22.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Florida Wetland & Coast (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty collection platform marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Bacteria / Archaea station. Place a magnified model of nucleus-free single cells “in” a spring or wetland patch. Label it: Domains Bacteria & Archaea — prokaryotes, no nucleus.
2
Protist station. Place phytoplankton or algae in the estuary water. Label it: Kingdom Protista — mostly single-celled eukaryotes.
3
Fungus station. Place a mushroom on a rotting log in the forest patch. Label it: Kingdom Fungi — heterotroph, decomposer.
4
Plant and animal stations. Place a real Florida plant (mangrove, longleaf pine, saw palmetto, or seagrass) and a real Florida animal (manatee, gopher tortoise, roseate spoonbill, alligator, or an insect). Label each with its kingdom — and, for the plant, its group (e.g., gymnosperm or angiosperm); for the animal, invertebrate or vertebrate.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk your collection and name each of the five organisms aloud, in about 60–90 seconds, stating its kingdom or domain and one reason it belongs there. End with one sentence: “Nearly the whole tree of life lives in one Florida landscape.”
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your full collection (all five labeled stations) plus a few typed sentences naming each organism and its kingdom or domain.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the module’s big idea — that nearly the whole tree of life is present in one Florida landscape — by building a labeled collection across five branches. Encourage real Florida species (mangrove, longleaf pine, manatee, gopher tortoise, alligator) and correct any invented organisms. Common sticking points: calling a mushroom a plant (fungi are heterotrophs, not autotrophs), and placing protists with bacteria (protists are eukaryotes, with a nucleus). A strong submission shows five correctly labeled stations spanning bacteria/archaea, protists, fungi, plants, and animals, and a walkthrough that names each organism’s kingdom or domain correctly.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
At least one organism is placed and labeled for each of the five branches (bacteria/archaea, protists, fungi, plants, animals).
☐
All organisms are real Florida life, each labeled with the correct kingdom or domain.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted naming each organism and its group.
