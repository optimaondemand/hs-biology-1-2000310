🌴 PART 1 · THE WHOLE TREE IN ONE STATE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of the last time you were outside in Florida — a park, a beach, a backyard. How many different kingdoms of life do you think were around you? (No wrong answer — just check in.)

Florida, branch by branch

Unseen in springs and wetlands live the domains Bacteria and Archaea. Estuaries float with protists — phytoplankton and seagrass algae. Forests hide fungi that decompose fallen wood. The plant kingdom stands in mangroves, longleaf pine, saw palmetto, and underwater seagrasses. And the animals range from swarming arthropod invertebrates to famous vertebrates: the Florida manatee, gopher tortoise, roseate spoonbill, and American alligator. The whole tree, in one state.

📱 PART 3 · CATALOGING LIFE WITH (AND AGAINST) AI

Who names all this life?

Florida’s biodiversity is far too vast for a handful of experts to catalog alone. Increasingly, ordinary people help — snapping photos on their phones and letting software suggest what they found. That is a real opportunity and a real trap at the same time.

🤖 AI AWARENESS · CAN AN APP NAME A SPECIES FOR YOU?
Apps like iNaturalist use computer-vision models — trained on millions of labeled photos — to suggest a species ID from a picture you take. The opportunity is huge: everyday observers become citizen scientists, and biodiversity surveys scale up far beyond what experts could do alone. But here are the pitfalls. The model can misidentify look-alikes — two similar wading birds, two similar snakes — with confident-sounding wrong answers. It is only as good as its training data, which is biased toward well-photographed species and popular regions, so rare or poorly documented organisms get missed or mislabeled. And a confident suggestion is still just a suggestion: it needs expert confirmation before it counts. Treat an AI species ID the way you treat any scientific claim — a hypothesis to check, not an authority to trust.
