**1. (Reading 22.01 — land adaptations, sequence)**
Which sequence correctly orders the plant groups from LEAST to MOST adapted to life on dry land?

- A) Flowering plants → conifers → ferns → mosses
- B) Mosses → ferns → conifers → flowering plants **✓**
- C) Ferns → mosses → flowering plants → conifers
- D) Conifers → flowering plants → mosses → ferns

*The trend runs mosses (nonvascular, low, need moisture) → ferns (vascular but seedless) → conifers/gymnosperms (seeds) → flowering plants/angiosperms (flowers and fruit). Each step — vascular tissue, then seeds, then flowers — loosens a plant's dependence on standing water and opens more of the land.*

**2. (Reading 22.01 — vascular tissue; targets "mosses grow tall")**
A moss stays low to the ground and lives only in damp places, while a fern of the same forest can grow much taller. What best explains the difference?

- A) The moss is a younger plant that has not yet finished growing
- B) The moss reproduces by seeds, while the fern reproduces by flowers
- C) The fern has vascular tissue to carry water upward, and the moss does not **✓**
- D) The fern gets far more sunlight than the moss does on the forest floor

*Mosses are nonvascular — they have no internal tubes to transport water — so they stay small and near moisture. Ferns are seedless vascular plants: their vascular tissue lifts water from the roots, letting them grow tall. Neither makes flowers, and mosses make neither seeds nor flowers.*

**3. (Reading 22.01 — gymnosperm vs. angiosperm; targets "ferns/mosses flower")**
A pine bears its seeds exposed on cones, while an oak encloses its seeds inside fruit and grows flowers. Which statement is correct?

- A) The pine is a gymnosperm (naked seeds) and the oak is an angiosperm (flowers) **✓**
- B) Both are ferns, because ferns produce seeds on cones and in fruit
- C) The pine is an angiosperm (flowers) and the oak is a gymnosperm (naked seeds)
- D) Only mosses and ferns produce flowers and enclosed seeds

*Gymnosperm means "naked seed": conifers like pines bear seeds openly on cones. Angiosperms are the flowering plants, which grow flowers and enclose their seeds in fruit. Ferns and mosses do not flower and do not make seeds — only angiosperms flower.*

**4. (Reading 22.02 — invertebrate vs. vertebrate; targets "most animals are vertebrates")**
Which statement about the animal kingdom is correct?

- A) Most animal species are vertebrates, like mammals, birds, and fish
- B) All animals have a backbone at some stage of their life cycle
- C) Invertebrates and vertebrates each make up about half of all animal species
- D) Most animal species are invertebrates, and most of those are arthropods **✓**

*The vast majority of animal species are invertebrates (no backbone), and arthropods — insects, crustaceans, and arachnids — are the most numerous animals of all. The familiar backboned animals (vertebrates) are actually a small minority of animal species.*

**5. (Reading 22.02 — classifying a described animal)**
A biologist describes an animal with a hard external shell, jointed legs, and no internal backbone. This animal is best classified as:

- A) A vertebrate, because its hard covering counts as a skeleton
- B) A protist, because it has no bones and no internal skeleton
- C) An invertebrate arthropod, because it has an external skeleton and no backbone **✓**
- D) A mammal, because it walks on jointed legs and has a hard covering

*An external skeleton (exoskeleton) and jointed legs describe an arthropod — an invertebrate. A backbone is internal; arthropods lack one entirely. Having a hard covering on the outside is not the same as having an internal skeleton with a spine.*

**6. (Reading 22.02 — body-plan trend: cephalization)**
Many active animals show cephalization. What does this term describe?

- A) The development of a hard outer shell that protects the soft body
- B) The concentration of nerves and sense organs into a head at the leading end **✓**
- C) The ability of an animal to make its own food by photosynthesis
- D) A body arranged in a circle around a center, like spokes on a wheel

*Cephalization is the gathering of nerves and senses into a head. It travels with bilateral symmetry and suits an animal that moves forward: putting the eyes, mouth, and brain at the front means the animal meets its environment prepared. A circular ("spokes") arrangement describes radial symmetry, not cephalization.*

**7. (Reading 22.03 — virus structure; targets "a virus is a cell/bacterium")**
Which statement correctly describes the structure of a virus?

- A) A virus is a very small single-celled organism, much like a bacterium
- B) A virus is a type of fungus that infects other organisms
- C) A virus is a tiny cell that has a nucleus but lacks mitochondria
- D) A virus is DNA or RNA inside a protein capsid, and it is not a cell **✓**

*A virus is not a cell at all. It is simply genetic material — either DNA or RNA — enclosed in a protein capsid, sometimes with an outer envelope. Because it is not a cell and has no metabolism, it is not a bacterium and is not placed in any of the three domains.*

**8. (Reading 22.03 — replication requires a host)**
Why can a virus not reproduce on its own?

- A) It lacks its own metabolism and protein-building machinery, so it needs a host cell **✓**
- B) It is too small to contain any genetic material that could be copied
- C) It can reproduce only when it is exposed to sunlight and warm water
- D) It reproduces only when antibiotics are present to switch on its genes

*A virus is an obligate intracellular parasite: it has no cell and no metabolism, so it cannot build proteins or copy itself alone. It must enter a living host cell and take over that cell's machinery to produce new viruses. Everything it "does," it does with borrowed equipment.*

**9. (Reading 22.03 — viruses ≠ bacteria; antibiotics misconception)**
A student has a viral cold and asks whether antibiotics will cure it. What is the best scientific answer?

- A) Yes — antibiotics kill all types of germs, including viruses and bacteria
- B) Yes — but only if the dose is high enough to reach the virus
- C) No — antibiotics target bacterial cells, and a virus is not a cell **✓**
- D) No — antibiotics target fungal cells, and a virus is a kind of fungus

*Antibiotics attack structures found in bacterial cells. A virus is not a cell and is not a bacterium, so there is nothing for an antibiotic to target. This is why antibiotics treat bacterial infections (like strep throat) but do nothing for viral infections (like the common cold or flu).*

**10. (Reading 22.04 — Florida biodiversity across kingdoms/domains)**
On a single Florida field trip a class records: bacteria in a spring, algal phytoplankton in an estuary, a mushroom on a log, a longleaf pine, and a manatee. Which list correctly assigns each organism to its group?

- A) Bacteria — protists; phytoplankton — plants; mushroom — fungi; longleaf pine — plants; manatee — animals
- B) Bacteria — fungi; phytoplankton — domain Bacteria; mushroom — protists; longleaf pine — plants; manatee — animals
- C) Bacteria — domain Bacteria; phytoplankton — animals; mushroom — plants; longleaf pine — plants; manatee — animals
- D) Bacteria — domain Bacteria; phytoplankton — protists; mushroom — fungi; longleaf pine — plants; manatee — animals **✓**

*These five organisms span five of the major branches of life: the domain Bacteria (nucleus-free prokaryotes), the protists (algal phytoplankton), the fungi (mushroom), the plants (longleaf pine), and the animals (manatee). One Florida landscape can hold nearly the whole tree of life — the module's central idea.*
