📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Are Viruses Alive?
Reading 22.03 and Lesson 22.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 22.03 and work through Lesson 22.03, including virus structure, how viruses replicate by hijacking a host cell, and the criteria of life. This assignment turns the in-lesson practice into a short graded CER.
Lesson focus: Using the criteria of life to argue whether viruses are alive
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
Write a short claim–evidence–reasoning response of about 120–200 words taking a position on whether viruses are alive. This is a graded assignment, but it is not the larger Module 7 CER essay — keep it focused and tight. It should take about 20–25 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Are viruses alive? Take a position and defend it using the criteria of life.
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Claim (2 pts). State your position in one clear sentence: are viruses alive, not alive, or in between? Commit to one answer.
2
Evidence (4 pts). Support your claim with at least three specific facts from the criteria of life. Consider: Is a virus made of cells? Can it carry out metabolism (use energy)? Can it reproduce on its own? Does it have genes and evolve? Use accurate terms — capsid, DNA or RNA, host cell, obligate intracellular parasite.
3
Reasoning (4 pts). Explain why your evidence supports your claim — which criteria you weight most heavily, and why. A strong answer also names the strongest point for the other side (for example, that viruses have genes and evolve) and responds to it.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
A clear one-sentence claim taking a definite position.
☐
At least three accurate pieces of evidence from the criteria of life (not a cell; no independent metabolism; cannot reproduce without a host; has genes and evolves).
☐
Reasoning that connects the evidence to the claim and addresses the strongest counterpoint. Note: full marks are possible for either “not alive” or “in between” — the score depends on the quality of evidence and reasoning, not which side you pick.
📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Proficient Developing Beginning Pts
Claim
2 pts		 Clear, definite position. Present but vague or hedged. Missing or unclear. 2 pts
Evidence
4 pts		 Three+ accurate criteria, correct terms. One or two, or minor errors. Little or inaccurate. 4 pts
Reasoning
4 pts		 Links evidence to claim; answers counterpoint. Partial link; no counterpoint. Restates facts without reasoning. 4 pts
Total: 10 pts
