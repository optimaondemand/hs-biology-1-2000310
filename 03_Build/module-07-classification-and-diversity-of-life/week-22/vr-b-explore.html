Week 22 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Observe a Virus Hijack a Host Cell
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will watch a virus replication scene up close: a virus attaches to a host cell, delivers its genetic material, and hijacks the cell’s machinery to build and release hundreds of new viruses. You will observe each stage, then use what you see to weigh the module’s big question: Is a virus alive? One thing to hold clearly — a virus is not a cell and cannot copy itself without a host; it borrows everything. Watching it happen makes the debate real. This is Lesson 22.03, observed in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 22.03 and Lesson 22.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Cell & Virus Theater (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the four stages of viral replication. Begin at the Attachment marker.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Stage 1 — Attachment. Observe the virus — genetic material (DNA or RNA) inside a protein capsid — latch onto the surface of a host cell. Place a sticky note: “A virus is not a cell; it must find a host.”
2
Stage 2 — Entry & takeover. Watch the virus deliver its genetic material and hijack the cell’s machinery. Place a sticky note: what part of the cell is the virus now using that it did not build?
3
Stage 3 — Copy & assemble. Observe hundreds of new virus parts being built and assembled inside the host. Place a sticky note: “The virus reproduces only by using the host — not on its own.”
4
Stage 4 — Release & the big question. Watch new viruses burst out to infect more cells. Then take four named snapshots, one per stage (name each “Attachment,” “Takeover,” “Assembly,” “Release”). At a debate marker, decide: given what you saw, is this virus alive? Note your position and one reason.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: name the four stages in order and write one or two sentences taking a position on whether the virus is alive, using one thing you observed as evidence.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students watch viral replication stage by stage and observe that the virus does nothing without a host cell — the core evidence for the “are viruses alive?” debate. The most important misconception to correct is that a virus is a kind of cell or bacterium: emphasize that it is genetic material in a protein capsid with no cell and no independent metabolism. A complete submission shows four correctly named snapshots in order and a note that takes a defensible position (alive, not alive, or in between) supported by an observation. This walk feeds directly into Assignment 22.03, the short viruses CER.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All four replication stages are visited in order (Attachment, Takeover, Assembly, Release) and observed.
☐
A sticky note at each stage records an observation about virus structure or how it uses the host cell.
☐
Four correctly named snapshots and the typed note (stages in order + a position on “is it alive?” with evidence) are submitted to Canvas.
