Lesson focus: The diversity of protists and the outsized importance of phytoplankton
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: classify described protists by lifestyle, explain why “protist” is a catch-all label, and explain the ecological importance of phytoplankton. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If protists are so varied, how do we make sense of them — and why should we care about the smallest ones?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Classify by lifestyle (4 pts, 1 each). For each described protist, write whether it is animal-like, plant-like, or fungus-like.
1. A single cell swims by beating tiny hairs and sweeps in bits of food. — ________
2. A drifting cell in ocean surface water uses sunlight to make its own food. — ________
3. A blob creeps over a rotting log, releasing enzymes and absorbing nutrients. — ________
4. A shape-shifting cell surrounds and engulfs its prey. — ________
2
Part B — Why a catch-all? (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why biologists say “protist” is a convenient label rather than a single clean group.
3
Part C — Why phytoplankton matter (4 pts). In two or three sentences, explain the ecological importance of phytoplankton. Name at least two reasons (Hint: think about oxygen and about food webs).
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: 1 = animal-like (protozoan), 2 = plant-like (alga), 3 = fungus-like (slime mold), 4 = animal-like (protozoan).
☐
Part B explains protists are the eukaryotes left over after plants, animals, and fungi — many only-distantly-related lineages, not one clade.
☐
Part C names two reasons: phytoplankton are a major source of atmospheric oxygen and the producers at the base of aquatic food webs.
