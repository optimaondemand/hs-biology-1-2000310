Week 21 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build the Three Domains Side by Side
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build three cells side by side — a Bacterium, an Archaeon, and a Eukaryotic cell — one for each domain of life. Then you will label the key differences and record a walkthrough explaining why it took molecular data, not appearance, to separate them. Building the three domains as things you can walk around makes the abstract argument concrete — and this build doubles as the planning space for your Module 7 CER. This is Lessons 21.01–21.04, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 21.01–21.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Cell Biology Lab — Model Bench (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty build bench marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Build the Bacterium (Domain Bacteria). Make a small prokaryotic cell: a loop of DNA in a nucleoid (no nucleus), ribosomes, a cell membrane, a peptidoglycan cell wall, and a flagellum. Add a sticky note: “Prokaryote · peptidoglycan wall.”
2
Build the Archaeon (Domain Archaea). Make a second prokaryotic cell that looks similar to the first — nucleoid, ribosomes, membrane, wall — but label its wall “no peptidoglycan (different chemistry)” and its membrane “different lipids.” Add a sticky note: “Looks like a bacterium — but molecules differ.”
3
Build the Eukaryotic cell (Domain Eukarya). Make a larger cell with a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (e.g., mitochondria). Add a sticky note: “Nucleus · the domain that includes us.”
4
Show the surprise. Draw or place a bracket grouping Archaea with Eukarya (labeled “closer relatives — molecular evidence”), even though the Bacterium and Archaeon look most alike. This visual is the heart of your CER.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk the three cells and explain aloud, in about 60–90 seconds, how each is built and why molecular sequence data — not how they look — place Archaea closer to Eukarya. Use the words prokaryote, domain, and molecular evidence.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of all three labeled cells with the Archaea–Eukarya bracket plus a few typed sentences explaining why molecular data, not appearance, define the domains.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the three domains and, crucially, the molecules-vs-morphology twist that anchors the Module 7 CER (claim → evidence → reasoning). Encourage them to keep their notes. Common sticking points: giving the Archaeon a nucleus (it is a prokaryote — nucleoid only), and bracketing Archaea with Bacteria because they look alike (the whole point is that molecular data group Archaea with Eukarya). A strong submission shows three correctly built and labeled cells, the Archaea–Eukarya bracket, and a recording that correctly uses “prokaryote,” “domain,” and “molecular evidence.”
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Three cells are built and correctly labeled (Bacterium and Archaeon as prokaryotes; Eukaryotic cell with a nucleus).
☐
A bracket or label groups Archaea with Eukarya as closer relatives, based on molecular evidence.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted explaining why molecules, not looks, define the domains.
