Lesson focus: How fungi are built and feed, and why they are not plants
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: describe fungal structure and nutrition, explain why a fungus is not a plant, and describe one role of fungi as a decomposer or mutualist. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A mushroom looks like a plant but works like nothing of the kind. What is a fungus, really — and what does it do for the living world?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Structure and nutrition (4 pts). In your own words, define each term and describe how a fungus feeds: hyphae, mycelium, chitin, and heterotrophic by absorption.
2
Part B — Not a plant (3 pts). A classmate insists fungi are plants because they grow in soil. In two or three sentences, give three reasons a fungus is not a plant.
3
Part C — A role fungi play (3 pts). Choose one role — decomposer or mutualist (mycorrhiza or lichen) — and explain in two or three sentences what the fungus does and why it matters to other living things.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: hyphae = threadlike filaments; mycelium = the mass of hyphae; chitin = the fungal cell-wall material; absorption = secreting enzymes to digest food outside, then absorbing it.
☐
Part B gives three reasons: fungi do not photosynthesize (are heterotrophs), have chitin (not cellulose) walls, and have a body of hyphae rather than roots/stems/leaves.
☐
Part C accurately describes either decomposition (breaking down dead matter, recycling nutrients) or a mutualism (mycorrhiza: nutrients-for-sugars with plant roots; lichen: fungus + alga/cyanobacterium).
