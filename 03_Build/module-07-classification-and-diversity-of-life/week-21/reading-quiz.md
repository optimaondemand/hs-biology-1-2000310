**1. (Reading 21.01 — the three domains)**
Into which three domains do biologists divide all living things at the highest level?

- A) Plants, Animals, and Fungi
- B) Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya **✓**
- C) Prokaryotes, Protists, and Eukaryotes
- D) Producers, Consumers, and Decomposers

*The three domains are Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. Domains sit above kingdoms as the deepest branches of the tree of life. Bacteria and Archaea are prokaryotic; Eukarya contains all organisms with a true nucleus (protists, fungi, plants, and animals).*

**2. (Reading 21.01 — why molecular data, not morphology; targets a common misconception)**
Bacteria and Archaea are both prokaryotes and look almost identical under a microscope. Why are they placed in two separate domains?

- A) Archaea are much larger than Bacteria and easy to tell apart by eye
- B) One group has a nucleus and the other does not
- C) Their molecular sequences (such as rRNA) and cell chemistry differ deeply, revealing they are distinct lineages **✓**
- D) Bacteria live on land while Archaea live only in the ocean

*Appearance grouped all prokaryotes together, but molecular sequence data revealed that Bacteria and Archaea are as different from each other as either is from a eukaryote. Neither has a nucleus (both are prokaryotic), so a nucleus cannot be the difference. Molecules, not morphology, define the domains.*

**3. (Reading 21.01 — Archaea closer to Eukarya; targets a common misconception)**
Molecular evidence shows that Archaea are most closely related to which group?

- A) Bacteria, since both are prokaryotic and look alike
- B) Eukarya, the domain that includes protists, fungi, plants, and animals **✓**
- C) Viruses, since both are very small
- D) None — Archaea are unrelated to all other life

*Although Archaea look like Bacteria, their molecular sequences place them closer to Eukarya (including us) than to Bacteria. This is the key surprise of the three-domain system: the two groups that look most alike are not the closest kin. Archaea are not simply "a kind of bacteria."*

**4. (Reading 21.02 — prokaryotic structure)**
Which statement correctly describes a prokaryotic cell?

- A) It has a nucleus that encloses its DNA in a membrane
- B) Its DNA sits in an open region called the nucleoid, with no membrane-bound nucleus **✓**
- C) It contains mitochondria and a nucleus like an animal cell
- D) It has no DNA at all

*Prokaryotes (Bacteria and Archaea) have no membrane-bound nucleus; their DNA lies in a region called the nucleoid. They also lack membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria, though they do have ribosomes, a membrane, and usually a cell wall.*

**5. (Reading 21.02 — bacterial cell wall)**
A tough mesh called peptidoglycan is found in the cell walls of which group?

- A) Bacteria **✓**
- B) Fungi
- C) Plants
- D) Animals

*Bacterial cell walls contain peptidoglycan. Archaeal walls are made of different materials and contain no peptidoglycan; fungal walls are made of chitin; plant walls are made of cellulose; animal cells have no cell wall at all. The wall's chemistry is one clue that Bacteria and Archaea are separate domains.*

**6. (Reading 21.02 — ecological roles; targets "all microbes are harmful")**
Which of the following is a beneficial and essential role played by many bacteria?

- A) They only cause disease in the organisms they infect
- B) They fix nitrogen from the air into a form plants can use, and decompose dead matter to recycle nutrients **✓**
- C) They prevent plants from ever obtaining nitrogen
- D) They destroy the oxygen that living things need

*Only a minority of bacteria are pathogens. Most are harmless or essential: nitrogen-fixing bacteria convert atmospheric nitrogen into a usable form (part of the nitrogen cycle), decomposers recycle nutrients, and gut bacteria aid digestion. Microbes are mostly beneficial, not mostly harmful.*

**7. (Reading 21.02 — Archaea as extremophiles)**
In which environment would you be most likely to find archaea living as extremophiles?

- A) A near-boiling hot spring or water that is extremely salty **✓**
- B) The inside of a healthy human eye
- C) A sealed, sterile glass jar
- D) The surface of the Moon

*Many well-known Archaea are extremophiles that thrive in harsh conditions such as near-boiling hot springs, very salty water, and airless mud (methanogens). These habitats would kill most other organisms, which is how the domain first drew scientists' attention.*

**8. (Reading 21.03 — protists as a catch-all; targets "protists are one clean group")**
Why do biologists describe the protists as a "catch-all" rather than a single clean group?

- A) Because every protist is exactly the same as every other
- B) Because they are all prokaryotes with no nucleus
- C) Because "protist" means all the eukaryotes that are not plants, animals, or fungi — a collection of many only-distantly-related lineages **✓**
- D) Because they are not really alive

*A protist is defined by exclusion: any eukaryote that is not a plant, animal, or fungus. Molecular data show these organisms are many distantly related lineages that share a level of organization (mostly unicellular, eukaryotic) rather than a close common ancestor. The single name hides great diversity.*

**9. (Reading 21.03 — phytoplankton as producers)**
Phytoplankton are photosynthetic protists that drift in sunlit ocean water. Why are they so important to life on Earth?

- A) They are the largest predators in the ocean
- B) They are a major source of atmospheric oxygen and form the base of most aquatic food webs **✓**
- C) They decompose dead animals on the ocean floor
- D) They have no real effect on other organisms

*Because they photosynthesize across the vast area of the oceans, phytoplankton produce a large share of Earth's oxygen and serve as the producers at the base of nearly every aquatic food web, feeding the grazers that feed the fish, seabirds, and whales. They are producers, not tiny animals or decomposers.*

**10. (Reading 21.04 — fungi are not plants; chitin, hyphae, absorption; targets "fungi are plants")**
A student claims a mushroom is a kind of plant. Which set of features shows that fungi are NOT plants?

- A) Fungi photosynthesize, have cellulose walls, and grow from seeds
- B) Fungi are heterotrophs that feed by absorption, have cell walls of chitin, and grow as threadlike hyphae **✓**
- C) Fungi have leaves, roots, and stems like all plants
- D) Fungi make their own food using chloroplasts

*Fungi make no food of their own — they are heterotrophs that secrete enzymes and absorb digested nutrients (absorptive nutrition). Their cell walls are chitin (not the cellulose of plants), and their body is a web of hyphae forming a mycelium, reproducing by spores. They have no chloroplasts and do not photosynthesize, so they are their own kingdom, not plants.*
