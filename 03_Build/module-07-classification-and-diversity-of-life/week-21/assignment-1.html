The Three-Domain System
Reading 21.01 and Lesson 21.01
Before you begin: finish Reading 21.01 and work through Lesson 21.01, including the three domains, why Bacteria and Archaea are separate, and how molecular data (not appearance) revealed that Archaea are closer to Eukarya.
Lesson focus: Molecular sequence data, not morphology, defines the domains of life
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: name the three domains, identify which are prokaryotic, and explain in a few sentences why molecular evidence beats appearance for grouping life. It should take about 15–20 minutes. This work feeds directly into your Module 7 CER.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If two microbes look identical, how can we know they belong to two of the deepest branches of life?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Name the domains (3 pts). Name the three domains of life. Then state which two are made of prokaryotic cells and which one has cells with a true nucleus.
2
Part B — True or false, and fix it (3 pts). Decide whether this statement is true or false, then rewrite it correctly in one sentence: “Because Bacteria and Archaea both lack a nucleus and look alike, molecular data show they are each other's closest relatives.”
3
Part C — Why molecules win (4 pts). In three to five sentences, explain why scientists define the domains using molecular sequence data rather than how organisms look. Use the Archaea–Eukarya relationship as your example: what would appearance alone have told us, and what did the molecules reveal instead?
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A names Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya; identifies Bacteria and Archaea as prokaryotic and Eukarya as having a nucleus.
☐
Part B marks the statement FALSE and rewrites it: molecular data actually show Archaea are more closely related to Eukarya than to Bacteria, even though they look like Bacteria.
☐
Part C explains that appearance grouped all prokaryotes together and hid the truth, while molecular sequences track ancestry directly and revealed Archaea's closer kinship with Eukarya.
