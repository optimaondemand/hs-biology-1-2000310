Lesson focus: The structure of prokaryotic cells and the world-running jobs they do
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: label the features of a prokaryotic cell, match ecological roles to their descriptions, and correct a common misconception about microbes. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How can the simplest cells on Earth run some of its most important chemical cycles?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Label the prokaryote (3 pts). A prokaryotic cell has these features. In your own words, briefly describe what each one is or does: nucleoid, cell wall, and binary fission.
2
Part B — Match the role to its description (4 pts, 2 each). Match each ecological role to the correct description.
Role Description (write the matching letter)
Nitrogen fixer 
Extremophile 
A. An organism that thrives in a harsh environment such as a near-boiling hot spring or very salty water; many are Archaea.
B. A bacterium that converts nitrogen gas from the air into a form plants can use.
3
Part C — Correct the misconception (3 pts). A classmate says, “Bacteria are germs — they only make you sick.” In two or three sentences, explain why this is wrong, naming at least two beneficial roles bacteria play.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A correctly describes the nucleoid (region holding the DNA, no nuclear membrane), the cell wall (protective outer layer; peptidoglycan in Bacteria), and binary fission (asexual splitting into two).
☐
Part B matches Nitrogen fixer → B and Extremophile → A.
☐
Part C explains that only a minority of bacteria are pathogens and names two benefits (e.g., decomposers, nitrogen fixers, gut microbiome).
