Lesson focus: Determining relatedness from nodes, and identifying a clade
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts on a described cladogram: name the closest relatives from the nodes, identify a monophyletic group, and explain why rotating branches does not change relationships. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
👀 THE CLADOGRAM (READ CAREFULLY)
A cladogram compares four taxa. Reading up from the base:
• The Turtle branches off first (lowest node).
• The Lizard branches off next.
• At the highest node, the lineage splits into Crocodile and Bird.
On the page, the taxa are drawn left to right in this order: Turtle, Lizard, Crocodile, Bird — so the Lizard and Crocodile happen to be drawn right next to each other.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why must relatedness be read from shared nodes rather than from which tips are drawn side by side?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Closest relatives (3 pts). Which taxon is the Crocodile’s closest relative? Explain your answer by referring to the most recent common ancestor (the highest shared node) — not the drawing order.
2
Part B — Identify a clade (3 pts). Do Crocodile + Bird plus their most recent common ancestor form a monophyletic group (clade)? State yes or no, and give the definition of a clade you are using to decide.
3
Part C — The rotation test (4 pts). A classmate redraws the tree so the Bird is now on the far left. They claim this makes the Turtle and Lizard the closest relatives. In one or two sentences, explain why they are wrong.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A names the Bird as the crocodile’s closest relative (they share the highest node), not the Lizard drawn beside it.
☐
Part B answers yes and defines a clade as an ancestor together with all of its descendants.
☐
Part C explains that rotating a branch reorders the tips but does not change the nodes, so the relationships (Crocodile + Bird closest) are unchanged.
