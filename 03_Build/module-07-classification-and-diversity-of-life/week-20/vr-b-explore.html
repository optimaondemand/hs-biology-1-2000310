Week 20 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Tour a Molecular-Clock Tree of Life
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk a large molecular tree of life, starting at the root and moving out through the three domains — Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. As you go, you’ll observe how the length of each branch represents how much a lineage’s sequences have diverged — a stand-in for time, the molecular-clock idea from Lesson 20.03. Walking the distances makes an abstract idea physical: longer branch, more change, more time. This also previews next week’s three domains.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 20.03 and Lesson 20.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Tree of Life Gallery — Molecular Clock Walk (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX at the root shows your explorer checklist and marks the three domain paths. Begin at the root.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Start at the root. Stand at the base of the tree — the last universal common ancestor. Read the marker explaining that every branch above shares this ancestor. Place a sticky note: “Root = shared ancestor of all life.”
2
Walk the three domains. Follow the paths out to Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya. At each domain marker, note one example organism shown there. Observe that Archaea and Eukarya branch from each other more recently than either does from Bacteria.
3
Read the branch lengths. Compare a long branch to a short one and place a sticky note explaining what the difference means: a longer branch = more sequence divergence = (usually) more time since that lineage split. This is the molecular clock made visible.
4
Capture your evidence. Take four named snapshots: one at the root, and one in each of the three domains, naming each snapshot by its domain so you can use them later.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: name the three domains and write one or two sentences explaining what a longer branch length represents on a molecular tree.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students walk a molecular tree of life and read branch length as a proxy for sequence divergence and time — the molecular-clock idea made spatial — while getting an early look at the three domains. The key understanding to check: a longer branch means more accumulated sequence change, generally corresponding to more time since divergence (a calibrated estimate, not an exact stopwatch). Watch for students who read the vertical position or drawing order of tips as relatedness rather than the branching pattern. A complete submission shows four correctly named snapshots (root + three domains) and a note that names the domains and correctly interprets branch length.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
The root and all three domains (Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya) are visited and observed.
☐
A sticky note explains what branch length represents (more divergence, usually more time).
☐
Four correctly named snapshots and the typed note (three domains + branch-length meaning) are submitted to Canvas.
