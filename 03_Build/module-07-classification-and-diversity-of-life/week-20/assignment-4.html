Lesson focus: Classification as a self-correcting science, illustrated by Woese and the three domains
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is a short written response (about a paragraph) with a two-part prompt: explain why classification is revised, then use the Woese example to show how it happened in a real case. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why is it a strength, not a weakness, that scientists revise the classification of life as new evidence arrives?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Why it revises (5 pts). In 3–4 sentences, explain why classification changes over time. Address: that a tree is a hypothesis based on current evidence; that groups can be merged, split, or moved as new evidence arrives; and why this self-correction is a strength of science rather than a flaw.
2
Part B — The Woese example (5 pts). In 2–3 sentences, describe how Carl Woese used ribosomal RNA (rRNA) sequences to show that “bacteria” were really two different groups, and how this led to the three-domain system. Note that his idea was initially resisted before the evidence won out.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A explains that a tree is an evidence-based hypothesis, that groups can be merged/split/moved, and that revising to fit evidence is a strength (self-correction).
☐
Part B states that Woese compared rRNA sequences, found “bacteria” split into Bacteria and Archaea, and proposed the three domains — initially resisted, then accepted as evidence grew.
☐
Writing is clear and uses the key terms correctly (hypothesis, evidence, rRNA, three domains).
