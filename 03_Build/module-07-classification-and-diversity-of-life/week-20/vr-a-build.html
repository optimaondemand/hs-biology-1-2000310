Week 20 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Cladogram from a Character Matrix
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a cladogram in space from a given character matrix — a table of which taxa have which shared derived characters. You’ll place each taxon on a branch, mark the outgroup, label the nodes with the trait that appears there, and then record a walkthrough explaining why each taxon sits where it does. Building the tree with your hands turns the reading rules from Lessons 20.01–20.02 into something you can walk around and defend.
🧩 YOUR CHARACTER MATRIX (“✓” = HAS THE TRAIT)
Taxon Backbone Four limbs Amniotic egg Hair
Lamprey (outgroup)✓   
Salamander✓✓  
Lizard✓✓✓ 
Mouse✓✓✓✓
Hint: the more shared derived characters a taxon has, the later (closer in) it branches. The order of first appearance is: backbone → four limbs → amniotic egg → hair.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 20.01–20.02 · the character matrix above · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Tree of Life Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the branch-building floor marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction and shows the character matrix.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Place the outgroup. Set the Lamprey at the base of the tree as your outgroup. Add a sticky note: “Outgroup — baseline; branches off first.”
2
Build the branches in order of shared derived characters. Add the Salamander next (shares four limbs), then the Lizard (adds the amniotic egg), then the Mouse at the top (adds hair). More shared traits → branches later/closer in.
3
Mark the nodes. At each branch point, place a labeled marker for the trait that appears there: four limbs, then amniotic egg, then hair. Each node is a common ancestor where that new trait arose.
4
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk your tree from base to tip and explain, in about 60–90 seconds, why each taxon sits where it does and what each node’s trait means. Use the words outgroup, shared derived character, node, and common ancestor.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your full cladogram (outgroup, all four taxa, and labeled nodes) plus a few typed sentences explaining why each taxon branches where it does.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students turn a character matrix into a cladogram by nesting shared derived characters, then defend each placement aloud. The correct tree is (Lamprey, (Salamander, (Lizard, Mouse))) — ordered by four limbs, amniotic egg, hair. Common sticking points: forgetting to designate the outgroup, treating the shared backbone as if it ranked the ingroup taxa, and reading relatedness from left-to-right placement rather than from the nodes. A strong submission shows the outgroup at the base, taxa in the correct branching order, labeled nodes, and a recording that correctly uses “outgroup,” “shared derived character,” “node,” and “common ancestor.”
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
The outgroup (Lamprey) is placed at the base and the four taxa are in the correct branching order.
☐
Nodes are labeled with the trait that appears there (four limbs, amniotic egg, hair).
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted justifying each placement.
