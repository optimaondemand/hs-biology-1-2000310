Stomata, guard cells, and the balancing act

Stomata are pores in the leaf epidermis (mostly on the underside), each flanked by two guard cells. When guard cells fill with water and become turgid, they bow apart and the stoma opens; when they lose water and go flaccid, it closes. The plant is always balancing: open to gain CO₂ for photosynthesis, or close to conserve water. Transpiration is not just a problem — it is also the engine that drives the cohesion-tension pull from Lesson 24.01.

🔬 PART 3 · MEASURING IT WITH A POTOMETER

Turning an invisible loss into a number

A potometer holds a cut leafy shoot sealed in a water-filled tube. As the shoot transpires, it pulls water along a thin graduated tube and an air bubble (or water meniscus) moves along the scale. By timing how fast the bubble moves, you measure water uptake — a good stand-in for transpiration. Change one condition at a time (a fan, a lamp, a bag) and record how the rate changes. That is a controlled experiment.
