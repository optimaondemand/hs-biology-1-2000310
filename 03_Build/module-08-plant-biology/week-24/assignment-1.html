📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Xylem and the Rising of Water
Reading 24.01 and Lesson 24.01
Before you begin: finish Reading 24.01 and work through Lesson 24.01, including how xylem carries water upward and how the cohesion-tension mechanism pulls it to the treetop.
Lesson focus: A tall tree drinks with no pump — the water is pulled from the top
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: put the steps of cohesion-tension in order, label where the pull comes from and what holds the water column together, and explain in a sentence why no cellular energy is needed for the lift. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does a plant raise water to the top of a tall tree without a pump and without spending its own energy?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Put the mechanism in order (4 pts, 1 each). Number the four steps of cohesion-tension from 1 (first) to 4 (last) in the table below.
Step (in mixed-up order) Order (1–4)
The cohesive water column is pulled upward through the xylem, from roots toward leaves. 
Water evaporates from the leaves (transpiration) through the stomata. 
Hydrogen bonding (cohesion) links the water into one continuous, unbroken thread. 
The water loss creates tension (negative pressure) at the top of the xylem. 
2
Part B — Name the parts (3 pts). Answer in a word or short phrase: (a) Where does the pull come from? (b) What force holds the water column together as one thread — and what causes that force? (c) What is the force of water clinging to the xylem walls called?
3
Part C — Explain the energy (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why the plant spends no cellular energy (no ATP) lifting the water — what actually powers the lift?
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A order: (1) Water evaporates from the leaves; (2) the loss creates tension at the top of the xylem; (3) hydrogen bonding/cohesion links the water into one thread; (4) the cohesive column is pulled upward. (Accept step 3 in position 3 or noted as the property that makes step 4 possible.)
☐
Part B: (a) evaporation/transpiration at the leaves; (b) cohesion, caused by hydrogen bonding between water molecules; (c) adhesion.
☐
Part C states that the sun’s energy drives evaporation at the leaves (the pull), so no ATP/pump is needed for the lift.
