Phloem and the Flow of Sugar

Before you begin: finish Reading 24.03 and work through Lesson 24.03, including phloem structure, source-to-sink pressure-flow, and how phloem contrasts with the xylem of Lesson 24.01.
Lesson focus: Do not reverse them — xylem and phloem do opposite jobs in opposite ways
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: fill in a comparison table contrasting xylem and phloem (the single most common mistake on this topic), then apply it to one real example. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How do the plant’s two transport tissues differ — in what they are made of, what they carry, which way, and what drives them?

Part B — Apply it (2 pts). A gardener scratches a ring of bark (which contains the phloem) all the way around a tree trunk. Sugar can no longer travel past the ring to the roots. In one or two sentences, name which transport tissue was cut and explain why the roots are the part that starves.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Xylem: dead cells; water + dissolved minerals; one-way, upward (roots→leaves); driven by transpiration pull (tension), no plant energy.
☐
Phloem: living cells; sugars (sucrose); bidirectional, source→sink; driven by a pressure difference from active sugar loading.
☐
Part B: the phloem was cut; the roots are sinks that depend on sugar delivered from the leaves (the source), so with phloem cut they can no longer receive it and starve.
