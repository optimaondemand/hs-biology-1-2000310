Soil, Nutrients, and the Life Underground
Reading 24.04 and Lesson 24.04
Before you begin: finish Reading 24.04 and work through Lesson 24.04, including the key mineral nutrients and their roles, nitrogen fixation by bacteria, the plant microbiome, and where AI helps and fails in agriculture.
Lesson focus: A plant is never really alone in the soil — and an AI diagnosis is a lead to check, not an authority
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each nutrient to its role, explain the nitrogen-fixing mutualism, and write one sentence interrogating an AI crop-identification claim. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What must a plant gather from the soil, who helps it, and how far should we trust software to diagnose a crop from a photo?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS

2
Part B — The nitrogen-fixing mutualism (4 pts). In two or three sentences, explain: (a) why a plant cannot use the nitrogen gas (N₂) in the air directly, (b) what nitrogen-fixing bacteria (like Rhizobium) do for the plant, and (c) what the plant gives the bacteria in return — naming this as a mutualism.
3
Part C — Interrogate the AI (2 pts). An app scans a photo of a crop leaf and reports, with high confidence, a specific disease. In one sentence, state what a careful scientist should do before acting on that answer — and why the app might be wrong.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: Nitrogen → B, Phosphorus → D, Potassium → C, Magnesium → A.
☐
Part B: plants can’t break apart N₂ directly; the bacteria convert N₂ into ammonia the plant can use; the plant supplies the bacteria sugar — both benefit, so it is a mutualism.
☐
Part C treats the ID as a hypothesis to verify (check the real plant, consider look-alike causes, confirm with an expert or more evidence) and gives a reason the model can err (biased/limited training data or confidently-wrong look-alikes).
