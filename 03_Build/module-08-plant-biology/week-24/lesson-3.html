📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A leaf makes the food — but a root in the dark, a swelling fruit, and a growing tip all need it. How does the sugar reach them?
Today you will follow the plant’s second river: phloem, the living tissue that carries sugar from a source to a sink by the pressure-flow mechanism. Most of all, you will lock in the difference between phloem and the xylem of Lesson 24.01 — because reversing the two is the single most common mistake on this topic, and today it disappears.

What phloem is, and where sugar flows

Phloem is made of living cells — sieve-tube elements kept running by attached companion cells. It carries sugars (mainly sucrose) made in photosynthesis, and it flows bidirectionally — up or down — from a source (where sugar is made or released, like a leaf) to a sink (where sugar is used or stored, like a root, fruit, or growing tip).

💧 PART 2 · PRESSURE-FLOW, STEP BY STEP

How a pressure difference moves the sap

At the source, sugar is actively loaded into the phloem; water then follows by osmosis (Module 3), raising the pressure there. At the sink, sugar is unloaded, water leaves, and the pressure drops. Sap flows in bulk from the high-pressure source to the low-pressure sink, carrying its sugar along. That is the pressure-flow hypothesis.

⚖️ PART 3 · DO NOT REVERSE THEM

Xylem vs. phloem — the misconception, killed

Keep the contrast crisp: xylem = dead cells, one-way, water & minerals UP, driven by transpiration pull (tension). Phloem = living cells, bidirectional, sugars from source to sink, driven by pressure (active loading). Test yourself.

Two rivers run in one trunk: water climbing up the dead pipes of the xylem, sugar flowing to wherever it is needed through the living pipes of the phloem.
