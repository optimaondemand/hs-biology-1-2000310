📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Transpiration, Stomata, and the Potometer
Reading 24.02 and Lesson 24.02
Before you begin: finish Reading 24.02 and work through Lesson 24.02, including how stomata and guard cells work and how wind, light, temperature, and humidity change the transpiration rate measured by a potometer.
Lesson focus: Transpiration is both the cost and the engine of a plant’s life — and it can be measured
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: predict how each of four conditions changes the transpiration rate on a potometer and give the reason, then explain the “transpiration is not just a problem” idea in a sentence or two. This is a warm-up for your Module 8 CER, which analyzes exactly this kind of data. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does the environment around a leaf speed up or slow down the water it loses — and why is that loss also useful to the plant?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Predict and explain (8 pts, 2 each). For each condition added to the potometer, write whether the transpiration rate Rises or Falls, and give a one-phrase reason.
Condition added Rises / Falls? Reason (short phrase)
A fan blows on the shoot (WIND)  
A bright lamp shines on it (LIGHT)  
The surrounding air is warmed (TEMPERATURE)  
A clear plastic bag is sealed over it (HUMIDITY)  
2
Part B — Not just a problem (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why transpiration is not merely a “leak” the plant would fix if it could — what useful job does it do?
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Wind → Rises (blows away the humid air layer at the leaf). Light → Rises (stomata open for photosynthesis). Temperature → Rises (faster evaporation). Humidity → Falls (moist air, gentle vapor gradient).
☐
Part B says transpiration is the pull (transpiration pull / tension) that drives water and minerals up the xylem — the engine of water transport — not only a cost.
