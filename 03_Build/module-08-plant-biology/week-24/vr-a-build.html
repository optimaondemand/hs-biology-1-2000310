Week 24 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Model Xylem & Phloem Transport
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build two side-by-side transport columns inside a model plant — one for xylem and one for phloem — so you can see, in space, how the plant’s two rivers run. The xylem column carries water up from the roots, pulled by transpiration; the phloem column carries sugar from a source to a sink. Then you will record a walkthrough contrasting the two. Building them side by side is the surest way to stop reversing them — the most common mistake on this topic. This is Lessons 24.01 and 24.03, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 24.01 and 24.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Plant Transport Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the model plant with two empty vertical channels marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Build the xylem column. In the left channel, stack hollow “dead-cell” segments into a continuous pipe from roots to leaves. Fill it with a chain of linked water beads to show one continuous, cohesive column, and show it being pulled upward by transpiration at the leaf at the top. Add an arrow pointing up.
2
Label the xylem. Attach a text label reading: “XYLEM — dead cells, one-way, water + minerals UP, driven by transpiration pull.”
3
Build the phloem column. In the right channel, stack “living-cell” segments. Place a source (a sunlit leaf loading sugar) and a sink (a root or a fruit using/storing sugar), and show sugar moving from source to sink. Add an arrow showing the flow can go up or down.
4
Label the phloem. Attach a text label reading: “PHLOEM — living cells, bidirectional, sugar source→sink, driven by pressure.”
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 60–90 seconds, walk between the two columns and contrast them out loud, point by point: cells (dead vs. living), cargo (water/minerals vs. sugar), direction (one-way up vs. bidirectional source→sink), and driving force (transpiration pull vs. pressure). End with: “Two rivers, one trunk — and they do opposite jobs.”
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot showing both labeled columns plus a few typed sentences contrasting xylem and phloem on all four features (cells, cargo, direction, driving force).
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the plant’s two transport systems by building them side by side and contrasting them aloud. The single most important error to watch for and correct is the xylem/phloem reversal — students who mix up which tissue is dead, which carries sugar, which direction it flows, and what drives it lose points on every exam. A strong submission clearly distinguishes xylem (dead cells, one-way water/minerals UP, transpiration pull) from phloem (living cells, bidirectional sugar source→sink, pressure). Listen for the correct driving force for each — that is the detail most often confused.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Two columns are built and correctly labeled: xylem (dead cells, water + minerals UP, transpiration pull) and phloem (living cells, sugar source→sink, pressure).
☐
The xylem shows one continuous, cohesive water column pulled up; the phloem shows sugar moving from a source to a sink.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) contrasts the two tissues on cells, cargo, direction, and driving force.
