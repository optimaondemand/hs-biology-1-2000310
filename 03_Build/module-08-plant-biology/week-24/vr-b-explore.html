Week 24 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Observe a Potometer Under Varied Conditions
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will observe a potometer measuring the water uptake of a leafy shoot — watching an air bubble travel along the graduated tube as the shoot transpires. Then you will change one condition at a time — wind, light, temperature, humidity — and record how the uptake rate changes for each. This is a controlled experiment: one factor changes, the rest stay steady. Most important: the data you gather here is exactly the kind your Module 8 CER essay analyzes. This is Lesson 24.02, observed in space, and it directly supports the Module CER.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 24.02 and Lesson 24.02 · your scientific notebook (for a small data table).
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Transpiration Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the bench shows your observer checklist and a blank data table. Begin at the potometer set to Baseline (still air, room light, room temperature).
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Baseline. Watch the air bubble move at normal room conditions. Time how far it travels in the set interval and record this as your baseline rate — every other reading is compared to it.
2
Add WIND (fan). Turn on the fan, watch the bubble, and record the new rate. Note whether it is faster or slower than baseline. Take a named snapshot: “Wind.”
3
Add LIGHT (lamp), then raise TEMPERATURE. Return to baseline, then switch on the bright lamp and record the rate (snapshot: “Light”). Return to baseline again, warm the air, and record the rate (snapshot: “Heat”).
4
Raise HUMIDITY (bag), then compare. Return to baseline, seal a clear bag over the shoot, and record the rate (snapshot: “Humidity”). Then review your table: three conditions should have raised the rate (wind, light, heat) and one should have lowered it (humidity). Note one sentence explaining the odd one out.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four named snapshots (Wind, Light, Heat, Humidity) to this Canvas assignment, plus your data table (baseline + four conditions, each marked faster or slower) and one or two sentences stating which conditions raised the rate, which lowered it, and why. Keep this data — you will use it in your Module 8 CER essay.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students run a controlled transpiration experiment, changing one variable at a time and comparing each reading to a baseline. Expect wind, light, and higher temperature to raise the uptake rate and higher humidity to lower it; the humidity result is the one students most often predict wrong, so it is worth probing in discussion. This walk is designed to feed directly into the Module 8 CER essay, which asks students to analyze potometer transpiration data and argue how the environment affects a plant’s water loss — encourage careful, labeled data recording, not just snapshots.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A baseline rate is recorded, and each of the four conditions (wind, light, heat, humidity) is tested one at a time and compared to it.
☐
Four correctly named snapshots (Wind, Light, Heat, Humidity) and a data table marking each condition faster or slower are submitted.
☐
A short note states that wind, light, and heat raised the rate while humidity lowered it — the data set for the Module 8 CER.
