**1. (Reading 23.01 — cell wall vs. cell membrane)**
A student says, "In a plant cell, the cell wall and the cell membrane are just two names for the same thing." Why is this incorrect?

- A) There is no cell membrane in a plant cell — only a wall
- B) The cell wall is inside the membrane and controls what enters the cell
- C) The cell wall is a rigid cellulose layer outside the membrane, while the membrane is a separate inner layer that controls what enters and leaves **✓**
- D) The membrane is made of cellulose and the wall is made of water

*A plant cell has both. The cell wall is a rigid layer of cellulose sitting outside the cell membrane, giving support and shape. The cell membrane is still present, tucked inside the wall, doing its usual job of controlling what passes in and out. The wall supports; the membrane controls.*

**2. (Reading 23.01 — structures unique to plant cells; targets animal/plant confusion)**
Which set of structures is found in plant cells but NOT in animal cells?

- A) Nucleus, mitochondria, and cell membrane
- B) Cell wall, central vacuole, and chloroplasts **✓**
- C) Ribosomes, cytoplasm, and DNA
- D) Cell membrane and mitochondria

*Plant and animal cells share the nucleus, mitochondria, membrane, ribosomes, and cytoplasm. What sets plant cells apart are the cellulose cell wall, the large central vacuole, and the plastids (such as chloroplasts). Animal cells have none of these three.*

**3. (Reading 23.01 — central vacuole and turgor; targets "why plants wilt")**
A houseplant that has not been watered for several days droops and goes limp. At the cell level, what has happened?

- A) The cellulose cell walls have dissolved
- B) The chloroplasts have stopped making food, so the plant collapsed
- C) The nucleus has shrunk and stopped working
- D) The central vacuoles have lost water, so turgor pressure dropped and the cells can no longer stay firm **✓**

*A full central vacuole presses outward against the cell wall, creating turgor pressure that keeps the plant firm and upright. When water is short, the vacuoles shrink, turgor pressure falls, and the plant wilts. Watering it refills the vacuoles and restores the pressure — the walls do not dissolve.*

**4. (Reading 23.01 — plasmodesmata)**
Tiny channels pass through plant cell walls, connecting the cytoplasm of neighboring cells so they can share water, nutrients, and signals. What are these channels called, and what animal-cell structure do they resemble in function?

- A) Plasmodesmata, which work like gap junctions between animal cells **✓**
- B) Chloroplasts, which work like mitochondria
- C) Vacuoles, which work like the nucleus
- D) Ribosomes, which work like the cell wall

*Plasmodesmata are channels that pass through the cell walls and connect the cytoplasm of neighboring plant cells, letting them share materials and signals. They are the plant-cell counterpart to the gap junctions that connect animal cells — the same problem of walled-off cells communicating, solved a plant's way.*

**5. (Reading 23.02 — the three tissue systems)**
A plant biologist points to the xylem and phloem running through a stem and asks which tissue system they belong to. What is the correct answer?

- A) The dermal tissue, because it covers the plant
- B) The ground tissue, because it makes up the bulk of the plant
- C) The vascular tissue, because it transports water and sugars **✓**
- D) None — xylem and phloem are not part of any tissue system

*The three tissue systems are dermal (the protective outer covering), ground (the bulk, doing photosynthesis, storage, and support), and vascular (the transport plumbing). Xylem and phloem are the two pipelines of the vascular tissue: xylem carries water and minerals upward, phloem carries sugars.*

**6. (Reading 23.04 — palisade vs. spongy mesophyll)**
In a leaf, which layer carries out MOST of the photosynthesis, and why?

- A) The spongy mesophyll, because its air spaces trap the most light
- B) The palisade mesophyll, because it is packed with chloroplasts and sits high in the leaf where light is strongest **✓**
- C) The lower epidermis, because that is where the stomata are
- D) The cuticle, because it is the first layer the light reaches

*The palisade mesophyll is made of tall column cells packed with chloroplasts, positioned just under the transparent upper epidermis where light is strongest — so most photosynthesis happens there. The spongy mesophyll below has loose cells and air spaces mainly for gas diffusion, not maximum light capture.*

**7. (Reading 23.04 — stomata function)**
What is the main function of the stomata found mostly on the underside of a leaf?

- A) To make the sugar produced by photosynthesis
- B) To carry water up from the roots into the leaf
- C) To store starch for the plant
- D) To let carbon dioxide enter and oxygen and water vapor exit the leaf **✓**

*Stomata are pores in the lower epidermis, opened and closed by guard cells. Through them, carbon dioxide enters the leaf (a raw material for photosynthesis) and oxygen and water vapor exit. Placing them on the shaded underside reduces water loss. Sugar is made in the chloroplasts, and water is delivered by the xylem — not by the stomata.*

**8. (Reading 23.02 / 23.04 — xylem vs. phloem)**
Which statement correctly describes what xylem and phloem carry?

- A) Xylem carries water and minerals upward, and phloem carries sugars **✓**
- B) Xylem carries sugars, and phloem carries water upward
- C) Both xylem and phloem carry only water
- D) Both xylem and phloem carry only sugars

*Xylem is the pipeline that carries water and dissolved minerals upward from the roots. Phloem is the pipeline that carries sugars — the food made in the leaves — to where the plant needs them. A simple way to hold it: xylem = water up, phloem = sugars.*

**9. (Reading 23.02 / 23.03 — dermal tissue and root function)**
On a root, the epidermis (dermal tissue) grows many thin root hairs. How do these root hairs help the plant?

- A) They protect the root tip as it pushes through soil
- B) They greatly increase the surface area for absorbing water and minerals **✓**
- C) They carry sugars down from the leaves to the root
- D) They make food by photosynthesis underground

*Root hairs are thin outgrowths of the root's dermal tissue (epidermis). By vastly increasing the root's surface area in contact with the soil, they let the plant absorb much more water and dissolved minerals. Protecting the tip is the job of the root cap; carrying sugars is the job of phloem; and roots do not photosynthesize.*
