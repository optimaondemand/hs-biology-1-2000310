Week 23 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build & Label a Leaf Cross-Section
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a giant, walk-through leaf cross-section and label it from the top down: cuticle, upper epidermis, palisade mesophyll (show the chloroplasts), spongy mesophyll (show the air spaces), lower epidermis with stomata and guard cells, and a vein (xylem + phloem). Then you will record a short walkthrough naming each layer and its function, ending by pointing to where photosynthesis happens. Building the leaf as a space you can step inside makes its layered design concrete — a flat green blade turns out to be a purpose-built factory. This is Lesson 23.04, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 23.04 and Lesson 23.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Leaf Interior / Plant Anatomy Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty layering frame marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — build from the top surface downward.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Top coat. Lay the cuticle across the very top and label it: waxy, waterproof — limits water loss. Just below it place the upper epidermis: transparent — protects and lets light through.
2
Palisade layer. Under the upper epidermis, build the palisade mesophyll as tall column cells and show the chloroplasts inside them. Label it: most photosynthesis happens here — positioned near the top for maximum light.
3
Spongy layer. Below the palisade cells, build the spongy mesophyll with visible air spaces between the cells. Label it: air spaces let CO₂, O₂, and water vapor diffuse.
4
Bottom and veins. Place the lower epidermis with stomata and guard cells (remember: stomata are mostly on the underside). Label it: CO₂ enters, O₂/water vapor exit. Then thread a vein through the middle showing xylem (water in) and phloem (sugar out).
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 60–90 seconds, walk your leaf from top to bottom naming each layer and its function. End by pointing to where photosynthesis happens — the chloroplasts in the mesophyll (especially the palisade layer).
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one full snapshot of your labeled leaf (all layers visible) plus typed labels naming each layer top to bottom and its function, and a sentence saying where photosynthesis happens.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the leaf's layered design by building it top to bottom and stating what each layer does. Two common errors to watch for and correct: confusing palisade and spongy mesophyll (palisade = tall, dense, chloroplast-packed, near the top, does most photosynthesis; spongy = loose, air-spaced, for gas diffusion), and forgetting that stomata are mostly on the underside (a water-saving position in the shade). A strong submission shows all layers in correct top-to-bottom order with functions, chloroplasts drawn into the mesophyll, and a walkthrough that correctly points to the mesophyll as the site of photosynthesis.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All layers are built and labeled in correct top-to-bottom order (cuticle, upper epidermis, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, lower epidermis with stomata, and a vein).
☐
Chloroplasts are shown in the mesophyll, air spaces are shown in the spongy layer, and stomata are placed in the lower epidermis.
☐
A spatial recording (or full snapshot + typed labels) is submitted, naming each layer and its function and pointing to where photosynthesis happens.
