🍃 PART 1 · THE LAYERS, TOP TO BOTTOM
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Stomata — the tiny pores that let gases in and out of a leaf — are found mostly on the underside of most leaves. Before you read on, why might the bottom be a smart place for them?

From cuticle to vein

Reading a leaf from the top down: the cuticle is the waxy, waterproof coat that limits water loss. Below it, the upper epidermis is a transparent protective layer that lets light through. Then the palisade mesophyll — tall column cells packed with chloroplasts, right where the light is strongest — is where most photosynthesis happens. Below that, the spongy mesophyll has loose cells and air spaces that let gases diffuse. At the bottom, the lower epidermis holds the stomata and their guard cells, where carbon dioxide enters and oxygen and water vapor exit. Threaded through it all are the veins: xylem delivering water, phloem carrying sugar away.

💡 A MOMENT IN HISTORY · THE FIRST PLANT ANATOMISTS
In the 1670s, two men working independently with the newly improved microscope founded the science of plant anatomy: Marcello Malpighi in Italy and Nehemiah Grew in England. Around 1671–1682 both published detailed studies of the inner structure of plants, describing the tissues and cells that make up roots, stems, and leaves. What is striking is that they could see the structure long before anyone understood its function: they described the tissues, cells and vessels of roots, stems and leaves without knowing that these were arranged around a reaction called photosynthesis. They were like early explorers charting a coastline before anyone knew what lay inland. Their patient looking — the willingness to spend hours at a lens simply to see what was there — is where every layer in today's lesson began. It is a small monument to the module's virtue of wonder: they studied plants closely for no reason other than that plants are astonishing.

🔬 PART 3 · WHERE PHOTOSYNTHESIS HAPPENS (BACK TO MODULE 4)

The layers exist to serve the reaction

Now the leaf's design and the chemistry of Module 4 lock together. Photosynthesis happens inside chloroplasts, concentrated in the mesophyll: the light reactions run in the thylakoid membranes, and the Calvin cycle runs in the stroma. Every layer serves that reaction. Trace one input to its route below.

🧠 TRACE IT · INPUTS AND OUTPUT
Photosynthesis in a leaf needs water and carbon dioxide, and produces sugar. Which route is correct?

This is the whole plant body finished: roots absorb, stems transport, leaves make food — and the leaf's layers are arranged, top to bottom, around the photosynthesis you learned in Module 4. Wonder is the right response: a flat green blade turns out to be a purpose-built factory.

💪 THE IDEA THIS BUILDS

The leaf completes the plant body: cuticle to protect, epidermis to shield and admit light, palisade to make food, spongy layer to move gases, stomata to breathe, veins to supply and ship. Every layer answers the needs of photosynthesis.
