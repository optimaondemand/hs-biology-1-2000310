🌱 PART 1 · THE ROOT'S THREE JOBS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
A carrot and a blade of grass both have roots, but they look nothing alike underground. Before reading on, why might a plant grow one thick main root instead of many thin ones?

Anchor, absorb, store

A root does three jobs: it anchors the plant, absorbs water and minerals, and often stores food. Root hairs boost absorption by hugely increasing surface area. A taproot system (carrot, dandelion) has one thick main root for reaching deep and storing; a fibrous root system (grasses) spreads many thin roots to grip soil. A tough root cap shields the growing tip as it pushes through soil, and vascular tissue runs up the center, carrying absorbed water upward.

🌲 PART 3 · READING A WOODY STEM

Even a tree trunk is the three systems

A woody stem looks nothing like a soft seedling, but it is built from the same tissue systems — grown massive. Reason through what wood and bark actually are.

🧠 THINK IT THROUGH
In a woody tree trunk, the wood is mostly xylem and the inner bark contains phloem. What does this tell you?

Roots and stems are the plant's foundation and framework: roots absorb and anchor below, stems transport and support above. Tomorrow you climb to the leaf, where the water the roots gathered and the stems lifted finally meets the light — and food gets made.
