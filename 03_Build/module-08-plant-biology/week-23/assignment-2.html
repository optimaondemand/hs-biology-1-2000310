📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Tissue Systems
Reading 23.02 and Lesson 23.02
Before you begin: finish Reading 23.02 and work through Lesson 23.02, including the three tissue systems — dermal, ground, and vascular — and the jobs each one does.
Lesson focus: Skin, bulk, and plumbing — three systems, one continuous body
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each plant structure to its tissue system, name the two vascular pipelines, and explain in a sentence why we say the three tissue systems run continuously through the whole plant. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How do just three tissue systems — skin, bulk, and plumbing — run through an entire plant and do all its work?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match structure to tissue system (5 pts, 1 each). Match each plant structure or job to the tissue system that does it.
Structure / job Tissue system (write the letter)
Waxy cuticle that limits water loss 
Xylem and phloem transport 
Leaf mesophyll doing photosynthesis 
Root hairs that absorb water 
Storage and support cells inside a stem 
A. Dermal tissue  ·  B. Ground tissue  ·  C. Vascular tissue
(Letters are used more than once.)
2
Part B — Name the pipelines (2 pts). Vascular tissue has two transport tubes. Name each and state what it carries.
3
Part C — One continuous body (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain what it means to say the three tissue systems run continuously through the whole plant, using water traveling from a root to a leaf as your example.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: cuticle → A, xylem/phloem → C, mesophyll photosynthesis → B, root hairs → A, storage/support in a stem → B.
☐
Part B: xylem carries water and minerals upward; phloem carries sugars.
☐
Part C explains that each system is one unbroken network running through root, stem, and leaf — so water absorbed by a root can travel through continuous vascular tissue all the way to a leaf.
