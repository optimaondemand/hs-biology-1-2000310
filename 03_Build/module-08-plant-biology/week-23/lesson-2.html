Tissue Systems
Lesson · Skin, Bulk, and Plumbing — Three Systems, One Body
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
A single cell cannot run a whole plant. How do a plant's cells organize themselves into a working body?
Yesterday you met the plant cell. Today those cells gather into tissues, and the tissues into just three systems — dermal, ground, and vascular — each one running continuously through the entire plant. By the end you will be able to name all three, describe what each does, and match a plant structure to the system doing its job.

🌳 PART 1 · SKIN, BULK, AND PLUMBING
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
A plant has no skeleton and no blood vessels, yet it protects itself, holds itself up, and moves water and food from root to leaf. Which of these three jobs would you guess is hardest for a tall plant?

Three systems, running through the whole plant

A plant is organized into three tissue systems, and each runs continuously through the whole plant — root, stem, and leaf together. Think of them as the plant's skin (dermal), its bulk (ground), and its plumbing (vascular). The dermal tissue is the outer epidermis that protects and, with its waxy cuticle, limits water loss; it grows guard cells and stomata for gas exchange, and root hairs for absorption. The ground tissue is the working bulk that does photosynthesis, storage, and support. The vascular tissue is the transport plumbing — xylem (water up) and phloem (sugars).

🧠 PART 3 · THE ONE-BODY IDEA

One system, running from root to leaf

The most important idea in this lesson is that each tissue system is continuous — it does not stop at the root and start again at the leaf. Test that idea below.

🧠 THINK IT THROUGH
Water is absorbed by a root hair deep in the soil and ends up in a leaf at the top of the plant. What does this tell you about the vascular tissue?

This is the frame for the rest of the week: a root is where dermal tissue grows absorbing hairs and vascular tissue runs up the center; a stem is where vascular bundles carry the load; a leaf is where ground tissue stacks up chloroplasts. Same three systems, shaped to each part — form follows function at the scale of the whole plant.
