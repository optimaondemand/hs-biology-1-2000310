📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Plant Hormones: Coordinating a Body Without Nerves
Reading 25.03 and Lesson 25.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 25.03 and work through Lesson 25.03, including auxin, gibberellins, ethylene, and abscisic acid (ABA) and what each does.
Lesson focus: Chemical signals that coordinate a plant without nerves
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each hormone to one effect, name the hormone that is a gas, and answer one short question about how plants coordinate themselves. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How do four chemical signals let a plant grow, ripen, and protect itself — with no brain and no nerves?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Hormone to effect (6 pts, 1.5 each). Match each hormone to its correct effect from the list below.
Hormone Effect (write the letter)
Auxin 
Gibberellins 
Ethylene 
Abscisic acid (ABA) 
A. Triggers fruit ripening and leaf/flower drop  ·  B. Promotes cell elongation; bending toward light (phototropism)
C. Closes stomata in drought; keeps seeds dormant  ·  D. Promotes stem elongation and seed germination
2
Part B — The gas (2 pts). One of these four hormones is a gas that can spread from fruit to fruit. Name it, and explain in one sentence why “one ripe apple spoils the barrel.”
3
Part C — No nerves (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain how hormones let a plant coordinate its growth and responses without a nervous system.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: auxin → B, gibberellins → D, ethylene → A, ABA → C.
☐
Part B: the gas is ethylene; a ripening apple releases ethylene gas that drifts to nearby fruit and speeds their ripening too.
☐
Part C: hormones are chemical signals made in one part of the plant that travel to another part and tell cells what to do, coordinating the whole plant without any nerves.
