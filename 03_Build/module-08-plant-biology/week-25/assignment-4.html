Lesson 25.04
CER Practice: Do Plants Sense and Respond?
Assignment (CER Practice) · 10 pts
✍️ THIS IS CER PRACTICE — NOT THE MODULE ESSAY
This is a short exercise to practice the claim–evidence–reasoning skill. It is not the graded Module 8 CER essay — keep it brief (a short paragraph, about 4–7 sentences). It builds the muscle you will use on the module essay.
📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Tropisms: Sensing and Responding Without Nerves
Reading 25.04 and Lesson 25.04 (and the in-lesson CER scaffold)
Before you begin: finish Reading 25.04, work through Lesson 25.04 including its CER scaffold, and (ideally) complete VR-B so you have observations to use as evidence.
Lesson focus: Plants sense and respond — with chemistry, not nerves
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Can plants sense and respond to their world — even without a nervous system?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
Write one short paragraph answering the focus question, using the three CER steps from the lesson scaffold:
1
Claim (3 pts). State a clear one-sentence answer to the focus question.
2
Evidence (4 pts). Give at least two specific pieces of evidence from this week (for example: phototropism, auxin moving to the shaded side, ABA closing stomata in drought, gravitropism, or thigmotropism).
3
Reasoning (3 pts). Explain how your evidence supports the claim — that a stimulus is sensed and produces a purposeful, directional response through chemical signals (hormones) rather than nerves.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Claim clearly states that plants do sense and respond, and that they do so without a nervous system.
☐
Evidence includes at least two accurate examples (e.g., a shoot bending toward light; auxin gathering on the shaded side; ABA closing stomata in a drought; roots growing down / shoots up; a vine coiling around a support).
☐
Reasoning links evidence to claim: a stimulus is detected and answered with directional growth driven by hormones, not nerves — genuine sensing and responding.
