Lesson 25.02
Double Fertilization, Seed and Fruit, and Germination
Lesson · From Pollen Tube to Sprouting Seedling
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Pollen has landed on the stigma — but the egg is buried deep in the ovary. How does the pollen reach it, and what does that meeting create?
Today you follow the pollen the rest of the way: a pollen tube grows down to an ovule and delivers two sperm. In the flowering plants’ signature double fertilization, one sperm makes the embryo and the other makes the endosperm. Then the ovule becomes a seed, the ovary becomes a fruit, and — when conditions are right — the seed germinates.

🌱 PART 1 · THE POLLEN TUBE AND TWO SPERM
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN

A tube to the egg

After pollination, the pollen grain on the stigma grows a pollen tube down through the style to reach an ovule in the ovary. The tube is the plant’s way of crossing that distance on dry land without needing water. Down it travel two sperm cells — and, unusually, both will be used.

🧩 PART 2 · DOUBLE FERTILIZATION: TWO SPERM, TWO PRODUCTS

What each sperm makes

In double fertilization, the first sperm + egg → zygote → embryo (the future plant); the second sperm + two polar nuclei → endosperm (nutrient-rich food for the embryo). Test the distinction below.

🍀 PART 3 · SEED, FRUIT, AND GERMINATION

Ovule → seed, ovary → fruit, then sprout

The ovule becomes the seed (embryo + stored food + protective seed coat). The ovary becomes the fruit, which protects and disperses the seeds — animals eat it, or wind and water carry it. When a seed has enough water, oxygen, and a suitable temperature, it germinates: the embryo lives on its stored food until its leaves reach the light and it can photosynthesize on its own. This is where the seed land-adaptation from Module 7 finally shows how it is built.

💪 THE IDEA THIS BUILDS

Double fertilization is the efficient heart of flowering-plant reproduction: one sperm builds the next generation (embryo), the other builds its food supply (endosperm), and the flower’s ovule and ovary become the seed and fruit that carry and feed it. With reproduction understood, Lesson 25.03 turns to a different question — how a plant coordinates its growth and timing, with no brain and no nerves, using chemical signals called hormones.
