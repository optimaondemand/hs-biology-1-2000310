Week 25 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Explore Phototropism & Auxin
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will observe a seedling bend toward a light source and watch, up close, why it happens: auxin gathers on the shaded side, and that side’s cells elongate more, so the shoot bends toward the light. You will recreate Darwin’s classic test by covering vs. uncovering the shoot tip to discover that the tip is what senses the light, and you will observe gravitropism — roots growing down, shoots growing up. Then you will capture the evidence that a plant genuinely senses and responds without nerves. This feeds directly into your in-lesson CER practice. This is Lessons 25.03 & 25.04, observed in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Readings 25.03 & 25.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Plant Response Chamber (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the light-and-gravity stations. Begin at the Phototropism marker.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Station 1 — Watch it bend. Turn on the side light and watch the seedling grow so that it bends toward the light. Place a sticky note: “Phototropism — growth toward light.”
2
Station 2 — See the auxin. Switch on the auxin overlay. Observe auxin accumulate on the shaded side and those cells elongate more than the lit side. Place a sticky note: which side has more auxin, and why does that bend the shoot toward the light?
3
Station 3 — Recreate Darwin’s test. Cover the shoot tip with a cap and shine the light — the shoot does not bend. Uncover the tip and try again — it bends. Place a sticky note: “The tip is what senses the light.”
4
Station 4 — Gravitropism & snapshots. Rotate the sprouting seed and watch the root grow down and the shoot grow up. Then take four named snapshots (name each “Bending,” “Auxin-shaded-side,” “Tip-covered,” “Gravitropism”). At the notebook marker, write one sentence of evidence that plants sense and respond without nerves.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: state which side of the shoot gets more auxin and why that bends it toward the light, and write one sentence of evidence that plants sense and respond without a nervous system. Save these observations — they are your evidence for the Lesson 25.04 CER practice.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students observe the auxin mechanism of phototropism directly — the single most important idea being that auxin gathers on the shaded side (not the lit side) and elongates those cells, bending the shoot toward the light. The Darwin tip-covering test shows that sensing happens at the tip while bending happens below. Correct the common error that the lit side grows faster. A complete submission shows four correctly named snapshots and a note that (a) identifies the shaded side as auxin-rich and (b) gives one line of evidence that plants sense and respond without nerves. This walk feeds directly into the Lesson 25.04 in-lesson CER practice and Assignment 25.04.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All four stations are visited (bending, auxin on shaded side, tip-covered test, gravitropism) and observed.
☐
A sticky note at each station records an observation, including that auxin gathers on the shaded side and that the tip senses the light.
☐
Four correctly named snapshots and the typed note (auxin mechanism + one line of sense-and-respond evidence) are submitted to Canvas.
