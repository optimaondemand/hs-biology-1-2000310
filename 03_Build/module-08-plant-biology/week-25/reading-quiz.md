**1. (Reading 25.01 — flower parts; male vs. female)**
Which structure is the MALE part of a flower, and what does its anther do?

- A) The carpel; its anther catches pollen
- B) The petal; its anther stores nectar
- C) The stamen; its anther produces pollen **✓**
- D) The ovary; its anther holds the egg cells

*The stamen is the male part of the flower. It consists of an anther, which produces pollen, held up by a stalk called the filament. The carpel (or pistil) is the female part — its stigma catches pollen and its ovary holds the ovules with the egg cells.*

**2. (Reading 25.01 & 25.02 — pollination vs. fertilization)**
A bee carries pollen from a flower's anther and deposits it on another flower's stigma. What has just occurred?

- A) Fertilization, because a sperm has joined an egg
- B) Pollination, the transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma **✓**
- C) Germination, because a new plant has begun to grow
- D) Double fertilization, because two sperm were delivered

*Pollination is simply the transfer of pollen from an anther (male) to a stigma (female). It is only the delivery of pollen. Fertilization — the joining of a sperm and an egg — happens later, after a pollen tube grows down to an ovule.*

**3. (Reading 25.02 — the two products of double fertilization)**
In the double fertilization of a flowering plant, two sperm are used. What are the TWO products they form?

- A) Two identical embryos, so the plant makes twins
- B) A seed coat and a fruit
- C) A stigma and a style
- D) A zygote that becomes the embryo, and the endosperm that nourishes it **✓**

*Double fertilization is a hallmark of flowering plants. One sperm fertilizes the egg to form a zygote, which becomes the embryo (the future plant). The other sperm joins two polar nuclei to form the endosperm, a nutrient-rich tissue that feeds the developing embryo.*

**4. (Reading 25.02 — ovule → seed, ovary → fruit)**
After fertilization, which two flower parts develop into the seed and the fruit?

- A) The stigma becomes the seed, and the style becomes the fruit
- B) The petal becomes the seed, and the sepal becomes the fruit
- C) The ovule becomes the seed, and the ovary becomes the fruit **✓**
- D) The anther becomes the seed, and the filament becomes the fruit

*After fertilization the ovule develops into the seed (embryo + stored food + protective seed coat), and the whole ovary ripens into the fruit, which protects the seeds and helps disperse them. A peach pit is the seed; the fleshy peach around it is the ripened ovary.*

**5. (Reading 25.02 — germination requirements)**
A dry seed has waited in a packet for a year. Once planted, what conditions must it have to germinate (sprout)?

- A) Water, oxygen, and a suitable temperature **✓**
- B) Only direct sunlight on the buried seed
- C) A fresh supply of pollen delivered to the seed
- D) Nothing external — a seed sprouts on a fixed timer

*Germination begins when a seed has enough water, oxygen, and a suitable temperature. It does not need sunlight to sprout (many seeds germinate underground in the dark); the young plant lives on the seed's stored food until its leaves reach the light and can photosynthesize.*

**6. (Reading 25.04 — definition of tropism)**
In biology, what is a "tropism"?

- A) The process by which a seed stores food for its embryo
- B) Directional growth of a plant in response to a stimulus **✓**
- C) The transfer of pollen from one flower to another
- D) The fusion of a sperm cell with an egg cell

*A tropism is directional growth in response to a stimulus — the plant grows toward or away from something in its environment. This is how a rooted plant "moves": not by traveling, but by growing in a chosen direction, using hormones rather than nerves.*

**7. (Reading 25.03 & 25.04 — auxin → phototropism; the mechanism)**
A shoot bends toward light coming from one side. Which hormone is responsible, and by what mechanism?

- A) Ethylene; it makes the lit side ripen and shrink
- B) Abscisic acid; it closes the cells on the shaded side
- C) Gibberellin; it makes the whole shoot grow straight up
- D) Auxin; it moves to the shaded side, so those cells elongate more and the shoot bends toward the light **✓**

*This is phototropism. Auxin (made at the shoot tip) moves to the shaded side of the shoot. Because auxin promotes cell elongation, the shaded-side cells stretch longer than the lit-side cells, and the unequal growth bends the shoot toward the light — no nerves required.*

**8. (Reading 25.03 — ethylene → ripening)**
A grocer places one ripe apple in a barrel of unripe apples, and soon the whole barrel ripens. Which hormone best explains this?

- A) Ethylene, a gas that triggers ripening and spreads from fruit to fruit **✓**
- B) Auxin, which makes the apples elongate
- C) Abscisic acid, which keeps the apples dormant
- D) Gibberellin, which makes the apples germinate

*Ethylene is a gaseous plant hormone that triggers fruit ripening and the drop of leaves and flowers. Because it is a gas, ethylene from a ripening apple drifts to nearby apples and speeds their ripening too — which is why "one ripe apple spoils the barrel."*

**9. (Reading 25.03 — ABA → closes stomata in drought; contrast with gibberellin)**
During a drought, a plant closes its stomata to conserve water. Which hormone signals this, and how does it differ from gibberellin?

- A) Gibberellin closes the stomata; abscisic acid promotes germination
- B) Ethylene closes the stomata; gibberellin ripens the fruit
- C) Abscisic acid (ABA) closes the stomata under drought stress, whereas gibberellin promotes seed germination and stem elongation **✓**
- D) Auxin closes the stomata; gibberellin bends the shoot toward light

*Abscisic acid (ABA) is the "stress" hormone: in drought it signals the stomata to close, reducing water loss, and it keeps seeds dormant. Gibberellin does nearly the opposite kind of job — it promotes seed germination (breaking dormancy) and stem elongation. ABA generally opposes the growth-promoting hormones.*
