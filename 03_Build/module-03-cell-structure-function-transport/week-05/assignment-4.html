Lesson focus: comparing plant and animal cells, and telling the wall from the membrane
📋 LAB OVERVIEW
In this lab you observe and draw three classic cells — onion (plant), Elodea (water plant), and cheek (animal) — using a real microscope or the virtual microscope from the lesson. You will label what you see, compare plant against animal, and pin down the difference between a cell wall and a cell membrane. Plan about 30–40 minutes. Work safely: if you use real cheek cells, take your own sample with a clean cotton swab and dispose of it properly; never share samples.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Looking at real cells, what can you see that tells a plant cell from an animal cell — and where exactly is the wall, versus the membrane?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Observe and draw three cells (6 pts). View onion epidermis, Elodea, and cheek cells (real or virtual). For each, make a labeled drawing or capture a labeled image. Label every structure you can see: cell membrane, cell wall (where present), nucleus, and chloroplasts (where present). Note the magnification you used.

✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All three cells are drawn or captured, labeled, and the observation table is filled in.
☐
A plant-only structure (cell wall and/or chloroplasts) is correctly named as absent from the cheek cell.
☐
The wall-versus-membrane explanation is correct: every cell has a membrane; only some (plants, many others) have a wall.
