📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Two Kinds of Cell, and Why Cells Stay Small
Reading 5.02 and Lesson 5.02
Before you begin: finish Reading 5.02 and work through Lesson 5.02, including prokaryote vs. eukaryote, the wall-versus-membrane distinction, and the surface-area-to-volume rule.
Lesson focus: why cells come in two types and why they stay small
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts. In Part A you sort features into the cell type they belong to (prokaryote, eukaryote, or both). In Part B you reason from a surface-area-to-volume calculation to explain why a cell that grows too large must divide. The whole assignment should take about 20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why can’t a cell just keep growing forever — and what does its geometry have to do with staying alive?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Sort the features (5 pts). For each feature, write whether it belongs to a Prokaryote only, a Eukaryote only, or Both.
Feature Prokaryote / Eukaryote / Both
a. Has a cell membrane 
b. Has a membrane-bound nucleus 
c. DNA located in a nucleoid (no nucleus) 
d. Has membrane-bound organelles 
e. Contains DNA as its genetic material 
2
Part B — Surface area, volume, and why cells divide (5 pts). Two cube-shaped model cells: Cell X has sides of 2 units (surface area 24, volume 8) and Cell Y has sides of 4 units (surface area 96, volume 64). (i) Compute the SA:V ratio for each (round to one decimal). (ii) State which cell has the more favorable ratio for exchanging materials. (iii) In two to three sentences, explain why a low SA:V ratio is a problem and why a cell that grows too large will instead divide.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All five features in Part A are correctly sorted, including “both” cases (membrane, DNA).
☐
Both SA:V ratios are computed correctly (Cell X = 3.0 : 1; Cell Y = 1.5 : 1) and Cell X is identified as more favorable.
☐
The explanation connects a low SA:V ratio to the membrane not keeping up, and to why dividing restores a high ratio.
