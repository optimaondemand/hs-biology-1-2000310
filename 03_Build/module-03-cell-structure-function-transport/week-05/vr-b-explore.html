Week 5 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Tour an Endosymbiosis Scene
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will step into a scene that plays out one of biology’s great events: a small free-living prokaryote being engulfed by a larger host cell and, over time, becoming a permanent organelle — a mitochondrion or a chloroplast. Watching the host membrane wrap around an already-membraned cell makes the double membrane obvious in a way a diagram never can, and it lets you find the very evidence you weighed in Lesson 5.03.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your notes from Reading 5.03 (the four lines of evidence) · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Endosymbiosis Scene — The Cell That Swallowed a Cell (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist. Begin there.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Watch the engulfment. Move to the host cell and watch the smaller free-living prokaryote get surrounded by the host’s membrane — without being digested. Pause at the moment the host membrane closes around the inner cell, and notice that two membranes now wrap it.
2
Find the evidence inside. Step into the now-organelle and locate two clues from Lesson 5.03: its own loop of circular DNA and its double membrane. Place a sticky note on each, naming it and writing one phrase on why it points to an ancient free-living cell.
3
Capture your evidence. Take two named snapshots — one clearly showing the double membrane, and one showing the organelle’s own circular DNA.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your two named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: what the double membrane and the organelle’s own circular DNA each suggest about where mitochondria and chloroplasts came from.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students explore an endosymbiosis scene, watch a prokaryote become an organelle, annotate the double membrane and circular DNA with sticky notes, and capture named snapshots. Common sticking points: catching the moment the host membrane closes (so the double membrane reads clearly), and taking a named snapshot rather than a system screenshot. Encourage students to explain why each clue matters — a double membrane is what you’d expect from a wrap-around engulfment; circular DNA is bacterial in character. This scene directly supports the CER reasoning in Assignment 5.03 and previews the energy organelles of Module 4.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
The engulfment is observed, and the double membrane and circular DNA are found and annotated with sticky notes.
☐
Each sticky note names the evidence and gives one phrase on why it points to a free-living origin.
☐
Two named snapshots and the typed note are submitted to Canvas.
