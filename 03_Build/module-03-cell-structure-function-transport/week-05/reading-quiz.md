**1. (Reading 5.01 — the three tenets)**
Which statement is one of the three tenets of cell theory?

- A) All cells contain a nucleus
- B) All cells come from pre-existing cells **✓**
- C) All cells have a cell wall
- D) All cells are the same size

*A core tenet is that every cell arises from a pre-existing cell (Virchow). Not all cells have a nucleus (prokaryotes lack one) or a wall (animal cells lack one), and cells vary widely in size.*

**2. (Reading 5.01 — history of cell theory)**
A Dutch cloth merchant with no formal scientific training ground his own lenses and became the first person to observe living microorganisms. Who was he?

- A) Robert Hooke
- B) Rudolf Virchow
- C) Antonie van Leeuwenhoek **✓**
- D) Theodor Schwann

*Leeuwenhoek, a draper in Delft, first saw living "animalcules." Hooke coined the word "cell" from cork (dead cell walls); Schwann helped establish that animals are cellular; Virchow gave the third tenet.*

**3. (Reading 5.02 — prokaryote vs. eukaryote)**
What single feature is used to classify a cell as eukaryotic rather than prokaryotic?

- A) The presence of a cell membrane
- B) The presence of DNA
- C) The presence of a membrane-bound nucleus **✓**
- D) The presence of a cell wall

*A eukaryote keeps its DNA inside a membrane-bound nucleus; a prokaryote does not. Both cell types have a membrane and DNA, and a cell wall is found in some of each — so none of those distinguish them.*

**4. (Reading 5.02 — cell wall vs. cell membrane misconception)**
A student claims, "If a cell has no cell wall, it cannot be a real cell." Why is this claim incorrect?

- A) Every cell has a cell wall, so the situation never happens
- B) Every cell has a cell membrane, but only some cells (such as plants and many bacteria) have a wall **✓**
- C) The cell wall and the cell membrane are the same structure
- D) Only prokaryotes are real cells

*The cell membrane is universal; the cell wall is not. Animal cells, for example, have a membrane but no wall and are fully real cells. The wall and membrane are distinct structures.*

**5. (Reading 5.02 — surface-area-to-volume)**
As a cell grows larger, why does its ability to exchange materials with its surroundings become a problem?

- A) Its volume increases faster than its surface area, lowering the surface-area-to-volume ratio **✓**
- B) Its surface area increases faster than its volume
- C) Its cell membrane disappears
- D) It loses its DNA

*Volume rises faster than surface area as a cell grows, so the SA:V ratio falls and the membrane can no longer supply the interior fast enough. This is why cells stay small and divide rather than growing without limit.*

**6. (Reading 5.03 — what endosymbiotic theory claims)**
According to the endosymbiotic theory, mitochondria and chloroplasts —

- A) were built from scratch by the nucleus
- B) are made of the same material as the cell wall
- C) were once free-living prokaryotes engulfed by a host cell **✓**
- D) are found only in prokaryotic cells

*Endosymbiotic theory proposes that these organelles descend from free-living prokaryotes that were engulfed and kept, not digested. They are found in eukaryotes, not prokaryotes, and are not assembled by the nucleus.*

**7. (Reading 5.03 — evidence for endosymbiosis)**
Which observation provides the strongest support for the endosymbiotic theory?

- A) Mitochondria and chloroplasts are colored
- B) Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own circular DNA and divide by binary fission, like bacteria **✓**
- C) Mitochondria and chloroplasts are found in the nucleus
- D) Mitochondria and chloroplasts are larger than the cell

*Their own bacterium-like circular DNA, their own ribosomes, a double membrane, and division by binary fission all point to a free-living bacterial origin. They are not located in the nucleus, nor larger than the whole cell.*

**8. (Reading 5.04 — comparing plant and animal cells)**
Under the microscope, a cell is rounded, has a nucleus, but shows no cell wall and no chloroplasts. This cell is most likely a —

- A) cheek (animal) cell **✓**
- B) onion (plant) cell
- C) Elodea (plant) cell
- D) bacterium

*An animal cell, such as a cheek cell, has a membrane and nucleus but no wall and no chloroplasts. Onion and Elodea are plant cells (they have a wall; Elodea also has chloroplasts), and a bacterium lacks a true nucleus.*

**9. (Reading 5.04 — light vs. electron microscopy)**
A researcher needs to see fine detail *inside* an organelle. Which microscope is the appropriate choice, and what is its main trade-off?

- A) A light microscope, because it can show living cells in motion
- B) An electron microscope, because it has much higher resolution but usually cannot show living cells **✓**
- C) A hand lens, because it is inexpensive
- D) A light microscope, because electrons cannot be focused

*The electron microscope resolves detail inside organelles, but the specimen is usually killed, so living cells cannot be observed. The light microscope can show living cells but lacks the resolution to see inside organelles.*
