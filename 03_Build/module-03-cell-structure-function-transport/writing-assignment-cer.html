Module 3 CER Writing Assignment
The Red Blood Cell in Three Solutions
Claim · Evidence · Reasoning · 12 pts · counts toward the CER writing category
📖 WHAT THIS IS
This is the writing that pulls Module 3 together. It draws on the membrane work of Week 7: the selectively permeable phospholipid bilayer, osmosis, and the three states of tonicity — hypotonic, hypertonic, and isotonic. It is assigned at the start of Week 5 and due at the end of Week 7, so you can build toward it as the module unfolds. Your teacher will read and score it personally — this one is not auto-graded.
🎯 YOUR TASK
Predict and justify what happens to a red blood cell placed in a hypotonic, a hypertonic, and an isotonic solution — and use that reasoning to explain why fluids given through an IV must be isotonic to the blood.
🧪 THE SCENARIO
A laboratory technician places identical drops of human blood into three beakers and watches the red blood cells under a microscope:
Beaker Solution around the cells What you must predict
APure (distilled) water — very low soluteWhat happens to the cell, and why?
BConcentrated salt water — very high soluteWhat happens to the cell, and why?
C0.9% saline — solute equal to the cell’s interiorWhat happens to the cell, and why?
Name each solution as hypotonic, hypertonic, or isotonic relative to the cell, then build your argument from there.
📝 BUILD YOUR ARGUMENT IN FOUR MOVES
Write in connected paragraphs, not a list. You decide the wording — these are the moves a strong CER makes, not a fill-in-the-blank.
1
Claim. In one or two sentences, state what determines the direction water moves across a red blood cell’s membrane, and therefore what will happen to the cell in each of the three beakers.
2
Evidence. Lay out the facts your claim rests on: that the membrane is a selectively permeable bilayer, that osmosis is the diffusion of water across it, and the solute conditions in each beaker (hypotonic A, hypertonic B, isotonic C). State your prediction for each cell — swell and possibly burst (lyse), shrivel (crenate), or stay the same.
3
Reasoning. This is the heart of the essay. For each beaker, explain why water moves the way it does — water diffuses toward the side with the higher solute (lower water) concentration until the two sides reach balance. Connect that movement to the outcome you predicted, and note the one key difference a plant cell would show (its cell wall resists bursting — not the same as the membrane). Address why a red blood cell, with no wall, is so vulnerable in pure water.
4
Integration — the IV question. Use everything above to answer the real-world stakes: why must fluid dripped into a patient’s bloodstream be isotonic (about 0.9% saline)? Explain what would go wrong if a hospital infused pure water, and what would go wrong with concentrated salt water — tying each back to the beaker that models it.
🧭 THE VIRTUE THIS PRACTICES · PERSEVERANCE
This module’s virtue is perseverance — the patience of Leeuwenhoek grinding his own lenses to see the unseen, and of Margulis holding to her evidence through years of rejection. A good CER asks the same patience of you: not to guess an answer, but to trace a chain of why from a molecule’s behavior all the way to a decision that keeps a patient alive. Follow the water, one careful step at a time, and let the reasoning carry the claim.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
A clear claim states that the direction of osmosis — and the cell’s fate — depends on the solute concentration outside relative to inside.
☐
All three solutions are correctly named (hypotonic / hypertonic / isotonic) and each cell’s outcome is correctly predicted (lyse / crenate / no change).
☐
The reasoning explains why water moves toward the higher solute concentration, links it to each outcome, and correctly handles the no-cell-wall point.
☐
The essay closes by applying the reasoning to IV fluids and reads as one connected argument (~400–700 words).
📊 CER SCORING RUBRIC (3 PTS × 4 CRITERIA = 12 PTS)
Criterion Strong (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1)
Claim Specific and accurate; ties the cell’s fate to the solute concentration outside versus inside. Somewhat vague or partly inaccurate. Missing, or just restates the prompt.
Evidence Names the bilayer/osmosis and all three tonicity conditions correctly, with the right predicted outcome for each cell. Some conditions or outcomes named, or described only partially. Conditions/outcomes missing, vague, or inaccurate.
Reasoning Explains why water moves toward higher solute, links it to each outcome, and handles the no-wall point; engages the mechanism, not just the result. Connects movement to outcome but with gaps in the why. States outcomes without explaining the cause.
Integration Applies the reasoning to IV fluids convincingly; reads as one coherent argument; within the word range. IV connection present but loosely tied to the rest. IV question unanswered or disconnected.
Total possible: 12 pts
