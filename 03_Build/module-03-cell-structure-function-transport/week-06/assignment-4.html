Plant vs. Animal Cells; Specialization

Before you begin: finish Reading 6.04 and work through Lesson 6.04, including plant vs. animal cell differences, the cell wall vs. membrane distinction, and cell specialization.
Lesson focus: how one set of DNA can build many different specialized cells
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts. In Part A you sort structures into plant only, animal only, or both, and you tell the cell wall apart from the cell membrane. In Part B you reason about specialization: how cells with identical DNA can be so different. The whole assignment should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If a muscle cell and a nerve cell carry exactly the same DNA, why do they look and act so differently?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Sort the structures (5 pts). For each structure, write whether it is found in a plant cell only, an animal cell only, or both.
Structure Plant / Animal / Both?
1. Cell wall 
2. Cell membrane 
3. Chloroplast 
4. Mitochondrion 
5. Large central vacuole 
Then, in one sentence, state the key difference between a cell wall and a cell membrane.
2
Part B — Specialization reasoning (5 pts). In three to five sentences, explain how a muscle cell and a nerve cell can be so different even though they carry the same DNA. Your answer should: (a) name the process (cells express different genes — differentiation); and (b) give one example of how a cell’s structure fits its function (for instance, why a red blood cell has no nucleus, or why a nerve cell is long).
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All five structures are correctly sorted (plant only / animal only / both).
☐
The wall-vs-membrane sentence shows the membrane is in all cells and the wall is a rigid extra layer outside it.
☐
Part B names differentiation/gene expression and gives one structure-fits-function example.
