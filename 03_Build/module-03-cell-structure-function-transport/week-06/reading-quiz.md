**1. (Reading 6.02 — the Golgi packages and ships)**
After a protein is built and folded, which organelle modifies it, packages it, and ships it to its destination?

- A) Ribosome, because it builds the protein
- B) Golgi apparatus, because it modifies, packages, and ships proteins **✓**
- C) Rough ER, because it folds the protein
- D) Nucleus, because it stores the instructions

*The Golgi apparatus is the cell's "post office" — it modifies, packages, and ships proteins and lipids. The ribosome only builds the protein and the rough ER only folds it; neither packages or ships.*

**2. (Reading 6.01 — structure of the nucleus)**
The nucleus is surrounded by a double membrane pierced by openings. What is the function of these nuclear pores?

- A) To produce ATP for the cell
- B) To allow certain molecules, such as copies of instructions, to pass in and out **✓**
- C) To build proteins from amino acids
- D) To digest worn-out cell parts

*Nuclear pores are gateways in the nuclear envelope that let molecules move in and out while keeping the DNA protected inside. ATP is made in mitochondria, proteins are built by ribosomes, and digestion is done by lysosomes.*

**3. (Reading 6.01 — rough vs. smooth ER)**
A cell that is actively making and exporting large amounts of protein would be expected to have a great deal of which organelle?

- A) Smooth ER, because it builds lipids
- B) Rough ER, because its ribosomes process proteins for export **✓**
- C) Central vacuole, because it stores water
- D) Cell wall, because it provides support

*Rough ER is studded with ribosomes and folds and processes proteins, especially those headed for export — so a protein-exporting cell has abundant rough ER. Smooth ER lacks ribosomes and handles lipids and detox.*

**4. (Reading 6.01 — ribosome function, bridge to Module 2)**
Ribosomes carry out which job in the cell?

- A) Joining amino acids to build proteins **✓**
- B) Capturing light energy to make sugar
- C) Storing the cell's genetic instructions
- D) Breaking down fatty acids

*Ribosomes build proteins by joining amino acids in the order specified by the DNA's instructions — connecting back to the amino-acid monomers of Module 2. Light capture is the chloroplast's job, DNA storage is the nucleus's, and fatty-acid breakdown is the peroxisome's.*

**5. (Reading 6.02 — lysosome function)**
Which organelle contains digestive enzymes that break down waste and worn-out cell parts?

- A) Golgi apparatus
- B) Ribosome
- C) Lysosome **✓**
- D) Chloroplast

*Lysosomes are membrane sacs full of digestive enzymes — the cell's recycling center. The Golgi packages and ships, ribosomes build proteins, and chloroplasts perform photosynthesis.*

**6. (Reading 6.03 — mitochondria, structure → function)**
The mitochondrion's inner membrane is folded into ridges called cristae. How does this structure support the mitochondrion's function?

- A) The folds store the cell's DNA
- B) The folds increase the surface area for the energy-releasing reactions of respiration **✓**
- C) The folds capture sunlight for photosynthesis
- D) The folds package proteins into vesicles

*Cristae increase the working surface inside the mitochondrion, allowing more of the reactions that release energy as ATP — a clear case of structure fitting function. DNA storage is the nucleus's job, sunlight capture is the chloroplast's, and packaging is the Golgi's.*

**7. (Reading 6.03 — endosymbiotic theory)**
Mitochondria and chloroplasts each contain their own small loop of DNA and have a double membrane. This evidence best supports the idea that —

- A) they were built by ribosomes in the nucleus
- B) they descend from free-living bacteria that were taken inside a larger cell long ago **✓**
- C) they are not really part of the cell
- D) they store the cell's main genetic instructions

*Their own DNA and double membranes are key evidence for the endosymbiotic theory: these organelles descend from once-free-living bacteria engulfed by a larger cell. The nucleus, not these organelles, holds the cell's main genetic instructions.*

**8. (Reading 6.04 — cell wall vs. cell membrane)**
Which statement correctly distinguishes the cell wall from the cell membrane?

- A) Every cell has a cell wall, but only plant cells have a membrane
- B) The cell membrane is found in all cells and controls what enters; the cell wall is a rigid extra layer outside it, found in plant cells but not animal cells **✓**
- C) The cell wall and the cell membrane are two names for the same structure
- D) The cell membrane is rigid, while the cell wall is thin and flexible

*All cells have a flexible cell membrane that controls what crosses. Only some cells (plants, fungi, many bacteria) also have a rigid cell wall outside the membrane for support. They are not the same structure, and it is the wall — not the membrane — that is rigid.*

**9. (Reading 6.04 — cell specialization)**
Nearly all of your cells contain the same DNA, yet a muscle cell and a nerve cell are very different. What best explains this?

- A) Muscle cells and nerve cells have completely different DNA
- B) Different cell types express different genes, so each builds the structures its job requires **✓**
- C) Nerve cells lost most of their DNA during development
- D) The cells were built from different kinds of atoms

*Through differentiation, cells with the same DNA express different genes — switching some instructions on and others off — so each cell type develops the structures it needs (structure fits function). The cells share the same DNA and are made of the same kinds of atoms.*
