📖 WHAT YOU ARE READING TODAY
In Reading 6.01 a protein was built by a ribosome and folded in the rough ER. But a finished part still has to be sorted, addressed, and delivered — and the cell needs a cleanup crew for what wears out. This reading meets the Golgi apparatus, lysosomes, vacuoles, and peroxisomes, and then ties them together as the endomembrane system — one connected manufacturing-and-shipping pathway. This is the anchor reading for Lesson 6.02.

The Golgi apparatus: the cell’s post office

The Golgi apparatus is a stack of flattened membrane sacs that receives proteins and lipids from the ER. Its job is to modify, package, and ship them — it puts finishing touches on each product, then sorts it into a tiny membrane bubble called a vesicle addressed to its destination. The post-office image is exact: the Golgi is where products are labeled and routed.

This is worth pinning down, because it is one of the most common test mix-ups: if a question asks which organelle packages and ships proteins, the answer is the Golgi apparatus — not the ribosome (which builds the protein) and not the ER alone (which folds it).

Cleanup, storage, and detox

Organelle Structure Function
Lysosome Membrane sac packed with digestive enzymes. Breaks down waste, worn-out parts, and invaders — the cell’s recycling center.
Vacuole Membrane-bound storage space; plants have one large central vacuole. Stores water, nutrients, and waste; in plants it also helps support the cell with internal pressure.
Peroxisome Small enzyme-filled sac. Breaks down fatty acids and detoxifies harmful substances, producing hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) and then destroying it.

A peroxisome both makes a dangerous byproduct and neutralizes it on the spot — a small example of structure built precisely for a self-contained job.

The endomembrane system: one connected pathway

None of these organelles works alone. Together with the nuclear envelope and the ER, they form the endomembrane system — a series of membranes linked into a single manufacturing-and-shipping pathway. Follow the route:

nuclear envelope → ER → Golgi → vesicles → cell membrane or lysosomes

Instructions leave the nucleus; the ER builds and folds; the Golgi modifies, packages, and addresses; vesicles carry the finished product to the cell’s surface for export or to a lysosome for use. The membranes are physically connected by traveling vesicles, the way a chain of conveyor belts links the stations of one factory.

💪 WHY THIS MATTERS

When you can name not just each organelle but the order in which they hand a product along, you understand the cell as a system, not a list. Keep the post-office picture for the Golgi — it answers the question that trips up the most students.
