Lesson focus: down the gradient is free; up the gradient costs ATP
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts. In Part A you classify five transport scenarios. In Part B you explain in writing why the sodium-potassium pump requires ATP. The whole assignment should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Diffusion is free. So what is the cell paying for when it spends ATP to move an ion across the membrane?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Classify each scenario (5 pts, 1 each). For each scenario, write whether it is Passive or Active, then name the specific type: simple diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion, active transport (pump), or bulk transport (endo-/exocytosis).
Scenario Passive or Active? Specific type
1. Oxygen diffuses straight through the bilayer into a cell, down its gradient.  
2. Glucose crosses through a carrier protein, moving down its gradient.  
3. A pump uses ATP to push 3 Na⁺ out and pull 2 K⁺ in, against their gradients.  
4. Water moves across a membrane toward the side with more solute.  
5. A white blood cell engulfs a whole bacterium in a membrane vesicle.  
2
Part B — Why the pump needs ATP (5 pts). In three to five sentences, explain why the sodium-potassium pump must use ATP while simple diffusion does not. Your answer should: (a) state which direction the pump moves the ions relative to their gradients; (b) contrast that with the downhill direction of diffusion; and (c) state the rule clearly — moving against a gradient requires energy, moving with it does not.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All five scenarios classified correctly: 1 passive/simple diffusion; 2 passive/facilitated diffusion; 3 active/active transport (pump); 4 passive/osmosis; 5 active/bulk transport (endocytosis).
☐
Part B says the pump moves ions against their gradients while diffusion goes with the gradient.
☐
The answer states the rule: against the gradient needs ATP; with the gradient does not.
