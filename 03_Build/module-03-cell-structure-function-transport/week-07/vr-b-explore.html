Week 7 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Red Blood Cells in Three Tanks
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will drop the same red blood cell into three different solution tanks — hypotonic, hypertonic, and isotonic — and watch what osmosis does to it. In one tank the cell swells and bursts; in another it shrivels; in the third it holds its shape. Seeing the three outcomes side by side, in space, makes tonicity concrete — and it gives you the visual evidence you’ll need for the Module 3 CER.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 7.03 and Lesson 7.03 · your scientific notebook (start your CER notes here).
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Osmosis Lab — Three Tanks (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the three labeled tanks. Begin there.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Hypotonic tank (pure water). Drop a red blood cell into the tank labeled hypotonic. Watch water move in and the cell swell and burst (lyse). Place a sticky note: which way did water move, and why?
2
Hypertonic tank (very salty). Drop a fresh cell into the hypertonic tank. Watch water move out and the cell shrivel (crenate). Place a sticky note explaining the direction of water movement.
3
Isotonic tank (~0.9% saline). Drop a fresh cell into the isotonic tank. Observe that there is no net change — the cell holds its normal shape. Place a sticky note: why is this the safe condition, and why must IV fluids match it?
4
Capture your evidence. Take three named snapshots — one of each tank’s outcome (swollen/burst, shriveled, unchanged). Name each snapshot with its tonicity so you can use them in your CER.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: for each tank, which way water moved and what happened to the cell — and one sentence on why an IV must be isotonic to blood.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students observe osmosis act on a red blood cell across hypotonic, hypertonic, and isotonic tanks and capture each outcome. Common sticking points: confusing the direction of water movement (remind them water chases the solute), and taking a named snapshot rather than a system screenshot. Push students to explain why water moved each way, not just what happened. A complete submission shows three named snapshots and a note correctly linking each outcome to osmosis. This activity is direct evidence-gathering for the Module 3 CER on RBC tonicity and IV fluids.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A red blood cell is placed in all three tanks and the outcome (swell/lyse, shrivel, unchanged) is observed in each.
☐
A sticky note in each tank explains the direction of water movement.
☐
Three named snapshots and the typed note (including the IV-fluid sentence) are submitted to Canvas.
