📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts. In Part A you predict the direction of net water movement for three membrane setups. In Part B you correct a common mistake about osmosis in writing. The whole assignment should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
When two solutions meet across a membrane, which way does water flow — and how can you tell every time without guessing?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Predict the water direction (6 pts, 2 each). Each row shows two sides of a beaker separated by a selectively permeable membrane that lets water cross but not the solute. For each row, write whether net water moves toward Side 1, toward Side 2, or no net movement.
Setup Side 1 Side 2 Net water moves toward…
a.10% salt2% salt 
b.5% sugar5% sugar 
c.pure water8% sugar 
2
Part B — Correct the mistake (4 pts). A classmate writes: “In osmosis, water always moves to the side that has more water.” In two to four sentences, explain what is wrong with this statement and state the rule correctly. Use the words solute and gradient, and explain why water moves toward the higher solute concentration.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All three predictions are correct: (a) toward Side 1, (b) no net movement, (c) toward Side 2.
☐
Part B identifies the error and restates the rule: water moves toward the higher solute concentration.
☐
The correction uses the words solute and gradient.
