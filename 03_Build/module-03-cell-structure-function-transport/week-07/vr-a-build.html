Week 7 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Model the Fluid-Mosaic Membrane
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a cell membrane with your hands — first the phospholipid bilayer, then the three kinds of membrane protein set among the lipids. A flat diagram makes the membrane look like a wall; building it in three dimensions makes the “fluid mosaic” obvious: heads facing the water, tails tucked inside, and proteins studded through like tiles in a mosaic. This is Lesson 7.01, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lesson 7.01 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Molecular Modeling room (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the workbench marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Build the bilayer. From the phospholipid IFX, place a row of phospholipids with their hydrophilic heads pointing up toward the water and their hydrophobic tails pointing down. Then add a second row facing the opposite way, tail to tail, to complete the bilayer.
2
Add the three proteins. Set into the bilayer one channel protein (a tunnel through the membrane), one carrier protein (one that would change shape to ferry a molecule), and one receptor protein (one with a binding site on the outer surface). Space them out like tiles in a mosaic.
3
Label everything. Place a sticky note on the hydrophilic heads and another on the hydrophobic tails (noting which faces water). Then label each of the three proteins with its name and its one-line job.
4
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk along your membrane and explain aloud, in about 60–90 seconds: why the bilayer arranges the way it does, and what each of the three proteins does. Use the words fluid mosaic and selectively permeable.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your labeled membrane plus a few typed sentences explaining why the bilayer forms and what each protein does.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the fluid mosaic model by building a two-row bilayer and inserting channel, carrier, and receptor proteins. Common sticking points: orienting the second lipid row tail-to-tail, distinguishing the three protein types, and starting a spatial recording. Watch for students who place all heads on one side — nudge them to remember that BOTH faces touch water. A strong submission shows a correctly oriented bilayer, three labeled proteins, and a recording that uses “fluid mosaic” and “selectively permeable” correctly.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A two-row phospholipid bilayer is built with heads facing the water on both sides and tails tucked inward.
☐
Channel, carrier, and receptor proteins are placed and each is labeled with its name and job.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted that explains the build.
