📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts. In Part A you complete a tonicity chart for a red blood cell. In Part B you reason through one IV-fluid scenario in writing — a short version of your CER. The whole assignment should take about 20–25 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
A red blood cell has no wall to protect it. So how does the solution around it decide whether the cell swells, shrivels, or survives unchanged?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Complete the tonicity chart (6 pts, 2 per row). For each solution, fill in (i) which way water moves (IN, OUT, or no net movement) and (ii) what happens to the red blood cell (swells/lyses, shrivels/crenates, or stays normal).
Solution outside the cell (i) Water moves… (ii) The red blood cell…
Hypotonic (e.g., pure water)  
Hypertonic (very salty)  
Isotonic (~0.9% saline)  
2
Part B — IV-fluid reasoning (4 pts). A nurse must choose an IV fluid for a patient. In three to five sentences, explain why the fluid must be isotonic to blood (about 0.9% saline). Build a mini-CER: state a claim (the fluid must be isotonic), give evidence (what would happen to red blood cells in a too-dilute or too-concentrated fluid), and give reasoning (connect it to osmosis — water moving toward the higher solute concentration).
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Chart is correct: hypotonic → water IN, cell swells/lyses; hypertonic → water OUT, cell shrivels/crenates; isotonic → no net movement, cell stays normal.
☐
Part B states a clear claim (the IV must be isotonic) supported by evidence about lysis and crenation.
☐
The reasoning ties the outcome to osmosis — water moving toward the higher solute concentration.
