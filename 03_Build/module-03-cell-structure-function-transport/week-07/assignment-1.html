Lesson focus: how structure makes the membrane selectively permeable
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts. In Part A you match each part of the membrane to its correct description. In Part B you explain, in your own words, why a phospholipid bilayer forms on its own in water. The whole assignment should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
No one builds the cell membrane brick by brick. So how does a wall that chooses what crosses it assemble all by itself?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match the membrane parts (5 pts). For each part in the left column, write the letter of the description that matches it from the right column. Each description is used once.
Membrane part Description
1. Phospholipid bilayerA. Catches a specific signaling molecule and passes a message into the cell.
2. Channel proteinB. The double layer of phospholipids, heads facing water and tails tucked inside, that forms the wall.
3. Receptor proteinC. Tucks among the tails and tunes how fluid the membrane stays.
4. CholesterolD. Forms a tunnel that lets specific particles, such as ions or water, pass through.
2
Part B — Why the bilayer self-assembles (5 pts). In three to five sentences, explain why phospholipids spontaneously form a bilayer in water. Your answer should: (a) use the words hydrophilic and hydrophobic; (b) say which part faces the water and which part hides from it; and (c) connect this arrangement to the idea that the membrane is selectively permeable — why small nonpolar molecules cross easily but ions need a protein.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
All four membrane parts are correctly matched to their descriptions.
☐
Part B explains that hydrophilic heads face water and hydrophobic tails hide from it, so the bilayer forms on its own.
☐
The answer links this arrangement to selective permeability — nonpolar molecules cross, ions need a protein.
