Week 18 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Simple Cladogram of Speciation Events
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a simple cladogram — a branching tree — with your hands. Starting from a single ancestral population, you will arrange a set of organisms onto branches according to the shared characters they have, and you will mark each branch point (node) as a speciation event: one population splitting into two. A flat picture of a tree can look like a finished diagram; building it in three dimensions makes you decide, at every fork, why the branch is there. This is Lesson 18.01, built in space. (Keep it simple — full cladistics comes in Module 7; here you are visualizing branching descent.)
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lesson 18.01 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Tree of Life Workshop (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the build platform marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction and the set of organism cards.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Place the root. Set the ancestral population card at the base of your build platform. Everything will branch upward from this single starting point.
2
Make the first split. Draw two branches up from the root and place a node marker at the fork. Label it “speciation event — one population becomes two.”
3
Sort by shared characters. Place your organism cards onto the branches so that organisms sharing more characters sit on nearer branches. Add more forks as needed, marking each one as a speciation event at its node.
4
Annotate two nodes. Choose two of your branch points and place a sticky note at each saying what shared character groups the organisms above it — and reminding yourself that the node is where gene flow stopped and the populations diverged.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk along your tree and explain aloud, in about 60–90 seconds: that each branch point is a speciation event, that shared characters group organisms onto nearby branches, and that the whole tree shows common descent from the root. Use the words speciation, shared character, and common ancestor.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your finished tree with the labeled nodes plus a few typed sentences explaining that each branch point is a speciation event and that the tree shows common descent.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize branching descent by building a simple cladogram and marking each node as a speciation event. Common sticking points: reading the tree as a ladder with one “most advanced” tip (it is not — all living tips are equally modern), and forgetting that a node represents an ancestral population splitting, not one species turning into another. Nudge students to justify each branch by a shared character. A strong submission shows a rooted branching tree with at least two nodes labeled as speciation events and a recording that uses “speciation,” “shared character,” and “common ancestor” correctly. This build previews the formal classification work of Module 7.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A rooted branching tree is built, with organisms sorted onto branches by shared characters.
☐
At least two branch points (nodes) are labeled as speciation events.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted that explains the branching as common descent.
