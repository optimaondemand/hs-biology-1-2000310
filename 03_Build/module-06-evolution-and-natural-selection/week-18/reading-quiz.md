**1. (Reading 18.01 — allopatric speciation; targets a common misconception)**
Two populations of birds are separated by a tall mountain range. Over many generations they develop different mating calls and can no longer interbreed. This is an example of which type of speciation?

- A) Sympatric speciation, because both populations remained in the same location
- B) Allopatric speciation, because a geographic barrier separated the populations **✓**
- C) Genetic drift only, because the mountains changed allele frequencies by chance
- D) Convergent evolution, because both groups independently evolved similar traits

*A geographic barrier — here, a mountain range — physically separates the populations and cuts off gene flow, which is the hallmark of allopatric speciation. The separated groups then diverge under selection and drift until they can no longer interbreed. Sympatric speciation happens without any geographic separation.*

**2. (Reading 18.01 — the biological species concept)**
By the biological species concept, what makes two populations members of the same species?

- A) They look very much alike in size, shape, and coloring
- B) They currently live in the same geographic area
- C) They eat the same kinds of food in the same habitat
- D) They can interbreed in nature and produce fertile offspring **✓**

*The biological species concept defines a species as populations whose members can interbreed and produce *fertile* offspring, so that genes keep flowing through the group. Looking alike, sharing a habitat, or sharing a diet are not the defining test — fertile offspring (continued gene flow) is.*

**3. (Reading 18.01 — reproductive isolation)**
Why is reproductive isolation necessary for one species to split into two?

- A) It stops gene flow, so the separated populations can diverge independently **✓**
- B) It makes the two populations look more different from each other at once
- C) It increases the number of offspring each population produces each year
- D) It prevents mutations from occurring in either of the separated populations

*Speciation requires that gene flow between the populations ends. As long as genes keep mixing between them, the populations stay one species. Once reproductive isolation stops that flow, natural selection and drift act on each population separately, and they diverge until they can no longer interbreed.*

**4. (Reading 18.02 — meaning of Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium)**
A population is in Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium. What does this tell you about that population?

- A) It has stopped reproducing, so no new alleles can enter the population
- B) It is evolving very rapidly, so allele frequencies shift each generation
- C) Its allele frequencies are not changing, so it is not evolving **✓**
- D) All of its members are genetically identical, so there is no variation

*Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium describes a population whose allele frequencies stay constant from generation to generation — a population that is not evolving. It is a null model: because real populations rarely meet its conditions, a departure from equilibrium is evidence that evolution is occurring.*

**5. (Reading 18.02 — which condition disrupts equilibrium)**
Which of the following would cause a population to depart from Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium?

- A) Random mating among all of the individuals in the population
- B) A very large population size, so that chance has little effect
- C) No migration into or out of the population during the generations
- D) Natural selection favoring one genotype over the others in each generation **✓**

*Equilibrium requires no natural selection, no mutation, no gene flow, random mating, and a very large population. Natural selection favoring one genotype violates a condition and shifts allele frequencies. The other choices are conditions that help maintain equilibrium, not disrupt it.*

**6. (Reading 18.03 — what Miller–Urey did and did not show)**
The Miller–Urey experiment (1953) sparked a mixture of simple gases and produced amino acids. What did this experiment demonstrate?

- A) That organic building blocks of life can form from simple gases and energy **✓**
- B) That a living cell can be created from simple gases and an energy source
- C) That life on Earth must have first arrived from outer space on meteorites
- D) That spontaneous generation of living organisms is true in everyday settings

*Miller and Urey showed that organic building blocks — amino acids — can form from simple gases given an energy source. The experiment did *not* create a living cell; the path from building blocks to a self-replicating cell remains an open question. It also did not revive spontaneous generation, which Pasteur had disproved.*

**7. (Reading 18.03 — spontaneous generation vs. the open question; targets a common misconception)**
A student says, "Since Pasteur disproved spontaneous generation, science has also answered how the first life on Earth originated." Why is this reasoning flawed?

- A) Pasteur's experiment actually proved that spontaneous generation occurs in everyday life
- B) They are different questions: Pasteur tested everyday spontaneous generation, not how life first arose **✓**
- C) Pasteur's experiment was never accepted, so spontaneous generation is still believed
- D) The origin of life has been fully explained and is no longer studied by scientists

*Pasteur's swan-neck flask (early 1860s) disproved the idea that life arises readily from non-living matter in everyday conditions. The origin-of-life question is different and narrower: how life first arose once, under early-Earth conditions. Disproving the first does not answer the second, which is still actively researched.*

**8. (Reading 18.04 — human and chimpanzee relationship; targets the key EOC misconception)**
Which statement correctly describes the relationship between humans and chimpanzees?

- A) Humans evolved directly from the modern chimpanzees we see today over millions of years
- B) Chimpanzees are slowly evolving into humans and will eventually reach that form
- C) Humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor from roughly 6–7 million years ago **✓**
- D) Humans and chimpanzees are unrelated species that only look somewhat similar

*Humans did not evolve from chimpanzees. The two are evolutionary cousins: both descend from a common ancestor that lived about 6–7 million years ago, after which the lineages branched separately. A modern chimpanzee is a fully modern species, not an ancestor or an "unfinished" human.*

**9. (Reading 18.04 — evolution has no goal; targets a common misconception)**
Which statement about human evolution is accurate?

- A) Human evolution was a branching bush of many related species, most now extinct **✓**
- B) Human evolution was a straight ladder of species leading directly to modern humans
- C) Only one human-like species existed at a time, each replacing the one before
- D) Evolution has a built-in goal of producing ever more intelligent species over time

*Human evolution was a branching bush, not a directed ladder — many related species existed, several at the same time, and most are now extinct. Evolution has no goal or direction; trends such as increasing brain size are patterns in a branching history, not steps toward a predetermined endpoint.*
