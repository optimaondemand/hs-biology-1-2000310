Before you begin: finish Reading 18.04 and work through Lesson 18.04, including the hominid bush, the human–chimpanzee common ancestor, and the module synthesis of evidence, mechanisms, and speciation.
Lesson focus: humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor — neither descends from the other
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: correct the “humans evolved from chimps” statement using the idea of a common ancestor, order a set of hominid species, and write one synthesis sentence pulling the module together. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If we did not evolve from chimpanzees, how exactly are we related to them — and how does our story fit the one argument of this module?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Correct the misconception (5 pts). A classmate writes: “Humans evolved from chimpanzees.” Rewrite this into a correct statement, and in two or three sentences explain the right relationship using the term common ancestor (include roughly when that ancestor lived).
2
Part B — Order the hominids (4 pts). Put these four species in order from earliest to most recent, and after each name the one trait or milestone given in parentheses.
Homo erectus (first to migrate out of Africa; used fire) · Homo sapiens (us, ~300,000 years ago) · Australopithecus afarensis (“Lucy,” ~3.2 mya; bipedal, small brain) · Homo habilis (early stone-tool use)
3
Part C — Synthesis sentence (1 pt). In one sentence, tie together the module’s three weeks — evidence (Week 16), mechanisms (Week 17), and speciation (Week 18) — into the single argument for common descent.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A states that humans and chimps share a common ancestor (~6–7 mya) and that neither descends from the other.
☐
Part B order: A. afarensis → H. habilis → H. erectus → H. sapiens, each with its trait.
☐
Part C connects evidence, mechanism, and speciation into one case for common descent.
