Week 18 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk the Miller–Urey Apparatus & Early-Earth Scene
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk through a life-size model of the Miller–Urey apparatus — the flask of early-atmosphere gases, the spark electrodes, the condenser, and the collecting trap — set within a stormy early-Earth scene. As you follow the loop, you will watch simple gases, sparked with energy, give rise to organic molecules that collect in the trap below, where amino acids accumulate. Walking the apparatus makes the experiment a process you move through, and makes its result precise: building blocks can form from simple chemistry — and no living cell appears anywhere in the flask. This is Lesson 18.03, walked in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 18.03 and Lesson 18.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Early-Earth Lab — The Miller–Urey Walk (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the four station markers. Begin at the gas flask at the top of the loop.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Station 1 — The gas flask. Stand inside the upper flask and observe the early-atmosphere gases (water vapor plus simple gases such as methane, ammonia, and hydrogen). Place a sticky note: what is in this flask, and what is not (no cells, no life)?
2
Station 2 — The spark. Watch the electric sparks fire between the electrodes (standing in for lightning on the early Earth). Place a sticky note: what is the spark’s job here? (It supplies the energy that drives the chemistry.)
3
Station 3 — The condenser. Follow the loop down through the condenser, where the sparked vapors cool and turn back to liquid, carrying newly formed molecules down toward the trap. Place a sticky note: where are the new molecules heading?
4
Station 4 — The collecting trap & capture your evidence. Examine the trap at the bottom where amino acids accumulate — the building blocks the experiment produced. Confirm there is no cell or living thing here. Then take four named snapshots, one at each station (Flask, Spark, Condenser, Trap), naming each so you can use them later.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: in one or two sentences, state what the experiment showed (building blocks such as amino acids can form from simple chemistry and energy) and what it did not show (a living cell).
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students walk the four parts of the Miller–Urey apparatus in order and see exactly where amino acids accumulate. The single most important learning outcome is the precise scope of the result: building blocks can form from simple chemistry; a living cell does not appear. Common sticking points: overstating the experiment as “creating life,” and confusing it with the disproved idea of spontaneous generation. Keep the discussion strictly within the science — there is no metaphysical claim to make here, for or against any position. A complete submission shows four named snapshots in correct order and a note that correctly states what the experiment did and did not show.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All four stations are visited in order (Flask, Spark, Condenser, Trap) and observed.
☐
A sticky note at each station records what that part of the apparatus does.
☐
Four named snapshots and the typed note (what it showed vs. did not show) are submitted to Canvas.
