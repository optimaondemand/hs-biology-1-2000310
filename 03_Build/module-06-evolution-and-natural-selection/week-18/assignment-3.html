📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
The Origin of Life
Reading 18.03 and Lesson 18.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 18.03 and work through Lesson 18.03, including the Miller–Urey experiment, the RNA-world hypothesis, protocells, and Pasteur’s disproof of spontaneous generation.
Lesson focus: reasoning carefully about what an experiment shows — and what remains open
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This is a reasoning assignment, not a recall one — there is no single neat answer. You will explain what the Miller–Urey experiment did and did not show, and distinguish the disproved idea of spontaneous generation from the open origin-of-life question. It should take about 20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
When does an experiment’s result get overstated — and how do you say clearly both what it proved and what it left unanswered?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — What Miller–Urey did and did not show (5 pts). In three to five sentences, describe what the Miller–Urey experiment did demonstrate (that organic building blocks such as amino acids can form from simple gases and energy) and what it did not demonstrate (that a living cell was created). Be specific about the difference.
2
Part B — Two different questions (4 pts). In two to four sentences, explain how “spontaneous generation” (which Pasteur disproved in 1859) differs from the still-open scientific question of how life first arose on the early Earth. Make clear why disproving one does not answer the other.
3
Part C — Intellectual honesty (1 pt). In one sentence, name one thing about the origin of life that scientists honestly do not yet know.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A clearly separates “building blocks can form” (shown) from “life was created” (not shown).
☐
Part B explains spontaneous generation (life arising easily in everyday conditions, disproved) vs. the narrow question of life’s single first origin (open).
☐
Part C names a genuine open question (for example, how building blocks became a self-copying cell).
📊 GRADING RUBRIC
Criteria Proficient Developing Beginning Pts
Did / did not show
5 pts		 Both sides accurate and clearly distinguished. One side weak or partly overstated. Overstates the result or is inaccurate. 5 pts
Two questions distinguished
4 pts		 Clearly separates the disproved idea from the open one. Partial distinction. Conflates the two. 4 pts
Open question named
1 pt		 Names a genuine open question. Vague. Missing or inaccurate. 1 pt
