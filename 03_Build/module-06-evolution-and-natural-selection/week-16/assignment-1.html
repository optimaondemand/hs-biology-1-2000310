Reading the Rocks
Reading 16.01 and Lesson 16.01
Before you begin: finish Reading 16.01 and work through Lesson 16.01, including how fossils form, the law of superposition, index fossils, and transitional fossils.
Lesson focus: The fossil record as evidence of descent with modification across deep time
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: identify the oldest rock layer, match index and transitional fossils to their definitions, and explain in a sentence or two what a transitional fossil shows. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How can layers of rock and the fossils inside them serve as a timeline of how life has changed?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Which layer is oldest? (3 pts). A cliff has four undisturbed rock layers, labeled top to bottom: Layer 1 (top), Layer 2, Layer 3, Layer 4 (bottom). Name the oldest layer, name the youngest, and state the rule (the law of superposition) that lets you decide.
2
Part B — Match the term to its definition (4 pts, 2 each). Match each term to the correct definition.
Term Definition (write the matching letter)
Index fossil 
Transitional fossil 
A. A fossil of a form that carries a mix of features from an older group and a newer one, capturing a major change partway through.
B. A fossil from a widespread but short-lived species, used to date the rock layer it is found in.
3
Part C — What does it show? (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain what a transitional fossil such as Tiktaalik shows about descent with modification. (Hint: what two groups does it bridge, and what does that tell us about how life changes?)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A names Layer 4 as oldest and Layer 1 as youngest, and states that deeper layers are older (law of superposition).
☐
Part B matches Index fossil → B and Transitional fossil → A.
☐
Part C explains that a transitional fossil bridges two groups (e.g., fish and four-legged land animals) and documents change over deep time.
