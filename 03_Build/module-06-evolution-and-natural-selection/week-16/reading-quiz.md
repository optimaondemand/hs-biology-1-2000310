**1. (Reading 16.01 — law of superposition)**
A geologist studies a cliff with four undisturbed rock layers. Which statement is correct about the relative ages of the layers?

- A) The top layer is the oldest because it has been exposed to the surface the longest
- B) All four layers are the same age because they belong to the same cliff face
- C) The bottom layer is the oldest because deeper layers were deposited first **✓**
- D) The middle layers are the oldest because they are the most protected from erosion

*By the law of superposition, in undisturbed rock the deepest layers were laid down first and are therefore the oldest, while layers nearer the surface are younger. This lets the fossil record be read, bottom to top, as a timeline of life.*

**2. (Reading 16.01 — index fossils)**
What makes a species useful as an index fossil for dating rock layers?

- A) It was geographically widespread but existed for only a short span of time **✓**
- B) It survived almost unchanged for hundreds of millions of years across many habitats
- C) It was very large and left big fossils that are easy for collectors to find
- D) It is still alive today, so scientists can compare it directly with living members

*An index fossil comes from a species that was widespread (so it appears in many places) yet short-lived (so it marks a narrow slice of time). Finding it in a layer anywhere pins that layer to the same age — like a postmark stamping a date on the rock. A long-surviving or still-living species would not pin down a narrow age.*

**3. (Reading 16.01 — what a transitional fossil shows)**
*Tiktaalik*, about 375 million years old, had fish scales and gills but also a flat head, a neck, and sturdy fin bones with wrist-like joints. What does such a transitional fossil best demonstrate?

- A) That fish alive today are slowly turning into land animals over time
- B) That all fossils are transitional forms between two living groups
- C) That fish and land animals have no evolutionary relationship at all
- D) That an ancient form can bridge two major groups and document change **✓**

*A transitional fossil carries a mix of features from an older group and a newer one — here, fish and four-legged land animals — capturing a major change partway through. It documents descent with modification across deep time. It does not show a living species transforming into another we see today.*

**4. (Reading 16.02 — homologous structures)**
A human arm, a whale flipper, and a bat wing all contain the same bones in the same arrangement, even though they are used for grasping, swimming, and flying. These are best described as:

- A) Analogous structures, because they perform different jobs
- B) Homologous structures, which point to a common ancestor **✓**
- C) Vestigial structures, because they are leftovers from earlier forms
- D) Identical structures, which carry no evolutionary meaning at all

*Homologous structures share the same underlying plan but may serve different functions. The shared bone arrangement is most simply explained by inheritance from a common ancestor whose limb was later modified for different ways of life — strong evidence of common ancestry.*

**5. (Reading 16.02 — homologous vs. analogous; targets a common misconception)**
A bird's wing and an insect's wing both produce flight but are built from completely different structures. A student concludes that birds and insects must be closely related. Why is this reasoning flawed?

- A) Shared function can evolve independently, so analogous structures do not prove close relationship **✓**
- B) Birds and insects are in fact the same kind of animal, so their wings must have identical bones
- C) Insects do not really fly the way birds do, so the comparison itself is invalid
- D) Only structures used for movement count as evidence of relationship in biology

*The wings are analogous — same function, different structure — and arise through convergent evolution, where unrelated lineages independently solve a similar problem. Shared structure (homology) indicates common ancestry; shared function alone does not. Confusing the two is one of the most common errors in this unit.*

**6. (Reading 16.03 — island biogeography)**
On a remote island far from any continent, biologists find lizard species that exist nowhere else on Earth, yet most closely resemble lizards of the nearest mainland. Under common descent, why is this the expected pattern?

- A) Because the island and the mainland happen to share nearly identical climates and habitats
- B) Because lizards can only survive in warm tropical regions close to the equator on any continent
- C) Because island species were created separately and only look similar by coincidence
- D) Because a few mainland ancestors reached the island and their descendants changed in isolation **✓**

*Island species are often endemic (found nowhere else) yet resemble the nearest mainland's species because a few ancestors arrived from that mainland and then diversified in isolation over generations. This pattern follows from descent from nearby ancestors — it is not explained by climate alone.*

**7. (Reading 16.03 — Darwin & Wallace)**
Which statement accurately describes how the theory of evolution by natural selection came to be published?

- A) Darwin published immediately after his Beagle voyage, and no other naturalist reached the idea
- B) Darwin delayed publishing; Wallace independently reached the same idea, and their work was presented jointly in 1858 **✓**
- C) Wallace published first in 1859, and Darwin rejected the idea of natural selection
- D) Darwin and Wallace were the same person, publishing under two different names at two different times

*Darwin developed the idea from his observations but delayed publishing for roughly two decades. In 1858 Alfred Russel Wallace independently arrived at the same idea; their work was presented jointly at the Linnean Society of London on 1 July 1858, and Darwin published* On the Origin of Species *in 1859. The episode illustrates courage in following evidence and honesty in sharing credit.*

**8. (Reading 16.04 — molecular evidence; targets a common misconception)**
Human and chimpanzee DNA are extremely similar. What does this similarity actually indicate?

- A) Humans evolved directly from modern chimpanzees over millions of years
- B) Humans and chimpanzees are unrelated species with similar DNA by coincidence
- C) Humans and chimpanzees share a relatively recent common ancestor **✓**
- D) Chimpanzees will eventually evolve into humans given enough time

*High sequence similarity means two species share a recent common ancestor — not that one evolved from the other. Humans did not evolve from living chimpanzees; both descend, with modification, from a shared ancestral species. The more similar two species' sequences are, the more recently their lineages split.*

**9. (Reading 16.04 — convergence of independent evidence)**
Why do scientists consider the case for common descent especially strong?

- A) Because independent lines of evidence (fossils, anatomy, biogeography, molecules) all converge **✓**
- B) Because a single exceptionally complete fossil proves it beyond any doubt
- C) Because evolution is aimed at producing better and more advanced organisms over time
- D) Because all scientists simply agree to accept it without ever testing it against new data

*The strength comes from convergence: fossils, comparative anatomy, biogeography, and molecular sequences were each gathered in different ways and could have disagreed, yet they point to the same family tree. When independent lines agree, coincidence is an unlikely explanation. (Note: evolution is not goal-directed or aimed at "improvement.")*
