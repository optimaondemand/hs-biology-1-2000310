Week 16 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build an “Evidence for Evolution” Station Set
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a small spatial exhibit — three or four labeled stations, one for each independent line of evidence for evolution: fossils and stratigraphy, comparative anatomy (homology), molecular evidence, and (optionally) biogeography. Then you will record a walkthrough explaining how these separate kinds of evidence converge on the same conclusion: common descent. Building the evidence as a space you can walk makes the argument concrete — and this exhibit doubles as the planning space for your Module 6 CER. This is Lessons 16.01–16.04, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 16.01–16.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Natural History Gallery (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty exhibit floor marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Build Station 1 — Fossils & Stratigraphy. Stack labeled rock layers (deeper = older) and place a transitional fossil such as Tiktaalik in a layer. Add a sticky note: “The fossil record shows change over deep time.”
2
Build Station 2 — Comparative Anatomy. Place models of a human arm, a whale flipper, and a bat wing side by side, with the matching bones color-coded. Add a sticky note: “Homologous structures — same plan, common ancestry.”
3
Build Station 3 — Molecular Evidence. Post a simple sequence-similarity chart (e.g., human–chimp very similar, human–frog less so). Add a sticky note: “More similar sequence = more recent common ancestor.” (Optionally add Station 4 — Biogeography: an island near a mainland with similar species.)
4
Connect the stations. Draw or place arrows from all stations pointing to a central sign you label “Common Descent.” This visual is the heart of your argument: independent lines converging on one conclusion.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. Walk the exhibit and explain aloud, in about 60–90 seconds, what each station shows and why having several independent lines of evidence agree makes the case for common descent strong. Use the words homologous, transitional fossil, and common descent.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one snapshot of your full exhibit (all stations and the central “Common Descent” sign) plus a few typed sentences naming each line of evidence and explaining why their convergence is convincing.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the four independent lines of evidence as labeled stations and then articulate why convergence is powerful. This build is deliberately the same architecture as the Module 6 CER (claim → three independent pieces of evidence → reasoning), so encourage students to keep their notes. Common sticking points: treating analogous structures as homologous at the anatomy station, and stating “humans evolved from chimps” at the molecular station (correct them to “shared common ancestor”). A strong submission shows three or four correctly labeled stations, arrows converging on “Common Descent,” and a recording that uses “homologous,” “transitional fossil,” and “common descent” correctly.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
At least three stations are built and correctly labeled (fossils, anatomy, molecular; biogeography optional).
☐
Arrows from the stations converge on a central “Common Descent” sign.
☐
A spatial recording (or snapshot + written explanation) is submitted explaining how the lines converge.
