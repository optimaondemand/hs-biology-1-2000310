Week 16 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Examine a Hominid Fossil / Skull Sequence
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk a sequence of hominid skulls — Australopithecus → Homo habilis → Homo erectus → Homo sapiens — laid out in order. At each, you will observe the trends: changing cranial capacity (braincase size) and changes in the jaw and brow. These fossils are transitional evidence in our own family tree. One thing to hold clearly: humans and other apes share a common ancestor — humans did not evolve “from” living chimpanzees. Walking the sequence makes the gradual change visible. This is Lesson 16.01’s fossil evidence, observed in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 16.01 and Lesson 16.01 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Hall of Human Origins — Skull Sequence (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the four skull markers. Begin at the Australopithecus station.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Station 1 — Australopithecus. Observe the relatively small braincase and the larger jaw and brow. Place a sticky note estimating its cranial capacity as “smallest” in the sequence.
2
Station 2 — Homo habilis. Compare to Station 1: note the somewhat larger braincase. Place a sticky note: what changed from the previous skull?
3
Station 3 — Homo erectus. Observe the still-larger braincase and the heavy brow ridge. Place a sticky note: describe the trend in braincase size so far.
4
Station 4 — Homo sapiens & capture your evidence. Observe the large rounded braincase, the smaller jaw, and the flatter brow. Then take four named snapshots, one at each station, naming each by its correct species so you can use them later.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four named snapshots to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: list the four species in order and write one or two sentences describing the trends you observed in cranial capacity and in the jaw and brow.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students walk a hominid skull sequence and observe trends — increasing cranial capacity and a reducing jaw and brow — as concrete transitional evidence. The most important misconception to correct is “humans evolved from chimpanzees”: emphasize that humans and other living apes share a common ancestor, and that the skulls here are extinct relatives in the human lineage, not living species. A complete submission shows four correctly named snapshots in order and a note that accurately describes the observed trends. This walk gives students a firsthand look at the fossil line of evidence they will use in the Module 6 CER.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All four skulls are visited in order (Australopithecus, H. habilis, H. erectus, H. sapiens) and observed.
☐
A sticky note at each station records an observation about braincase size or jaw/brow shape.
☐
Four correctly named snapshots and the typed note (species in order, trends described) are submitted to Canvas.
