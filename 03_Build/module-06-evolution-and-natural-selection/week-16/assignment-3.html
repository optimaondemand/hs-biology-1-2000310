Reading 16.03 and Lesson 16.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 16.03 and work through Lesson 16.03, including island endemics, the Galápagos finches, and Australia’s marsupials.
Lesson focus: How the geographic distribution of species is evidence of descent from nearby ancestors
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two short reasoning parts about how geography becomes evidence for common descent. It should take about 15 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why should the unique animals of a remote island look more like the nearest mainland’s species than like animals from a faraway continent with the same climate?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Island resemblance (6 pts). In three or four sentences, explain why island species tend to resemble the species of the nearest mainland under common descent. Be sure to mention (1) that a few ancestors reached the island from the nearby mainland, and (2) that their descendants then changed over generations in isolation.
2
Part B — The finch puzzle (4 pts). The Galápagos hold more than a dozen finch species found nowhere else, differing mainly in beak shape, yet all resembling one mainland finch. In one or two sentences, explain what this pattern suggests about where these finches came from and how so many kinds arose.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A explains arrival of nearby-mainland ancestors AND change in isolation as the reason for resemblance.
☐
Part B states the finches descend from a mainland ancestor and diversified to use different island foods.
