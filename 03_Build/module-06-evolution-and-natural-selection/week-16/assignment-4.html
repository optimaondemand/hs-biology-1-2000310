Lesson focus: Reading molecular evidence — and why converging lines of evidence are powerful
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
You will read a small table of protein sequence similarity, rank which species are most closely related to humans, justify your ranking, and explain why molecular and fossil evidence agreeing strengthens the conclusion. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If two species’ proteins are nearly identical, what does that tell you about when they last shared a common ancestor?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
The table below shows how similar each species’ version of a protein (hemoglobin) is to the human version. A higher percentage means a more similar sequence.
Species (compared with human) Protein sequence similarity to human
Chimpanzee100%
Mouse87%
Chicken69%
Frog54%
Values are simplified for this exercise to show the pattern, not exact published figures.
1
Part A — Rank the relatives (4 pts). List the four species in order from most closely related to humans to least closely related.
2
Part B — Justify it (4 pts). In two or three sentences, explain the rule you used: why does higher sequence similarity mean a more recent common ancestor? (Important: the high human–chimp similarity means we share a recent common ancestor — not that humans evolved from chimpanzees.)
3
Part C — Why convergence matters (2 pts). In one sentence, explain why having molecular evidence agree with fossil and anatomical evidence makes the conclusion of common descent stronger.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A ranks: chimpanzee, mouse, chicken, frog (most to least related).
☐
Part B states that more similar sequences mean fewer accumulated changes, so a more recent shared ancestor — and avoids the “humans from chimps” error.
☐
Part C explains that independent lines of evidence agreeing makes coincidence unlikely and the conclusion more trustworthy.
