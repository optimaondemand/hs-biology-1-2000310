**1. (Reading 17.01 — four conditions of natural selection)**
Which set of conditions must be present for natural selection to occur in a population?

- A) Identical individuals, no inheritance, and unlimited resources available for every offspring that is born
- B) Large body size, great physical strength, and a long lifespan in each individual
- C) A goal the species is working toward and an environment that never changes at all
- D) Heritable variation, overproduction of offspring, and differential reproductive success **✓**

*Natural selection requires heritable variation, overproduction of offspring (a struggle for existence), and differential reproductive success — the better-fitted individuals leave more offspring, so favorable heritable traits become more common. Evolution has no goal, and strength alone is not a requirement.*

**2. (Reading 17.01 — fitness misconception; targets a common misconception)**
In biology, what does it mean to say one organism has higher "fitness" than another?

- A) It leaves more surviving, reproducing offspring in its environment **✓**
- B) It can run faster or fight better than competitors in its habitat
- C) It is physically stronger and larger than other members of its group
- D) It lives the longest, whether or not it ever reproduces

*Biological fitness means reproductive success in a particular environment — the number of surviving offspring left behind — not strength, size, or speed. A small, well-camouflaged animal that raises many young is fitter than a large, strong one that is eaten before it breeds. "Survival of the fittest" does not mean "survival of the strongest."*

**3. (Reading 17.01 — populations evolve, not individuals; classic beetle item)**
A population of beetles lives on dark soil. The beetles range from pale tan to dark brown, color is inherited, and birds hunt them by sight. After many generations, which prediction is correct?

- A) Each individual beetle gradually turns dark brown to hide from the hunting birds
- B) The beetles sense the danger and decide to become darker to avoid being eaten
- C) The population becomes mostly dark brown because dark beetles reproduce more **✓**
- D) Nothing changes, because beetle color cannot affect survival or reproduction at all

*Individuals do not change their own color; populations evolve. Pale beetles are spotted and eaten more, so they leave fewer offspring. Because color is heritable, the proportion of dark-brown beetles rises across generations. Beetles cannot "decide" or "try" to change.*

**4. (Reading 17.02 — directional selection / peppered moth)**
As soot from industry darkened tree bark in Britain, dark peppered moths became far more common than light ones, then declined again after clean-air laws reduced the soot. This is an example of which type of selection?

- A) Stabilizing selection, which favors the average form of the trait
- B) Directional selection, which pushes the trait toward one extreme **✓**
- C) Disruptive selection, which favors both extremes over the middle
- D) Artificial selection, which relies on humans choosing who breeds

*When one extreme of a trait is favored and the population shifts that way, it is directional selection. The peppered moth (industrial melanism) is the classic case: dark moths were favored on soot-darkened bark, then light moths rebounded when the bark lightened. The environment changed, and the population tracked it.*

**5. (Reading 17.02 — stabilizing vs. disruptive selection)**
In a bird population, both small-beaked and large-beaked birds survive and reproduce well, but medium-beaked birds do poorly because only small soft seeds and large hard seeds are available. Which type of selection is this?

- A) Stabilizing selection, because the average form is preserved in the population
- B) Directional selection, because the population shifts toward one extreme
- C) Disruptive selection, because both extremes are favored over the middle **✓**
- D) Genetic drift, because the change is random and unrelated to fitness

*Disruptive selection favors both extremes over the intermediate, which can split a population into two specialized forms. Stabilizing selection would favor the middle; directional would shift toward one extreme; drift is random change unrelated to fitness.*

**6. (Reading 17.02 — artificial selection)**
Modern corn was bred over thousands of years from a wild grass called teosinte, and all dog breeds descend from wolves. How does this artificial selection compare with natural selection?

- A) It uses the same mechanism, but humans rather than the environment choose who breeds **✓**
- B) It works through a completely different mechanism than natural selection uses
- C) It does not involve heredity at all, since breeders only change the environment
- D) It shows that organisms can change their own traits on purpose within one lifetime

*Artificial selection uses the same engine as natural selection: heritable variation plus differential reproduction. The only difference is the selector — humans choose who breeds instead of the environment. Darwin used selective breeding as evidence that selection could reshape species.*

**7. (Reading 17.03 — founder vs. bottleneck effect)**
A disease sweeps through a large population of lizards, killing all but a dozen survivors whose allele frequencies do not match the original population. The survivors rebuild the population. This is an example of:

- A) The founder effect, in which a few individuals start a new population
- B) Directional selection, in which one extreme of a trait is favored
- C) Gene flow, in which individuals migrate between populations
- D) The bottleneck effect, in which a sudden crash leaves few survivors **✓**

*A bottleneck effect is a sudden crash (disease, disaster, over-hunting) that leaves a small, non-representative set of survivors, drastically reducing genetic diversity — as seen in cheetahs and northern elephant seals. A founder effect instead involves a few individuals leaving to start a new population. Both are forms of genetic drift.*

**8. (Reading 17.03 — drift vs. selection; gene flow)**
Which statement correctly distinguishes the mechanisms of evolution?

- A) Genetic drift occurs only in very large populations, where chance matters most
- B) Genetic drift is random change in allele frequencies, but selection is non-random **✓**
- C) Gene flow between populations makes them more different from each other
- D) Mutation can only remove existing alleles and can never create new ones

*Drift is random — it changes allele frequencies by chance, with the largest effect in small populations — while selection is non-random because it favors traits that improve reproductive success. Gene flow (migration between populations) makes populations more similar, and mutation is the ultimate source of new alleles.*

**9. (Reading 17.04 — antibiotic resistance; targets a common misconception)**
A bacterial population is exposed to an antibiotic and becomes resistant over several generations. Which explanation is correct?

- A) A few cells were already resistant by random mutation, and they survived and reproduced **✓**
- B) The bacteria gradually tried harder to become resistant until they finally succeeded in surviving
- C) The antibiotic triggered new resistance mutations in each cell it touched
- D) The antibiotic taught the bacteria how to survive its own effects

*Antibiotic resistance is natural selection. Resistant variants arise by random mutation before the drug is applied; the antibiotic does not teach, and bacteria do not "try." The drug acts as a selecting environment — it kills susceptible cells, and the pre-existing resistant cells survive and reproduce, so the population becomes resistant.*
