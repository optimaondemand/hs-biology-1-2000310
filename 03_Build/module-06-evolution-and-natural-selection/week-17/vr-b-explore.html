Week 17 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Observe an Antibiotic-Resistance Population Shift
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will enter a scene showing a bacterial population growing on a culture plate. A few cells are already resistant (marked) and most are susceptible. You will add an antibiotic and watch what happens: the susceptible cells die, while the pre-existing resistant cells survive and repopulate the plate. By observing — not changing anything yourself — you will see natural selection happen in real time. The key idea to carry out of this scene: the drug selects cells that were already resistant; it does not create the resistance. This is Lesson 17.04, walked in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 17.04 and Lesson 17.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Microbiology Bay — Resistance on a Plate (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your observer checklist. Begin at the culture plate before any antibiotic is added.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Observe the starting plate. Look closely at the bacterial population before any drug. Most cells are susceptible; a few are already marked as resistant. Place a sticky note: about how many resistant cells are present, and did they appear because of any drug? (No — they arose earlier, by random mutation.)
2
Capture the “before” snapshot. Take a snapshot of the full plate and name it “Before antibiotic.”
3
Add the antibiotic and watch. Use the dropper to apply the antibiotic to the plate. Observe the susceptible cells die off while the resistant cells survive untouched. Place a sticky note: which cells died, and which survived — and why?
4
Let the survivors repopulate. Advance the scene so the surviving resistant cells reproduce and refill the plate. Notice the population is now almost entirely resistant. Capture an “After” snapshot of the repopulated plate.
5
Write the takeaway. On the Drawpad, write one sentence answering: did the antibiotic create resistance or select it? Be ready to defend your answer with what you observed before the drug was added.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your two named snapshots (“Before antibiotic” and “After”) to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: explain in two or three sentences why the population became resistant, making clear the resistant cells existed before the drug and that the drug selected rather than created them.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students observe selection in a bacterial population: pre-existing resistant cells survive an antibiotic while susceptible cells die, then repopulate the plate. The whole point is to defeat the “the drug taught/caused it” misconception — students should see resistant cells present before any drug is added. Common sticking points: claiming the antibiotic created the resistance, or that the bacteria “tried” to survive. Push students to point to the before-snapshot as evidence that the variants pre-existed. A complete submission shows clearly named before/after snapshots and a note stating the drug selected, not created, resistance.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
The plate is observed before the drug (noting pre-existing resistant cells), during (susceptible cells die), and after (resistant cells repopulate).
☐
Two named snapshots (“Before antibiotic” and “After”) are captured.
☐
The typed note correctly states the drug selected pre-existing resistant cells and did not create resistance.
