Evolution You Can Watch
Reading 17.04 and Lesson 17.04
Before you begin: finish Reading 17.04 and work through Lesson 17.04, including the four conditions in bacteria and the misconception that bacteria “try” or are “taught” to resist.
Lesson focus: antibiotic resistance as natural selection — the drug selects, it does not teach
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has two parts: explain step by step why exposing bacteria to an antibiotic produces a resistant population — naming variation, selection, and reproduction — and correct a “the antibiotic taught them” statement. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does dosing bacteria with an antibiotic leave behind a resistant population — and why is it wrong to say the drug “taught” them to resist?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Explain the mechanism (7 pts). In a short paragraph (4–6 sentences), explain step by step how a bacterial population becomes mostly resistant after antibiotic treatment. Your answer must name and use these three words correctly: variation (resistant cells already exist by mutation), selection (the drug kills susceptible cells), and reproduction (survivors multiply and pass on resistance).
2
Part B — Correct the statement (3 pts). A news headline reads: “Antibiotics teach bacteria to fight back.” In two or three sentences, explain what is wrong with this and rewrite it accurately. Make clear that the resistance variants pre-existed and that the drug selects rather than causes resistance.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A walks through variation → selection → reproduction in the correct order, using all three terms accurately.
☐
Part A makes clear resistant cells existed before the drug (the drug did not create them).
☐
Part B corrects the “teaching” language: bacteria do not try/learn; the drug selects pre-existing variants.
