Chance and Migration
Reading 17.03 and Lesson 17.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 17.03 and work through Lesson 17.03, including genetic drift, the founder and bottleneck effects, gene flow, and mutation.
Lesson focus: drift, gene flow, and mutation — and why chance matters most in small populations
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three parts: identify founder vs. bottleneck effects in scenarios, explain why drift matters more in small populations, and contrast drift with natural selection. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
When a population changes by pure chance rather than by fitness, what happened — and why does the size of the population matter so much?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Founder or bottleneck? (6 pts, 2 each). For each scenario, identify founder effect or bottleneck effect and give a one-line reason.
Scenario Effect & reason
A storm blows a few birds from the mainland to a distant island, where they start a new population whose allele frequencies differ from the mainland flock. 
Northern elephant seals were hunted down to a few dozen animals before recovering; today the species has strikingly low genetic diversity. 
A disease sweeps a large insect population, leaving only a small, non-representative group of survivors that rebuilds the population. 
2
Part B — Why size matters (2 pts). In two or three sentences, explain why genetic drift has a much bigger effect in a small population than in a large one. (A coin-flip comparison is welcome.)
3
Part C — Drift vs. selection (2 pts). In one or two sentences, state the key difference between genetic drift and natural selection. (Hint: which one is random, and which tracks fitness?)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: birds-to-island = founder; elephant seals = bottleneck; disease survivors = bottleneck — each with a correct reason.
☐
Part B explains that chance pulls small samples away from the average far more than large ones.
☐
Part C states drift is random while selection is non-random (tracks fitness).
