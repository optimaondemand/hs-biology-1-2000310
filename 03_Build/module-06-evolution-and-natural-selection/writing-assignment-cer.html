📖 WHAT THIS IS
This is the writing that pulls Module 6 together. Over three weeks you studied the evidence for evolution (the fossil record, comparative anatomy, biogeography, and molecular sequences), the mechanisms that drive it (natural selection, genetic drift, gene flow, mutation), and how populations split into new species. Now you will do what Darwin did: gather independent observations and build them into a single argument. Your task is to choose at least three independent lines of evidence and weave them into one reasoned case for common descent — the idea that today’s species are descended, with modification, from shared ancestors. It is assigned at the start of Week 16 and due at the end of Week 18, so the whole module can inform your argument. Your teacher will read and score this one personally — it is not auto-graded.
🧬 THIS IS AN OPEN-INQUIRY CER
Unlike the more guided essays earlier in the year, this one leaves the construction to you. You choose which three (or more) lines of evidence to argue from, you decide the order, and you build the reasoning that ties them into one conclusion. The evidence bank below is a memory aid, not a script — a strong essay selects from it and explains, in its own words, why the evidence points where it does. There is no single “correct” set of three; there is only an argument that is well-reasoned or one that is not.
🎯 YOUR TASK
Evaluate at least three independent lines of evidence for evolution and construct a single, unified argument for common descent — explaining not only what each line shows, but why several independent lines pointing to the same conclusion make the case so strong.
🧬 YOUR EVIDENCE BANK (CHOOSE AT LEAST THREE)
These are the independent lines you studied this module. Each is independent — gathered by different scientists, using different methods, on different kinds of data. Choose at least three to argue from.
Line of evidence What it shows · example to cite
Fossil recordDeeper rock layers hold older life (superposition); transitional fossils show intermediate forms — e.g., Tiktaalik (fish→tetrapod, ~375 mya) or whale ancestors with hind limbs.
Comparative anatomyHomologous structures — the same forelimb bones in human arm, whale flipper, and bat wing — point to a shared ancestor; vestigial structures (e.g., whale pelvic bones) are remnants of that ancestry.
BiogeographyIsland species most resemble the nearest mainland species (e.g., the Galápagos finches); distributions make sense if species descend from ancestors that lived nearby.
Molecular evidenceAll life shares DNA and a nearly universal genetic code; the more similar two species’ DNA/protein sequences, the more recently they shared an ancestor (links to Modules 2 & 5).
Observed changeEvolution watched in real time — antibiotic resistance in bacteria, peppered-moth color shifts — shows the mechanism (selection on heritable variation) actually works.
A note on the words: common descent is the claim that living species share ancestors; natural selection is one mechanism that drives the change. Your argument is about the first — use the mechanisms as support, but keep your claim about shared ancestry.
📝 BUILD YOUR ARGUMENT IN FOUR MOVES
Write in connected paragraphs, not a list. The wording is yours — these are the moves a strong CER makes, not a fill-in-the-blank.
1
Claim. State, in one or two sentences, that the species alive today share common ancestors — and that several independent lines of evidence converge to support this. Make it a real claim you intend to defend, not a definition copied from the bank.
2
Evidence. Present your three (or more) chosen lines, each with a specific example — not “fossils show evolution,” but a named transitional fossil, a named homologous structure, a real biogeographic pattern, or a sequence-similarity comparison. Specificity is what separates an argument from an opinion.
3
Reasoning — why the lines converge. This is the heart of the essay. For each line, explain why it points to common descent (for example: why do the same bones, rearranged for different jobs, suggest a shared ancestor rather than coincidence?). Then make the move that matters most: explain why independent lines — collected by different methods, with no reason to agree by chance — arriving at the same conclusion makes that conclusion far stronger than any single line alone. As a check on your reasoning, address one honest counter-consideration: how is a homologous structure (evidence of relatedness) different from an analogous one (e.g., a bird’s and an insect’s wing — similar function, separate origins), and why does that distinction strengthen rather than weaken your case?
4
Integration. Close by connecting the trace to the module’s bigger story. The variation evolution works on comes from meiosis and mutation (Module 5); the relationships your evidence reveals are exactly what the next module, classification (Module 7), organizes into the tree of life. You may also reflect on the module’s virtue, courage: Darwin spent some twenty years gathering evidence and anticipating objections before he published — the same patient, evidence-first habit your essay is practicing now.
🧡 THE VIRTUE THIS PRACTICES · COURAGE
This module’s virtue is courage — the courage to follow evidence wherever it leads, even into controversy. Darwin did not rush; he assembled independent lines of evidence over two decades and answered objections before his critics could raise them. Your CER honors that habit by doing the same on a smaller scale: choosing your evidence deliberately, reasoning honestly about what it does and does not show, and facing the hardest distinction (homologous versus analogous) rather than stepping around it. Building a careful argument from converging evidence — and being willing to defend it — is itself a form of intellectual courage.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
A clear claim argues that today’s species share common ancestors, supported by converging evidence.
☐
At least three independent lines of evidence are presented, each with a specific named example.
☐
Reasoning explains why each line points to common descent and why independent lines converging is powerful; the homologous-vs-analogous distinction is addressed.
☐
The essay reads as one connected argument and ties to variation (Module 5) and/or classification (Module 7) — (~400–700 words).
📊 CER SCORING RUBRIC (3 PTS × 4 CRITERIA = 12 PTS)
Criterion Strong (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1)
Claim Specific and arguable; asserts common descent supported by converging evidence. Somewhat vague, or states evolution without committing to common descent. Missing, or just restates the prompt.
Evidence Three or more independent lines, each with a specific named example. Three lines named but general, or fewer than three, or examples thin. One line, or evidence described inaccurately.
Reasoning Explains why each line supports common descent AND why convergence of independent lines is powerful; handles homologous vs. analogous. Links evidence to the claim but with gaps; convergence or the homology distinction underdeveloped. Lists evidence without explaining why it supports the claim.
Integration Reads as one coherent argument and ties to variation (M5) and/or classification (M7); within the word range. Components present but loosely connected. Listed independently without connection.
Total possible: 12 pts
