📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Biomes & Climatographs
Reading 30.04 and Lesson 30.04
Before you begin: finish Reading 30.04 and work through Lesson 30.04, covering the major biomes, how temperature and precipitation determine a biome, and how to read a climatograph.
Lesson focus: Climate draws the map of life
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match biomes to their climates, read two described climatographs and name the biome each shows, and explain in a sentence or two why climate determines biome. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Can two numbers — how warm and how wet — really tell you what kind of life a place holds?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match biome to climate (5 pts, 1 each). Match each biome to its climate description from the list.

2
Part B — Read the climatographs (3 pts). Each description below is a climatograph in words. Name the biome each one describes.
Climatograph 1: The temperature line is high and nearly flat all year (about 27°C every month). The precipitation bars are tall in every month, well over 200 mm. → Biome? (1.5 pts)
Climatograph 2: The temperature line is warm most of the year, but the precipitation bars are tiny (near zero) in almost every month. → Biome? (1.5 pts)
3
Part C — Why climate rules (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why an ecologist can identify a biome knowing only its temperature and precipitation.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: tropical rainforest → A, desert → B, tundra → C, temperate forest → D, taiga → E.
☐
Part B: Climatograph 1 = tropical rainforest (hot + wet all year); Climatograph 2 = desert (warm + almost no precipitation).
☐
Part C explains that temperature and precipitation decide which plants can grow (they set what photosynthesis and survival allow), and the plants determine the whole community — so climate determines the biome.
