**1. (Reading 30.01 — habitat vs. niche; targets the most common mix-up)**
A student writes, "A bird's habitat and its niche are two words for the same thing." Why is this incorrect?

- A) Habitat and niche both describe only what an animal eats, since feeding is the main way a species is defined
- B) Habitat is the organism's full role and way of life, while niche is simply the physical place where it lives
- C) Only plants have a habitat, because they stay in one place, while only animals have a niche, because they move around
- D) Habitat is the place where the organism lives, while niche is its full role: what it eats, when it is active, and how it interacts **✓**

*Habitat is an organism's "address" — the physical place it lives. Niche is its "profession" — its complete role: what it eats, when it is active, where it nests, and how it affects other species. Two species can share a habitat while occupying very different niches, so the two terms are not the same.*

**2. (Reading 30.01 — levels of organization; targets correct ordering)**
Which sequence lists the ecological levels of organization correctly from **smallest to largest**?

- A) Organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome → biosphere **✓**
- B) Organism → community → population → biome → ecosystem → biosphere
- C) Population → organism → ecosystem → community → biosphere → biome
- D) Biosphere → biome → ecosystem → community → population → organism

*The ecological hierarchy runs from a single organism, to a population (one species in an area), to a community (all populations together), to an ecosystem (that community plus its nonliving surroundings), to a biome (a large climate region), to the biosphere (all life on Earth).*

**3. (Reading 30.01 — abiotic factors are nonliving; targets the biotic/abiotic split)**
Which list contains **only abiotic** factors of a pond ecosystem?

- A) Fish, algae, water temperature, and sunlight
- B) Sunlight, water temperature, salinity, and pH **✓**
- C) A decaying leaf, sunlight, pH, and a frog
- D) Dissolved oxygen, bacteria, and a lily pad

*Abiotic factors are the nonliving physical and chemical parts of the environment — sunlight, temperature, water, soil, pH, and salinity. Fish, algae, frogs, bacteria, lily pads, and even a once-living decaying leaf are biotic. Only choice B lists three nonliving factors.*

**4. (Reading 30.02 — distinguishing symbioses by who benefits; mutualism)**
Inside coral tissue, algae called zooxanthellae make sugars the coral uses, and the coral gives the algae shelter. Both partners benefit. What is this relationship?

- A) Commensalism (+ / 0)
- B) Parasitism (+ / −)
- C) Mutualism (+ / +) **✓**
- D) Predation (+ / −)

*When both species benefit, the relationship is mutualism (+ / +). The coral gains sugars from the algae's photosynthesis, and the algae gain shelter and access to light. This partnership is the foundation of the coral reef.*

**5. (Reading 30.02 — commensalism vs. parasitism; targets the +/0 vs +/− confusion)**
A barnacle attaches to a whale's skin and is carried to richer feeding waters, while the whale is essentially unaffected. This is best classified as:

- A) Commensalism, because one benefits and the other is unaffected **✓**
- B) Parasitism, because the barnacle lives on the whale and feeds there
- C) Mutualism, because the two organisms live together in close contact
- D) Competition, because they share the same ocean habitat

*Commensalism is a +/0 relationship: one species benefits (the barnacle gets transport and food access) while the other is neither helped nor harmed (the whale). It is not parasitism, because the whale is not harmed, and not mutualism, because the whale gains nothing.*

**6. (Reading 30.02 — predation vs. competition; targets who is helped/harmed)**
Two hawks hunt the same field of mice, and neither can catch as many as it would alone. Which interaction is this, and what is its effect on the two hawks?

- A) Predation; one hawk is helped and the other is harmed
- B) Mutualism; both hawks are helped by sharing the field (+ / +)
- C) Commensalism; one hawk is helped and the other unaffected
- D) Competition; both hawks lose out on the shared prey (− / −) **✓**

*Competition occurs when organisms need the same limited resource — here, the mice. Both competitors are harmed (− / −), because each would do better without the other. Predation would describe a hawk eating a mouse (+ / −), not two hawks competing.*

**7. (Reading 30.03 — Florida ecosystems; keystone species and fire)**
In Florida's longleaf pine savanna, the gopher tortoise digs long burrows that shelter hundreds of other animal species. Because its impact on the community is far greater than its numbers, the gopher tortoise is best described as:

- A) A primary producer that captures energy for the community
- B) A keystone species with outsized effects on its community **✓**
- C) A parasite that feeds on the pine trees' tissues
- D) An abiotic factor that shapes the soil and habitat

*A keystone species has an effect on its community out of proportion to its abundance. The gopher tortoise's burrows shelter many other species (especially during the fires this ecosystem depends on), so losing it would unravel much of the community — the definition of a keystone species.*

**8. (Reading 30.04 — climate determines biome; targets the two-factor idea)**
An ecologist can often identify a biome knowing only two measurements about a place. Which two?

- A) Its latitude and its elevation above sea level
- B) Its soil color and its average daily wind speed
- C) Its average temperature and its annual precipitation **✓**
- D) Its number of animal species and plant species

*A biome is defined by its climate, and climate comes down mainly to two abiotic factors: average temperature and annual precipitation. Because plants (and the animals that depend on them) grow only where warmth and water allow, those two numbers largely determine which biome forms.*

**9. (Reading 30.04 — reading a climatograph)**
A climatograph shows a temperature line that stays high and nearly flat all year and precipitation bars that are tall in every month. Which biome does it describe?

- A) Subtropical desert
- B) Treeless arctic tundra
- C) Cold northern taiga
- D) Tropical rainforest **✓**

*On a climatograph, temperature is the line and precipitation the bars. A consistently hot, flat temperature line with heavy rain every month (no dry season) is the signature of a tropical rainforest. A desert would show tiny bars; tundra and taiga would show a temperature line that drops steeply in winter.*
