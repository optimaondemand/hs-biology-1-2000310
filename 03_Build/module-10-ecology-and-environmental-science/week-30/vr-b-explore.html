Week 30 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Explore Everglades vs. Coral-Reef vs. Mangrove Biomes
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will tour three of Florida's signature ecosystems in turn — the Everglades, a Florida Keys coral reef, and a coastal mangrove shore — and observe what makes each one distinct. In each scene you will capture a labeled snapshot and note two things: the key abiotic factor that defines the place and one characteristic organism that lives there. The one big idea to carry away: each ecosystem is organized around a different defining factor — a freshwater cycle, a warm sunlit sea, and salt — exactly as Lesson 30.03 described. This is Lesson 30.03, observed in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 30.03 and Lesson 30.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Florida Biomes Tour — Everglades / Coral Reef / Mangrove (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the three scenes. Begin at the Everglades marker.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Everglades. Walk the shallow “river of grass.” Find the sawgrass, an alligator, and a wading bird. Take a snapshot named “Everglades.” Note its defining abiotic factor (the seasonal wet/dry water cycle) and one characteristic organism.
2
Coral reef. Descend onto the Florida Keys reef. Find living coral and the fish it shelters. Take a snapshot named “Coral Reef.” Note its defining abiotic factor (warm, clear, sunlit seawater) and one characteristic organism (for example, the coral polyp and its zooxanthellae).
3
Mangrove. Wade into the mangrove fringe. Find the arching prop roots and the young fish sheltering among them. Take a snapshot named “Mangrove.” Note its defining abiotic factor (high salinity) and one characteristic organism (for example, the red mangrove).
4
The big idea. At the summary marker, answer in a sentence: how does the defining abiotic factor differ across the three scenes? (Hint: freshwater cycle vs. warm salt sea vs. high salinity — each ecosystem is organized around a different factor.)
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots (Everglades, Coral Reef, Mangrove) to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: for each snapshot, give the defining abiotic factor and one characteristic organism, and write one sentence comparing how the defining factor differs across the three.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: The point of this tour is Lesson 30.03's big idea — that each Florida ecosystem is organized around a different defining abiotic factor. Encourage students to name that factor before listing organisms, so the observation drives the identification rather than the reverse. A common error is treating all three as “just wetlands”; press students to contrast freshwater (Everglades) with salt sea (reef) and salt-tolerant shore (mangrove). A complete submission shows three correctly named snapshots, each with its defining abiotic factor and one characteristic organism, plus a comparison sentence.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All three scenes are visited in order (Everglades, coral reef, mangrove).
☐
Three named snapshots are captured, each noting the defining abiotic factor and one characteristic organism.
☐
The typed note correctly compares how the defining abiotic factor differs across the three ecosystems.
