The Map of Life: Biomes, Climate, and the Climatograph
Lesson · Terrestrial & Aquatic Biomes, Climate as Determinant, Reading Climatographs
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Give a scientist just two numbers about a place — how warm and how wet — and they can name what kind of life grows there without ever visiting. How?
Today you zoom out to the whole planet. You will meet the world's major biomes, see how two abiotic factors — temperature and precipitation — decide which biome forms, and learn to read a climatograph, the graph that lets you name a biome from its weather alone.

🌎 PART 1 · TWO NUMBERS DRAW THE MAP
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
A rainforest and a desert can sit at the same latitude under the same strong sun. Before reading on, what do you think makes one lush and the other bare?

Biomes and the climate that makes them

A biome is a large region defined by its climate and the characteristic community that climate produces. Climate comes down to two abiotic factors: average temperature and annual precipitation. The major land biomes run from tropical rainforest (hot and wet) through grassland and temperate forest to desert (dry), taiga (cold), and tundra (frigid and dry). Aquatic biomes split mainly by salinity into freshwater and marine. The deep idea: climate determines biome — which is why the same biome type reappears wherever the temperature-and-precipitation recipe repeats, and why the food webs of every biome ultimately trace back to photosynthesis (Module 4), which needs both warmth and water.

📊 PART 2 · READING A CLIMATOGRAPH

One graph, two data series

A climatograph shows a place's climate month by month: temperature as a line and precipitation as bars, over the twelve months. To name the biome, ask how warm (read the line — high and flat = tropical; a deep winter dip = cold biome) and how wet, and when (read the bars — tall all year = rainforest; tiny all year = desert; tall in one season only = wet/dry grassland or savanna).

Climatograph interpreter. Each row describes what a climatograph shows. Read the temperature line and precipitation bars, then pick the biome.
✅ NAME THE BIOME FROM ITS CLIMATOGRAPH
Temperature line is high and nearly flat all year (~26°C); precipitation bars are tall every single month.
Temperature line is warm; precipitation bars are tiny (near zero) in almost every month.

💪 THE IDEA THIS BUILDS

Two numbers — temperature and precipitation — draw the entire map of life, from Florida's wetlands to the Arctic tundra, and a climatograph lets you read that map from a single graph. This is also why a changing climate is a biological question, not just a weather one: shift the two numbers and you shift where life can live. That is the thread the rest of this module follows — energy flow, human impact, and stewardship — as the course's long argument, from atoms all the way to ecosystems, comes to rest on the living Earth we are responsible for.
