📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Yellowstone Wolves Case Study
Reading 31.04 and Lesson 31.04
Before you begin: finish Reading 31.04 and work through Lesson 31.04, including the removal and 1995 return of the wolves, the cascade that followed, and what scientists still debate.
Lesson focus: A keystone predator, a real cascade, told honestly
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: put the Yellowstone cascade in order, explain why the wolf is a keystone species, and state one open question scientists still debate. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How does one returning predator ripple all the way down to willows, beavers, and streambanks — and how do we describe that without overstating it?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Order the cascade (5 pts, 1 each). Number these events 1–5 in the order they occurred after the wolves returned in 1995.
Event Order (1–5)
Beavers, songbirds increase; streambanks stabilize 
Wolves are reintroduced to Yellowstone 
Willow, aspen, and cottonwood recover in places 
Elk numbers fall and elk avoid exposed streambanks 
Young streambank trees are browsed less 
2
Part B — Why a keystone (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why the gray wolf is called a keystone species in Yellowstone.
3
Part C — Stay honest (3 pts). In two or three sentences, state one thing scientists are still debating about the Yellowstone cascade, and explain why a careful scientist would avoid the claim that “wolves changed the course of the rivers.”
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A order: (1) wolves reintroduced; (2) elk numbers fall / elk avoid streambanks; (3) young trees browsed less; (4) willow, aspen, cottonwood recover; (5) beavers, songbirds increase and streambanks stabilize.
☐
Part B explains the wolf's effect on the whole community is disproportionately large relative to its abundance — its presence or absence reshapes elk, plants, and the species beyond them.
☐
Part C names a real open question (e.g., how much recovery is due to wolves vs. climate, drought, other predators; uneven results) and explains that the “rivers” claim overstates what the evidence firmly shows.
