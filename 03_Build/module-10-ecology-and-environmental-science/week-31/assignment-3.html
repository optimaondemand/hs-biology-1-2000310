Before you begin: finish Reading 31.03 and work through Lesson 31.03, including biomagnification, keystone species, and trophic cascades.
Lesson focus: Toxins concentrate upward; one keystone predator can hold a whole web
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: order a toxin's concentration up a food chain, define a keystone species in your own words, and trace a short trophic cascade. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why does a poison end up worst at the top of a food web, and how can removing one predator change an entire community?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Rank the toxin (5 pts). In the chain algae → small fish → large fish → osprey, a persistent toxin (mercury) is present. Rank the four organisms from lowest to highest toxin concentration (4 pts), then name the process in one word (1 pt).

Part B — Define a keystone (2 pts). In one sentence, define a keystone species in your own words, and name one example (such as sea otters or the sea star Pisaster).
3
Part C — Trace a cascade (3 pts). Sea otters are removed from a kelp forest. In two or three sentences, trace the trophic cascade that follows (think: urchins, kelp, and the species that depend on kelp).
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: 1 = algae, 2 = small fish, 3 = large fish, 4 = osprey (concentration rises up the levels); the process is biomagnification.
☐
Part B defines a keystone species as one whose effect on its community is disproportionately large relative to its abundance, with a valid example.
☐
Part C traces: otters removed → sea urchins increase → urchins graze kelp down to bare rock → species that depend on the kelp forest decline.
