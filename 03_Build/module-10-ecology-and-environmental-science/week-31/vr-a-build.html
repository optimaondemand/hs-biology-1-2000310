Week 31 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Construct a Food Web & Energy Pyramid (10% Rule)
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a food web from a set of species, then stack an energy pyramid on top of it that shows the ~90% loss of energy between each level. Finally you will record a short walkthrough explaining why the pyramid narrows toward the top and why energy is never recycled. Building the web and the pyramid as objects you can walk around makes the abstract 10% rule physical — you can literally see the energy shrinking as you climb. This is Lessons 31.01–31.02, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Readings 31.01–31.02 and Lessons 31.01–31.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Ecosystem Modeling Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty web-building frame marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — sort the species by role before you connect them.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Sort the species. From the species tray (grass, shrubs, grasshopper, rabbit, mouse, frog, snake, hawk, fox, plus fungi and bacteria), place each on the correct shelf: producers, primary consumers, secondary/tertiary consumers, and decomposers.
2
Draw the web. Connect the species with energy arrows pointing from the eaten to the eater. Give at least two consumers more than one food source, so your diagram is a true food web, not a single chain.
3
Stack the pyramid. Beside the web, build an energy pyramid from scaled blocks. Start the producer level at 10,000 units and size each level above it at 10% of the one below: 10,000 → 1,000 → 100 → 10. Label each block with its trophic level and its energy value.
4
Mark the loss. Between each pair of levels, place a heat-loss arrow pointing out of the pyramid, labeled “~90% lost as heat and life processes.” This shows where the energy goes — and that it does not come back.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 60–90 seconds, walk your web into your pyramid and explain why the pyramid narrows (only ~10% passes up) and why energy is not recycled (it flows one way and is lost as heat, while matter cycles).
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one full snapshot of your food web and a full snapshot of your labeled energy pyramid (all four levels and the heat-loss arrows visible), plus two typed sentences: one on why the pyramid narrows, one on why energy is not recycled.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the 10% rule by scaling the pyramid physically. Two common errors to watch for and correct: building a single chain instead of a web (require at least one consumer with two food sources), and stating that “energy is recycled” (it is not — energy flows one way and is lost as heat; only matter cycles). A strong submission shows an interconnected web, a correctly scaled 10,000→1,000→100→10 pyramid with heat-loss arrows, and a walkthrough that correctly explains both the narrowing and the one-way flow.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Species are sorted into producers, consumers, and decomposers, and connected into an interconnected food web (at least one consumer has more than one food source), with energy arrows pointing eaten → eater.
☐
The energy pyramid is scaled by the 10% rule (10,000 → 1,000 → 100 → 10), each level labeled, with heat-loss arrows marking the ~90% lost between levels.
☐
A spatial recording (or full snapshots + two typed sentences) is submitted, explaining why the pyramid narrows and why energy is not recycled.
