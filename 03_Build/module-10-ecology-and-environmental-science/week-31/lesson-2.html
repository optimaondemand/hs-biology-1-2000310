Lesson 31.02
The 10% Rule: Why Energy Flows and Is Never Recycled
Lesson · Trophic Levels, Energy Pyramids, the One-Way Flow of Energy
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Why can a meadow feed thousands of grasshoppers but only a single hawk? And why does energy, unlike matter, never come back around?
Today you learn the rule that shapes every food chain on Earth: only about 10% of the energy at one level reaches the next; the rest is lost, mostly as heat. You'll draw that loss as an energy pyramid and settle the unit's central idea — the one most people get wrong: energy flows and is lost; matter cycles and is reused.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to use the 10% rule to calculate the energy available at each trophic level and explain why food chains rarely exceed four or five links.
2
I will be able to explain why an energy pyramid narrows toward the top and why top predators are few.
3
I will be able to distinguish the one-way flow of energy (lost as heat) from the cycling of matter through an ecosystem.

📈 PART 1 · TROPHIC LEVELS AND THE 10% RULE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
A lion eats an antelope, which ate grass. Where do you think most of the grass's energy ends up — in the antelope, in the lion, or somewhere else entirely?

Ten percent up, ninety percent gone

A trophic level is a feeding position: producers are the first level, herbivores (primary consumers) the second, and so on up. When energy passes from one level to the next, only about 10% is captured and stored; the other ~90% is lost — spent on the organism's own life processes and radiated away as heat. This is the 10% rule, and it is why food chains are short (rarely more than four or five links) and why top predators are few: after a few 90% losses there is almost nothing left to support another level.

🧮 PART 2 · RUN THE PYRAMID YOURSELF

Do the 10% math

A meadow's producers store 10,000 units of energy (Level 1). Apply the 10% rule one step at a time and choose the right answer for each level.

🧮 ENERGY-PYRAMID CALCULATOR · SELECT THE CORRECT VALUE
Level 2 — primary consumers (herbivores). About how much energy do they capture from the 10,000 units in the producers?
Level 3 — secondary consumers (carnivores). About how much energy reaches them from the herbivores' 1,000 units?
Level 4 — the top predator. About how much of the original 10,000 units is left for it, and what does that tell you?

Notice the shape: 10,000 → 1,000 → 100 → 10. Stack those as bars and you have drawn an energy pyramid — broad at the base, narrow at the top. The pyramid narrows because ~90% is lost at each step.

🔥 PART 3 · THE IDEA TO GET EXACTLY RIGHT

Energy flows; matter cycles

Here is the sentence to underline: energy is not recycled. It enters as sunlight, flows one way up the pyramid, and is steadily lost as heat that cannot be gathered back and reused. That is why the Sun must keep shining — an ecosystem needs a constant new supply because its energy is always leaking away. Matter is different: the atoms of carbon and nitrogen are returned to the soil by decomposers and taken up again by producers. So matter goes round and round; energy makes a one-way trip.

The exam trap: many students write that “energy is recycled in an ecosystem.” It is not. Say it correctly in your notebook: energy flows through the ecosystem and is lost as heat; only matter cycles. You will build this exact pyramid in this week's VR-A.
