Week 31 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Walk a Trophic Cascade (Yellowstone Wolves)
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will tour a Yellowstone valley in two states — wolves absent (before 1995) and wolves present (after their return) — and capture the trophic cascade as it ripples down the food web: from wolf to elk to willow and aspen to beaver, songbird, and streambank. The one big idea to carry away: removing or restoring a single keystone predator changes the whole community, not just its prey. This is Lessons 31.03–31.04, observed in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 31.03–31.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Yellowstone Valley — Before & After (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the cascade to look for. Begin in the Wolves Absent scene.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Wolves absent. Walk the “before” valley. Notice the large, bold elk herd lingering at the streambank and the short, over-browsed willow and aspen. Take a snapshot named “Before — no wolves.” Note what the streambank looks like.
2
Wolves return. Step into the “after” scene. Find the wolf pack and watch the elk moving away from the exposed streambank. Take a snapshot named “Keystone — wolf + wary elk.” Note how the elk's behavior has changed.
3
Follow the cascade down. Walk the recovering streambank: taller willow and aspen, a beaver and its dam, and songbirds in the new growth. Take a snapshot named “Cascade — willow, beaver, songbird.” Note which species returned as the trees recovered.
4
The big idea — told honestly. At the summary marker, answer in a sentence or two: how did returning one predator ripple all the way down to the willows and beavers? Then add one sentence naming something scientists still debate (e.g., how much of the recovery is due to wolves vs. climate or other factors) — and avoid overstating that “wolves changed the rivers.”
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots (Before — no wolves; Keystone — wolf + wary elk; Cascade — willow, beaver, songbird) to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note tracing the cascade from wolf to elk to willow to beaver/songbird, and one sentence naming an open question scientists still debate.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: The point of this walk is to make the keystone predator + trophic cascade concrete — one predator's removal and return reshapes the whole valley, top to bottom. Encourage students to physically trace the chain from the wolf down to the beaver dam. Two things to reinforce: the cascade runs downward from the top predator, and good science stays honest about magnitude (the recovery is real but uneven, and its full cause is still studied). A complete submission shows three correctly named snapshots, a note tracing the full cascade, and a stated open question — without the overstated “wolves changed the rivers” claim.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Both scenes are visited (wolves absent, then wolves present) and the cascade is traced from wolf to elk to willow/aspen to beaver/songbird.
☐
Three named snapshots are captured, each noting what changed at that step of the cascade.
☐
The typed note traces the full cascade, names the wolf as the keystone species, and states one open question scientists still debate (without overstating the effect).
