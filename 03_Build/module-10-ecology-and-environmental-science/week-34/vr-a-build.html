Week 34 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Biodiversity & Invasive-Species Florida Scene
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a diverse Florida habitat (for example, an Everglades wetland or a coral reef), populate it with native species and link them into a simple food web, and then introduce an invasive species — a Burmese python or a lionfish. You will model and annotate how the invader disrupts the native web: which native species it removes, and what happens to the species connected to those. Finally you will record a walkthrough explaining the damage. Building the invasion as a space you can walk makes this week's whole argument concrete — diversity brings stability, and an unchecked invader tears it down. This is Lessons 34.01 and 34.03, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or a desktop VR app · your completed Readings 34.01 and 34.03 and Lessons 34.01 and 34.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Florida Habitat Builder / Ecosystem Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty habitat platform marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — build the native community before you introduce the invader.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Choose and build the habitat. Pick one Florida ecosystem — an Everglades wetland (for the python) or a coral reef (for the lionfish). Lay down the setting: water, plants, and terrain. Label it with the ecosystem's name.
2
Populate with natives. Add at least five native species across trophic levels — producers, primary consumers, and predators (e.g., for the Everglades: sawgrass, marsh rabbits, raccoons, wading birds, a bobcat). Label each.
3
Link the food web. Draw arrows showing who eats whom (energy flow). This is your before state — a diverse, connected community.
4
Introduce the invader. Place the Burmese python (wetland) or lionfish (reef). Annotate why it spreads unchecked (no natural predators here) and mark the native species it removes. Show the knock-on effect: fade or flag the species that decline when their food or predator is gone.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 60–90 seconds, walk your scene, name the native species, then show the invader and explain how it disrupts the web and drives biodiversity down. End by naming why the invader had no natural check.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit two snapshots (one before the invader, one after) plus typed labels naming the native species, the invader, and at least two native species the invader harms — and one sentence on why the invader spread unchecked.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the diversity-stability-invasion story by building a native food web and then watching an invader tear through it. Two things to watch for and reinforce: that the invader's success comes from a lack of natural predators/controls in the new range (not from being “stronger”), and that its impact ripples through the web (removing one prey or predator affects the species connected to it), which is exactly why lost redundancy reduces stability. A strong submission shows a labeled native community with a food web, a correctly placed invader, at least two harmed natives marked, and a walkthrough that names why the invader had no natural check.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
A labeled Florida habitat is built and populated with at least five native species linked into a food web.
☐
An invasive species (Burmese python or lionfish) is introduced and annotated, with at least two harmed native species marked and the knock-on effect shown.
☐
A spatial recording (or before/after snapshots + typed labels) is submitted, explaining the invader's impact and why it spread without a natural check.
