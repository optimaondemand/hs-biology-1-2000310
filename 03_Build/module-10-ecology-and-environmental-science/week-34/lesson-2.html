🦡 PART 1 · HIPPO AND THE LEADING CAUSE
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Before reading on: if you had to guess the single biggest reason wild species are declining worldwide, what would you say it is?

Habitat destruction leads — and fragmentation compounds it

The single largest cause of biodiversity loss is habitat destruction — clearing forests, draining wetlands, and paving land for farms, roads, and cities. An organism with nowhere to live cannot survive. A subtler form is habitat fragmentation: a continuous habitat broken into small, isolated patches. Even modest area loss can be devastating, because small populations lose genetic diversity (recall the Florida panther from Module 6), struggle to find mates, and wink out. The other HIPPO letters — Invasives, Pollution, human Population growth, Overharvesting — plus climate change pile on from there, and they rarely act alone.

🌬 PART 3 · HOW THE OTHER PRESSURES WORK

Different mechanisms, same result

Pollution degrades air, water, and soil — fertilizer runoff triggers algal blooms that suffocate fish, and some toxins build up through the food chain to hit top predators hardest (a payoff of the trophic levels you studied earlier this module). Overexploitation — overfishing, poaching — takes wild organisms faster than they can reproduce. Climate change shifts temperature and rainfall faster than species can follow: warming bleaches coral reefs, rising seas drown coastal mangroves and marshes, and shifting seasons throw off the timing between flowers and their pollinators. Different routes to harm; all of them reduce the variety you learned to value yesterday.

The idea to remember: HIPPO + climate change name the drivers, and habitat destruction leads. These pressures reach back across the whole course — the genetic diversity lost to fragmentation (Module 6), the food chains disrupted by pollution and invasives (Module 10). Naming a cause correctly is where responsible action begins.
