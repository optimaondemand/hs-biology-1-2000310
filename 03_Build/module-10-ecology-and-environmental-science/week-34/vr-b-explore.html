Week 34 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Observe Invasive Python & Lionfish Impact Stations
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will tour a set of before-and-after stations that show two Florida communities — an Everglades wetland with the Burmese python and a coral reef with the lionfish — each displayed twice: once as it was before the invader arrived, and once after. At each station you will observe how native populations change and note exactly how the invader disrupts the community. The one big idea to carry away: an invasive species, arriving without natural controls, drives native biodiversity down and destabilizes the whole web. This is Lessons 34.02 and 34.03, observed in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Lessons 34.02 and 34.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Invasive-Species Impact Gallery (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your observer checklist and the four stations to visit. Begin at the Everglades — Before marker.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Everglades — Before. Walk the wetland as it was: many native mammals (raccoons, opossums, marsh rabbits, bobcats) and the birds and plants around them. Note the variety. Take a snapshot named “Everglades Before.”
2
Everglades — After. Enter the same wetland after the Burmese python established itself. Observe how the native mammal populations have crashed. Take a snapshot named “Everglades After” and note which species declined.
3
Reef — Before & After. Move to the coral-reef stations. In Before, observe the abundant native reef fish; in After, observe the reef once the lionfish has moved in and stripped many small native fish. Take snapshots named “Reef Before” and “Reef After,” noting the change.
4
The big idea. At the summary marker, answer in a sentence: what did both invaders do to the native biodiversity, and why couldn’t the ecosystem stop them? (Hint: no natural predators/controls; native species had no defenses.)
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your four named snapshots (Everglades Before/After, Reef Before/After) to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: for each ecosystem, name at least one native species that declined, and write one sentence explaining why neither community could stop its invader.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: The before/after contrast is the whole point — students should see native biodiversity drop when the invader arrives, not just read about it. Push them to connect the observation to the mechanism from Lesson 34.03: both invaders thrive because they left their natural predators and competitors behind, and the native species never evolved defenses against them. A complete submission shows four correctly named snapshots, at least one declining native species named per ecosystem, and a note that correctly explains the “no natural controls” reason the communities could not resist.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All four stations are visited in order (Everglades Before/After, Reef Before/After) and the change in native populations is observed at each.
☐
Four named snapshots are captured, each noting the native species that declined after the invader arrived.
☐
The typed note correctly explains that both invaders drove native biodiversity down and that the communities could not stop them because the invaders had no natural controls.
