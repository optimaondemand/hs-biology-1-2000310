Module 10 CER Writing Assignment
Three Independent Witnesses: The Case for Human-Driven Climate Change
Claim · Evidence · Reasoning · Integration · 12 pts · counts toward the CER writing category
📖 WHAT THIS IS
This is the final CER of the course, and it asks you to do what a scientist does when a question is large and consequential: gather independent lines of evidence and judge whether they converge. Your subject is anthropogenic (human-caused) climate change. This is not a lesson in slogans — it is a lesson in weighing evidence. You will examine three independent records — the temperature record, the atmospheric CO₂ record and its carbon fingerprint, and shifts in living things — and build a reasoned argument about what they show, together. This CER is assigned at the start of Week 30 and due at the end of Week 35, so the whole module can inform it. Your teacher reads and scores this one personally — it is not auto-graded.
🧠 THIS IS A CONTESTED, OPEN-ENDED CER
This is the least-scaffolded essay of the year. You are not filling in a template — you are evaluating evidence and building the argument yourself. Two things are worth naming honestly. First, the evidence that Earth is warming and that human activity is the main driver is backed by a strong scientific consensus built from many independent records; genuine scientific uncertainty is mostly about how much and how fast, not whether. Second, the truly open question — the one reasonable people still debate — is what we ought to do about it: that is a question of values and stewardship, and your reasoning there should be grounded, fair, and your own. Argue from the evidence, not from a side.
🎯 YOUR TASK
Using three independent lines of evidence, evaluate the case that recent climate change is largely human-caused — then reason from that biology to a position on our responsibility as members of the biotic community.
📈 YOUR EVIDENCE BANK · THREE INDEPENDENT LINES
These records come from different instruments, different scientists, and different fields — that independence is the heart of your argument. Choose at least three (you may use all three lines below, or bring in another you can support accurately from the module).
Line of evidence What the record shows
The temperature recordGlobal average surface temperature has risen roughly 1.1 °C since the late 1800s, with most of the rise since the mid-20th century. Thermometer records on land and sea agree, and are corroborated by warming oceans and shrinking ice.
Atmospheric CO₂ & its carbon fingerprintThe Keeling Curve (continuous measurements begun at Mauna Loa in 1958) shows CO₂ rising from about 315 ppm then to over 420 ppm today; ice cores show a pre-industrial level near 280 ppm. The isotopic signature of the added carbon matches carbon from fossil fuels — a chemical fingerprint tying the rise to burning ancient carbon (Week 32 carbon cycle).
Shifts in living thingsIndependently of any thermometer, life is responding: species’ ranges are shifting toward the poles and up mountains, springs arrive earlier, and the timing of migration, breeding, and flowering (phenology) is shifting. The organisms are, in effect, a second set of instruments.
Notice the logic you met in Module 6: when independent lines of evidence — gathered in different ways, for different reasons — all point the same direction, the conclusion is far stronger than any one line alone. That convergence is your argument’s backbone.
🧬 YOUR CONCEPT BANK
These are ideas from across the module (and the year). Use the ones that build your reasoning — a strong essay chains them, cause to effect.
Concept What it says · how it connects
The carbon cycle (Week 32)Photosynthesis, respiration, and combustion move carbon between air, organisms, and reservoirs. Fossil fuels are ancient photosynthetic carbon; burning them shifts that carbon to the atmosphere far faster than natural processes remove it.
Greenhouse effectGases such as CO₂ trap outgoing heat. More CO₂ means more heat retained — the physical link between the CO₂ record and the temperature record.
Species range & niche (Week 30)Each species is adapted to a range of abiotic conditions. When those conditions move, ranges and timing move with them — which is why living things register climate change.
Convergence of independent evidence (Module 6)Independent records that agree are hard to explain away together. This is the same reasoning that made the case for common descent — now applied to climate.
Stewardship & the land ethic (Week 34)Leopold’s argument that humans are “plain members and citizens” of the biotic community — the bridge from the biology to the responsibility question.
📝 BUILD YOUR ARGUMENT IN FOUR MOVES
Write in connected paragraphs, not a list. The wording is yours — these are the moves a strong CER makes.
1
Claim. In one or two sentences, state what the evidence, taken together, best supports — for example, that recent global warming is real and is driven mainly by human addition of CO₂ to the atmosphere. Make it a claim you will defend, not a slogan.
2
Evidence. Present your three independent lines specifically — cite the actual records (e.g., the ~1.1 °C rise; CO₂ from ~280 to over 420 ppm and its fossil-fuel isotopic fingerprint; poleward/upslope range shifts and earlier springs). Be concrete: numbers and named records are your evidence.
3
Reasoning. This is the heart of the essay. Explain why these lines support your claim — the greenhouse mechanism links rising CO₂ to rising temperature; the isotopic fingerprint ties the extra CO₂ to burning fossil carbon; the biological shifts confirm the warming independently of any thermometer. Then make the key move: argue why three independent lines converging is far stronger than any single line, and address at least one honest counter-consideration (natural climate variation, or the real uncertainty about rate and magnitude) — and why it does not overturn the convergent case.
4
Integration — the responsibility question. Tie the biology to the module’s virtue, Responsibility. Given what the evidence shows, and given Leopold’s claim that we are members of the biotic community, take a reasoned position on what our responsibility is. This part is genuinely open — a strong essay does not sloganeer in either direction; it reasons from the evidence to a defensible stance on stewardship and the common good.
✨ THE VIRTUE THIS PRACTICES · RESPONSIBILITY
This module’s virtue is responsibility — the stewardship of a world we did not make and do not own alone. A whole year of biology has led here: the atoms and water of Module 2, the cells and energy of Modules 3 and 4, the DNA that connects all living things through Modules 5 and 6, and finally the webs of energy and matter that bind every organism to every other. To understand that argument is to understand that we are, as Leopold wrote, plain members and citizens of the biotic community. Responsibility begins with honest evaluation of the evidence — the same habit of mind this course has trained since the first week — and continues in what we choose to do with what we have understood.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
A clear claim states what the evidence, taken together, best supports.
☐
Evidence presents at least three independent lines, cited concretely (real records and numbers), not just named.
☐
Reasoning explains the mechanisms, argues why convergence of independent lines is powerful, and fairly addresses at least one counter-consideration.
☐
Integration reads as one connected argument and takes a reasoned, evidence-grounded position on responsibility/stewardship.
📊 CER SCORING RUBRIC (3 PTS × 4 CRITERIA = 12 PTS)
Criterion Strong (3) Developing (2) Beginning (1)
Claim Specific and defensible; states what the combined evidence best supports. States a position but vague or hedged into meaninglessness. Missing, or just restates the prompt.
Evidence Three independent lines, cited concretely with real records/numbers. Two lines, or three named only vaguely; some detail missing. One line, or evidence largely inaccurate.
Reasoning Explains mechanisms, argues why convergence is powerful, and fairly weighs a counter-consideration. Links evidence to claim but convergence or counterpoint is thin. Lists evidence without explaining why it supports the claim.
Integration One coherent argument; reasons from the biology to a defensible stewardship position; within word range. Components present but the position or connection is loose. Listed independently; no position taken.
Total possible: 12 pts
