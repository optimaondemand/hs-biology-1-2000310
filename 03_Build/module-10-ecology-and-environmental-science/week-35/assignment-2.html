📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Ecosystem Services & Sustainability
Reading 35.02 and Lesson 35.02
Before you begin: finish Reading 35.02 and work through Lesson 35.02, including the four categories of ecosystem service and the definition of sustainability.
Lesson focus: What ecosystems give us, and how to use it without using it up
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: sort real benefits into the four service categories, define sustainability in your own words, and judge whether a practice is sustainable and explain why in terms of future generations. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What does a healthy ecosystem give us — and what does it mean to take it responsibly?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Sort the service (5 pts, 1 each). Write the category (Provisioning, Regulating, Supporting, or Cultural) for each benefit below.
Benefit from an ecosystem Category
A forest supplies timber for building 
Bees pollinate an orchard's fruit trees 
Decomposers cycle nitrogen back into the soil 
A Florida spring draws swimmers and kayakers 
A coastal mangrove buffers a town from storm surge 
2
Part B — Define it (2 pts). In one sentence, define sustainability in your own words, making clear that it concerns future generations.
3
Part C — Judge a practice (3 pts). A fishing fleet catches fish faster than the population can reproduce. In two or three sentences, state whether this is sustainable and explain why, using the idea of future generations.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: timber → Provisioning; pollination → Regulating; nitrogen cycling → Supporting; spring recreation → Cultural; mangrove storm buffer → Regulating.
☐
Part B defines sustainability as meeting present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs.
☐
Part C says the practice is not sustainable because catching faster than the fish reproduce depletes the stock, leaving less (or none) for future generations.
