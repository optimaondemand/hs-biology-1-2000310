Lesson focus: How a persistent toxin biomagnifies up a food web
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three parts: match the key terms to their meanings, explain why top predators are hit hardest, and then write the short claim–evidence–reasoning argument you drafted in the lesson. This is CER practice — it is not the graded Module 10 CER essay. It should take about 20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Was Rachel Carson's claim that DDT was causing a “silent spring” scientifically sound?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match term to meaning (4 pts, 1 each). Match each term to its meaning from the list below.
Term Meaning (write the letter)
Bioaccumulation 
Biomagnification 
Persistent 
Fat-soluble 
A. Builds up inside one organism faster than it can be shed  ·  B. Rises in concentration at each higher trophic level of a food web  ·  C. Does not break down quickly, so it lingers for years  ·  D. Dissolves into fatty tissue and is stored rather than washed out
2
Part B — Why the top? (2 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why fish-eating birds such as eagles and ospreys carried the highest DDT loads and suffered most, even though they never touched the sprayed fields directly.
3
Part C — Mini-CER (4 pts). Write the short argument you drafted in the lesson (about 4–6 sentences) answering the focus question. Include a clear claim, at least two pieces of evidence (e.g., biomagnification, thinning eggshells, recovery after the 1972 ban), and reasoning that links the evidence to the claim.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: bioaccumulation → A, biomagnification → B, persistent → C, fat-soluble → D.
☐
Part B links the top position to biomagnification: the predator takes in the accumulated toxin of everything it eats, so concentration is highest at the top.
☐
Part C states a claim, gives at least two pieces of evidence, and reasons that DDT's persistence and biomagnification (plus recovery after the ban) show Carson's claim was scientifically sound.
