Week 35 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Tour the Bioaccumulation of DDT up a Food Web
Solo VR activity · ~25 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk a lake food web where a persistent toxin — DDT — concentrates at each higher trophic level. Starting in the water and moving up through algae → zooplankton → small fish → large fish → a top-predator raptor, you will read a concentration meter at each level and watch the number climb, ending at the osprey whose eggshells thin. Seeing the poison magnify with your own eyes makes the mechanism from Lesson 35.01 unforgettable: energy is lost up a web, but a stored toxin is gathered. This VR pairs directly with Lesson 35.01.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 35.01 and Lesson 35.01 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: DDT Food Web / Lake Ecosystem (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the shoreline shows your explorer checklist and the concentration meter you will read at each level. Begin at the water's edge with the Algae marker.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Producers. At the algae level, read the concentration meter (it will show a very low value). Take a snapshot named “1 Algae” and record the number. Note: this matches the low level in the water itself.
2
Primary consumers. Move up to the zooplankton, which eat huge numbers of algae. Read the meter (now higher), snapshot it as “2 Zooplankton,” and record the number. Ask yourself why it rose.
3
Up the fish. Continue to the small fish, then the large fish. Read and snapshot the meter at each (“3 Small fish,” “4 Large fish”), recording the climbing numbers. The concentration should rise sharply at each step.
4
Top predator. Reach the osprey (or eagle) at the top of the web. Read the meter — now by far the highest — and examine its nest: the eggshells are visibly thin and cracked. Take a snapshot named “5 Raptor” showing both the meter and the nest.
5
The big idea. At the summary marker, answer in a sentence or two: why is the concentration highest in the top predator, even though the water was barely polluted? (Hint: connect it to biomagnification and to why energy behaves the opposite way up a web.)
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your five named snapshots (Algae, Zooplankton, Small fish, Large fish, Raptor), each showing the concentration meter, plus a short typed note: list the five numbers you recorded (showing the rise), and write one or two sentences explaining why the top predator carries the highest load and how that connects to the thin eggshells.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 25 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: The whole point is for students to see the concentration climb at each trophic level and connect it to the thin eggshells at the top — the mechanism behind Silent Spring. Two things to watch for: students who think the top predator eats the “most poison directly” (the point is that it inherits the accumulated load of everything below it), and confusion between energy (lost up the web) and a persistent toxin (gathered up the web). A complete submission shows five correctly named snapshots with a clearly rising set of numbers and a note that correctly attributes the high top-predator load to biomagnification and links it to eggshell thinning.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All five levels are visited in order (algae, zooplankton, small fish, large fish, raptor) and the concentration meter is read at each.
☐
Five named snapshots are captured showing the rising concentration, ending with the raptor's meter and thinned eggshells.
☐
The typed note lists the five rising numbers and correctly explains why the top predator carries the highest load, linking it to eggshell thinning.
