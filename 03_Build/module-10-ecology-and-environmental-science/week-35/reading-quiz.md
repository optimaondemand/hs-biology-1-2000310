**1. (Reading 35.01 — biomagnification; why top predators are hit hardest)**
DDT washes into a lake at a very low concentration, yet fish-eating birds such as ospreys end up with by far the highest concentration in their bodies. Why?

- A) The ospreys drink far more of the contaminated lake water than any other animal, so they absorb the most DDT
- B) DDT is chemically attracted to feathers and bone, so only birds absorb it from the lake and nothing else in the food web does
- C) The birds spray DDT directly onto their nests while preening, which concentrates it in their bodies
- D) A persistent toxin is not lost between levels but concentrates upward, so the top predator accumulates the toxin of everything below **✓**

*This is biomagnification. Unlike energy, which is mostly lost at each step of a food web, a persistent, fat-soluble toxin is stored and passed along with the meal. A top predator eats many prey across its life, so it accumulates the combined toxin of every level beneath it — the highest concentration of all.*

**2. (Reading 35.01 — eggshell thinning; targets "DDT poisoned adults directly")**
DDT did not usually kill adult raptors outright, yet their populations still crashed. What was the main reason?

- A) DDT built up as fat that made the adult birds too heavy to fly, so they starved before they could breed
- B) DDT interfered with calcium in the females' eggshells, making shells so thin that few chicks hatched **✓**
- C) DDT discolored the birds' feathers, making them so conspicuous that predators killed many adults
- D) The adults stopped eating fish because DDT dulled their sense of smell and hunting ability

*The accumulated DDT disrupted how female raptors laid down calcium in their eggshells. The shells came out dangerously thin, so parents cracked their own eggs while incubating them. The crash was a reproductive failure driven by thin shells, not a mass poisoning of adults.*

**3. (Reading 35.01 — recovery after the DDT restrictions; reasoning from evidence)**
Bald eagle, osprey, and peregrine falcon populations recovered in the decades after most uses of DDT were banned in the United States in 1972. What does this recovery best support?

- A) Raptors are naturally immune to pesticides, so some unrelated factor must have caused the decline
- B) Eggshell thinning was never connected to DDT, so the recovery must have been a coincidence
- C) DDT was a major contributor to the earlier declines, because removing it was followed by recovery **✓**
- D) Raptors rapidly evolved thicker eggshells within a few years of the ban, so selection explains it

*When a suspected cause is removed and the affected populations rebound, that pattern strongly supports the claim that the cause mattered. It does not by itself prove that nothing else contributed, but it fits the evidence that DDT thinned eggshells and reduced hatching success. It cannot be explained by instant evolution, and the birds were not immune.*

**4. (Reading 35.02 — ecosystem service categories; regulating)**
A coastal wetland filters pollutants out of water and buffers a town from storm surge. These benefits are best classified as which category of ecosystem service?

- A) Regulating services **✓**
- B) Provisioning services
- C) Cultural services
- D) Supporting services

*Regulating services are the ways ecosystems keep natural processes steady and safe — water purification, flood and storm control, climate regulation, and pollination. Filtering water and buffering storm surge are classic regulating services. Provisioning would be a harvested good; cultural would be recreation or beauty; supporting would be a slow foundation like nutrient cycling.*

**5. (Reading 35.02 — ecosystem service categories; supporting vs. others)**
Nutrient cycling and soil formation are called *supporting* services rather than provisioning or cultural services. Why?

- A) Because they are the physical goods that people harvest from nature and carry home to use
- B) Because they provide recreation, beauty, and spiritual value to the people who visit nature
- C) Because they are the slow, underlying processes that make the other services possible **✓**
- D) Because they occur only in the ocean, where nutrients cycle and sediments build up slowly

*Supporting services are the foundational processes — nutrient cycling, soil formation, primary production — on which every other service depends. You cannot harvest a crop (provisioning) or filter water (regulating) unless soil forms and nutrients cycle first. They are the ecological equivalent of a foundation beneath a building.*

**6. (Reading 35.02 — definition of sustainability)**
Which statement best defines sustainability?

- A) Using as many natural resources as quickly as possible to maximize the present economy's growth
- B) Meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own **✓**
- C) Leaving nature completely untouched, harvesting nothing so that no resources are ever used
- D) Replacing all natural ecosystems with managed farmland so that production is guaranteed forever

*Sustainability is fundamentally about time: it means using resources in a way that does not rob future generations. A fishery is sustainable if we catch no faster than the fish reproduce. It is neither reckless maximum use (A) nor total non-use (C) — it is use that can continue indefinitely.*

**7. (Reading 35.04 — humans as both members and shapers)**
Biology supports the view that humans are *both* members and shapers of ecosystems. Which pair of statements best captures this dual role?

- A) We are heterotrophs whose carbon cycles like any organism's (member); we can alter food webs and the climate (shaper) **✓**
- B) We stand entirely outside the food webs of nature (member); and we have no effect on climate or ecosystems (shaper)
- C) We are producers that make our own food (member); and we never change our surroundings (shaper)
- D) We are immune to biomagnification (member); and we only ever restore ecosystems, never harm them (shaper)

*Humans are members of the living world — heterotrophs in food webs, our carbon riding the carbon cycle, and not exempt from biomagnification. We are also uniquely powerful shapers, able to move toxins through webs (as with DDT), change the atmosphere, and both damage and restore ecosystems. This dual role is exactly why the module's virtue is responsibility.*

**8. (Cumulative synthesis — trace a carbon atom; Modules 4, 8, 10)**
Follow one carbon atom. It leaves the air as carbon dioxide, becomes part of a sugar in a leaf, is eaten by an animal, and later returns to the air. Which sequence of processes does it pass through?

- A) Cellular respiration builds it into sugar in the leaf, then photosynthesis in the animal releases it to the air
- B) Photosynthesis builds it directly into DNA, then digestion in the animal releases it to the air as CO₂
- C) It stays locked in the sugar when eaten, is stored as animal fat, and never returns to the air
- D) Photosynthesis builds it into sugar, then cellular respiration breaks the sugar down and releases it to the air **✓**

*In the leaf's chloroplast, photosynthesis captures CO₂ and builds the carbon into sugar. When an animal eats the plant, its mitochondria break that sugar down in cellular respiration, releasing the carbon again as CO₂ — returning it to the carbon cycle, ready to be captured by another leaf. One atom threads through cells, energy pathways, and a global cycle.*

**9. (Cumulative synthesis — the DNA thread; Modules 5, 6, 7)**
The same molecule, DNA, ties together three units of the course. Which statement correctly matches DNA's role in genetics, evolution, and classification?

- A) DNA carries usable energy for cells (genetics); stores fat that natural selection acts on (evolution); and builds the walls that sort species (classification)
- B) DNA is important only in genetics (heredity); it plays no role in evolution, and it is not used to sort organisms in classification
- C) DNA is copied and inherited (genetics); its mutations supply variation for selection (evolution); its sequences reveal relatedness (classification) **✓**
- D) DNA is made by ribosomes in genetics, by chloroplasts in evolution, and by mitochondria in classification, each in different cells

*DNA is one molecule doing one job across time. In genetics it is inherited and copied, with occasional mutations. In evolution those mutations are the raw variation that natural selection acts on, and shared sequences are strong evidence of common descent. In classification, comparing shared DNA sequences reveals relationships and lets us build the tree of life — the reason molecular data can reveal the domains of life.*
