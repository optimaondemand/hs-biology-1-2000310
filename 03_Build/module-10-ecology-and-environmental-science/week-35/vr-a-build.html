Week 35 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Year-Spine “Atoms → Ecosystems” Walk
Solo VR activity · ~35 minutes · 10 pts (completion) · Capstone synthesis
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
This is the capstone build of the whole course. You will assemble a walkable gallery with one station for each major stage of the year's spine, in order: atom/water → macromolecule → cell → energy → DNA → evolution → diversity → plant → body → ecosystem. Then you will record a walkthrough narrating, station by station, how each stage depends on the one before it. Turning the year into a space you can walk makes the “one long argument” of Lesson 35.03 physical: ten topics become ten steps of a single staircase. This is your best possible review before the Week-36 Final.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 35.03 and Lesson 35.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Synthesis Gallery / Open Build Space (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the long empty corridor marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — build station 1 (atom/water) at the near end and work toward the far end (ecosystem).
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Lay out ten stations. Along the corridor, place ten labeled markers in order: 1 Atom/Water, 2 Macromolecule, 3 Cell, 4 Energy, 5 DNA, 6 Evolution, 7 Diversity, 8 Plant, 9 Body, 10 Ecosystem. Order matters — build them small-to-large, near end to far end.
2
Give each station one object. Build or place one simple model per station — e.g., a water molecule, a protein chain, a cell, an ATP/leaf, a DNA double helix, a branching tree, a set of different organisms, a plant, a human figure, and a food web. Keep them simple; recognizable beats perfect.
3
Draw the dependency arrows. Between each station and the next, place an arrow labeled with how the later stage depends on the earlier (e.g., atoms→macromolecules: “water and carbon build the molecules of life”; energy→DNA: “copying DNA takes energy”; plant→ecosystem: “photosynthesis feeds the whole web”).
4
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 90–120 seconds, walk the corridor from atom to ecosystem, naming each station and stating the arrow — how it depends on the stage before. End at the ecosystem by naming one bridge that runs the whole way (e.g., a carbon atom, or DNA).
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one wide snapshot of the full corridor (all ten stations visible in order) plus typed labels naming each station and, for each arrow, one sentence on how the later stage depends on the earlier.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 35 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: This is a synthesis build, not a content build — the models can be rough. What matters is the order (small to large) and the dependency arrows that make the “one long argument” explicit. Two things to watch for: stations placed out of order (especially energy, DNA, and evolution), and arrows that merely restate the topic rather than naming a real dependence. A strong submission shows all ten stations in correct order, a labeled arrow between each pair naming a genuine dependency, and a walkthrough that ends by tracing one bridge (carbon atom or DNA) across several units.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All ten stations are built and labeled in correct small-to-large order (atom/water through ecosystem), each with one simple model.
☐
A labeled dependency arrow connects each station to the next, naming how the later stage depends on the earlier.
☐
A spatial recording (or wide snapshot + typed labels) is submitted, walking the spine in order and ending by tracing one bridge (carbon atom or DNA) across several units.
