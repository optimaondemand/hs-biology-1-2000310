Lesson focus: Reasoning from evidence — a warm-up for your Module 10 CER
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each piece of evidence to what it is, decide one true/false about convergence, and write one sentence interrogating an AI climate-projection claim. It is a direct warm-up for your Module 10 CER. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
Why is a conclusion supported by three independent lines of evidence stronger than one backed by a single measurement?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match the evidence (5 pts, 1 each). Match each piece of evidence to what it is from the list below.
Evidence What it is (write the letter)
A century of thermometer readings shows a rising global average 
The Keeling Curve of steadily rising atmospheric CO₂ since 1958 
The carbon-isotope signature of the added CO₂ matches ancient-plant carbon 
Species ranges shifting poleward and springs arriving earlier 
Physics, chemistry, and biology all pointing to the same conclusion 
A. The instrumental temperature record (a physics measurement)  ·  B. The atmospheric CO₂ record (a chemistry measurement of a gas)  ·  C. The fossil-fuel fingerprint that ties the added CO₂ to fossil-fuel burning  ·  D. Biological shifts — changes in species' ranges and timing (phenology)  ·  E. Convergence of independent lines of evidence
2
Part B — True or false (2 pts). Write True or False and correct it in a few words if false: “The three lines of evidence are convincing because they all come from the same instrument and the same measurement.”
3
Part C — Interrogate the AI claim (3 pts). A machine-learning model produces a detailed map projecting exactly where a species will live in 50 years. In one or two sentences, explain the most scientific way to treat that projection, and name one reason such a model could be wrong.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: thermometers → A, Keeling Curve → B, isotope signature → C, range/timing shifts → D, three fields converging → E.
☐
Part B: False — the lines are convincing precisely because they come from independent sources (physics, chemistry, biology) using different methods; that independence is what makes their agreement hard to explain away.
☐
Part C treats the projection as a hypothesis to test (verify against real observations), not a fact, and names a real weakness (e.g., biased or incomplete training data, or that a projection about the future cannot yet be checked).
