Population Growth
Reading 33.01 and Lesson 33.01
Before you begin: finish Reading 33.01 and work through Lesson 33.01, including the four growth factors, the exponential (J-shaped) curve, the logistic (S-shaped) curve, and biotic potential.
Lesson focus: The two shapes of population growth, and what changes a population's size
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each population term to its meaning, decide one true/false about exponential growth, and explain in a sentence or two why a J-curve cannot continue forever. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
What makes a population grow, and why does real growth eventually bend from a J into an S?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match term to meaning (5 pts, 1 each). Match each population term to its meaning from the list below.
Term Meaning (write the letter)
Immigration 
Emigration 
Exponential growth 
Logistic growth 
Biotic potential 
A. Individuals moving into a population — adds members  ·  B. Individuals moving out of a population — removes members  ·  C. J-shaped growth under unlimited resources  ·  D. S-shaped growth that slows and levels off as resources run short  ·  E. The fastest a species could reproduce under perfect conditions
2
Part B — True or false (2 pts). Write True or False and correct it in a few words if false: “A population can keep growing exponentially forever as long as it keeps reproducing.”
3
Part C — Explain the bend (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why a real population's growth eventually changes from a J-shaped curve into an S-shaped curve. Name what causes the slowdown.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: immigration → A, emigration → B, exponential growth → C, logistic growth → D, biotic potential → E.
☐
Part B: False — no population can grow exponentially forever; the environment always runs short of some resource (food, water, space), so growth slows or the population crashes.
☐
Part C names limited resources and rising competition (as the population crowds) as the cause of the slowdown toward carrying capacity.
