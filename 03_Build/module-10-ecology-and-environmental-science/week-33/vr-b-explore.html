Week 33 · VR-B (Explore / Observe)
Explore Primary vs. Secondary Succession Over Time
Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will walk two time-lapse scenes and watch a community rebuild before your eyes. In the primary-succession scene you will move along a timeline from bare rock → lichens → thin soil → shrubs → mature forest, watching pioneer species slowly build the soil that everything else needs. In the secondary-succession scene you will walk a post-fire recovery, where the soil survived and the forest returns far faster. Stepping through time makes the module’s key contrast physical: one path must build its own ground first; the other starts on ground already made. This is Lesson 33.03, observed in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 33.03 and Lesson 33.03 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Succession Timeline Walk (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your explorer checklist and the stages to look for. Begin at the Bare Rock marker of the primary-succession path.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Primary path — start on bare rock. Walk the primary-succession timeline from the start. Watch lichens appear on bare rock, then mosses, then a thin soil forming, then grasses and shrubs. Take a snapshot named “Primary — pioneers.” Note: there was no soil at the start.
2
Primary path — reach the climax. Follow the timeline to its end, where a mature (climax) forest stands. Take a snapshot named “Primary — climax forest.” Note roughly how much time this path represents (centuries).
3
Secondary path — after the fire. Cross to the secondary-succession scene, a forest just after a fire. Notice the soil is still there. Walk the timeline as grasses, then shrubs, then young trees return, ending at a recovered forest. Take a snapshot named “Secondary — recovery.”
4
The big idea. At the summary marker, answer in a sentence: why did the secondary-succession scene recover so much faster than the primary one? (Hint: look at what was on the ground at the start of each.)
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots (Primary — pioneers, Primary — climax forest, Secondary — recovery) to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note answering the big-idea question: one sentence explaining why secondary succession was faster (because the soil remained).
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: The whole point of this walk is the module’s key contrast — that soil is what separates slow primary succession (which must build soil from bare rock) from fast secondary succession (which begins with soil already present). Encourage students to look at the ground at the start of each scene, not just the plants. A complete submission shows the three named snapshots and a note that correctly attributes the faster secondary recovery to the surviving soil — not merely to “fewer plants to grow” or “better weather.”
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Both timelines are walked in order — the primary path from bare rock to climax forest, and the secondary path from post-fire to recovered forest.
☐
Three named snapshots are captured (Primary — pioneers, Primary — climax forest, Secondary — recovery).
☐
The typed note correctly explains that secondary succession was faster because the soil remained after the disturbance.
