**1. (Reading 33.01 — J-curve vs. S-curve)**
Two populations are graphed over time. Population X keeps rising ever more steeply and never levels off; population Y rises quickly and then flattens out. Which statement is correct?

- A) X shows logistic growth and Y shows exponential growth
- B) X shows exponential (J-shaped) growth and Y shows logistic (S-shaped) growth **✓**
- C) Both show exponential growth
- D) Both show logistic growth

*Exponential growth makes a J-shaped curve that keeps climbing because resources are treated as unlimited. Logistic growth makes an S-shaped curve that levels off as limited resources slow growth. X keeps rising (exponential); Y levels off (logistic).*

**2. (Reading 33.01 — growth factors; targets the "immigration vs. emigration" mix-up)**
Which pair of factors *adds* individuals to a population?

- A) Births and immigration **✓**
- B) Deaths and emigration
- C) Births and emigration
- D) Deaths and immigration

*A population gains members through births and immigration (individuals moving in) and loses members through deaths and emigration (individuals moving out). Growth = (births + immigration) − (deaths + emigration).*

**3. (Reading 33.01 — "if a population grows exponentially, what is absent?")**
A newly introduced population on an island grows exponentially for several months. Which condition best explains why exponential (J-shaped) growth is possible during this time?

- A) Predators, disease, and competition are keeping the population in check
- B) The population has already reached its carrying capacity
- C) Resources are effectively unlimited and factors like predation and competition are not yet limiting growth **✓**
- D) Emigration is greater than immigration

*Exponential growth occurs when resources are effectively unlimited and the usual brakes — predation, disease, competition, food shortage — are not yet acting. Once those limiting factors kick in, growth slows and the curve bends toward the S shape. It cannot continue forever.*

**4. (Reading 33.02 — carrying capacity; targets "K is a fixed wall")**
Which statement about carrying capacity (K) is most accurate?

- A) K is a fixed, permanent number that a population reaches and never leaves
- B) K is the maximum population an environment can sustain, and it can shift as the environment changes **✓**
- C) K is the fastest rate at which a species can possibly reproduce
- D) K is the starting size of a population

*Carrying capacity is the maximum population an environment can sustain over the long run. It is not a fixed wall: populations often overshoot it and fall back, and K itself rises or falls as conditions (rainfall, food, disturbance) change. The fastest possible reproduction rate is biotic potential, a different idea.*

**5. (Reading 33.02 — density-dependent vs. density-independent)**
A sudden hard freeze kills roughly the same fraction of an insect population whether that population is crowded or sparse. This limiting factor is best classified as:

- A) Density-dependent, because it involves the weather
- B) Density-independent, because its effect does not depend on how crowded the population is **✓**
- C) Density-dependent, because it reduces the population
- D) A biotic factor, because insects are living things

*Density-independent factors — weather, natural disasters, fire — strike with the same force regardless of population density. Density-dependent factors (competition, predation, disease, food shortage) hit harder as a population becomes more crowded. A freeze does not "count heads," so it is density-independent.*

**6. (Reading 33.02 — predator–prey cycles)**
In the classic lynx–snowshoe hare cycle, the hare population rises and then falls, and the lynx population follows the same pattern a step behind. Which best explains this rhythm?

- A) Abundant hares support more lynx; more lynx then reduce the hares; fewer hares then starve the lynx, letting hares recover **✓**
- B) The lynx and hares are competing for the same plants
- C) A density-independent factor controls both populations equally
- D) The hares eat the lynx when the lynx become abundant

*Predator and prey are linked in a feedback loop: plentiful prey let predators increase, more predators drive prey down, scarce prey then starve predators, and the reduced predation lets prey recover — so the two populations oscillate, the predator's peak lagging behind the prey's.*

**7. (Reading 33.03 — primary vs. secondary succession; the soil test)**
What is the key difference between primary and secondary succession?

- A) Primary succession happens in water and secondary succession happens on land
- B) Primary succession begins where there is no soil (bare rock), while secondary succession begins where soil remains after a disturbance **✓**
- C) Primary succession is fast and secondary succession is slow
- D) Only secondary succession ends in a climax community

*Primary succession starts on bare rock, lava, or sand with no soil, so pioneer species must build soil first — a process taking centuries. Secondary succession follows a disturbance (like a fire) that leaves the soil intact, so recovery is much faster. Both trend toward a climax community.*

**8. (Reading 33.03 — pioneer species and why secondary is faster)**
After a wildfire, a forest regrows within a few decades, but a new lava flow may take centuries to reach a mature forest. What best explains why the burned forest recovers so much faster?

- A) The fire added new nutrients that lava lacks entirely
- B) Lichens grow faster on ash than on rock
- C) The burned site still has soil (with nutrients, roots, and seeds), while the lava flow must first build soil from bare rock **✓**
- D) Secondary succession skips the climax community stage

*Secondary succession is faster because the soil survived the disturbance — it already holds nutrients, roots, and buried seeds, so plants return quickly. Primary succession on bare lava must spend a very long time building soil (via pioneer lichens and mosses) before larger plants can grow. Soil is the deciding difference.*

**9. (Reading 33.04 — three independent lines of evidence and convergence)**
Scientists cite three lines of evidence for a changing climate: the instrumental temperature record, the rising atmospheric CO₂ record with its fossil-fuel isotope fingerprint, and poleward/earlier shifts in species' ranges and timing. Why does drawing on three *independent* lines make the conclusion stronger than relying on any one alone?

- A) Because three measurements are always exactly three times as accurate as one
- B) Because independent lines from different fields (physics, chemistry, biology) converging on the same conclusion is much harder to explain away than a single measurement **✓**
- C) Because biological evidence is the only kind that truly counts
- D) Because using more data automatically removes all uncertainty about the rate and magnitude

*When independent lines of evidence — gathered by different methods in different fields — all point to the same conclusion, that convergence is far harder to explain away than any single result. This is the same reasoning used for common descent in evolution. It does not erase all uncertainty (debate remains about rate and magnitude), but it makes the core conclusion robust.*
