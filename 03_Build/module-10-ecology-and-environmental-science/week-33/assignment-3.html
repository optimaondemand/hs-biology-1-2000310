📖 WHAT THIS BUILDS ON
Ecological Succession
Reading 33.03 and Lesson 33.03
Before you begin: finish Reading 33.03 and work through Lesson 33.03, including primary succession (bare rock, no soil, pioneer species), secondary succession (soil remains), and the climax community.
Lesson focus: How a community rebuilds — and why soil is the whole difference in speed
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: classify five scenarios as primary or secondary succession, put a succession sequence in order, and explain in a sentence or two why soil makes secondary succession faster. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
When life reclaims a place, what decides whether it takes decades or centuries?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Primary or secondary? (5 pts, 1 each). For each scenario, write P (primary) or S (secondary).
Scenario P or S?
Lichens colonize a fresh lava flow with no soil 
A forest regrows in the years after a wildfire 
The new island of Surtsey is colonized from bare rock 
An abandoned farm field slowly returns to woodland 
Bare rock left by a retreating glacier is colonized by moss 
2
Part B — Put it in order (2 pts). Rewrite these stages of primary succession in the correct order, from first to last: shrubs · bare rock · mature forest (climax) · lichens and mosses · grasses in thin soil.
3
Part C — Why the difference in speed? (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why secondary succession is usually much faster than primary succession. Name the one feature that makes the difference.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: lava → P, forest after fire → S, Surtsey → P, abandoned field → S, glacier-scraped rock → P.
☐
Part B order: bare rock → lichens and mosses → grasses in thin soil → shrubs → mature forest (climax).
☐
Part C names soil: secondary succession starts with soil already present (nutrients, roots, seeds), so it skips the centuries of soil-building that primary succession must do first.
