Week 33 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Model Exponential vs. Logistic Growth Curves
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build two population-growth curves as large 3D objects you can walk around: a J-shaped exponential curve that keeps shooting upward, and an S-shaped logistic curve that rises and then levels off. You will mark the carrying capacity (K) as a line across the logistic curve where it flattens, then record a short walkthrough explaining what limits growth as the population approaches K. Building the two shapes with your hands — and standing where the S-curve bends over — makes the difference between “unlimited” and “limited” growth concrete. This is Lessons 33.01 and 33.02, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Readings 33.01–33.02 and Lessons 33.01–33.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Population Dynamics Lab / Graphing Space (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty graphing frame marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — start with the exponential curve.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Set up the axes. Build the two axes of the frame: time along the bottom and population size up the side. Label them.
2
Build the J-curve. Shape the exponential curve: slow at first, then bending ever more steeply upward with no leveling off. Label it: exponential — unlimited resources, keeps rising.
3
Build the S-curve. Next to it, shape the logistic curve: a slow start, a steep middle, and a leveling-off at the top. Label it: logistic — limited resources, growth slows and levels off.
4
Mark carrying capacity. Lay a horizontal line across the level where your S-curve flattens and label it K (carrying capacity). Notice that the J-curve blows right past it — a population that ignores its limits.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 60–90 seconds, walk both curves, name each one, point to K on the logistic curve, and explain what limits growth as the population approaches K (competition for food and space, predation, disease — the density-dependent factors).
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one full snapshot of both labeled curves (with K marked on the logistic curve) plus two or three typed sentences naming each curve and explaining what limits growth near K.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize the two growth shapes and locate carrying capacity by building it. Two common errors to watch for and correct: drawing the S-curve as a J-curve (the logistic curve MUST level off — that leveling is the whole point), and placing K anywhere but the flattening level (K is where growth stalls, not the peak of the J-curve). A strong submission shows both curves clearly distinguished, K marked at the S-curve’s plateau, and a walkthrough that correctly names density-dependent limiting factors as the cause of the slowdown.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Both curves are built and labeled — a J-shaped exponential curve that keeps rising and an S-shaped logistic curve that levels off — on axes of time and population size.
☐
Carrying capacity (K) is marked as a line across the level where the logistic curve flattens.
☐
A spatial recording (or full snapshot + typed sentences) is submitted, naming both curves and explaining what limits growth as the population approaches K.
