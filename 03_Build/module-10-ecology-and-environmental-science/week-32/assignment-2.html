The Nitrogen Cycle
Reading 32.02 and Lesson 32.02
Before you begin: finish Reading 32.02 and work through Lesson 32.02 — why organisms cannot use N₂ gas directly, and the five steps of the cycle (fixation, nitrification, assimilation, ammonification, denitrification) and the bacteria behind them.
Lesson focus: Bacteria keep nitrogen moving
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each nitrogen-cycle step to what it does, answer one true/false about using nitrogen gas, and explain in a sentence or two why bacteria are essential to the cycle. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
If nitrogen makes up most of the air, why does life still depend on bacteria to get it?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match step to what it does (5 pts, 1 each). Match each nitrogen-cycle step to what it does, using the list below.

2
Part B — True or false (2 pts). Write True or False and correct it in a few words if false: “Plants get the nitrogen they need by absorbing nitrogen gas (N₂) directly from the air.”
3
Part C — Why bacteria matter (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain why the nitrogen cycle would stop without bacteria. Name at least one specific step bacteria perform. (A connection to Module 7 earns full credit for reasoning.)
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: fixation → A, nitrification → B, assimilation → C, ammonification → D, denitrification → E.
☐
Part B: False — plants cannot use N₂ directly; the gas must first be fixed by bacteria into ammonium/nitrate, which plants absorb from the soil.
☐
Part C explains that bacteria carry out most steps (e.g., fixation, nitrification, denitrification), so without them nitrogen could never be made usable or cycled — the biosphere would run short.
