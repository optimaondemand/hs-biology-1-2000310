Human Disruption of the Cycles
Reading 32.04 and Lesson 32.04
Before you begin: finish Reading 32.04 and work through Lesson 32.04 — how burning fossil fuels adds excess carbon, how fertilizer adds excess nitrogen and phosphorus, and the eutrophication chain that ends in a hypoxic dead zone.
Lesson focus: Cycles pushed past their pace
📋 ASSIGNMENT OVERVIEW
This assignment has three short parts: match each human activity to the cycle it disrupts, put the eutrophication chain in order, and explain in a sentence or two why the decomposers — not the algae — cause the oxygen crash. It should take about 15–20 minutes.
🎯 FOCUS QUESTION
How can adding nutrients — not poison — end by suffocating the life in a lake?
📝 STUDENT DIRECTIONS
1
Part A — Match activity to effect (4 pts, 1 each). Match each human activity to what it does to the cycles, using the list below.
Human activity Effect (write the letter)
Burning fossil fuels 
Fertilizer runoff into a lake 
Oceans absorbing extra CO₂ 
Decomposers consuming a dead algal bloom 
A. Adds excess carbon to the atmosphere faster than the cycle can reabsorb it  ·  B. Adds excess nitrogen and phosphorus, removing the nutrient limit on algae  ·  C. Uses up the water's dissolved oxygen, creating a hypoxic dead zone  ·  D. Makes seawater gradually more acidic (ocean acidification)
2
Part B — Order the eutrophication chain (3 pts). Number these five events 1–5 in the order they happen: algae die · nutrient runoff enters the water · a hypoxic dead zone forms and fish die · algae bloom explosively · decomposers use up the oxygen.
3
Part C — The key mechanism (3 pts). In one or two sentences, explain what actually removes the oxygen in a dead zone. Be sure to make clear that it is the decomposers, not the algae, and say why.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA
☐
Part A: burning fossil fuels → A, fertilizer runoff → B, oceans absorbing CO₂ → D, decomposers on dead algae → C.
☐
Part B order: (1) nutrient runoff enters the water, (2) algae bloom explosively, (3) algae die, (4) decomposers use up the oxygen, (5) hypoxic dead zone forms and fish die.
☐
Part C states that decomposers (bacteria) respire as they break down the dead algae, consuming the dissolved oxygen — the living algae had actually added oxygen while blooming.
