Week 32 · VR-A (Build / Model)
Build a Biogeochemical Cycle with Flows
Solo VR activity · ~30 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔬 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will build a walk-through model of one biogeochemical cycle — the carbon cycle or the nitrogen cycle (your choice). You place the major reservoirs as large 3D nodes — atmosphere, organisms, soil, and ocean/sediment — then draw labeled arrows for each process that moves matter between them. Finally, you record a walkthrough tracing a single atom through a full loop, back to where it began. Building the cycle as a space you can step inside makes the week's master idea concrete: matter cycles, the same atoms used again and again. This is Lessons 32.01 and 32.02, built in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Readings 32.01–32.02 and Lessons 32.01–32.02 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Biogeochemical Cycles Lab (Optima VR library)
On arrival: go to the empty cycle-building platform marked by the Whiteboard IFX. A sticky note there gives your first instruction — choose your cycle, then place your reservoirs before drawing any flows.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Choose your cycle — carbon or nitrogen — and place the four reservoirs as labeled nodes spread around the platform: atmosphere, organisms, soil, and ocean/sediment.
2
Draw the flows. Connect the reservoirs with labeled arrows, one per process. Carbon: photosynthesis (air→organisms), respiration and decomposition (organisms→air), combustion (fuel→air), ocean absorption (air→ocean). Nitrogen: fixation (air→soil), nitrification (within soil), assimilation (soil→organisms), ammonification (organisms→soil), denitrification (soil→air).
3
Add one human-input arrow in a second color: for carbon, fossil-fuel combustion pouring extra CO₂ into the atmosphere; for nitrogen, fertilizer adding extra nitrogen to the soil/water. Label it “human disruption.”
4
Grab an atom token from the reservoir bin and physically walk it around a complete loop, following your arrows from one reservoir all the way back to where it started — proving your flows actually connect into a cycle.
5
Record your walkthrough. Start a spatial recording. In about 60–90 seconds, carry your atom through one full loop, naming each reservoir and each process it passes through, and end by pointing to your human-disruption arrow and saying what it does to the cycle.
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your spatial recording to this Canvas assignment. If you can’t record, submit one full snapshot of your completed cycle (all reservoirs and labeled flows visible) plus a typed note naming each process in order and tracing one atom through a full loop.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 30 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: Students externalize a cycle by building its reservoirs and connecting them with named flows, then prove it loops by carrying an atom all the way around. Two common errors to watch for and correct: drawing arrows that don’t close into a loop (a real cycle must return the atom to its start — e.g., denitrification sending nitrogen back to the air), and reversing a process direction (photosynthesis moves carbon from air into organisms, not the reverse). A strong submission shows four labeled reservoirs, correctly directed and labeled flows, one human-disruption arrow, and a walkthrough that traces a single atom through a complete loop.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
All four reservoirs (atmosphere, organisms, soil, ocean/sediment) are placed and labeled, with correctly directed, labeled arrows for each process of the chosen cycle.
☐
One human-disruption input arrow is added and labeled (fossil-fuel CO₂, or fertilizer nitrogen).
☐
A spatial recording (or full snapshot + typed note) is submitted that traces one atom through a complete loop, naming each reservoir and process.
