Solo VR activity · ~20 minutes · 10 pts (completion)
🔎 WHAT YOU’RE DOING
You will tour two scenes where humans push a biogeochemical cycle out of balance. In the first, a fossil-fuel combustion scene — a power plant or highway — pours extra carbon dioxide into the air. In the second, fertilizer runoff flows off farmland into a lake and triggers eutrophication: an algal bloom, a die-off, and a suffocating dead zone. Your job is to observe each disruption, capture evidence, and identify which cycle it alters. This is Lesson 32.04, observed in space.
🔧 WHAT YOU’LL NEED
A VR headset (recommended) or the ENGAGE desktop app · your completed Reading 32.04 and Lesson 32.04 · your scientific notebook.
📍 HOW TO JOIN
Device: VR headset or desktop · Session type: temporary
Session ID: posted by your teacher at the top of this Canvas assignment
Location: Cycles Under Pressure (Optima VR library)
On arrival: a Web URL Image IFX by the entry shows your observer checklist and the two disruption scenes to visit. Begin at the Emissions marker.
📝 WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
1
Emissions scene. Walk into the fossil-fuel combustion scene and watch the labeled CO₂ streaming from the smokestacks and tailpipes into the atmosphere. Take a snapshot named “Emissions.” In your notebook, name which cycle this alters and how (excess carbon added to the air faster than it is removed).
2
Runoff scene. Move to the farmland-and-lake scene. Watch fertilizer wash off the fields into the water. Take a snapshot named “Runoff.” Note which cycles this alters (nitrogen and phosphorus) and the nutrients involved.
3
Follow the dead-zone chain. Use the scene’s time slider (or walk the stations) to watch the lake move through the stages: algal bloom → die-off → decomposers using up the oxygen → hypoxic dead zone with a fish kill. Take a snapshot named “Dead Zone.”
4
The big question. At the summary marker, answer in a sentence: in the dead zone, what actually used up the oxygen — the algae, or something else? (Hint: watch the decomposers.)
📤 WHAT TO SUBMIT
Upload your three named snapshots (Emissions, Runoff, Dead Zone) to this Canvas assignment, plus a short typed note: for each disruption, name which cycle(s) it alters, and write one sentence answering the big question about what uses up the oxygen in a dead zone.
⏱️ TIME · ABOUT 20 MINUTES IN-HEADSET
Facilitator note: The point of this walk is to attach each abstract disruption to a cycle and to nail the eutrophication mechanism. Two common errors to watch for and correct: saying fossil-fuel burning “makes new carbon” (it releases ancient stored carbon faster than the cycle reabsorbs it), and blaming the living algae for the oxygen loss (the oxygen is stripped out later by decomposers respiring on the dead algae). A complete submission shows three correctly named snapshots, each disruption matched to the right cycle(s), and a note that correctly credits decomposer respiration for the dead zone’s oxygen crash.
✅ SUCCESS CRITERIA (COMPLETION)
☐
Both disruption scenes are visited and the eutrophication stages are followed in order.
☐
Three named snapshots (Emissions, Runoff, Dead Zone) are captured, each with the cycle(s) it alters identified.
☐
The typed note correctly identifies that decomposers respiring on the dead algae — not the algae themselves — use up the dissolved oxygen.
