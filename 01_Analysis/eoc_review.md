# Florida Biology EOC Review Guide

## Overview of the Florida Biology End-of-Course Exam

The Florida Biology 1 EOC assesses students on the Next Generation Sunshine State Standards (NGSSS) for Biology. The exam includes multiple-choice questions, multi-select questions, and written-response items. It is administered in the spring and accounts for 30% of a student's final course grade.

Key assessment domains and approximate weight:
- Molecular and Cellular Biology (35-40%)
- Classification and Evolution (20-25%)
- Ecosystems and Ecology (20-25%)
- Human Anatomy and Physiology (15-20%)

## Unit-by-Unit EOC Review

### Unit 1: Nature of Science

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Scientific method: hypothesis, theory, law (know the differences)
- Experimental design: independent variable, dependent variable, constants, control group
- Data analysis: interpreting graphs and tables
- Science and society: peer review, reproducibility, the difference between science and pseudoscience

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- A theory is just a guess (wrong — a theory is a well-supported explanation backed by extensive evidence)
- A hypothesis that is not supported is a failed experiment (wrong — disconfirmation is valuable data)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. A student wants to test whether fertilizer affects plant height. Which of the following is the independent variable?
   A) Plant height B) Type of fertilizer C) Amount of water D) Sunlight

2. Which of the following best describes a scientific theory?
   A) An educated guess about what will happen in an experiment
   B) A law that has been proven beyond all doubt
   C) A well-tested explanation supported by substantial evidence
   D) A hypothesis that has been tested once

3. A scientist publishes results claiming a new diet cures cancer. What is the most important next step for the scientific community?
   A) Immediately accept the results as true B) Attempt to replicate the experiment
   C) Reject the results without review D) Fund additional advertising

**EOC Review Strategy:** Make a three-column chart: Hypothesis | Theory | Law — definitions, examples, key differences. Practice identifying IV and DV in 10 different experiment descriptions.

### Unit 2: Macromolecules and Properties of Water

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Four macromolecules: carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids
- Monomers and polymers for each
- Functions of each macromolecule
- Water properties: polarity, cohesion, adhesion, specific heat, universal solvent
- Enzyme structure and function; denaturation

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- Lipids are polymers of fatty acids (lipids are not true polymers — they are assembled differently)
- Enzymes are consumed in reactions (enzymes are catalysts; they are reused)
- DNA is a carbohydrate (DNA is a nucleic acid)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. Which property of water allows it to resist temperature changes and moderate climate?
   A) Cohesion B) Adhesion C) High specific heat D) Polarity

2. A student heats an enzyme to 90 degrees Celsius and finds it no longer catalyzes its reaction. What most likely happened?
   A) The substrate was consumed B) The enzyme was denatured C) The pH changed D) The enzyme evolved

3. Which macromolecule serves as the primary long-term energy storage molecule in animals?
   A) Carbohydrates B) Proteins C) Lipids D) Nucleic acids

**EOC Review Strategy:** Create a macromolecule comparison table (monomer, polymer, function, example). Draw a graph of enzyme activity vs. temperature and vs. pH and explain both curves.

### Unit 3: Cell Theory, Structure and Function

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Three tenets of cell theory
- Prokaryote vs. eukaryote (key differences)
- Plant vs. animal cell (key differences)
- Organelle functions: nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplast, ribosome, ER, Golgi, vacuole, lysosome, cell membrane, cell wall
- Fluid mosaic model of the cell membrane
- Cell transport: diffusion, osmosis, facilitated diffusion, active transport
- Osmosis and tonicity: hypertonic, hypotonic, isotonic

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- The cell wall is the same as the cell membrane (different structures, different functions)
- Active transport moves molecules from low to high concentration using no energy (it requires ATP)
- Diffusion always requires a carrier protein (only facilitated diffusion does)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. Which organelle is responsible for sorting and packaging proteins for secretion?
   A) Mitochondria B) Ribosome C) Golgi apparatus D) Lysosome

2. A red blood cell is placed in a solution with a higher solute concentration than the cell's interior. What will happen?
   A) Water will move into the cell B) Water will move out of the cell
   C) Solutes will move into the cell D) No movement will occur

3. Which of the following is found in plant cells but NOT in animal cells?
   A) Mitochondria B) Cell membrane C) Cell wall D) Ribosome

**EOC Review Strategy:** Draw a large cell diagram and label all organelles without looking. For cell transport, draw arrows showing the direction of water and solute movement in hyper-, hypo-, and isotonic solutions.

### Unit 4: Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Photosynthesis equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6O2
- Light-dependent reactions (thylakoid): uses light, produces ATP, NADPH, O2
- Calvin cycle / light-independent reactions (stroma): uses ATP and NADPH, produces glucose
- Cellular respiration equation: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6CO2 + 6H2O + ATP
- Glycolysis (cytoplasm), Krebs cycle (mitochondrial matrix), electron transport chain (inner mitochondrial membrane)
- ATP as cellular energy currency
- Relationship between photosynthesis and cellular respiration

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- Plants only photosynthesize, not respire (plants do both)
- Cellular respiration is the same as breathing (respiration is cellular; breathing is gas exchange)
- The Calvin cycle requires direct light (it requires ATP and NADPH from light reactions, but not light directly)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. Which stage of cellular respiration produces the most ATP?
   A) Glycolysis B) Krebs cycle C) Electron transport chain D) Fermentation

2. A student covers half a leaf with foil. After several hours in sunlight, which part of the leaf will test positive for starch?
   A) The covered half B) The uncovered half C) Both halves equally D) Neither half

3. Which of the following is a product of the light-dependent reactions used in the Calvin cycle?
   A) Glucose B) Carbon dioxide C) Oxygen D) ATP and NADPH

**EOC Review Strategy:** Make a two-column comparison: Photosynthesis vs. Cellular Respiration (reactants, products, location, energy direction). Memorize both equations. Practice tracing a carbon atom through both processes.

### Unit 5: Plant Structure and Function

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Plant tissues: dermal, ground, vascular (xylem and phloem)
- Xylem: transports water and minerals upward (dead cells, one-way)
- Phloem: transports sugars bidirectionally (living cells)
- Transpiration and stomata: guard cells, water loss, gas exchange
- Cohesion-tension theory of water transport
- Plant organs: roots, stems, leaves — structure and function
- Plant hormones: auxin (phototropism), gibberellins, ethylene, abscisic acid

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- Xylem transports sugars and phloem transports water (reversed — a very common error)
- Transpiration is a problem for plants rather than a driving mechanism for water transport
- All plants are autotrophs (most are; exceptions like parasitic plants exist)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. Which vascular tissue transports water and dissolved minerals from roots to leaves?
   A) Phloem B) Xylem C) Epidermis D) Mesophyll

2. A plant is placed near a window and grows toward the light. Which hormone is responsible?
   A) Gibberellin B) Ethylene C) Abscisic acid D) Auxin

3. Where does most gas exchange occur in a leaf?
   A) Xylem B) Phloem C) Stomata D) Cuticle

**EOC Review Strategy:** Draw a cross-section of a leaf and label all tissues. Create a plant hormone chart: hormone, function, example effect. The xylem/phloem confusion is the most common error — create a memorable mnemonic.

### Unit 6: Genetics, Mitosis, Meiosis, and the Cell Cycle

**Key EOC concepts:**
- DNA structure: double helix, nucleotides, base pairing (A-T, G-C), antiparallel strands
- DNA replication: semiconservative, leading and lagging strands
- Cell cycle: G1, S, G2, M (mitosis), cytokinesis
- Mitosis phases: PMAT — Prophase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase
- Meiosis: two divisions, produces 4 haploid cells, crossing over, independent assortment
- Mitosis vs. meiosis: purposes, products, genetic outcomes
- Mendelian genetics: dominant/recessive, homozygous/heterozygous, genotype/phenotype
- Punnett squares: monohybrid and dihybrid crosses
- Non-Mendelian inheritance: incomplete dominance, codominance, sex-linkage, multiple alleles
- Chromosomal disorders: trisomy, monosomy
- Mutations: point, frameshift, silent, missense, nonsense

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- Meiosis produces 4 genetically identical cells (wrong — each is genetically unique due to crossing over and independent assortment)
- Dominant means more common in a population (wrong — dominant refers to expression, not frequency)
- Mitosis and meiosis produce the same types of cells (wrong — mitosis: 2 diploid identical; meiosis: 4 haploid unique)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. During which phase of mitosis do chromosomes line up along the cell's equator?
   A) Prophase B) Metaphase C) Anaphase D) Telophase

2. A woman who is a carrier for hemophilia (X^H X^h) and a man with hemophilia (X^h Y) have children. What percentage of their daughters will have hemophilia?
   A) 0% B) 25% C) 50% D) 100%

3. A mutation changes a single base pair in a codon from UUU (Phe) to UUC (also Phe). What type of mutation is this?
   A) Missense B) Nonsense C) Frameshift D) Silent

4. Which of the following correctly describes a difference between mitosis and meiosis?
   A) Mitosis produces haploid cells; meiosis produces diploid cells
   B) Mitosis produces genetically unique cells; meiosis produces identical cells
   C) Mitosis produces 2 daughter cells; meiosis produces 4 daughter cells
   D) Mitosis occurs only in reproductive organs; meiosis occurs throughout the body

**EOC Review Strategy:** This is the heaviest EOC unit. Create a mitosis vs. meiosis comparison table. Practice 10 Punnett squares across all inheritance patterns. Memorize DNA base pairing rules and draw the replication process from memory.

### Unit 7: Evolution, Classification, and Origins of Life

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Evidence for evolution: fossil record, comparative anatomy (homologous, analogous, vestigial), biogeography, molecular evidence (DNA similarity), observed evolutionary change
- Natural selection: variation, heredity, differential reproduction, adaptation
- Mechanisms of evolution: natural selection, mutation, genetic drift, gene flow
- Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium and conditions that disrupt it
- Speciation: allopatric, sympatric; reproductive isolation
- Classification: Linnaean system (Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species)
- Three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya
- Phylogenetic trees and cladograms: how to read and construct
- Origin of life: Miller-Urey, RNA world hypothesis, abiogenesis

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- "Survival of the fittest" means physically strongest (fitness = reproductive success in a specific environment)
- Evolution is directed toward a goal or "improvement" (evolution has no direction or purpose)
- Humans evolved from chimpanzees (humans and chimps share a common ancestor, but neither evolved from the other)
- A mutation always harms an organism (mutations can be neutral, beneficial, or harmful)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. A population of lizards lives on a green island. Green lizards survive better than brown lizards because predators cannot see them. Over many generations, what would you predict?
   A) All lizards become green through mutation
   B) Brown lizards learn to camouflage themselves
   C) Green lizards become more common in the population
   D) The island's color changes to match the lizards

2. Which of the following is NOT evidence for evolution?
   A) Fossil record showing gradual changes over time
   B) Homologous bone structures in vertebrate forelimbs
   C) Similar DNA sequences across distantly related species
   D) Organisms within the same species looking similar

3. Two populations of the same bird species are separated by a mountain range for thousands of years. They develop different mating calls and can no longer interbreed. Which type of speciation occurred?
   A) Sympatric B) Allopatric C) Convergent D) Coevolution

**EOC Review Strategy:** Create an evidence-for-evolution organizer with specific examples for each type. Practice reading and constructing cladograms. Draw the classification hierarchy as a narrowing funnel.

### Unit 8: Ecology

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Levels of organization: organism, population, community, ecosystem, biome, biosphere
- Abiotic vs. biotic factors
- Energy flow: food chains, food webs, trophic levels, 10% energy rule, energy pyramids
- Producers, consumers (primary, secondary, tertiary), decomposers
- Biogeochemical cycles: carbon, nitrogen, water, phosphorus
- Population dynamics: exponential vs. logistic growth, carrying capacity, limiting factors
- Interspecific interactions: predation, competition, mutualism, commensalism, parasitism
- Ecological succession: primary and secondary
- Biodiversity and conservation: habitat loss, invasive species, human impact

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- Energy is recycled through ecosystems (wrong — matter cycles; energy flows through and is lost as heat at each level)
- Decomposers are consumers (decomposers occupy a separate role — they break down dead matter and return nutrients)
- Carrying capacity is a fixed number (it changes as environmental conditions change)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. A field currently dominated by grasses is abandoned. Over time, shrubs, then small trees, then large trees appear. This process is called:
   A) Primary succession B) Secondary succession C) Carrying capacity D) Ecological niche

2. If 10,000 units of energy are available at the producer level of a food chain, how much energy is available to secondary consumers?
   A) 10,000 B) 1,000 C) 100 D) 10

3. Which type of interspecific interaction benefits one organism while harming the other?
   A) Mutualism B) Commensalism C) Predation D) Competition

**EOC Review Strategy:** Draw a complete food web for a local Florida ecosystem and trace energy flow. Create a biogeochemical cycle diagram for carbon with at least four steps. Graph both exponential and logistic population growth and label key features.

### Unit 9: Biotechnology, PCR, and DNA

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Central dogma: DNA → RNA → Protein
- Transcription: DNA → mRNA (in nucleus)
- Translation: mRNA → protein (at ribosome)
- Codons and the universal genetic code
- Genetic engineering: restriction enzymes, recombinant DNA, plasmids
- PCR: polymerase chain reaction — amplifies specific DNA sequences
- Gel electrophoresis: separates DNA fragments by size; smaller fragments travel farther
- DNA fingerprinting: forensic applications, paternity testing
- CRISPR and gene editing: mechanism and applications
- GMOs: benefits and concerns
- Cloning

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- Gel electrophoresis separates DNA by charge (wrong — all DNA has the same charge/mass ratio; separation is by fragment size)
- Larger DNA fragments travel farther in gel electrophoresis (wrong — smaller fragments travel farther)
- Transcription and translation occur in the same location (transcription occurs in the nucleus; translation at the ribosome)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. In gel electrophoresis, which DNA fragments travel the greatest distance from the well?
   A) Largest fragments B) Fragments with the highest charge C) Smallest fragments D) Fragments with the most base pairs

2. During translation, which molecule carries the correct amino acid to the ribosome?
   A) mRNA B) DNA C) tRNA D) rRNA

3. A scientist cuts DNA from two different organisms with the same restriction enzyme and combines them. The resulting DNA is called:
   A) Cloned DNA B) Recombinant DNA C) Plasmid DNA D) Amplified DNA

**EOC Review Strategy:** Draw the central dogma as a flowchart with locations in the cell. Practice reading a codon chart. Draw a gel electrophoresis result and rank bands by fragment size. Create a biotechnology tools chart: tool, what it does, application.

### Unit 10: Human Body Systems

**Key EOC concepts:**
- Homeostasis and negative feedback loops
- Major organ systems and their functions: circulatory, respiratory, digestive, excretory, nervous, endocrine, immune, reproductive, skeletal, muscular, integumentary
- Heart structure: four chambers, valves, blood flow pathway
- Gas exchange: alveoli, oxygen and carbon dioxide exchange
- Immune system: innate vs. adaptive; antigens, antibodies, B cells, T cells, memory cells; vaccines
- Nervous system: neurons, synapses, CNS vs. PNS, reflex arcs
- Endocrine system: hormones, feedback loops, key glands and hormones (insulin, glucagon, estrogen, testosterone, ADH)
- Digestive system: organs and their roles; mechanical and chemical digestion; enzyme specificity
- Kidney and nephron: filtration, reabsorption, excretion

**Common EOC misconceptions:**
- Arteries always carry oxygenated blood (pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood to the lungs)
- Vaccines contain the active disease (vaccines contain weakened, killed, or partial pathogens, not the active disease)
- The nervous system and endocrine system do the same thing (both coordinate body functions, but nervous = fast/electrical; endocrine = slow/chemical)

**EOC Practice Questions:**
1. A person's body temperature rises above normal. Sweat glands activate and blood vessels dilate. What type of feedback is this?
   A) Positive feedback B) Negative feedback C) Hormonal cascade D) Neural reflex

2. Which chamber of the heart pumps oxygenated blood to the body?
   A) Right atrium B) Right ventricle C) Left atrium D) Left ventricle

3. A student receives a flu vaccine. How does the vaccine protect against future infection?
   A) It kills all flu viruses in the body immediately
   B) It causes the immune system to produce memory cells
   C) It prevents viruses from entering the body
   D) It temporarily weakens the immune system

**EOC Review Strategy:** Create an organ systems summary table: system, major organs, primary function, connection to homeostasis. Draw a complete negative feedback loop for blood glucose. Trace the path of blood through the entire cardiovascular system in order.

## Final EOC Review Strategies (Cumulative)

**Big picture connections to review:**
- DNA structure → replication → transcription → translation → protein function (connects Units 2, 3, 6, 9)
- Cell structure → cell function → organism function → body systems (connects Units 3, 4, 5, 10)
- Genetic variation → natural selection → evolution → biodiversity → ecology (connects Units 6, 7, 8)

**High-yield study methods:**
1. Concept mapping: connect terms across units visually
2. Practice Punnett squares daily in the two weeks before the exam
3. Draw from memory: cell diagram, DNA replication, food web, feedback loop, blood circulation path
4. Use the CER prompts from each unit as oral practice — explain your reasoning aloud
5. Practice interpreting graphs: population growth curves, energy pyramids, gel electrophoresis images

**Common student errors on the Florida Biology EOC:**
- Confusing mitosis and meiosis outcomes
- Confusing xylem and phloem
- Misreading gel electrophoresis results (larger bands = smaller fragments)
- Confusing the location of transcription vs. translation
- Misidentifying the type of selection in an evolution scenario
- Confusing arteries and veins (remember: arteries leave the heart; veins return to it)
